Dear Dr. Fernandez,

We are a materials group in Mechanical Engineering at BYU (PI: Sterling Baird), and we
would like to use your facility as an outside academic user. Your services page lists
microwave digestion with HF, an HF introduction system, and NIST 86a (aluminum) among your
reference materials, which is why we are writing to you rather than handling HF ourselves.

**Samples.** An aluminum–silicon–magnesium alloy, AlSi10Mg (nominally about 89–90 wt% Al,
9–11 wt% Si, 0.2–0.45 wt% Mg, under 0.55 wt% Fe). We would like to send (1) the gas-atomized
feedstock powder and (2) pieces of parts printed from it by laser powder bed fusion, about
three samples to start. We can send powder, drillings/chips, or small solid pieces,
whichever you prefer, and several hundred milligrams of each.

**Why.** We are calibrating quantitative SEM-EDS on these alloys and need an independent
bulk composition to compare against. Silicon is a major element here (about 10 wt%), so
we need it to stay in solution. Our understanding is that a closed-vessel HF digestion with
no evaporation to dryness keeps Si, and that open heating or drying down loses it as SiF₄.
Magnesium precipitating as MgF₂ is our other concern.

**Questions.**

1. Can you digest this material so that Si is fully retained (closed vessel, no dry-down,
   boric acid or similar), or would you recommend another route, such as alkaline fusion or
   NaOH dissolution?
2. Which elements would you report, and with what uncertainty on the major elements?
   We are most interested in Al, Si, Mg and Fe. Later alloys will add Cu, Zn, Mn, Cr, Ni,
   Ti, Zr, Sn, Li, Sc, Ce and Er; we note Ti, Zr and Sn are in your HF-group suite.
3. Could you run a certified aluminum alloy alongside ours as a check, for example NIST 86a
   or an Al–Si casting-alloy CRM?
4. Would laser-ablation ICP-MS on a polished solid piece be an option instead of
   dissolution, and how would it be calibrated for an Al–Si alloy?
5. An estimated cost for three samples, and how you would like the samples packaged and
   shipped, or whether we may drop them off in person.

We would fill in your sample submission form before shipping.
