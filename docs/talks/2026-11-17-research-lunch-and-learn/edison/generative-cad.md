**Question.** How accurate are large language models at generating parametric CAD models from natural-language descriptions (for example CadQuery, build123d or OpenSCAD code generation, and text-to-CAD), and which techniques improve correctness, such as geometric validation with a CAD kernel, visual feedback from rendered images, iterative self-refinement or multi-agent review? Summarize benchmarks, reported success rates and remaining failure modes for producing manufacturable parts, including laboratory automation hardware.

# Accuracy of language-generated parametric CAD

**Short answer.** Large language models can often produce *executable* CadQuery, build123d, OpenSCAD, or FreeCAD code for relatively simple parts. They are substantially less reliable at satisfying exact dimensions, feature relationships, assembly constraints, and manufacturing requirements. There is no meaningful single “text-to-CAD accuracy”: studies variously score compilation, resemblance to a reference shape, satisfaction of prompt-specific tests, or engineering suitability, and these measures can disagree sharply. For example, a fine-tuned text-to-CAD model had only **2% invalid outputs** on one complex benchmark tier but approximately **0.04 volumetric intersection-over-union** with the references. (wang2026text2cadbenchabenchmark pages 6-7, wang2026text2cadbenchabenchmark pages 7-8)

The following studies illustrate the range of measured outcomes; their percentages **should not be pooled or compared as if they used the same definition of success**.

A more demanding independent test reinforces the distinction between running and being right. On **Text2CAD-Bench**, 600 examples have geometric or construction-sequence descriptions; reported results allow up to **three error-feedback retries**. GPT-5.2’s invalidity rate on L1 geometric prompts was **11.1%**, rising to **68.0%** on L3 prompts involving operations such as sweeps, lofts, and shells. On 100 application-oriented L4 cases, GPT-5.2 had **61% invalid outputs**; a visual judge scored its functional-feature fidelity **4.05/10 among valid outputs**, not among all attempts. Those conditional scores can therefore conceal substantial failure. (wang2026text2cadbenchabenchmark pages 12-14, wang2026text2cadbenchabenchmark pages 6-7, wang2026text2cadbenchabenchmark pages 7-8)

## What improves correctness?

**Execute, measure, and test against the specification.** Running generated code catches syntax and CAD-operation failures, but an independent CAD-kernel check is needed for solid validity, bounding dimensions, volume, hole counts and positions, and component intersections. CADSmith combined such measurements with refinement: on its 100-part CadQuery benchmark, execution rose from **95%** for one-shot generation to **100%** for its full pipeline, while mean Chamfer distance to reference solids fell from **28.37 to 0.74**. These are combined-system results, **not** an ablation proving that kernel measurements alone caused the entire gain. (barkley2603cadsmithmultiagentcad pages 4-6, barkley2603cadsmithmultiagentcad pages 6-7)

Prompt-specific executable tests provide a stronger check than a generic shape score. On CADTESTBENCH, Claude’s iterative execution baseline passed **all tests** on **58.0%** of detailed and **71.5%** of abstract prompts; adding CADTests and intermediate-geometry logs raised those rates to **62.5%** and **81.0%**, respectively. In a separate 125-generation expert audit, the test-based pass decision achieved **0.938 accuracy** against human consensus, although the experts themselves agreed only moderately and finite test suites can miss violations. In a 13-task **build123d/OpenCascade** study, a *measure-before-claiming-completion* protocol lowered the rate of valid-looking, explicitly claimed, but specification-wrong outputs from **14.1% to 3.2%**; actual task success increased from **64.1% to 77.6%**. An ablation suggests that enforcing the completion-claim contract, not simply offering feedback, was important. (mallis2026texttocadevaluationwith pages 7-8, mallis2026texttocadevaluationwith pages 8-9, rodrigues2026doneisnot pages 1-3, rodrigues2026doneisnot pages 6-8)

**Use rendered views as complementary evidence, not as a geometric oracle.** CADCodeVerify asks visual questions about renders and revises code. On CADPrompt’s 200 objects, GPT-4 zero-shot compilation increased from **92.0% to 94.0%**, while median point-cloud distance improved from **0.153 to 0.132**. CADSmith’s controlled removal of its three-view render left its kernel-based reviewer intact but, on its complex tier, lowered mean surface F1 from **0.85 to 0.74** and raised mean Chamfer distance from **1.42 to 49.68**. Conversely, CADTESTBENCH found **no gain** from adding four-view images to its execution-feedback baseline: for GPT-5.2, abstract-prompt all-tests pass remained **69.5%**, with higher token use. Visual feedback is thus task- and pipeline-dependent; an attractive render does not establish dimensions or hidden interfaces. (alrashedy2024generatingcadcode pages 7-9, barkley2603cadsmithmultiagentcad pages 6-7, mallis2026texttocadevaluationwith pages 7-8)

**Iterate with discriminating feedback; do not assume more turns help.** Query2CAD’s 57 FreeCAD tasks rose from **53.6% strictly judged correct on the first attempt** to **73.2% after one refinement** and **76.7% after up to three**. Its human-written corrections were more useful than image captions alone, limiting the autonomy of that result. CADSmith corrected some initially executable but badly shaped parts through its execution and geometric loops. However, later Query2CAD rounds added little, and another multi-agent study warned that repeatedly carrying unsuccessful revisions forward can obstruct convergence. (badagabettu2024query2cadgeneratingcad pages 4-6, barkley2603cadsmithmultiagentcad pages 6-7, ocker2503fromideato pages 5-8)

**Separate planning and review roles when useful, but evaluate the entire system and its cost.** CADSmith’s planner, coder, independent validator, and refiner improved its one-shot baseline, yet that comparison changes several components at once. In the build123d study, a planner–coder–checker arrangement attained approximately **95.5% precision when it claimed completion**, but claimed completion less often and **did not improve actual success** over one-shot generation. For OpenSCAD, CRAFT’s structured parameter plan, catalog retrieval, multi-view review, and repair exposed **13.1 parameters per model versus 4.2** for a single GPT-5.2 call; its overall fine-detail surface F1 was nevertheless **0.2530 versus 0.2587**. A budget-aware comparison found that five direct samples reached **0.2651 F1**, and adding visual correction to direct generation could worsen geometry. This is evidence for editable structure and selective benefits of review—not a blanket claim that more agents outperform well-prompted, comparably resourced generation. (barkley2603cadsmithmultiagentcad pages 4-6, rodrigues2026doneisnot pages 6-8, rafi2026craftcorrectiveand pages 5-6, rafi2026craftcorrectiveand pages 8-9, rafi2026craftcorrectiveand pages 9-11)

Where a part must carry loads, **test physics as well as geometry**. In a physics-in-the-loop CAD study, **49/83 (59.0%)** FEA-feedback designs versus **6/27 (22.2%)** without FEA feedback met its target safety-factor interval; adding planning reduced mean iterations to convergence from **10.77 to 4.44** in a separate ablation. These are simulated structural criteria, not proof of fabrication or service life. (berger2026physicsintheloopahybrid pages 4-6, berger2026physicsintheloopahybrid pages 6-7)

## Manufacturability and laboratory-automation hardware

A manufactured part imposes requirements beyond a closed, reference-shaped solid. **MUSE** explicitly evaluates 106 CadQuery design instances against code execution, watertight/manifold/self-intersection/overlap checks, and separate functionality, manufacturability, and assemblability rubrics. Its strongest listed model, GPT-5.5, executed on **77.36%** of cases and passed *all geometric checks* on **68.87%**; its **52.36% final score is an average rubric score after the failure funnel, not the percentage of parts proven manufacturable**. The study did **not physically manufacture** its outputs and does not simulate the complete assembly sequence or quantitatively test strength. The source’s cropped benchmark table provides a useful visual comparison of that execution-to-geometry-to-intent drop. (dong2605musebenchmarkingmanufacturable pages 6-7, dong2605musebenchmarkingmanufacturable pages 9-11, dong2026musebenchmarkingmanufacturable media 2c506f68)

There is limited but concrete evidence that agent-generated parametric parts can be built. **RocketSmith** generated build123d rocket-component scripts, fabricated and assembled **four** FDM rockets, and flight-tested them: all launched stably, but only **two were recovered in reflyable condition**. One suffered lower-airframe structural failure following the ejection charge and another had recovery-deployment problems. This demonstrates both feasibility and why slicing, physical fit checks, measured mass, and operational tests remain necessary after CAD validation. It is *not* a measured success rate for arbitrary text-to-CAD prompts or for laboratory equipment. (pak2026rocketsmithagenticadditive pages 13-19, pak2026rocketsmithagenticadditive pages 1-5, pak2026rocketsmithagenticadditive pages 23-28)

**For laboratory automation specifically, the evidence is thinner.** Published self-driving-lab work describes genuinely 3D-printed fixtures and labware—including a PLA plate made to fit a pipetting robot—but that is **not evidence that an LLM generated the plate’s parametric CAD from natural language**. Likewise, LLM systems that produce liquid-handler scripts address *robot programming*, not the design of new physical hardware. The reviewed CAD-generation benchmarks do not establish a general, physically verified success rate for LLM-designed lab-automation parts. (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 7-8, gao2025autonomousliquidhandlingrobotics pages 1-4)

For a proposed pipette adapter, plate holder, fluidic device, or robot fixture, the unresolved checks include measured mating tolerances and tip seating force, stiffness and fatigue under repeated motion, appropriate wall thickness and print orientation, material resistance to reagents and cleaning agents, fluid containment where relevant, and contamination or sterilization behavior. Reviews note that common printed plastics may swell or contaminate solvent-exposed work, layered surfaces can retain residues and microbes, and insufficient printed-part stiffness can impair robotic precision or pipette-tip pressing. **A recommended validation sequence**, inferred from these failures, is: formalize dimensions and interfaces; execute the parametric code; run kernel-based, requirement-specific tests across parameter changes; inspect multiple views; check process and load constraints; then print, measure fit, and test the actual laboratory operation under its intended chemical and cleaning conditions. No compile rate, render score, or simulation result substitutes for those final tests. (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 7-8, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 8-9, nel2021thedesignof pages 51-53, dong2605musebenchmarkingmanufacturable pages 9-11)
