2. Same rod bored ~3" deep, packed with powder, capped with a press-fit Al plug so the powder is compacted (the Al-cup idea from #16 and the Edison write-up above).

Bartosz's caveat: loose powder in the crucible often never fully melts. Particles stay discrete and sinter even well above the melting point, so some of the charge needs to already be molten for them to dissolve. Induction gives some stirring (the melt pulses) but he doesn't like relying on it; master alloy is better, and a small high-melting addition can just clump in one spot. For making master alloys from powder, arc melting is the better tool.

Crucible: max feedstock diameter **20 mm** (gap between the sealing rod and the crucible wall), inner height ~10–12 cm. Material can stand slightly proud but must not overfill once molten.
