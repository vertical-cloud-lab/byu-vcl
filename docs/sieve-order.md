# Sieves for atomized powder (#222)

The atomized Al 4047 has chunks in it, and an LPBF print needs it cut to the usual
laser powder bed fusion range, **20–63 µm**. Three 3" all-stainless Gilson sieves, stacked
coarse to fine over a pan, do both in one pass. 3" rather than 8" because each batch is tens of
grams: less powder stays in the mesh, and they are easier to clean between alloys. BYU Chem
Stores has no online catalog. Ask them first (801-422-2678, chemstores@chem.byu.edu), and if
they have nothing, order these.

**What to keep.** The powder on top of the #635 (20–63 µm) is the print fraction. Weigh every
fraction, because the yield tells you how many atomization runs a print needs.

**Using them.**
- Sieve with the cover on, wearing a dust mask. Fine Al is a combustible dust.
- Meshes at 20 µm clog (blind) when shaken dry by hand. Tap the frame and brush the underside
  gently, and do small amounts at a time. If it is too slow, ask whether the lab has a sieve
  shaker.
- Clean between alloys and keep each mesh for one alloy if possible. Never poke the #635 mesh.
