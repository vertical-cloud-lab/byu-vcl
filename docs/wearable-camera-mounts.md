# Wearable POV mounts: alternatives to the neck mount + phone

Issue [#290](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/issues/290). Many of the lab's videos
were shot with a phone on a magnetic neck mount. It works, but it gets uncomfortable over long
sessions. This page looks for something more comfortable that can still take a phone held by a
ring magnet (MagSafe, or a magnet ring stuck to an Android phone or case).

Prices, ratings and stock below were read from Amazon US on **2026-10-20**, from the CubXL Pi
rather than a CI runner (see [How this was gathered](#how-this-was-gathered)). Weights are each listing's own
figure. Nothing has been bought or tested here yet.

For camera glasses, and for smart glasses that are also safety eyewear, see
[smart-safety-glasses.md](smart-safety-glasses.md).

## Short version

**There is no lighter neck mount.** Every phone neck mount checked lists about 190–310 g before the
phone goes on. With a 170–230 g phone and a case, that's about 400–600 g hanging from the back of the
neck. Because the phone sits on a stem in front of the chest, it also pulls the band into the neck.
That weight comes from the design, so changing brands won't fix it. What helps is putting the weight
somewhere else, or carrying much less of it:

1. **A chest harness**, if you want to keep using phones. Nothing touches the neck; the weight is
   carried by both shoulders and a band around the ribs. About $25.
2. **A 52–53 g wearable camera on a magnetic pendant** (Insta360 GO Ultra or DJI Osmo Nano). This is
   about an eighth of what you wear now and by far the most comfortable, but it costs $389–450 and it
   isn't a phone.
3. **If you keep a neck mount, get one with a chest strap.** It weighs no less, but the strap ties the
   holder to your chest, so the phone stops swinging and levering on your neck.

To attach a ring-magnet phone magnetically to a clamp-style harness, add a MagSafe holder with a
GoPro-style base, e.g. [ThtRht](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F4CRKZ9K) ($7.97, 4.3 from 38 ratings,
reviewers mixed on how well its magnet holds). Check that its prongs match the harness's. The clamp
alone works with any phone, ring or no ring.

The harness's own weight matters less than where it sits. Even the 329 g CM028 hangs nothing from
the back of the neck: the load is split between two shoulder straps and the chest band. The phone
sits at the sternum rather than the upper chest, so expect a slightly lower angle than in the earlier
videos.

Overheating limits these more than battery does. Amazon's review summaries say the GO 3S "only
lasts about 10–15 minutes and overheats after about 16 minutes", and the Osmo Nano "only lasts about
five minutes in hot conditions". So skip the GO 3S for long takes. For the other two, try a full-length
lab session (or record below 4K) before the camera replaces a phone. A magnet pendant holds the camera
through clothing, so the less fabric between them the better. With a lab coat on, wear the pendant
under the coat so the camera holds through one layer, and clip on the safety cord.

## Ring magnets

- iPhone 12 and later have MagSafe built in and fit everything above.
- Phones without built-in magnets need a ring. The neck mounts ship one: TELESIN includes a
  "magnetic piece", SmallRig a magnetic ring, NEEWER two rings. A case with its own MagSafe magnet
  ring usually holds better than a thin adhesive plate. Amazon's summary of the NEEWER GP16 reviews
  says its magnet is strong enough to pull stick-on rings off phones. The SEANCHEER H02's review summary specifically mentions
  it working with a Google Pixel 9.
- Everything here holds the phone by magnet alone (apart from the clamp harnesses), and reviews of
  nearly every listing are mixed on how well it holds. **Use the tether** that most of them include.
  At a bench, a phone that falls off can land in the sink or the fume hood.

## Not worth it

- **Head straps for a phone.** The [ULANZI head strap](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0GRV53MY7) weighs
  220 g on its own, so with a phone that's ~400 g on your forehead instead of your neck.
- **Clip-on magnetic holders**, e.g. the [Spigen MagFit Clip](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F1QZ5CZ3)
  (100 g, 18 N). It's built for backpack straps, and several reviewers say the magnet isn't strong
  enough.
- **Backpack-strap holders.** They only work if you're wearing a backpack.
- **Neck mounts sold as "lightweight".** As the table shows, none of them is.

## How this was gathered

Amazon serves bot checks to datacenter IPs such as a GitHub Actions runner, so every Amazon request
went out from the **CubXL Pi** over Tailscale SSH. It was the Pi with the most headroom; the
stream-cam Pis are carrying live streams. Before using it I checked it read-only: load average
0.00, no CubOS job running, and the deck-camera script was the only lab software up. On the Pi
it ran **`curl` only**, capped at `--limit-rate 300k` with 8–25 s between requests. Each page was
streamed back gzip-compressed and parsed on the runner. Nothing was installed or written on the Pi.

- 16 searches: 13 returned results; 3 got Amazon's ~1.4 kB JavaScript interstitial (`bm-verify`).
  The first block came after ~10 requests at 8–14 s spacing. The next two searches, at 15–25 s, were
  blocked as well, but all 21 product pages fetched after them came through. Search pages are
  challenged more readily than product pages.
- [`docs/wearable-camera-mounts-amazon-2026-10-20.json`](wearable-camera-mounts-amazon-2026-10-20.json)
  holds what was parsed: every search result (title, price, rating, sponsored flag) and, for the 21
  products, the listed weight, materials, feature bullets and Amazon's "Customers say" summary. The
  first query, a connectivity test, wasn't kept. Raw HTML isn't committed.
- "Customers say" is Amazon's AI summary of the reviews, not a quote from any buyer. Treat it as rough.
- Several listings disagree with themselves on weight (SmallRig 5127: 6.8 oz in the description,
  8 oz in the spec table), and none has been weighed here.

To redo or extend it, from a runner on the tailnet:

```bash
python3 -I scripts/amazon_via_pi/fetch.py /tmp/amzn/raw search "chest harness mount phone magsafe"
python3 -I scripts/amazon_via_pi/fetch.py /tmp/amzn/raw dp B0D2RMJVDM B0D3DRXLN9
python3 -I scripts/amazon_via_pi/summarize.py /tmp/amzn/raw /tmp/amzn/summary.json
```

`--pi-env RPI_STREAM_CAM` (or another prefix with `_USERNAME`/`_HOSTNAME` variables) picks a
different Pi. Keep the default 15–25 s spacing or slower, and expect the odd search to be blocked: it
shows up in the summary as `"blocked": true` with 0 results.
