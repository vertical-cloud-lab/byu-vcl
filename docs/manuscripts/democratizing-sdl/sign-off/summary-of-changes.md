# What changed since the decision letter

**DD-PER-12-2024-000410, *Digital Discovery*.** Rejected at editorial assessment on 9 January 2025, with an invitation to resubmit a substantially revised version. Corresponding author for the revision: Sterling G. Baird. Status on 27 November 2026: written; waiting on co-authors.

The editor, Joshua Schrier, asked for three things: **(1)** take a position and use the projects to validate it; **(2)** say explicitly how each project supports or undermines it; **(3)** supply at least work-in-progress repositories for the three projects that had none (P1, P3, P7).

**Other changes**

- Retitled, because Doloi *et al.* (*Digital Discovery*, 2025) now holds our old title; §1 says how the papers differ.
- Sterling G. Baird is corresponding author, Brenden Pelkie stays first author, and Seth Leavitt joins for the revision. Contributions are per author (CRediT), as RSC requires above ten authors.
- References went from 32 to 45, all checked against Crossref; four preprints are now cited as published papers.
- Figures 1–9 are in citation order. Figure 1, the labour analysis, is new; Figures 4 and 8 were recovered from material the teams had submitted.
- New ESI: project descriptions as contributed (S1), the survey (S2), the evidence for every audit score (S3) and the labour analysis (S4). The analysis code is public; its Zenodo DOI follows at acceptance.
- §6 turns the findings into obligations for funders, journals, institutions and builders, building on this journal's own hardware guidelines. It commits every project to a public repository at submission and an archival deposit by publication. Generative-AI use is disclosed, as RSC requires.

**What the audit of our own projects found (§§5–6).** One of nine hardware projects is completely documented. Projects explain how to *run* their systems better than how to *buy and build* them. Some defects only a stranger would hit, such as merge-conflict markers in a firmware release and a Zenodo deposit with empty design folders. Two projects have no licence file. A v2 claim that troubleshooting is "the weakest column" did not survive the audit.

**What we need from you** is in your section of the per-author checklist. Every author approves the text and checks their affiliation, CRediT role, funding, conflicts and ORCID. Each team also confirms what is said about its project, its Table 5 row and the §6 deposit commitment.
