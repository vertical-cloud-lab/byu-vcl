## Notes on the picks

- **Fewer orders.** Orders 3 and 4 hold one item each. To drop Harbor Freight, buy any 10–12 in
  tongue-and-groove pliers on Amazon instead. (If 9 in is too small for the "big wrench",
  Harbor Freight's largest groove-joint pliers are the DOYLE 12 in, SKU 64466.) To drop Home Depot, the DeWalt TOUGHGRIP 120-pc set
  at Lowe's (rank 4 in [lab-drill-and-bits.md](lab-drill-and-bits.md)) is the fallback, but
  check that it has the 2.5–5 mm metric hex bits first.
- **Don't buy the Milwaukee bit set on Amazon.** The Amazon listings titled "48-32-5155" come from
  third-party sellers ("for Milwaukee", "compatible with", "replacement"), not Milwaukee.
- **Soldering iron.** The request was for the same iron the PCS uses, thought to be from Amazon.
  Its model isn't recorded anywhere here, so the Hakko is a stand-in. If the PCS iron is different,
  check that it takes T18 or 900M tips, or the ruthex insert tips won't fit.
- **Solder** is leaded 63/37, which is the easiest to work with. Wash hands after use. If the lab
  wants lead-free, swap it for an SAC305 spool.
- **Brushes around aluminum powder.** Of the three, brass is the non-sparking one. Use it, or
  the nylon, inside the atomizer.
- **Y fitting.** The photo shows a Y (PY6-style) push-to-connect union for 6 mm OD tube, the
  same 6×4 mm PE tube the rePowder air and argon lines use. Those lines run at up to 8 bar, so
  pick a fitting rated 10 bar or more, not one of the 8 bar ones. If a true T was meant, the
  same fitting comes as a 6 mm union tee (often sold as PE-6).
- **Files.** Diamond, because the file will also touch the tungsten, which dulls ordinary steel
  files. Aluminum clogs the grit, so brush it out with the brass brush as you go.
