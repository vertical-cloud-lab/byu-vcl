Question: Has anyone published a replicate-run repeatability study (several runs at identical settings) for ultrasonic atomization of molten metal into powder, reporting the run-to-run variation in particle size distribution, yield or sphericity?

Yes. **Yes—for particle-size distribution, with important qualifications.** Hinrichs et al., *Metals* **11**, 1723 (2021), DOI [10.3390/met11111723](https://doi.org/10.3390/met11111723), deliberately produced **three separate ultrasonic-atomized powder batches** (S1–S3) to assess process stability and reproducibility. Each came from a separately prepared rod of nominal Mo–20Si–52.8Ti (at.%). The paper gives one operating recipe for the batches—35 kHz, 60% vibration amplitude, 160 A arc current, and 25 L/min argon—and does not describe changing those settings between rods. This is a same-*nominal-setting* production-batch comparison, not three measurements of one powder sample. (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 2-4)

The clearest numerical run-to-run result is the **number-count-based particle-size *d*90**. Figure 3 also plots separate particle-size and aspect-ratio distributions for all three batches. (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction media 9bc38190, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction media 83a81446)

|---|---:|
| S1 | 63 |
| S2 | 72 |
| S3 | 78 |


*Table: Hinrichs et al. used separately cast rods of nominal Mo–20Si–52.8Ti (at.%) and reported common settings of 35 kHz, 60% amplitude, 160 A arc current, and 25 L/min Ar. Figure 3 provides number-count PSD and aspect-ratio curves for each batch, but no per-batch *d*10/*d*50, yield, or numerical sphericity values. (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 2-4, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction media 9bc38190)*

Thus *d*90 spans **63–78 µm**, a 15 µm between-batch range; the authors describe the size distributions as showing good reproducibility. They report that more than 85% of particles were 40–80 µm. These are particle-*count* distributions derived from image analysis, not mass-weighted sieve yields. (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7)

**What the study does not establish:** It gives no batch-specific *d*10/*d*50 or formal between-run variance estimate. It reports **45–50 wt.%** of the input rods becoming powder below 100 µm, but does not assign a yield to each of S1–S3. It states that over 90% of particles had an aspect ratio below 1.2 and plots batch-wise aspect-ratio curves, but gives no numerical between-batch sphericity statistic. The common settings are reported as a recipe, rather than accompanied by independently logged actual operating conditions for each run; the rods were prepared separately, so this is process-plus-feedstock batch reproducibility, not a study that isolates machine-only variation. (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 2-4, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction media 9bc38190, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction media 83a81446)

Other seemingly relevant reports should not be mistaken for the same experiment: Bałasz et al. (2023) compare a Ti6Al4V **ultrasonic** powder with a **gas-atomized** powder, not two repeated ultrasonic production runs; Wróbel et al. (2026) explicitly list reproducibility studies *across multiple atomization runs* as **future work**. I found a published three-batch precedent for **PSD repeatability**, but not, in these examined papers, a comparably explicit run-by-run quantitative study of **yield or sphericity repeatability**. (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 8-10, wrobel2026enhancingniobiumpowder pages 12-14)
