Question: For ultrasonic atomization of molten metals (sonotrode or vibrating-plate ultrasonic atomization, e.g. AMAZEMET rePowder, 3D Lab ATO Lab / ATO One, or lab-built 20-60 kHz systems), what published data exist on run-to-run or batch-to-batch repeatability at fixed process settings? Report, with numbers and citations: (a) the spread in particle size distribution (D10/D50/D90, span, or mass fraction in 15-45 um or 20-63 um) across replicate runs (standard deviation or coefficient of variation, and the number of replicates); (b) the spread in yield (fraction of the charge converted to powder, and fraction in the target size cut); (c) the spread in sphericity, circularity, aspect ratio or satellite fraction; (d) run-to-run variation in composition or oxygen pickup. Prioritize aluminum alloys (AlSi10Mg, Al-Si, 6xxx such as 6061/6063, Scalmalloy) but include other alloys (Ti, Fe, Ni, Cu, Mg, refractory). If true replicate data are absent, say so explicitly and give the closest proxies (error bars in parameter studies of amplitude, frequency or melt feed rate; repeated-measurement scatter), and list the process variables the literature identifies as the dominant sources of variability (amplitude, frequency, melt superheat, melt flow rate, sonotrode/plate material and temperature, wetting, oxygen).

# Run-to-Run Repeatability of Ultrasonic Atomization of Molten Metals: A Critical Review of Published Data

## Overview and Key Finding

**True fixed-setting, batch-to-batch replicate data for ultrasonic atomization (UA) of molten metals are extremely scarce in the open literature.** After a comprehensive search across journal publications and patents covering sonotrode/vibrating-plate UA systems (including AMAZEMET rePowder, 3D Lab ATO, and lab-built 20–60 kHz systems), only one study — Hinrichs et al. (2021) on Mo–20Si–52.8Ti — explicitly produced and compared multiple powder batches at nominally identical process settings to assess reproducibility. All other studies report single batches or parameter-variation campaigns that can only serve as proxies for process variability. No published data were found for true replicate runs on aluminum alloys (AlSi10Mg, AlSi12, 6061, 6063, Scalmalloy) or any other alloy system beyond the Mo-Si-Ti case. The following sections present what numerical data exist, organized by the requested categories.

## (a) Spread in Particle Size Distribution Across Replicate Runs

**True replicate data exist only for one alloy system.** Hinrichs et al. (2021) produced three independent UA batches (S1, S2, S3) of Mo–20Si–52.8Ti at.% alloy using a commercially available 3D Lab ATO system. More than 85% of particles fell in the 40–80 µm range across all three batches, with fewer than 6.4% below 40 µm and less than 0.2% below 20 µm (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7). The d90 values were 63, 72, and 78 µm for S1, S2, and S3, respectively — a range of 15 µm or approximately ±12% around the mean d90 of ~71 µm (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7). No D10, D50, span, or formal standard deviations/CVs for the PSD percentiles were reported.

**For aluminum alloys, no replicate-run PSD statistics have been published.** The closest proxy is Yankin et al. (2025), who varied arc angle, current, and vibration amplitude for AlSi12 on a 3D Lab ATO (35 kHz). Across their seven deliberate parameter changes, D10 ranged from 38.7 to 44.7 µm (variation 13.5%), D50 from 52.3 to 54.7 µm (variation 4.4%), D90 from 66.4 to 72.7 µm (variation 8.7%), and span from 0.40 to 0.64 (variation 37.5%) (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 3-4). These represent parameter-sensitivity ranges, not replicate scatter; measurement standard deviations were not reported despite at least three laser-diffraction readings per sample (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 2-3).

For 316L stainless steel (recycled via PBF-LB), Bashmakova et al. (2026) atomized material under 18 combinations of current (70–160 A) and vibration amplitude (60–80%) at 35 kHz, with three rods per condition. Across conditions: d10 = 12–15 µm, d50 = 23–34 µm, d90 = 45–49 µm, and span = 1.00–1.46 (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 9-11). D90 was notably stable (range ≈4 µm) because the powder was sieved at the upper end, while d50 showed wider variation, and the span ranged from 1.00 to 1.46 depending on conditions. However, within-condition (rod-to-rod) statistics were not reported (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 2-5).

For Ti6Al4V, Bałasz et al. (2023) reported two UA batches with count-based PSD values of D10/D50/D90 = 40.2/52.0/60.8 and 41.2/51.4/63.4 µm, and span values of 0.37 and 0.43, respectively (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 8-10). The D50 values differed by ~1 µm, suggesting modest batch-to-batch variation, but with n = 2 and different underlying conditions, no statistical conclusion can be drawn.

For niobium, Wróbel et al. (2026) reported a single-batch PSD of D10/D50/D90 = 44.12/54.99/66.85 µm and span = 0.413 (wrobel2026enhancingniobiumpowder pages 10-12). No replicate data exist.

## (b) Spread in Yield

**No study reports replicate-run yield statistics (SD or CV) at fixed settings.** The available yield data vary considerably across alloys and equipment configurations, but always reflect parameter studies rather than replicate production runs:

- **Mo–Si–Ti (Hinrichs 2021):** 45–50 wt.% of input rods converted to particles <100 µm, reported as a pooled range across three batches without batch-specific values (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7).
- **AlSi12 (Yankin 2025):** Approximately 75% of the total powder passed a 63 µm sieve and was considered suitable for SLM (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 2-3).
- **316L recycled (Bashmakova 2026):** Usable-powder yield ranged from 47% to 63% across the 18 process conditions. The highest yield (63%) was at minimum current (70 A) and lowest amplitude (60%). At amplitude-grouped averages: 59% at 60%, 53% at 70%, and 51% at 80% amplitude (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 13-15).
- **Ti6Al4V (Bałasz et al. 2023):** 94% of feed wire converted to powder <100 µm; 70.3% by mass in the 43–83 µm (63 ± 20 µm) range; after gas-flow optimization, >98% recovery was claimed (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 7-8, scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 8-10).
- **Recycled Ti alloy (Ciftci et al. 2025, AMAZEMET rePowder):** Over 85% of atomized powder was below 100 µm (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6).

The yield variation across process conditions for 316L (47–63%, a range of 16 percentage points) provides the closest proxy for the sensitivity of yield to process perturbations (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 13-15).

## (c) Spread in Sphericity, Circularity, Aspect Ratio, and Satellite Fraction

**No published study reports run-to-run variability (SD or CV) for sphericity or satellite fraction.** The available data are:

- **Mo–Si–Ti (Hinrichs 2021):** Greater than 90% of particles had aspect ratio <1.2 across all three batches, with particles described as satellite-free (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7). No batch-specific sphericity distributions were tabulated.
- **Nb (Wróbel 2026):** Mean sphericity 0.94; satellites, agglomerates, and oxidation defects were not observed (wrobel2026enhancingniobiumpowder pages 10-12).
- **316L recycled (Bashmakova 2026):** Sphericity coefficient ranged from 0.84 to 0.90 across 18 process conditions, compared with 0.56 for virgin gas-atomized powder (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 7-9). The range of 0.06 across conditions is a proxy for process-induced shape variability.
- **Ti6Al4V (Bałasz et al. 2023):** Mean sphericity 0.88–0.90 for the two reported UA entries (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 8-10).
- **Sn–58Bi (Yang et al. 2026):** At the optimized condition (30% amplitude, 199 °C, Ar), sphericity = 0.87 and satellite index = 1.07. Sphericity decreased and satellite index increased with melt temperature (yang2026effectsofultrasonic pages 5-10, yang2026effectsofultrasonic pages 10-15).

## (d) Run-to-Run Variation in Composition and Oxygen Pickup

**The Hinrichs et al. (2021) Mo–Si–Ti dataset is the only published study with batch-specific oxygen measurements at fixed nominal UA settings.** The three batches yielded: S1 = 1450 ± 20 wt.ppm O, S2 = 1330 ± 10 wt.ppm O, S3 = 1280 ± 30 wt.ppm O (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7). Starting from a common as-cast oxygen content of ~460 ppm, the oxygen pickup during atomization was approximately 820–990 ppm per batch. The inter-batch mean is ~1353 ppm, with a batch-to-batch sample standard deviation of approximately 87 ppm and coefficient of variation of approximately 6.4%. ICP-OES analysis showed no significant changes in alloy composition (Mo, Si, Ti) across the three batches (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 7-9, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7).

For **316L stainless steel** (Bashmakova et al. 2026), the main alloying elements (Cr, Ni, Mo) remained within specification across the two extreme atomization conditions (70A/60% vs. 160A/80%). The most notable compositional change was a three-fold reduction in manganese content (from 0.88 wt.% in virgin powder to 0.27–0.28 wt.% in both atomized samples), attributed primarily to Mn evaporation during PBF-LB and atomization (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 11-13). Oxygen content was consistently elevated relative to the virgin powder, with higher current yielding higher oxygen and larger vibration amplitude tending to reduce it; nitrogen remained nearly unchanged (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 13-15).

For **recycled Ti alloy** (Ciftci et al. 2025, AMAZEMET rePowder), the oxygen pickup during UA from machining chips was approximately 220 ppm, with total powder oxygen below 600 ppm, meeting ASTM F2924 requirements (ciftcli2025alloydevelopmentfrom pages 3-6).

## Absence of True Replicate Data for Aluminum Alloys

**No published study was found reporting true replicate-run (fixed-setting, batch-to-batch) UA data for any aluminum alloy** — including AlSi10Mg, AlSi12, 6061, 6063, or Scalmalloy. The Yankin et al. (2025) AlSi12 study comes closest by reporting PSD across multiple process conditions on a 3D Lab ATO system, but each condition was run once, and measurement-level uncertainty was not published (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 2-3, yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 3-4). The same gap exists for copper (only single-batch characterizations exist for UA of recycled copper), magnesium alloys, nickel-based superalloys, and high-entropy alloys processed by sonotrode UA.

Key quantitative proxies include:

- **AlSi12 parameter study (Yankin 2025):** Span (the most sensitive PSD metric) varied by 37.5% across three process variables, while D50 varied by only 4.4% (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 3-4, yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 1-2).
- **316L parameter study (Bashmakova 2026):** D50 ranged from 23 to 34 µm (a factor of ~1.5×) and yield from 47% to 63% across 18 conditions; 70% vibration amplitude conditions showed anomalous PSD fluctuations attributed to transitional capillary-wave breakup (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 9-11).
- **Sn–58Bi temperature study (Yang 2026):** D50 ranged from 91.6 to 106.3 µm (16% variation) across a 300 °C superheat range, with competing effects of viscosity reduction and oxidation (yang2026effectsofultrasonic pages 10-15).
- **Process stability failures (Bałasz 2024, 316L):** 5 of 16 planned atomization runs were excluded due to process instability, predominantly at high wire-feed speeds or low currents, illustrating the sharp boundaries of the stable operating window (bałasz2024aninvestigationof pages 2-4).

## Summary and Conclusions

1. **True batch-to-batch replicate data are essentially absent** for UA of molten metals. Only Hinrichs et al. (2021) provide a three-batch comparison for Mo–Si–Ti, finding d90 values of 63–78 µm, consistent >85% yield in the 40–80 µm range, aspect ratios <1.2 for >90% of particles, and oxygen contents of 1280–1450 ppm with a batch-to-batch CV of ~6.4% (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7).

2. **For aluminum alloys, no replicate-run statistics exist** in the published literature through mid-2026. PSD and yield data are available for AlSi12 but only as single-run parameter studies (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 3-4, yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 2-3).

3. **Parameter-sensitivity studies provide proxy bounds:** At fixed frequency, D50 appears relatively stable (CV < 5% for AlSi12 across parameter changes), while the distribution span and the fine-particle fraction (D10) are more sensitive (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 3-4). Yield varies by ~16 percentage points across current/amplitude settings for 316L (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 13-15).

4. **Sphericity and satellite metrics** are reported condition-by-condition but never with replicate-based uncertainty. Values of 0.84–0.94 for sphericity and satellite-free to satellite-index 1.07 are typical for optimized UA conditions across alloys (wrobel2026enhancingniobiumpowder pages 10-12, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 7-9, yang2026effectsofultrasonic pages 5-10).

5. **Oxygen pickup** is a systematic concern. The one three-batch oxygen dataset shows ~6.4% CV (hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 4-7); alloy composition was stable across batches for Mo-Si-Ti and across process conditions for 316L (bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 11-13, hinrichs2021flexiblepowderproduction pages 7-9).

6. **The dominant process variables** identified as sources of powder variability are, in approximate order of influence: ultrasonic frequency (theoretical), electrical current/arc power (strongest experimentally observed), arc/torch angle, vibration amplitude, melt superheat/temperature, wire-feed rate, atmosphere purity, sonotrode wetting, and chamber gas-flow pattern (yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 3-4, bashmakova2026ontheultrasonic pages 13-15, yang2026effectsofultrasonic pages 10-15, yankin2025effectofultrasonic pages 1-2, scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 7-8, scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 4-5).

This review confirms a significant gap in the published UA literature: the absence of formal repeatability studies following standard metrological practice (replicate production runs, reported SD/CV, documented number of replicates) for any alloy system, and particularly for the aluminum alloys of greatest interest for additive manufacturing.
