# q4_low_cost_open_hardware_sdl

## Question

State of the art in LOW-COST, OPEN-SOURCE, MODULAR self-driving lab hardware: Cartesian-gantry and 3D-printer-derived platforms, Opentrons OT-2 based SDLs, and 'science jubilee' style toolchanger systems. Cite specific published platforms with DOIs and their approximate build cost. Emphasise papers whose contribution is the VERSATILITY or MODULARITY of one platform across several different measurement modalities (i.e. the same robot reconfigured for optical, electrochemical, thermal, rheological tasks) rather than a single-purpose demonstrator - what is the strongest published precedent for a 'one platform, many instruments' claim, and how did those papers quantify or demonstrate versatility? Also: which of them handle POWDER dosing as well as liquids, and how is mixing/stirring implemented?

## Answer

## State of the Art in Low-Cost, Open-Source, Modular Self-Driving Lab Hardware

### 1. Overview of Platform Categories

Two comprehensive reviews—Lo et al.'s "Frugal Twin" review (lo2024reviewoflowcost pages 10-11, lo2023reviewoflowcost pages 12-14, lo2023reviewoflowcost pages 8-12) and Doloi et al.'s review of 3D-printing for laboratory automation (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 19-20, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 9-10, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 18-19)—provide the most complete surveys of these platforms to date. The following sections summarize each major architecture class, specific published platforms, their approximate costs, and how they handle versatility, powder dosing, and mixing.

### 2. Cartesian-Gantry and 3D-Printer-Derived Platforms

This is the largest and most heterogeneous category of low-cost SDL hardware. These platforms typically repurpose the XYZ Cartesian motion systems of consumer 3D printers or CNC routers, replacing the print head with laboratory-relevant tools such as syringes, pipettes, or sensors.

**FINDUS** is an open-source, modular liquid-handling workstation using an XYZ Cartesian gantry to control pipettes. Its build cost is under $400, and it achieves pipetting errors below 0.3% (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 9-10). **Osmar** (~$700) is a Cartesian-gantry microsyringe autosampler using 3D-printer G-code mechanics for liquid and gas sampling, including chromatography (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 19-20). **EvoBot** (~$500) is a particularly modular open-source robot whose hardware and software can be adapted for droplet placement, mixing, droplet manipulation, pipetting, and biological-material handling with feedback control for real-time adjustments (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11). **Sidekick** (~$710) uses an armature-based design intended to reduce part count and assembly complexity (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11). The **Ender3 Syringe Pump** ($170) replaces the printer extrusion head with a 3D-printed liquid-handling setup and uses the printer's Cartesian motion system directly (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 10-11).

The **PANDA-film** platform represents a more sophisticated integration of the Cartesian-gantry approach. Built around a modified CNC gantry with a 300 × 400 mm² working area, it features a 3D-printed process automation widget (PAW) that provides a standardized interface for exchangeable tools (quinn2026anautomatedsystem pages 1-4, quinn2026anautomatedsystem pages 8-12, quinn2026anautomatedsystem pages 4-8). The PAW integrates six modules: a three-electrode electrochemical cell, electromagnetic vial capper/decapper, telecentric-lens imaging system, robotic pipette, programmable LED lighting, and Arduino-based embedded control. PANDA-film demonstrates particularly strong multi-modality by combining automated electrodeposition, reflectance-based film-thickness measurement, and a novel top-down optical contact-angle assay on a single gantry. The contact-angle method was validated across eight substrate materials, yielding RMSE of 1.3° and MAE of 1.1° relative to conventional side-view goniometry (quinn2026anautomatedsystem pages 12-16). The platform's modular PAW design allows different tools to be integrated without permanently modifying the gantry (quinn2026anautomatedsystem pages 8-12, quinn2026anautomatedsystem media e6175061, quinn2026anautomatedsystem media 9c12f6c3).

### 3. Opentrons OT-2–Based SDL Platforms

The Opentrons OT-2, a commercially available open-source liquid handler (~$10,000), has become one of the most widely adopted platforms for building SDLs because of its Python API, SLAS-standard deck, and relatively low price (nazeri2025leveragingflexiblepipettebased pages 1-2, fiskerbødker2025ampere2anopenhardware pages 1-2).

**AMPERE-2** is an open-hardware robotic platform for automated electrodeposition and electrochemical validation of multi-element catalysts, built around an OT-2 (fiskerbødker2025ampere2anopenhardware pages 1-2, fiskerbødker2025ampere2anopenhardware pages 2-3, fiskerbødker2025ampere2anopenhardware pages 3-4). Its custom hardware includes an array reactor with 15 CNC-machined HDPE conical wells, interchangeable electrode tools (nickel, carbon, or platinum), reagent and tool cartridges, a cleaning bath, peristaltic pumps, temperature sensors, an Arduino controller, and a potentiostat. Mixing is achieved via a 50 W, 44 kHz ultrasonic transducer integrated into the cartridge base, supplemented by two 17 W heating elements with PID temperature control (fiskerbødker2025ampere2anopenhardware pages 3-4). The platform demonstrated reproducible OER overpotential measurements with 16 mV uncertainty and completes one sample in approximately 65 minutes (fiskerbødker2025ampere2anopenhardware pages 1-2).

**BRINE** (Bayesian Robotic Investigator of Novel Electrolytes) is a cost-effective closed-loop SDL for electrolyte discovery, also built on an OT-2 (ramezani2026brineacosteffective pages 2-3, ramezani2026brineacosteffective pages 1-2). It uses a modified P1000 pipette carrying a custom 3D-printed parallel-platinum-strip electrode for two-electrode electrochemical impedance spectroscopy. A Bayesian optimization engine autonomously selects compositions, runs experiments, analyzes data, and updates the search. BRINE tested approximately 230 unique aqueous electrolyte compositions in under 20 hours, identifying conductivities up to 32.13 S/m (ramezani2026brineacosteffective pages 1-2). Mixing relies on repeated pipetting and dispensing rather than mechanical stirring.

**Nazeri et al.** demonstrated an elegant minimal conversion of the OT-2 into a dual-function preparation and imaging platform by developing a 3D-printed adapter (~$110) that lets a pipette mount carry a USB camera, achieving real-time monitoring of hydrogel crosslinking (nazeri2025leveragingflexiblepipettebased pages 1-2, nazeri2025leveragingflexiblepipettebased pages 2-4).

### 4. Science Jubilee–Style Toolchanger Systems

The **Jubilee** platform, originally developed as an open-hardware, multi-tool digital fabrication system, has been adapted for laboratory science under the name **Science Jubilee** (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 169-174, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 174-178, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 189-193, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 1-6, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 165-169). The base Jubilee kit costs less than $2,000, can be assembled from readily available parts and 3D-printed components, and requires no previous building skills (lo2024reviewoflowcost pages 10-11). Its defining feature is automatic tool changing: a Duet3D-controlled kinematic coupling enables the gantry to pick up and park different tools during a single protocol.

The Science Jubilee Python-based software separates machine control from tool-specific actions (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 174-178). The tool library supports four categories of tools: motor-controlled tools connected to the Duet3D board; GPIO-actuated tools; externally triggered or microcontroller-controlled tools; and peripheral deck tools (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 174-178). Examples include OT-2 pipettes, syringes, peristaltic pumps, filament extruders, electromagnets, lights, heaters, sonicator probes, stir plates, and hot plates (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 174-178).

The most substantial published demonstration of Science Jubilee is the **Sonication Station** for CdSe nanocrystal synthesis, described in Politi et al. (DOI: 10.1039/D3DD00033H) (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 121-125, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 125-130). This configuration equipped a Jubilee with a QSonica Q125 sonicator probe and camera tool. An OT-2 prepared precursor solutions, and the Jubilee sonicated each well in a 96-well plate at 50% amplitude from a 100 W, 20 kHz source. The platform tested 625 unique synthesis conditions in triplicate (1,875 total samples) in less than two months (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 121-125, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 130-134). UV-Vis extinction and photoluminescence spectroscopy were performed externally on microplate readers. A separate demonstration used a Jubilee with a Raspberry Pi camera and OT-2 P300 pipette for an autonomous color-matching campaign with Bayesian optimization (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 189-193).

Politi's thesis further demonstrated the Jubilee ecosystem as part of a broader materials acceleration platform for deep eutectic solvents, incorporating high-throughput electrochemical characterization (EIS, cyclic voltammetry, chronopotentiometry), UV-Vis spectroscopy, photoluminescence spectroscopy, and IR bolometry for phase-transition temperature screening using the open-hardware PhasIR system (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 50-55, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 108-113, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 113-117). While these characterization steps were not all performed by a single Jubilee simultaneously, the thesis demonstrates a workflow vision in which Jubilee's tool-changing capability could ultimately unify synthesis, processing, and multiple characterization modalities on one platform. Proposed future additions include an SLAS-footprint hot plate, solid dispenser, and autosampler for connecting experiments to characterization instruments (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 189-193).

### 5. The Strongest Precedent for "One Platform, Many Instruments"

**RoboChem-Flex** (DOI: 10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-73xqf) provides the strongest published precedent for demonstrating a single SDL platform across diverse reaction classes and analytical modalities (pilon2025aflexibleand pages 1-4, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 4-9, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 9-14, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 14-19, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 19-23). This low-cost (~$5,000), modular self-driving laboratory combines customizable in-house-built hardware, 3D-printed components, and an open-source Python-based software framework incorporating modular Bayesian optimization. The platform supports both fully autonomous closed-loop and human-in-the-loop configurations, the latter leveraging shared departmental analytical instruments to minimize cost.

RoboChem-Flex was validated across **six diverse case studies** spanning photocatalysis (trifluoromethylation, deoxygenative C–H alkylation, hydrogen–deuterium exchange), biocatalysis (enzymatic reduction), thermal cross-couplings (Buchwald–Hartwig amination with transfer learning), and enantioselective catalysis (photocatalytic [2+2] cycloaddition) (pilon2025aflexibleand pages 1-4, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 4-9, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 9-14, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 14-19, pilon2025aflexibleand pages 19-23). Supported measurement modalities include inline benchtop ¹⁹F NMR, UHPLC-UV/MS, and low-cost Raman spectroscopy. Versatility was quantified through case-study breadth, successful preparative-scale reactions, and the demonstration of advanced optimization strategies (multi-objective Bayesian optimization, transfer learning from one reaction to another requiring only four new experiments) rather than a formalized modularity score.

**PANDA-film** (DOI: 10.1063/5.0309809) provides a strong precedent for multi-modal *characterization* within a single gantry: one platform combines electrochemical deposition, film-thickness measurement, and optical contact-angle imaging. Its contact-angle validation across eight substrates with quantified error metrics (RMSE 1.3°, MAE 1.1°) is notably rigorous (quinn2026anautomatedsystem pages 1-4, quinn2026anautomatedsystem pages 12-16).

**Science Jubilee** offers the strongest *architectural* argument for a "one platform, many instruments" claim, as its automatic toolchanger hardware and modular Python software are explicitly designed to support arbitrary tool additions (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 169-174, politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 174-178). However, the demonstrated breadth to date (sonication + liquid handling + camera on the Jubilee; EIS, CV, UV-Vis, PL, and IR bolometry in the broader thesis workflow) is partly aspirational, with not all modalities operating through the toolchanger in a single demonstrated campaign.

The **Chemputer** (>$30,000) and **mini-Chemputer** (~$10,000) technically connect to the broadest instrument suite—hotplates, photoreactors, flow reactors, rotary evaporators, NMR, UV-Vis, IR, and electrospray-MS—but exceed the "frugal" cost threshold and constitute a modular instrument network rather than a single reconfigurable gantry (lo2023reviewoflowcost pages 12-14).

### 6. Powder Dosing and Mixing/Stirring

Powder and solid handling remains a widely recognized bottleneck for low-cost SDLs. The Lo et al. review explicitly noted that low-cost SDL examples for solid-state materials were "effectively nonexistent" and proposed colored wax powder melting/mixing as a frugal-twin surrogate (lo2023reviewoflowcost pages 8-12).

**Miau** (~$900) is the most directly relevant low-cost Cartesian platform for powder dosing: it uses a dual-function gripper to manipulate tin capsules and transfer sub-milligram powders for isotopic and elemental analyses (doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 19-20, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 18-19). However, Miau is a single-purpose dosing/autosampling system rather than a multimodal SDL.

**STORMS** (STOchastic Robotic MicroSampling) is a recently reported system for high-throughput sub-milligram and milligram solid sampling. Rather than attempting deterministic mass control, it leverages stochastic sampling combined with robotic automation and glass encapsulation, demonstrating high precision across a range of solid materials with gains in speed and reproducibility over conventional sampling workflows (hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 7-11).

For deep eutectic solvent (DES) synthesis, Politi and Rodriguez's high-throughput protocol adapted the standard solid-mixing synthesis route by pre-dissolving solid quaternary ammonium salts into liquid hydrogen-bond-donor solvents, enabling dispensing by liquid handlers (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 60-64). This is a common workaround: rather than dosing solids directly, the solid feedstocks are dissolved into stock solutions and then handled as liquids.

The Science Jubilee platform has proposed but not yet demonstrated a solid dispenser tool (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 189-193).

**Mixing and stirring** are implemented in diverse ways across platforms:
- **Ultrasonic mixing**: AMPERE-2 uses a 44 kHz, 50 W ultrasonic transducer built into the cartridge base as the primary mixing mechanism (fiskerbødker2025ampere2anopenhardware pages 3-4). The Jubilee Sonication Station employs a 20 kHz sonicator probe for both sonochemical synthesis and as an implicit mixing mechanism (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 125-130).
- **Magnetic stirring**: The Geneva-wheel platform by Salley et al. uses magnetic stirring in each of its 24 reactor positions, coordinated by Geneva-wheel-driven parallel dispensing (lo2024reviewoflowcost pages 10-11, hao2025autonomousmaterialssynthesis pages 7-11).
- **Pipette-based mixing**: BRINE and several OT-2-based systems rely on repeated aspiration and dispensing cycles for mixing (ramezani2026brineacosteffective pages 2-3).
- **Slug-flow mixing**: RoboChem-Flex achieves mixing through pumped slug flow and reactor residence in continuous flow reactors (pilon2025aflexibleand pages 4-9).
- **Peripheral stirring**: The Jubilee tool library supports stir plates and hot plates as deck-mounted peripherals (politi2024opensciencematerialsacceleration pages 174-178).
- **Chemputer**: Connects to conventional hotplate/stirrer modules within its networked synthesis hardware (lo2023reviewoflowcost pages 12-14).

### 7. Summary and Outlook

No single platform has yet demonstrated fully general reconfiguration across optical, electrochemical, thermal, and rheological measurement modalities from a single low-cost gantry. The Science Jubilee (~$2,000) offers the strongest *architectural* foundation for such universality due to its automatic tool-changing design, but its published demonstrations to date focus on sonochemical synthesis and color-matching rather than spanning all four modality classes. RoboChem-Flex (~$5,000) provides the strongest *empirical* evidence of one platform operating across multiple chemistry and measurement types in published case studies, though it is a flow-chemistry system rather than a Cartesian gantry. PANDA-film demonstrates the most tightly integrated multi-modal characterization (electrochemistry + optics) on a single CNC gantry. True powder dosing remains largely unaddressed in the low-cost SDL landscape, with Miau (~$900) and STORMS being the primary published examples, and most platforms resorting to pre-dissolving solids into stock solutions as a practical workaround.
