# q2_inline_spectroscopy_sensor_fusion

## Question

State of the art in coupling IN-LINE / IN-SITU VIBRATIONAL SPECTROSCOPY (ATR-FTIR, Raman, NIR, UV-Vis) to automated liquid-handling or flow systems as a real-time composition sensor, and in SENSOR FUSION where spectroscopy is measured SIMULTANEOUSLY with a physical property such as ionic conductivity on the same sample. Cite specific papers with DOIs. Cover: (a) combined ATR-FTIR + conductivity flow cells for electrolyte speciation; (b) chemometrics (PLS, MCR-ALS, classical least squares) versus neural-network / nonlinear models for predicting concentrations of all species from a spectrum of a concentrated multicomponent electrolyte where ion pairing makes the spectral mixing NONLINEAR and Beer-Lambert additivity fails; (c) how much training data those models needed and how they were validated on held-out or blind test sets; (d) Raman versus IR practicality for fiber-coupled integration into a robot.

## Answer

# State of the Art: In-Line Vibrational Spectroscopy Coupled to Automated/Flow Systems and Sensor Fusion for Electrolyte Composition Monitoring

## Overview

The coupling of vibrational spectroscopy (ATR-FTIR, Raman, NIR, UV-Vis) with automated liquid-handling or flow systems as real-time composition sensors has advanced substantially, driven by process analytical technology (PAT) frameworks and the emerging self-driving laboratory paradigm. Simultaneously, sensor fusion — the combination of spectroscopy with physical property measurements such as ionic conductivity on the same sample — has been demonstrated in several application domains. The following synthesizes the current literature across the four requested areas.

## (a) Combined Spectroscopy + Conductivity Flow Cells for Electrolyte Speciation

The most mature demonstrations of spectroscopy–conductivity sensor fusion come from industrial process monitoring rather than battery electrolyte research per se. Bryan, Levitskaia, and Casella (2013) developed a fiber-coupled monitoring system for radiochemical reprocessing streams in which Raman spectroscopy and Vis-NIR absorbance probes were deployed remotely in flowing PUREX-process aqueous and organic streams, supplemented by conductivity, Coriolis density, temperature, and flow measurements (bryan2013spectroscopiconlinemonitoring pages 1-3). Raman was used primarily for concentrated Raman-active species (uranyl, oxo-anions, water, organics), while Vis-NIR absorption tracked actinides whose concentrations made Raman quantification impractical. Conductivity and density provided independent bulk-composition constraints, and PLS chemometric models trained on static spectra were validated with online centrifugal-contactor extraction tests.

In the CO₂-capture domain, Kachko et al. (Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 2015, 54, 5769–5776; DOI: 10.1021/acs.iecr.5b00691) demonstrated a comprehensive multi-sensor chemometric system fusing NIR absorption spectroscopy with in-line conductivity, density, pH, sound velocity, and refractive index for real-time monitoring of aqueous amine solvents at a postcombustion CO₂-capture pilot plant (kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 6-7, kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 1-2, kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 3-4). A temperature-dependent PLS model was constructed with 53 designed compositions (fractional factorial, 5 levels) and predicted AMP, PZ, and CO₂ concentrations with average deviations of ±1.8%, ±1.3%, and ±3.9%, respectively, validated against offline LC-MS and hot-phosphoric-acid analysis over nearly 850 hours of pilot-plant operation (kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 6-7, kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 7-8). The NIR flow-through cell used a 5 mm path length with fiber-optic coupling and a tungsten-halogen source spanning 1017–2044 nm (kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 3-4).

While no single published study was identified that integrates exclusively an ATR-FTIR crystal and a conductivity electrode into a single monolithic flow cell specifically for battery electrolyte speciation, the architectural patterns above — spectroscopy plus conductivity plus other physical properties, all measured in series or in parallel on a flowing bypass stream — provide a proven blueprint. The Carrillo-Bohórquez, Kumar, and Kuroda perspective (J. Phys. Chem. C 2024, 128, 18695–18710; DOI: 10.1021/acs.jpcc.4c06002) explicitly advocates coupling spectroscopy with transport measurements including conductivity as essential for characterizing high-concentration electrolytes, because spectroscopic signatures alone are ambiguous when extensive ion pairing and aggregation are present (carrillobohorquez2024thechallengeof pages 10-14, carrillobohorquez2024thechallengeof pages 30-33).

## (b) Chemometrics (PLS, MCR-ALS, CLS) vs. Neural Network / Nonlinear Models

### Linear approaches and their limitations

Classical least squares (CLS) and PLS regression assume an approximately linear relationship between absorbance and concentration (Beer–Lambert additivity). For dilute or moderately concentrated mixtures where components absorb independently, these methods are highly effective. Angulo et al. (Digital Discovery 2022, 1, 35–44; DOI: 10.1039/d1dd00027f) showed that for aqueous mixtures of up to six components measured by FTIR in a flow cell, simple linear regression (including PCR) achieved R² = 0.955–0.986 and MAE = 0.28–0.52 wt%, consistently outperforming a one-hidden-layer artificial neural network (ANN) with R² = 0.854–0.977 (angulo2022machinelearningenhanced pages 1-3, angulo2022machinelearningenhanced pages 8-9). However, these mixtures were prepared under approximately additive conditions, and the authors used Beer's law to generate synthetic training spectra (angulo2022machinelearningenhanced pages 3-4).

### MCR-ALS and blind source separation

Maggioni, Kocevska, and Rousseau (2019) applied an MCR-ALS blind source separation workflow (SVD rank estimation → ICA initialization → constrained MCR-ALS with non-negativity and mass-balance closure) to simultaneous Raman and ATR-FTIR spectra of a five-sodium-salt aqueous simulant (maggioni2019blindsourceseparation pages 1-2, maggioni2019blindsourceseparation pages 2-3). IR source/reference correlations exceeded 0.95, but residuals near nitrate/nitrite bands revealed deviations attributed to nonlinear nitrate–nitrite interactions, producing misleading components — direct evidence that Beer–Lambert superposition breaks down even in relatively simple multicomponent ionic solutions (maggioni2019blindsourceseparation pages 2-3). The authors concluded that MCR-ALS works well under linear conditions but generates spurious spectral components when nonadditivity is present.

### Nonlinear approaches: ANN, SVR, and quadratic PLS

When Beer–Lambert additivity fails — as in concentrated electrolytes where ion pairing, aggregation, and solvent-restructuring change spectral shapes nonlinearly with concentration — nonlinear models are needed. Zavala-Ortiz et al. (Biotechnol. Bioeng. 2022, 119, 535–549; DOI: 10.1002/bit.27997) conducted a rigorous three-way comparison of PLS, ANN regression (ANNR), and support-vector regression (SVR) for in-situ NIR monitoring under nonlinear conditions. For glucose, R² values were 0.85, 0.93, and 0.98 and RMSEP was 3.37, 2.29, and 1.32 mM for PLSR, SVR, and ANNR, respectively (zavala‐ortiz2022comparisonofpartial pages 25-29, zavala‐ortiz2022comparisonofpartial pages 12-15, zavala‐ortiz2022comparisonofpartial pages 1-5). SVR was the most precise overall, while ANNR generalized best across culture-medium changes (zavala‐ortiz2022comparisonofpartial pages 18-22, zavala‐ortiz2022comparisonofpartial pages 15-18).

Blanco et al. (Chemom. Intell. Lab. Syst. 2000, 50, 75–82; DOI: 10.1016/s0169-7439(99)00048-9) showed that for intrinsically nonlinear NIR systems, quadratic PLS variants and PC-ANN produced comparable results, with PC-ANN slightly superior but requiring more optimization effort. Critically, they found that scatter correction (SNV) could linearize scattering-induced nonlinearity sufficiently for standard PLS, but intrinsic absorption-concentration nonlinearity required genuinely nonlinear methods (blanco2000nircalibrationin pages 1-2).

### Relevance to concentrated electrolytes

The Carrillo-Bohórquez et al. (2024) perspective establishes that high-concentration electrolytes (HCEs) present particular challenges: overlapping free-solvent, coordinated-solvent, contact-ion-pair, and aggregate spectral signatures; very short IR path lengths required by high absorption; ambiguous Raman band assignments where identical spectral signatures arise from chemically distinct species; and spectral responses that are non-additive because increasing salt concentration lowers the dielectric constant, strengthens electrostatic interactions, and drives extensive ion association (carrillobohorquez2024thechallengeof pages 10-14, yu2021beyondlocalsolvation pages 1-2, carrillobohorquez2024thechallengeof pages 6-10, carrillobohorquez2024thechallengeof pages 22-26). The perspective recommends combining spectroscopy with molecular dynamics simulation for interpretation and highlights high-throughput Raman as a promising avenue for rapid screening of compositional spaces (carrillobohorquez2024thechallengeof pages 22-26, carrillobohorquez2024thechallengeof pages 18-22).

## (c) Training Data Requirements and Validation Strategies

Training data requirements vary significantly with model complexity and mixture dimensionality:

- **Linear regression/PCR for FTIR (Angulo et al., 2022):** Performance for ternary aqueous mixtures saturated at approximately 40 training spectra; PCA–LR was nearly insensitive to training size above ≈25 spectra. ANN required substantially more training spectra and exhibited greater variability with training-set size (angulo2022machinelearningenhanced pages 8-9, angulo2022machinelearningenhanced pages 5-6). Experimental data used an 80/20 random train/test split repeated 200 times, and simulated spectra provided unlimited augmentation under the Beer–Lambert assumption (angulo2022machinelearningenhanced pages 9-10).

- **Multi-sensor PLS for CO₂ capture (Kachko et al., 2015):** A 5^(3−1) fractional factorial design generated 53 compositions (12 excluded for precipitation), with approximately 27 training and 26 testing compositions measured at three temperatures (25, 35, 40 °C) (kachko2016inlinemonitoringof pages 24-28, kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 2-3). Latent-variable selection used leave-one-out cross-validation based on RMSEP (kachko2015realtimeprocessmonitoring pages 3-4). Final validation used offline analytical methods at the pilot plant.

- **ANN/SVR for in-situ NIR (Zavala-Ortiz et al., 2022):** 168 spectra from six bioreactor cultures were split 135/33 using the Kennard–Stone algorithm for calibration/validation. Venetian-windows cross-validation selected PLS latent variables and SVR hyperparameters, and ANN used early stopping to limit overfitting. An independent culture medium served as an external blind test (zavala‐ortiz2022comparisonofpartial pages 5-9, zavala‐ortiz2022comparisonofpartial pages 9-12).

- **MCR-ALS blind source separation (Maggioni et al., 2019):** Required only 17 samples (14 mixtures + 3 water blanks), but this is a self-modeling technique estimating the number of sources from SVD rank rather than training a regression model. Validation was performed by matching recovered spectra to a pure-component reference library (maggioni2019blindsourceseparation pages 1-2, maggioni2019blindsourceseparation pages 2-3).

A general trend emerges: linear models (CLS, PLS, PCR) require modest training sets (25–50 samples for 3–6 components), while nonlinear models (ANN, SVR) demand more data and careful regularization to avoid overfitting, but deliver superior performance when Beer–Lambert additivity fails.

## (d) Raman vs. IR Practicality for Fiber-Coupled Robotic Integration

Raman spectroscopy holds substantial practical advantages for integration into robotic and automated flow platforms:

1. **Minimal water interference:** Water has low polarizability and produces relatively weak Raman scattering, enabling direct measurements in aqueous electrolytes through glass or quartz, particularly in the biochemical/chemical fingerprint region of ~500–1800 cm⁻¹ (jurina2023applicationofspectroscopy pages 9-11, klaverdijk2025rapidcalibrationof pages 22-26). In contrast, mid-IR spectroscopy is severely hampered by strong water absorption, requiring ATR configurations with very short (~μm) effective path lengths.

2. **Fiber-optic compatibility:** Raman uses visible or near-IR excitation wavelengths (typically 532, 633, or 785 nm) that transmit efficiently through standard silica optical fibers, enabling remote probe heads connected by fiber cables of arbitrary length. This is well established in nuclear reprocessing, where fiber-coupled Raman probes are deployed in shielded hot cells (bryan2013spectroscopiconlinemonitoring pages 1-3). Autoclavable immersion probes and flow-cell assemblies for Raman are commercially available for bioprocessing (klaverdijk2025rapidcalibrationof pages 22-26).

3. **Demonstrated microfluidic and robotic integration:** Nelson et al. (Anal. Chem. 2021, 93, 1643–1651; DOI: 10.1021/acs.analchem.0c04225) combined micro-Raman with micro-UV-Vis absorbance for online monitoring of microfluidic liquid-liquid extraction flows, using quartz chips to minimize spectral interference (nelson2021enablingmicroscaleprocessing pages 5-6, nelson2021enablingmicroscaleprocessing pages 1-3). Seifrid et al. (Acc. Chem. Res. 2022, 55, 2454–2466; DOI: 10.1021/acs.accounts.2c00220) described self-driving laboratories using fiber-coupled UV-Vis absorbance and photoluminescence flow cells with robotic sample transfer orchestrated by ChemOS (seifrid2022autonomouschemicalexperiments pages 6-8).

4. **Mid-IR limitations for robotic integration:** ATR-FTIR requires intimate contact between the sample and an internal reflection element (typically diamond, ZnSe, or Ge), which constrains flow-cell geometry and makes remote fiber coupling difficult. Mid-IR fibers (chalcogenide, silver halide) exist but are fragile, expensive, and have limited transmission windows compared to silica fibers for Raman. The shallow ATR penetration depth is advantageous for highly absorbing aqueous samples but limits sensitivity for dilute analytes (jurina2023applicationofspectroscopy pages 18-20).

5. **Raman weaknesses:** Intrinsically weak Raman scattering cross-sections can be overwhelmed by fluorescence from impurities or colored solutions, and sensitivity for some ionic species (e.g., phosphate) may be inadequate (maggioni2019blindsourceseparation pages 1-2, klaverdijk2025rapidcalibrationof pages 22-26). The complementarity of Raman and ATR-FTIR is well illustrated by Maggioni et al. (2019), where phosphate was strongly IR-active but weakly Raman-active (maggioni2019blindsourceseparation pages 1-2).

6. **Miniaturization and portability:** Ilchenko et al. (Nat. Commun. 2024, 15; DOI: 10.1038/s41467-024-47044-7) demonstrated centimeter-scale miniaturized Raman spectrometers with performance comparable to research-grade systems, suggesting that compact Raman modules are increasingly feasible for integration into robotic platforms.

## Summary and Outlook

The current state of the art demonstrates that vibrational spectroscopy can serve as a real-time composition sensor in automated and flow systems, and that fusing spectroscopy with physical properties like conductivity provides complementary constraints essential for resolving complex electrolyte compositions. However, a significant gap exists: no published study has yet demonstrated a purpose-built ATR-FTIR + conductivity flow cell specifically optimized for real-time speciation of concentrated multicomponent battery electrolytes with ion-pairing nonlinearity, together with a nonlinear chemometric model trained and blind-validated for that system. The closest existing work combines sensor fusion in simpler systems (CO₂-capture amines, nuclear waste simulants) or addresses the spectroscopic challenges of high-concentration electrolytes at a fundamental characterization level without real-time closed-loop implementation. Raman spectroscopy, with its superior fiber-coupling and water tolerance, appears to be the most practical modality for near-term robotic integration, while ATR-FTIR offers superior sensitivity and species coverage for many ionic analytes but at the cost of more constrained optical interfacing.
