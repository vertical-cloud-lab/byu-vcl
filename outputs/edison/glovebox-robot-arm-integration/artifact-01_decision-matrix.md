| **(a) Purpose-built automation-ready glovebox** — LC Technologies LC-100/LC-180 or a custom A-Lab-GPSS-style enclosure | **$34K–$50K quoted for LC box**, before robot, dosing equipment, engineered powder controls, and custom integration. Highest initial cost but lowest retrofit uncertainty. | **Best prospect.** Specify guaranteed loaded performance—not merely an empty-box acceptance test—of ≤1 ppm O₂ and H₂O, adequate recirculation, automated pressure control, analyzers, interlocked load-lock, and recovery testing. Custom double-glovebox precedent shows that floor-mounted arms, a transfer rail, and densely integrated powder-processing stations are feasible (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002). | **Highest potential.** Purpose-designed geometry, stable atmosphere, grounded dosing station, local powder capture, and integrated balance isolation minimize uncontrolled environmental and transfer variables. A-Lab GPSS demonstrates automated air-free powder dispensing, mixing, heating, grinding, and sample preparation, although some characterization remained manual for robustness and cost (pqac-00000002). | **Medium unless explicitly controlled.** Internal robots and powder dosing increase dust, lubricant, polymer-vapor, and furnace-off-gas loading. Require local source capture, replaceable prefilters ahead of the purifier, segregated “dirty” processing, sealed powder containers, and differential-pressure monitoring. | **Medium–high, but designable.** Purchase removable floor plates, glove-port reach to service points, spare feedthroughs, lifting/removal provisions, thermal monitoring, and an isolation/service vestibule. Put controllers, power supplies, PCs, and other high-heat/high-outgassing components outside wherever possible. Dense glovebox layouts otherwise make collisions and service access difficult (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000027). | **High if specified initially.** Extra penetrations, blanked KF/ISO ports, removable work decks, rail provisions, automated antechamber, Ethernet/fiber, 24/48-VDC and AC feedthroughs, and independent powder and clean zones support later expansion. Vertical stacking and sidewall placement of frequently serviced modules are proven space-saving strategies (pqac-00000002). | **Preferred for BYU if true unattended internal powder processing is the objective.** Select this only after a vendor accepts an automation-and-metal-powder user-requirements specification, conducts a loaded factory/site acceptance test, and supports NFPA/AHJ review. It provides the strongest atmosphere and data-integrity foundation but does not by itself make a standard robot or glovebox compliant for combustible metal dusts. |
| **(b) Retrofit a used purifier-equipped glovebox with an internal arm** — MBraun MB200MOD (~$12K), Vigor SG1200-750TS (~$14K), or refurbished Labconco Protector + AtmosPure (~$28K) | **Lowest-to-moderate box price**, but total installed cost can approach a new system after purifier regeneration/repacking, analyzers, leak repair, feedthroughs, reinforced floor, controls, arm installation, guarding, filters, and commissioning. Reserve a substantial contingency. | **Potentially high, but condition-dependent.** A sound recirculating purifier can achieve sub-ppm operation; require helium leak testing, analyzer calibration, glove/seal replacement, purifier-capacity and regeneration verification, and a loaded recovery test before acceptance. An MBraun enclosure has published precedent for an automated Chemspeed workstation, but the paper does not identify its glovebox model or arm installation details (pqac-00000026). | **Potentially high after qualification.** Existing chamber dimensions, vibration, airflow over the balance, inaccessible corners, and residual contamination can introduce batch effects. Establish blank runs, witness coupons, O₂/H₂O time-series logging, gravimetric control charts, and powder carryover tests before scientific use. | **Highest of the three options.** Retrofitted arm gearboxes, cables, plastics, greases, and open powder operations share the recirculation loop with molecular sieve and oxygen-removal media. Fine Al/Mg/Si dust is also an ignition and cross-contamination hazard; aluminum dust can have MIE below 10 mJ, and grounding, ignition-source control, and powder-specific inerting validation remain necessary (pqac-00000037, pqac-00000039). | **Highest.** The arm may not fit through the antechamber, and major service can require opening and repurging the box. Legacy floors may need structural reinforcement; ports may be poorly located; drawings, spare parts, and PLC access may be unavailable. Avoid unless the arm can be removed in modules through a service port or isolated vestibule. | **Medium–low.** Economical for a fixed workflow if chamber depth, ceiling clearance, purifier flow, antechamber size, payload, reach, and collision envelope are already adequate. Retrofitting penetrations or enlarging the chamber can eliminate the initial savings. | **Recommended only as a staged engineering platform after a favorable inspection.** Of the used choices, prefer the unit with documented purifier health, supported controls, adequate chamber volume and service access—not simply the lowest purchase price. A used MBraun or Vigor can be attractive, but reject any unit that cannot pass loaded atmosphere, leak, grounding, and powder-containment acceptance tests. |
| **(c) External robot plus automated antechamber/load-lock** — robot remains in air; internal dosing machine operates in the glovebox | **Potentially lowest-risk staged purchase.** A $3K purge-only Labconco is inexpensive, but purifier, analyzers, vacuum hardware, automated doors/valves, controls, and safety interlocks are additional; the external arm is also additional. A purge-only box should not be budgeted as a sub-ppm production system until upgraded and validated. | **High with a purifier-equipped box and validated vacuum/purge recipe; inadequate for demanding work with purge-only operation.** Interlocked doors and pressure/O₂/H₂O permissives prevent operator shortcuts. Published transfer systems demonstrate transition chambers, glovebox networks, vacuum holders, and automated docking, but quantitative gas use and sub-ppm excursions are rarely reported (pqac-00000031, pqac-00000032, pqac-00000033). | **High for internal dosing if samples remain sealed during external handling.** External robotics avoids adding arm-derived heat and contaminants to the analytical environment. Data quality depends on repeatable carrier geometry, sealed containers, barcode/LIMS custody, and logging of chamber pressure and O₂/H₂O for every transfer. | **Lowest robot-related risk.** The arm, gearbox, belts, lubricants, and most cables stay outside the recirculation loop. Powder can still contaminate the purifier, so use capped vessels, local filtration, spill trays, and a “dirty” transfer vestibule. | **Lowest.** Robot service does not disturb the glovebox atmosphere. The critical maintenance items become door seals, valves, vacuum pump, carrier alignment, and interlock sensors. A published automated vacuum carrier achieved docking in under 60 s, although that result is not a glovebox sub-ppm purge-cycle specification (pqac-00000031). | **Highest modularity for phased development.** External arms can serve multiple stations, carriers can be standardized, and the glovebox can later receive an internal compact manipulator. Throughput is limited by load-lock cycling and by the need for an internal mechanism—or human glove operation—to move carriers between the chamber and dosing station. | **Best near-term risk-adjusted architecture for BYU.** Buy a sound purifier-equipped enclosure—or add a qualified purifier before reactive work—install the powder doser internally, and automate sealed-carrier transfer with the arm outside. This sacrifices fully unattended internal manipulation but minimizes purifier contamination, thermal/outgassing uncertainty, and atmosphere-breaking robot maintenance. Do **not** rely on the $3K purge-only box for quantitative Al/Mg/AlSi10Mg studies until sub-ppm performance and combustible-powder safeguards are demonstrated. |
