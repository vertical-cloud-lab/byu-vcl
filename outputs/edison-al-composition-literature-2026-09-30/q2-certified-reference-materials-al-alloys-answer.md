### Microhomogeneity Considerations for LPBF AlSi10Mg Validation

A critical issue for your application is the **microstructural scale mismatch** between conventional cast aluminum-alloy CRMs and LPBF AlSi10Mg material. 

**LPBF AlSi10Mg microstructure** is characterized by:
- **Cellular dendrite size**: approximately 0.4–1.0 µm (dymnich2026thermalinducedintragranularresidual pages 1-2, giovanni2019alsimgalloysproduced pages 45-50)
- **Si-rich eutectic boundaries**: approximately 200 nm thick (giovanni2019alsimgalloysproduced pages 45-50)
- **Cellular spacing**: 0.5 ± 0.1 µm in melt-pool center, 0.9 ± 0.2 µm at melt-pool boundary (cauwenbergh2021unravellingthemultiscale pages 7-8)
- **Si distribution**: Continuous or semi-continuous Si-enriched network (≈29.6 wt% Si, 1.7 wt% Mg) surrounding supersaturated α-Al cells (≈2.0–2.7 wt% Si) (cauwenbergh2021unravellingthemultiscale pages 9-10, cauwenbergh2021unravellingthemultiscale pages 10-12)

In contrast, **conventional cast Al-Si-Mg alloys** (A356/A357-type CRM discs) exhibit:
- **Secondary dendrite arm spacing (SDAS)**: approximately 30–40 µm for typical casting conditions (giovanni2019alsimgalloysproduced pages 99-103)
- **Eutectic Si morphology**: Coarse plate-like or acicular silicon particles in unmodified condition, or fibrous/coral-like morphology after Sr/Na modification (giovanni2019alsimgalloysproduced pages 27-32)
- **Feature scale**: 10–100× coarser than LPBF structures

This **two-orders-of-magnitude difference** in microstructural scale means that standard cast CRM discs may not be ideal secondary standards for validating LPBF AlSi10Mg EDS measurements at the cellular scale (5–15 kV beam, approximately 1–3 µm interaction volume). The cast CRMs remain suitable for bulk-composition verification and instrument performance checks, but users should be aware that localized EDS spot analyses on coarse eutectic features in cast CRMs will not replicate the ultrafine cellular-network averaging that occurs in LPBF material.

### Homogeneity Assessment Criteria for EPMA/EDS Reference Materials

Borkhodoev (2010) describes a rigorous statistical approach for assessing micrometer-scale homogeneity using two-factor analysis of variance (ANOVA) applied to hierarchical replicate measurements across multiple specimen areas, focus positions, and counting cycles (borkhodoev2010assessmentofreference pages 3-4, borkhodoev2010assessmentofreference pages 1-2, borkhodoev2010assessmentofreference pages 4-4). The method separates variance components into residual/reference-sample inhomogeneity, focusing effects, and measurement/counting error. **Acceptance criteria** state that residual inhomogeneity should not exceed one-third of the admissible root-mean-square deviation for the intended analysis, with relative standard-error limits of 3% for major elements, 10% for common/trace elements, and 30% for ultratrace elements (borkhodoev2010assessmentofreference pages 3-4). For SEM-EDS, homogeneity is supported when replicate compositions from distinct micrometer-scale regions agree within their statistically expected uncertainties, and relative deviations (RDEV) remain below approximately 10% (goldstein2018quantitativeanalysisthe pages 18-18, giunto2026accuratesemedsquantification pages 22-25).

### Published Validation Studies Using Aluminum-Alloy Standards

A quantitative validation study (Teng, 2020) used a **Mg45-Al28-Zn27 cast alloy** with ICP-AES reference composition of Mg 44.74 wt%, Al 27.76 wt%, Zn 27.50 wt%. Independent EPMA/WDS raster analyses (five analyses over 253 × 189 µm² areas) measured Mg 44.75 ± 0.19 wt%, Al 28.82 ± 0.27 wt%, and Zn 27.91 ± 0.41 wt%, demonstrating agreement within measurement uncertainty (teng2020highresolutionxraya pages 106-111, teng2020highresolutionxray pages 106-111). The study also validated f-ratio, standardless, and standard-based SEM-EDS quantification methods against certified mineral standards (kyanite, albite, orthoclase) containing aluminum, achieving accuracy better than 5% (teng2020highresolutionxraya pages 132-136, teng2020highresolutionxray pages 132-136).

**General EPMA/EDS accuracy benchmarks** from the literature indicate:
- **EPMA-WDS** with k-ratio protocol and PAP φ(ρz) matrix corrections: approximately 95% of analyses fall within **±2.5% relative deviation (RDEV)** from the expected composition (goldstein2018quantitativeanalysisfrom pages 7-9, goldstein2018quantitativeanalysisfrom pages 6-7)
- **SDD-EDS** with k-ratio protocol and PAP corrections: accuracy fits within the EPMA-WDS RDEV distribution (goldstein2018quantitativeanalysisfrom pages 7-9)
- **Standardless EDS**: 95% of analyses within **±25% relative error** (goldstein2018quantitativeanalysisfrom pages 7-9)
- **Automated SEM-EDS** (AutoEMXSp): 97.6% of cluster-centroid RDEV values below 10%, and 98.8% of absolute deviations below 2 atomic percent, validated on 74 single-phase materials including Al₂O₃, AlPO₄, and aluminosilicates (giunto2026accuratesemedsquantification pages 21-22)

A comparative study (Seidel et al., 2021) benchmarked EDXRF, WDXRF, and LIBS against spark-OES for nine commercial alloys including three certified aluminum-alloy reference materials (EB 313, BAM-311, EB 315a), demonstrating that spark-OES serves as a reliable validation method for aluminum-alloy composition (seidel2021comparisonofelemental pages 1-2, seidel2021comparisonofelemental pages 2-4, seidel2021comparisonofelemental pages 4-5).

### Interlaboratory Precision Data for Al-Si Alloys

Although **ASTM E1251, ASTM E3061, and EN 14242** are the relevant standards for interlaboratory testing of aluminum-alloy chemical analysis, **numeric repeatability (r) and reproducibility (R) values for Si and Mg** in Al-Si alloys from these specific standards were **not recovered** from the searched literature. General interlaboratory round-robin test terminology and methodology were found (pqac-00000-unnamed papers on round-robin tests), but users must consult the original ASTM and EN standard documents directly to obtain the certified precision statistics. Typical interlaboratory performance parameters include repeatability standard deviation, reproducibility standard deviation, z-scores, and relative standard deviations, as described in the round-robin literature framework.

For the user's planned work with **Al-Cu-Li, Al-Zr-Er-Sc, Al-Ce-Mg, and Al-Mn-Cr-Zr** alloys, the recovered evidence provides limited options:
- **Lithium**: Brammer AA SQ-18 contains B, Li, and Na (each 0.02 wt%), but note that **Li is not reliably quantifiable by conventional SEM-EDS** due to its low atomic number and characteristic X-ray energy (steelUnknownyearwww.brammerstandard.com pages 1-2)
- **Cerium**: Brammer R Al Ce is listed as a Ce-bearing aluminum setting-up sample (40 mm diameter × 25 mm), but the Ce concentration was not recovered (steelUnknownyearwww.brammerstandard.com pages 4-5)
- **Zirconium**: Brammer AL RC20/02 contains Zr 0.17 wt% (60 mm diameter × 25 mm) (steelUnknownyearwww.brammerstandard.com pages 4-5); numerous purity-aluminum samples list Zr as a trace element
- **Scandium and Erbium**: No dedicated high-concentration Al-Sc or Al-Er CRMs were identified in the searched papers. Trace Sc is listed in Brammer purity-aluminum setting-up samples (steelUnknownyearwww.brammerstandard.com pages 1-2)

Users working with these advanced alloys may need to develop in-house reference materials or contact specialized CRM producers not covered in the retrieved literature.

### Summary and Recommendations

1. **For AlSi10Mg LPBF calibration**, no exact composition-matched CRM was found. The closest alternatives are NIS-CRM067 (Si 0.77%, Mg 0.71%) or Brammer BS 6061-type samples, but these are cast materials with coarse microstructures unsuitable for validating cellular-scale EDS measurements.

2. **NIST SRMs** (1258-I, 1259, 85B, 87A) are documented for aluminum-alloy calibration, but full certificates for 1256b, 1255b, 855a, 856a, 853a, and 1240c must be obtained from NIST directly.

3. **BAM-311, EB 313, EB 315a** and **NIS-CRM series** are fully certified aluminum CRMs with documented use in validation studies.

4. **Brammer Standard** offers dozens of aluminum setting-up samples covering a broad compositional range, supplied as spark-OES discs (typically 50–65 mm diameter × 25–50 mm height), but individual certificates must be verified and microhomogeneity confirmed before treating them as quantitative secondary standards.

5. **Microhomogeneity is critical**: Cast CRM discs have SDAS of 30–40 µm and coarse eutectic Si, whereas LPBF AlSi10Mg has 0.4–1 µm cellular structures with 200 nm Si-rich boundaries—a scale mismatch that limits the suitability of conventional cast CRMs for validating sub-micrometer LPBF microstructures (dymnich2026thermalinducedintragranularresidual pages 1-2, giovanni2019alsimgalloysproduced pages 45-50, cauwenbergh2021unravellingthemultiscale pages 9-10, cauwenbergh2021unravellingthemultiscale pages 7-8, giovanni2019alsimgalloysproduced pages 99-103).

6. **Validation accuracy**: Well-controlled EPMA/EDS with k-ratio methods achieves ±2.5% RDEV on 95% of analyses, while standardless EDS has ±25% relative error (goldstein2018quantitativeanalysisfrom pages 7-9, goldstein2018quantitativeanalysisfrom pages 6-7, giunto2026accuratesemedsquantification pages 21-22).

7. **Interlaboratory precision data** for Si and Mg from ASTM E1251, E3061, and EN 14242 were not recovered; users should consult the original standards.

8. **Advanced alloys**: Limited CRM availability for Li, Sc, Ce, Er, Zr in aluminum; Brammer offers trace-level standards, but dedicated high-concentration CRMs were not identified.

**Pricing information** was not published for any of the recovered CRMs. Users should contact producers directly (NIST: www.nist.gov/srm; BAM: www.bam.de; Brammer: www.brammerstandard.com; NIS Egypt) for current availability, certificates, and pricing.
