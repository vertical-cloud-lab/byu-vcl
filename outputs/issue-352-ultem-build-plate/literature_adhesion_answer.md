## Scope and main finding

**Direct, quantitative measurements of adhesion *to the build plate* are much scarcer than measurements of interlayer strength or part warpage.** The retrieved plate-detachment studies test PLA/ABS, PETG, and HIPS/PP—not ULTEM, PPSU, PEEK, or PEKK. High-temperature-polymer studies do quantify warpage, and several document workable platform configurations, but they do not establish a peel or pull-off threshold at which ULTEM welds irreversibly to a PEI sheet. A *polyimide* (PI, often Kapton) film must not be confused with a *polyetherimide* (PEI, ULTEM) sheet. (spoerk2018effectofthe pages 3-4, kascak2024adhesiontestingdevice pages 8-9, santoso2025effectofbuild pages 2-5, vakharia2023multimaterialadditivemanufacturing pages 2-5)

## What temperature relative to Tg does—and does not—predict

Spoerk’s controlled first-layer study is the clearest experimental basis for the rule that heating a bed through the **deposited amorphous polymer’s Tg** can sharply improve contact and shear-off resistance. It also shows this is **not an assurance of easy release**: the hotter PLA–glass combination damaged glass. Conversely, its ABS adhered better to PI than glass, illustrating that surface chemistry and compliance matter alongside temperature. In Santoso’s different material pair, HIPS-on-PEI adhesion peaked near the **HIPS**, not the PEI-sheet, Tg. These results cannot be numerically extrapolated to ULTEM-on-PEI: the polymers, surface, contact time, part geometry, testing temperature, and failure mode change. (spoerk2018effectofthe pages 5-7, spoerk2018effectofthe pages 3-4, santoso2025effectofbuild pages 2-5, santoso2025effectofbuild pages 8-10)

**A hot nozzle is not the same as an entire PEI sheet being above Tg.** One ULTEM 9085 study reports filament Tg **186 °C** and prints on a **200 °C PVA-coated glass bed**; another reports an approximately **177 °C** Tg for its 9085 material. PEI/ULTEM 1010 annealing experiments use **220–235 °C** above that material’s Tg and find better interlayer properties, especially at **225 °C**. This supports the *plausibility* of chain mobility and interfacial welding when compatible PEI surfaces are held sufficiently hot, but neither experiment observes molecular diffusion across **ULTEM part–PEI build-sheet** interfaces or measures their removal force. Tg is a transition range, not a universal instantaneous welding threshold; the actual sheet-interface temperature, chain mobility, contact duration, grade compatibility and cooling history would have to be established. (vakharia2023multimaterialadditivemanufacturing pages 2-5, lee2026rapidpreheatingfor pages 6-11, yilmaz2022rheologycrystallinityand pages 1-2, yilmaz2022rheologycrystallinityand pages 3-4, yilmaz2022rheologycrystallinityand pages 6-7)

PEEK and PEKK require an additional qualification: their crystallization kinetics introduce shrinkage and may restrict chain motion. The contrasting PEEK/CF-PEEK chamber-warping trends above, and Lin’s discussion of slower-crystallizing PEKK, show why an amorphous-polymer bed-Tg adhesion rule should **not** be treated as a quantitative warpage or release law for these semicrystalline feedstocks. (lin2024processstructurepropertyrelationshipsof pages 21-26, lin2024processstructurepropertyrelationshipsof pages 39-42)

## Same-polymer welding and choices for a removable interface

**Conclusion on the specific hypothesis:** welding of hot, compatible PEI against PEI is **physically credible**, and published ULTEM interlayer annealing experiments support thermally enhanced bonding. **I did not find a controlled report showing that ULTEM printed onto a bare PEI sheet near/above its Tg becomes impossible to remove, nor a PEI-on-PEI plate peel-force measurement demonstrating that mechanism.** The HIPS-on-PEI data establish neither proposition; PI-film data are not PEI-sheet data. “Impossible to remove” should therefore be described as a *potential practical failure mode*, not a demonstrated threshold or established universal outcome. (yilmaz2022rheologycrystallinityand pages 1-2, yilmaz2022rheologycrystallinityand pages 6-7, santoso2025effectofbuild pages 8-10, spoerk2018effectofthe pages 3-4)

Documented ways of **avoiding direct contact with a bare PEI sheet** include:

* **Glass plus an intervening PVA coating/raft:** Vakharia et al. printed ULTEM 9085 and CF-9085 at a **200 °C bed** and **390 °C nozzle** on **PVA-coated glass**, using an ULTEM 9085 raft in the illustrated print. This documents *successful printing*, **not** quantified release on cooling or a guarantee against glass damage. (vakharia2023multimaterialadditivemanufacturing pages 2-5)
* **Glass covered with PI tape and PVP adhesive:** Shuttleworth’s PEI printing development used a **150 °C bed** and **365 °C nozzle**; after an aluminium platform buckled by about **1.5 mm**, it adopted **3 mm borosilicate glass** and manufacturer-advised **PI tape plus polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP)** for glass adhesion. This creates a non-PEI contact layer but was **not** tested as a controlled anti-welding or release-force comparison. (shuttleworth2020adigitalmanufacturing pages 71-75)
* **A different sacrificial/replaceable substrate:** Shuttleworth also reported successful small PEI prints on a **sanded carbon-fibre surface** at **150 °C**, without visible warping after removal and cooling; the study supplies neither a force measurement nor evidence that this surface is equivalent to glass-fibre/epoxy **G10/FR4**. Avoid treating those composites as interchangeable. (shuttleworth2020adigitalmanufacturing pages 71-75)
* **PEI sheet with an adhesion coating is *not* a demonstrated release solution:** Kothavade et al. found that a **different thermoplastic polyimide** would not reliably adhere to bare glass and instead printed at **130 °C** on a **PEI surface plus nano-polymer adhesive**. The printed resin was *not* ULTEM, its reported Tg was **245–250 °C**, and no PEI-on-PEI removal study was performed. An adhesive may increase hold-down; it should not be presumed to prevent permanent bonding unless its release behavior is tested. (kothavade2024extrusion3dprinting pages 2-4, kothavade2024extrusion3dprinting pages 4-5)

Thus, **a barrier or dissimilar, replaceable top surface is the defensible way to avoid intentionally printing PEI directly onto PEI**, while separately validating that the chosen PI tape, PVA/PVP coating, glass or composite plate both holds the specific print during cooling and releases it without substrate damage. The available examples establish process feasibility, not a comparative ranking of release forces for ULTEM, PC, PPSU, PEEK and PEKK; no experimentally validated release recommendation for bare G10/FR4 or metal across those feedstocks follows from these sources. (vakharia2023multimaterialadditivemanufacturing pages 2-5, shuttleworth2020adigitalmanufacturing pages 71-75, roschli2022buildplatedesign pages 3-7, spoerk2018effectofthe pages 7-8)
