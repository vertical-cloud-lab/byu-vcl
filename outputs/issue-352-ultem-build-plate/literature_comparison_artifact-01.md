# Edison artifact-01: ranked recommendation by printer class (comparison query)

| Rank | Bed Surface + Treatment | Suitability Rating | Key Advantage | Key Risk | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **A1 — Bed ≈120°C; chamber ≈65°C** | Borosilicate glass + PI (Kapton) film/tape + **Magigoo HT** | ★★★★☆ | Flat, thermally stable glass; PI is heat-resistant; renewable adhesive improves grip and acts as a release barrier | Large ULTEM parts may still curl because 120°C is far below either grade’s Tg | Best practical compromise for a temperature-limited printer, but this exact combination is an engineering extrapolation. Use a 10–20 mm brim and slow first layer. PEI showed significant warping at 120°C, whereas PI/PVP on glass was validated at 150°C (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000055, pqac-00000056, pqac-00000023) |
| **A2** | High-Tg G10/FR4 Garolite, lightly abraded + Magigoo HT | ★★★☆☆ | Texture and slight compliance can provide mechanical keying; inexpensive and replaceable | Ordinary FR4 epoxy can soften, discolor, warp, or delaminate during prolonged high-temperature cycling | Use only laminate with a documented Tg or temperature index comfortably above 120°C. No controlled ULTEM adhesion-force, release, or lifetime study was located, so this ranks below validated glass systems |
| **A3** | Flexible spring steel with PEI coating + thin Magigoo HT barrier | ★★☆☆☆ | Flexing after complete cooling can simplify release; adhesive barrier reduces direct PEI-on-PEI contact | Limited thermal benefit at 120°C means ULTEM warpage remains likely; coating can scratch, bubble, or tear | PEI-coated steel produced 53.48 N mean tear-off force for PETG versus 33.09 N on bare aluminium, but this is not ULTEM validation (pqac-00000001, pqac-00000026) |
| **A — Not recommended** | Dimafix; bare glass; bare aluminium; ordinary PVA glue stick | ★☆☆☆☆ | Low cost or easy application | Dimafix loses performance above ≈125°C; bare glass is unreliable; aluminium may distort; ordinary PVA lacks documented durability for this duty | Dimafix could not restrain long PEI parts at 120–125°C. Smooth glass required treatment, while a heated aluminium plate developed ≈1.5 mm edge deviation (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000000, pqac-00000005) |
| **B1 — Bed 160–200°C; chamber ≥150°C** | Borosilicate glass + **Vision Miner Nano Polymer Adhesive** or Magigoo HT | ★★★★★ | Dimensionally stable substrate with a renewable interface that supplies strong hot adhesion without sacrificing the plate | Coating must be uniform; excessive first-layer compression can impede release; glass can chip if pried | Best open-platform recommendation. Nano adhesive on glass printed ULTEM 1010 at a 200°C bed and 80°C chamber; at least four coats and a brim were recommended. A ≥150°C chamber is a reasoned improvement, not a condition directly tested there (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000008, pqac-00000016) |
| **B2** | Borosilicate glass + PI tape + PVP adhesive | ★★★★☆ | Validated at a 150°C bed with minimal warp; PI provides a replaceable interface and avoids direct PEI-to-PEI welding | Tape seams, bubbles, and failure of its pressure-sensitive backing can limit life below the film’s intrinsic temperature capability | A 3 mm glass plate with PI/PVP replaced a thermally distorted aluminium bed. PI film had a reported Tg of ≈348.6°C, but the tape adhesive is normally the limiting component (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000029) |
| **B3** | Smooth PEI/ULTEM sheet + Magigoo HT release/barrier coat | ★★★★☆ | High chemical affinity gives strong retention; testing found slight warp at 150°C and no significant warp at 180°C | Direct molten PEI on hot PEI may weld locally and remove material from the bed or part; the sheet softens as temperature approaches Tg | Use a continuous barrier coat, avoid over-squish, and cool fully before removal. Magigoo HT held elongated PEI geometries; the fusion warning is a materials-based precaution rather than a measured result in that study (pqac-00000041, pqac-00000043, pqac-00000056) |
| **B4** | Stratasys Fortus/F900 disposable build sheet + machine-generated support foundation or raft | ★★★★☆ | Qualified, repeatable industrial workflow; disposable interface prevents cumulative bed damage and the support foundation mediates release | Proprietary consumables, cost, and waste; exact sheet chemistry, process setpoint, and removal force were not disclosed in the retrieved evidence | Industrial benchmark when qualification outweighs cost. Fortus 450mc and F900 chamber limits are ≤220°C and ≤225°C respectively; these are machine limits, not confirmed ULTEM setpoints (pqac-00000027, pqac-00000033) |
| **B5** | Sanded carbon-fibre composite plate | ★★★☆☆ | At a 150°C bed, thin PEI specimens adhered well and showed no visible warp or shrinkage after cooling | Performance depends on composite resin, lay-up, and flatness; expansion mismatch and repeated sanding can shorten life | Promising but supported only by limited trials on approximately 1 mm parts; removal force and long-term degradation were not measured (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000002) |
| **B — Not recommended** | Bare aluminium; bare glass; Dimafix; uncoated PEI sheet near or above 200°C | ★☆☆☆☆ | Minimal preparation | Aluminium can buckle, bare glass can release prematurely, Dimafix degrades, and direct hot PEI-on-PEI contact creates avoidable welding risk | Use a renewable, temperature-rated interlayer. The best measured warpage outcome occurred at a 180°C bed with Magigoo HT; 120°C caused significant curvature and 150°C slight post-release edge warp (pqac-00000000, pqac-00000005, pqac-00000041, pqac-00000056) |


*Table: Ranked build-surface recommendations for temperature-limited and industrial high-temperature PEI/ULTEM printers. Ratings distinguish directly reported results from engineering extrapolations and identify important release and surface-damage risks.*
