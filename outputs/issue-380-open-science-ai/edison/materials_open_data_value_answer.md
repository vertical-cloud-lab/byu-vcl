# Measured impact of open materials databases on machine-learning discovery

**Bottom line.** These resources have demonstrably supplied training data, benchmarks and candidate structures at a scale that individual laboratories could rarely reproduce. The strongest *measured* impacts are Materials Project (MP) usage, the size and reuse of the databases, predictive-model benchmarks, and several experimentally tested discovery workflows. Those measures are **not** a monetary return on investment: the available economic estimates concern broader computational materials engineering, not a controlled cost–benefit evaluation of any of the seven databases. (horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 9-11, barrosluque2026theopenmaterials pages 1-2, jain2016researchupdatethe pages 1-4)

## Scale, uptake and scientific output

| Resource | Documented scale and use | Discovery or ML result—and level of validation |
|---|---|---|
| **Materials Project** | A 2025 account reports **178,627 materials** across 51,298 chemical systems and **more than 600,000 registered users**. It describes thousands of website visitors and millions of data records downloaded daily; since 2015, MP has issued **more than 145,000 material-data DOIs**. These are different measures: a downloaded record is not a person or a publication. (horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 4-6, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 1-4, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 9-11) | MP structures and relaxation trajectories support ML potentials and downstream screening. In one independently reported experiment, a search retrieving **21,576 Li-containing MP materials** led to the Li₃OCl interlayer; the tested Li|Li₃OCl|Li₃InCl₆|LiCoO₂ cell retained **76.36% capacity after 1,000 cycles**. This is a database-supported screening-and-experiment result, not evidence that the database alone discovered the material. (horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 16-19, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 6-9) |
| **OQMD** | Its authors report **more than one million computed compounds by July 2022**. The retrieved primary account provides no comparable registered-user or download total. (shen2022reflectionsonone pages 1-3) | An OQMD-based ML/high-throughput study predicted **21 stable mixed-anion X₄Y₂Z compounds**; these are predictions, not reported syntheses. Separately, database-supported thermochemical-water-splitting screening led to **two mixed perovskites that were synthesized and cycled**, with favorable redox behavior and fuel production. The reviewed passage does not name their formulas. (shen2022reflectionsonone pages 7-9) |
| **AFLOW** | A 2024 account reports **over 3.5 million entries**, searchable through AFLUX and REST interfaces; it gives no platform-wide user count. (toher2024aflowforalloys pages 2-3) | The AFLOW-integrated disordered enthalpy–entropy descriptor guided synthesis and characterization of new high-entropy borides and carbonitrides. The study explicitly reports **eight borides synthesized for validation**, including (HfMoNbTaZr)B₂, and reports X-ray diffraction for carbonitride compositions. Its larger screened set must not be counted as synthesized discoveries. (divilov2024disorderedenthalpy–entropydescriptor pages 1-2, divilov2024disorderedenthalpy–entropydescriptor pages 6-7, divilov2024disorderedenthalpy–entropydescriptor pages 10-11) |
| **NOMAD** | Counts depend on what is counted and when: an account gives **over 50 million total-energy calculations as of March 2018**; a 2024 roadmap describes **over 13 million repository entries** from more than 50 codes and 500 contributors. These figures are not an interchangeable time series. Downloading did not require registration in the earlier account, limiting inference about unique users. (draxl2019thenomadlaboratory pages 5-7, bauer2024roadmapondatacentric pages 34-36) | NOMAD provides normalized, reusable calculations and a browser-based AI toolkit. A documented ML competition used **2,400 training and 600 test** oxide structures and received **883 submitted solutions**; those are benchmark participation and predictions, not experimentally confirmed new materials. (draxl2019thenomadlaboratory pages 1-3, draxl2019thenomadlaboratory pages 10-12) |
| **JARVIS** | Its 2020 description reports approximately **40,000 JARVIS-DFT materials, one million calculated properties and 25 ML models**. The distinct **2024 JARVIS-Leaderboard** reports **1,281 contributions to 274 benchmarks**, using 152 methods and over eight million data points. Neither count is a count of discovered materials or users. (choudhary2020thejointautomated pages 1-5, choudhary2024jarvisleaderboardalarge pages 1-2) | JARVIS connects computed properties, ML, comparisons with experiments and inter-laboratory benchmarks. The retrieved platform papers do **not** establish a platform-wide count of newly synthesized ML-discovered compounds. (choudhary2020thejointautomated pages 1-5, choudhary2024jarvisleaderboardalarge pages 1-2) |
| **Alexandria** | Its 2026 expansion reports **5.8 million DFT structures**, including **175,000 on the computed convex hull**. Its force-field training release, sAlex25, contains **14 million off-equilibrium structures**. No platform user total was found. (cavignac2026aidrivenexpansionand pages 1-2) | A generative/ML workflow proposed **119 million candidates** and added **1.3 million DFT-validated compounds**, including **74,000 newly predicted stable materials**. “DFT-validated” and “on the convex hull” do **not** mean synthesized. Fine-tuning on the data yielded a reported stability-benchmark **F1 of 0.895** and energy error of **21.2 meV/atom**. (cavignac2026aidrivenexpansionand pages 1-2, cavignac2026aidrivenexpansionand pages 9-10) |
| **OMat24** | The 2026 dataset paper reports **over 110 million DFT calculations**, predominantly non-equilibrium configurations for training interatomic models. A DFT calculation is not a distinct material. No verified platform-wide user or download count was found. (barrosluque2026theopenmaterials pages 1-2) | OMat24-trained models report stability-classification **F1 greater than 0.9** and formation-energy accuracy of approximately **20 meV/atom** on the stated computational benchmark. This establishes predictive performance, **not** an experimentally synthesized OMat24-derived discovery. (barrosluque2026theopenmaterials pages 1-2) |

**Citation statistics require a denominator and a date.** Citations to a database’s founding article, a later update, software, an individual dataset and papers that merely use its data cannot be summed into one platform-impact figure. The retrieved bibliographic records attach citation counts to *particular papers*—for example, **712** to the 2020 JARVIS article, **585** to the 2019 NOMAD article, **75** to the 2022 OQMD article, **616** to the 2025 MP article, **39** to the 2026 Alexandria article, and **560** to the 2026 OMat24 article—but supply neither a consistent citation-index methodology nor a common observation date. They should therefore be treated as indicative paper-level counts, **not comparable, audited database-wide totals**. (choudhary2020thejointautomated pages 1-5, draxl2019thenomadlaboratory pages 5-7, shen2022reflectionsonone pages 1-3, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 1-4, cavignac2026aidrivenexpansionand pages 1-2, barrosluque2026theopenmaterials pages 1-2)

## Funding, licensing and economic return

**Funding.** The publications identify support but generally **do not disclose an annual operating budget or cumulative expenditure for each database**. MP is described as a US Department of Energy Public Reusable Research Data Resource; this designation is not a dollar-valued appropriation. OQMD acknowledges NIST/CHiMaD, NSF MRSEC and DOE awards; AFLOW-related work identifies NSF **DMR-2219788** and ONR **N000142112515**; NOMAD-related work identifies EU Horizon 2020 centre-of-excellence grant **951786**, an ERC grant and Max Planck support. Award identifiers establish funding sources, **not the amount spent on a database**. The retrieved JARVIS, Alexandria and OMat24 platform evidence likewise does not supply a comparable audited database budget. (dyatkin2022privateandpublic pages 2-3, shen2022reflectionsonone pages 12-13, toher2024aflowforalloys pages 4-6, bauer2024roadmapondatacentric pages 34-36, choudhary2024jarvisleaderboardalarge pages 1-2, cavignac2026aidrivenexpansionand pages 1-2, barrosluque2026theopenmaterials pages 1-2)

**Licensing needs to be checked at the data-object level.** OQMD’s account identifies **CC BY 4.0**; NOMAD’s earlier repository account describes contributed raw calculations made public under **CC BY 3.0**, with a possible embargo of up to three years. The OMat24 paper identifies the **dataset** as permissively licensed **CC BY**. Alexandria’s authors state that their data, models and workflows are available under Creative Commons licences, without establishing that every item has the same variant. MPContribs says contributors retain ownership and control, while the 2025 MP paper is marked **CC BY-NC 4.0**. Crucially, a *paper’s* non-commercial publication licence does **not**, on its own, prove that all core MP records or every contributed dataset changed to non-commercial terms. The retrieved evidence does not establish the date or scope of a database-wide licensing change; users considering commercial ML training must check the particular download and contributed dataset rather than infer permission—or a prohibition—from the article licence. (shen2022reflectionsonone pages 1-3, draxl2019thenomadlaboratory pages 5-7, barrosluque2026theopenmaterials pages 1-2, cavignac2026aidrivenexpansionand pages 1-2, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 9-11, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 1-4, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 16-19)

**Cost–benefit and ROI.** A 2016 analysis of collaborative *ab initio* databases cites documented examples of **tens of millions of dollars saved** and returns **up to 7:1** for **some forms of computational materials engineering**. A separate molecular-modelling assessment gives scenario-dependent returns of approximately **3:1–9:1**. Neither isolates the contribution, full creation-and-maintenance costs, counterfactual research outcomes or monetized downstream benefits of MP, OQMD, AFLOW, NOMAD, JARVIS, Alexandria or OMat24. Accordingly, **there is no defensible database-specific dollar benefit, benefit–cost ratio or ROI for these seven resources in the retrieved evidence**. Their reported user activity, scientific discoveries and model speed/accuracy establish important scientific utility but cannot be converted into financial savings without an explicit attribution and cost model. (jain2016researchupdatethe pages 1-4, goldbeck2012theeconomicimpact pages 28-31, goldbeck2012theeconomicimpact pages 41-44, horton2025accelerateddatadrivenmaterials pages 9-11)

Finally, the distinction between *candidate generation*, *DFT confirmation* and *laboratory validation* is consequential. Even very large predicted-stability catalogues cannot be reported as experimentally discovered materials; measured performance also remains sensitive to the database’s computational conventions and to the application-specific experimental workflow. The most substantiated conclusion is substantial, measurable **research infrastructure impact**, alongside a substantial and currently unquantified **database-specific economic return**. (cavignac2026aidrivenexpansionand pages 1-2, barrosluque2026theopenmaterials pages 1-2, shen2022reflectionsonone pages 7-9, jain2016researchupdatethe pages 1-4)
