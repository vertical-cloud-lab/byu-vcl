# Laboratory automation and self-driving laboratories: market evidence

**Bottom line.** There is a substantial, established market for *clinical* laboratory automation, but it should not be presented as the market for research self-driving laboratories (SDLs). The accessible market reports and studies document growing research activity, operating cloud labs, and useful commercial instruments; they do **not** establish a defensible stand-alone global SDL revenue figure, SDL growth rate, or profitability for the named private companies. (nam2025revolutionizinglaboratorypractices pages 2-4, nam2025revolutionizinglaboratorypractices pages 11-12, jung2025researchtrendanalysis pages 25-26, zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 12-14)

## Size, growth, and the measurement boundary

Nam and Park’s 2025 clinical-laboratory review puts the **2023 global total laboratory automation (TLA) systems market at US$5.57–6.1 billion**. TLA here means automation across clinical specimen handling, testing, and post-analytical processes—not AI-directed research experiments. Their references identify a **QYResearch report updated June 2024**, covering 2024–2030, and a **Precedence Research report updated August 2024**, covering 2024–2034. The accessible material does not reproduce a verifiable forecast dollar value or CAGR from those reports; assigning one to the SDL segment would be unjustified. (nam2025revolutionizinglaboratorypractices pages 2-4, nam2025revolutionizinglaboratorypractices pages 11-12)

The same review reports **115 South Korean institutions operating TLA in 2024**, including **36 of 47 tertiary hospitals (77%)** and **58 of 331 general hospitals (18%)**. Its market-share figure puts **North America at 38%** and depicts **Siemens, Roche, and Beckman Coulter at 34%, 31%, and 25%**, respectively, in the reported clinical-automation vendor breakdown. These illustrate adoption and concentration within the *clinical* category, not an SDL company ranking. (nam2025revolutionizinglaboratorypractices pages 2-4, nam2025revolutionizinglaboratorypractices media 099f385e)

Research activity is growing, but it is a different metric: Jung and colleagues analyzed **352 SDL papers published during 2004–2023** and found a marked rise after **2019**. A 2025 study surveying publicly identifiable cloud labs found **15 example organizations worldwide**, explicitly **not** an exhaustive census. Neither publications nor provider counts measure revenue. (jung2025researchtrendanalysis pages 25-26, zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 12-14, zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 9-12)

## How the businesses capture value

* **Instruments and integration.** Manufacturers sell liquid handlers, modules, analytical instruments, consumables, and support. A robot is not itself an SDL: experiment-selection algorithms, instrument interfaces, scheduling, measurements, and data infrastructure must close the loop. In 2024, Roche researchers integrated an **Opentrons OT-2** with their in-house **AutoLab** orchestration software using the robot’s API, illustrating why instrument sales and the integration/software layer are distinct sources of value. (thieme2024deepintegrationof pages 1-2, thieme2024deepintegrationof pages 5-6)
* **Software.** Protocol-generation, orchestration, sample tracking, analysis, and data management can be developed internally, distributed openly, or supplied commercially. The open-source **OT-Mation** project, published in **2025**, converts experimental-design CSV files into executable OT-2 Python protocols; it demonstrates a software complement to purchased hardware, not evidence of software-market revenue. (laverick2025otmationanopensource pages 1-2)
* **Cloud labs and services.** A provider operates expensive physical facilities while customers remotely specify experiments and obtain measurements and records; charges may combine access commitments and usage. Academic authors distinguish **Emerald Cloud Lab’s (ECL’s)** broad instrument-access approach from **Strateos’s** predefined workflows emphasizing drug discovery and synthetic biology. A 2025 review reports an **ECL minimum financial commitment exceeding US$250,000**, but this secondary-source figure is not independently verified pricing or ECL revenue. Moving capital spending from customer to provider does not eliminate infrastructure or operating costs. (arias2024scientificdiscoveryat pages 2-2, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 4-6, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 6-7, arias2024scientificdiscoveryat pages 8-8)

## What happened at the named companies

**Emerald Cloud Lab.** A 2025 cloud-lab landscape report lists ECL’s Austin facility at **105,000 ft² and more than 230 instruments**; it lists the ECL–Carnegie Mellon facility at **20,000 ft² and more than 200 instruments**. Its **2021** CMU entry refers to the announced project, **not proof that it was fully operational in 2021**. More concretely, a **2025** published study remotely performed copper-nanocluster experiments at ECL in Austin and CMU’s automated laboratory in Pittsburgh, using different handlers and spectrometers across sites; models trained on **40 samples** predicted successful synthesis. This demonstrates research use and cross-site validation, not ECL profitability or a measured company-wide productivity gain. (zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 12-14, montoyagonzalez2025crosslaboratoryvalidationof pages 1-2)

**Strateos/Transcriptic.** Strateos emerged from the **Transcriptic–3Scan merger**; the landscape report lists a **14,000-ft² facility and approximately 200 instruments** under its **2019** company entry. Its original remote, workflow-oriented offering was followed by an announced shift toward **on-site cloud labs**, reported in academic discussion in **2024**. A substantive operating example is the DARPA SD2 *Round Trip* project: its open software was integrated with the **Strateos robotic cloud laboratory** and, by the **2022** paper, had been applied to **nearly 100 experiments**. The authors report roughly **two orders of magnitude faster experimental design and analysis** than prior ad hoc processes and describe a project-workflow staffing comparison of approximately **ten versus three people**. Those are results for the **combined software and experimental workflow**; they do not isolate a Strateos-only effect or establish Strateos revenue, margins, or profitability. (zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 12-14, arias2024scientificdiscoveryat pages 8-8, bryce2022roundtripan pages 9-10, bryce2022roundtripan pages 3-4, bryce2022roundtripan pages 1-2)

**Opentrons.** Its commercially sold, programmable OT-2 offers a lower-cost route to *in-house* automation rather than remote access to a provider’s lab. Published indicative prices differ with date and configuration: approximately **US$7,500 starting cost** in a **2024** review; about **US$10,000** as a **2025** review’s entry-level estimate; and approximately **US$20,000–30,000** with pipettes and accessory modules for a **2024** protein-workflow study. In that study, researchers processed **96 proteins in parallel**, with a proposed capacity of hundreds per user per week. These are product and application figures, **not** Opentrons sales, market share, or profits. (lo2023reviewoflowcost pages 2-5, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 6-7, nortonbaker2024enablinghighthroughputenzyme pages 1-2)

The industrial economics are instructive. Roche’s **2024** OT-2/AutoLab study reports **30 minutes less hands-on work per MetID assay**, approximately **half the labor cost per sample**, agreement with manual results of **ICC 0.998**, and replicate variability averaging **less than one-third** of the manual procedure’s. Nevertheless, at the studied assay volume the authors say the labor saving **did not fully offset hardware usage within five years**; sharing the machine across workflows might improve payback. This is stronger evidence than assuming every affordable robot rapidly pays for itself. (thieme2024deepintegrationof pages 5-6)

The landscape report cautions that provider facility measurements can include offices, instrument counts are reported inconsistently, and much information comes from company statements. The retrieved evidence supplies **no independently verified annual revenue, customer counts, profitability, valuation outcome, or definitive exit outcome** for ECL, Strateos, or Opentrons. Facility scale, funding or a published experiment should not be substituted for those business outcomes. (zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 12-14, zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 20-25)

## Open source: adoption benefits and cost limits

Open designs and code let labs modify instruments, share protocols, and build applications atop a purchased platform. Illustrations include the **2025** OT-Mation software; the **2025** open-source OT-2 cell-culture system that cut hands-on plate-passaging time by **more than half** in its reported tests; and a **2025** review’s account of the 3D-printed **FINDUS** liquid handler costing **under US$400 to construct**. The latter is a *construction* figure, not a like-for-like quotation for a validated, serviced commercial workstation. A 2024 review also describes a roughly **US$450** educational synthesis platform as a “frugal twin”: useful for learning and prototyping, but potentially less capable or precise than the research system it approximates. (laverick2025otmationanopensource pages 1-2, courville2025opensourcecellculture pages 1-2, doloi2025democratizingselfdrivinglabs pages 9-10, lo2024reviewoflowcost pages 5-6)

Openness does not remove the cost of calibration, skilled integration, analytical instruments, consumables, quality control, or maintenance. Roche notes the OT-2’s limited deck space, absence of plate transport and liquid-level detection, and eight-channel maximum head. A **2026 preprint** further argues that incompatible instrument-control conventions cause labs to rebuild integrations even when orchestration software is open source. Thus, open hardware and software plausibly lower the **entry price and reuse cost**, while validated interoperability and sufficient instrument utilization determine whether they lower the **cost per reliable result**. (thieme2024deepintegrationof pages 1-2, lo2024reviewoflowcost pages 5-6, gottstein2026talkingtoinstruments pages 1-4, thieme2024deepintegrationof pages 5-6)

**Assessment:** The demonstrated opportunity spans several different businesses—clinical automation equipment, research instruments, workflow software, and experiment-as-a-service. Published case studies substantiate technical utility and some workflow savings. They do **not**, on the evidence available here, substantiate a precise SDL market CAGR or the financial success of the three private companies. (nam2025revolutionizinglaboratorypractices pages 2-4, jung2025researchtrendanalysis pages 25-26, zilgalvis2025yingchiangjeffreylee pages 12-14, thieme2024deepintegrationof pages 5-6)
