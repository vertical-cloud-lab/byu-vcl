### Markforged Holding Corporation (MarkForged, Inc.)

founded 2013 · US · origin: founder_startup · openness 0→0 · acquired · $42.5M (acquisition_price, 2026-05) · funding $136.8M · revenue $85.1M (2024) · confidence: high · tool: WebFetch (search budget exhausted)

**What openness did.** Markforged was closed by design and relied on patents both offensively and defensively; the patent strategy cost it a $17.3M verdict and an ~$25M settlement in 2024. Openness had no visible role in its rise (SPAC at ~$2.1B) or fall (resold for $42.5M in 2026); the decline tracked weak revenue growth, losses and post-SPAC re-pricing rather than licensing choices.

**Takeaway.** Markforged is a SPAC-era closed-hardware story: about $137M of venture money and a ~$2.1B SPAC valuation in 2021, then a 1-for-10 reverse split, a patent loss, a $116M take-private by Nano Dimension in 2025 and a $42.5M resale to Stratasys in 2026. Its proprietary, cloud-tied printers changed hands twice in thirteen months, which is the vendor-continuity risk a lab takes on when it buys closed AM hardware.

**Timeline.** 2021: merger $2.10B, one (SPAC sponsored by A-star, led by Kevin Hartz; NYSE: AONE); 2025: acquisition $116.0M, Nano Dimension Ltd.; 2026: acquisition $42.5M, Stratasys Ltd.

**Risk events.** 2018: sued by Desktop Metal over metal-printing patents and alleged IP theft; a July 2018 ruling favoured Markforged; Jul 2021: listed via the 'one' SPAC at ~$2.1B implied equity value on ~$70M revenue; Apr 2024: jury verdict of $17.3M for Continuous Composites (patent case); Sept 2024 settlement with $18M paid 2024-10-10 plus $1M/$2M/$4M due in Q4 2025-2027, secured on patent rights; 2023-2024: revenue fell from $93.8M to $85.1M with net losses of $103.6M and $85.6M; 1-for-10 reverse split effective 2024-09-19; Apr 2025: taken private by Nano Dimension at $5.00/share (~$116M); CEO Shai Terem and CFO resigned; May 2026: resold to Stratasys for $42.5M, about 2% of the 2021 SPAC equity value (my arithmetic)

**Robustness.** Low as an independent entity: two changes of owner in about a year, with the composites business heading into Stratasys and the binder-jetting line staying with Nano Dimension. Customers rely on closed, cloud-connected software and proprietary consumables, so continuity depends on the new owner's support decisions. An installed base in '10,000 facilities across 70 countries' (2021) gives the acquirer a reason to keep supporting it.

**Physical-science relevance.** Not a laser powder-bed fusion vendor. Its metal line (Metal X: 17-4 PH, copper, H13, Inconel 625, D2; FX10 Metal Kit; PX100 binder jetting) is extrusion- or binder-based plus sintering, and it offers no aluminium alloy; its continuous-carbon-fibre parts are marketed to 'replace machined aluminum parts'. Relevant to a lab mainly for strong fixtures, grippers and enclosures for automated rigs, not for alloy development. Closed cloud software limits scripted, self-driving-lab use.
