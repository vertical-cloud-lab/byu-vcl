# Open-source commercialization in two self-driving-lab repositories

This digest supports [byu-vcl issue 380](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/issues/380), "The value of open science in the age of AI". It draws on every issue, pull request, conversation comment, inline review comment, review body, discussion, discussion comment and discussion reply in [`AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab`](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab) and [`sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo`](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo), downloaded with `gh api` on 2026-11-23, including content hidden behind "load more". The companion file [`coded_threads.csv`](coded_threads.csv) has one row for each of the 118 threads judged relevant. In this digest, "Sterling" is sgbaird, who started SDL-demo and led the AC Training Lab (later renamed AC Dev Lab); "SDL-demo" is self-driving-lab-demo; and "AC" is the Acceleration Consortium.

## Key findings

- **Selling was tried once, by an individual, and it barely paid.** Sterling sold the SDL-demo light-mixing kit through GroupGets group buys. Of the $80 list price, $15.57 went to GroupGets, $51.19 to parts and $6 to labour, which left $2.24 before shipping overhead. His next step was to hand ordering, assembly and testing to GroupGets ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124)). Apart from a DigiKey parts cart for self-assembly, the corpus has no other sales channel, no sales volumes and no revenue figures.
- **The institution buys rather than sells.** ac-dev-lab turned a ~$109-115 CAD camera kit into internal infrastructure: a 15-kit DigiKey order cost $1,461.87, and cameras went to at least five AC SDLs, a course rig and the Seifrid group's lab ([ac-dev-lab #289](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/289), [ac-dev-lab #340](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/340)). The repository never discusses selling, licensing out or spinning off its designs.
- **Closed commercial equipment is the institution's main pain point.** Problems include proprietary arm firmware with weak vendor support ([ac-dev-lab #56](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/56)), a centrifuge with no API that was run through a reverse-engineered community driver ([ac-dev-lab #174](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/174)), a decapper with no system documentation ([ac-dev-lab #123](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/123)), a proprietary connector ([ac-dev-lab #339](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/339)) and vendor API documentation that may not be publishable ([ac-dev-lab #532](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/532)).
- **Paid cloud-lab access was designed but never built.** The 2022 options (pricing tiers, credits, science-as-a-service, competitions; [SDL-demo #91](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/91), [SDL-demo #85](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/85)) became per-student credit quotas in a 2025-26 course ([ac-dev-lab #152](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/152)). The Stripe credits example was opened as an issue and never developed ([ac-dev-lab #244](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/244)).
- **Both projects run on free tiers and individual effort.** Certificate changes and an API deprecation broke users' builds ([SDL-demo #245](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/245), [SDL-demo #261](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/261)). Paid tiers were cut ([ac-dev-lab #155](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/155)) and student turnover stalled work ([ac-dev-lab #336](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/336)). From mid-2025 AI agents drafted BOMs and supplier searches quickly, but their errors needed human checking ([ac-dev-lab #289](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/289), [ac-dev-lab #296](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/296), [ac-dev-lab #339](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/339)).

### How threads were selected

1. Read all 200 records in `candidates_core1.json` (101 threads, or 98 after merging converted pairs).
2. Read the titles of all 816 threads in `thread_index.csv` and flagged any that touched the brief: kits, BOMs, costs, selling, vendors, licensing, partners and industry, cloud access, sustainability and funding, open hardware and replication, education and outreach, crowdfunding venues, Opentrons and Bambu.
3. Scored every thread by how many of its records matched seven keyword families (prices and purchasing; selling and kits; business, licensing and industry; funding; cloud access; education; replication and BOMs), ignoring dependabot and coveralls.
4. Ran targeted searches for licences and IP, start-ups and spin-offs, "commercial", funding and sponsorship, named intermediaries and vendors, "proprietary" and "reverse-engineered", "replicable" and "low-cost", and counts of users, kits and devices.
5. Read the flagged threads. Short and central threads were read in full, including every SDL-demo discussion on these topics and ac-dev-lab #56, #139, #174, #289, #296, #297, #340, #370, #371, #373, #374 and #471. Long threads were read as keyword-in-context extracts plus the key comments by the main participants, for example ac-dev-lab #11 (73 records), #15 (130), #142 (17), #152 (44), #161 (115) and #459 (65).
6. Kept a thread if it held substantive evidence on any topic in the brief. Threads whose only hits were incidental were dropped, such as "startup" meaning a boot script, licence text in tool output, or AI-tool token costs. General lab-outfitting purchases were also dropped, apart from one representative (ac-dev-lab #500) kept to show scale. Result: **118 coded threads** (42 SDL-demo, 76 ac-dev-lab), including 60 of the 98 candidate threads.

`stance_on_commercialization` codes:

- **pro**: the thread pursues or endorses a commercial route (selling kits, paid access, productising).
- **con**: it rejects a commercial route or documents harm from dependence on proprietary commercial products.
- **neutral**: it weighs commercial options descriptively (prices, procurement, mixed views).
- **na**: commercialization is not at issue; the thread is included as evidence on replication, access, education or sustainability.

Counts are SDL-demo pro 8, neutral 13, con 0, na 21; ac-dev-lab pro 1, neutral 25, con 1, na 49. Copilot-authored PRs are attributed to the requester (sgbaird) where the request is visible.

### Verification

A script checked every quotation in section (b) against the downloaded corpus. Each one is an exact substring of the body of the record it links to, and is 40 words or fewer. Authors and dates come from the same record. Every value in section (d) was checked the same way. When a comment pastes someone else's words (an email, a vendor reply, a licence or an essay), the original speaker is named and the person who posted it is given.

### Limitations

- The corpus has no sales volumes, revenues, user counts or download statistics. Every number is something a participant typed.
- Linked material was not read: GroupGets pages, emails, Slack, Google Docs and papers.
- Copilot text is agent output. In ac-dev-lab #289 and #297 it contained errors that sgbaird corrected.
- Keyword-led selection can miss threads that discuss commercialization in other words, and long technical threads were read through extracts.
- Stance codes are judgement calls.

## (b) Themes with evidence

### 1. Selling kits: venues, fees and thin margins

Codes: `selling_venues`, `kits_bom_costs`.

Only self-driving-lab-demo tried to sell anything. In July 2022 Sterling applied to Crowd Supply and pitched Pimoroni on a product with explicit specifications: price, desk footprint, setup time and no soldering ([SDL-demo #52](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/52)). In November 2022 he wrote up venues (Crowd Supply, GroupGets, Tindie, Etsy, eBay, Amazon, Tayda, LabMaker, experiment.com), US sales and income tax, and FCC rules for kits. He noted that kits are easier to sell than assembled products and that crowdfunding sites usually handle fulfilment and taxes ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124)). He then ran two GroupGets group buys of pre-assembled kits alongside a DigiKey cart for self-assembly ([SDL-demo #51](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/51), [SDL-demo #173](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/173)). The May 2023 cost breakdown shows the economics: $2.24 profit on an $80 kit. His response was to hand fulfilment to GroupGets, not to raise the price. The corpus has no later Crowd Supply discussion and no sales volumes. ac-dev-lab sells nothing.

- "the self-driving lab demo can be purchased in one or two online shopping carts for less than $100, takes up less than a square foot of desk space, and can be set up in less than an hour" — sgbaird, 2022-07-03, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/52#discussioncomment-3615595) (from Sterling's message to Pimoroni)
- "If you don't generate any profit, you effectively don't exist in the eyes of the IRS" — sgbaird, 2022-11-04, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124)
- "Tindie has a maker community, but doesn't handle sales tax for you, which is a pain, whereas Etsy is more oriented towards crafting but does take care of sales tax." — sgbaird, 2022-11-06, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124#discussioncomment-4066764)
- "If it's a DIY kit, it's almost impossible to differentiate between defects and user error." — sgbaird, 2022-12-07, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124#discussioncomment-4328589) (GroupGets' reply, posted by sgbaird)
- "Importantly, for the next round, I plan to fully hand off the parts ordering, kit assembly, and kit testing to GroupGets." — sgbaird, 2023-05-04, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124#discussioncomment-5808814)
- "there's a GroupGets campaign running for this until Feb 24, 2023, if you're interested in purchasing a pre-assembled one" — sgbaird, 2023-01-07, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/51#discussioncomment-4616894)

### 2. Designing the BOM around supply chains

Codes: `kits_bom_costs`.

Component availability shaped both projects. The 2022 Raspberry Pi shortage moved SDL-demo from the RPi 4 to the Pico W after Crowd Supply said a campaign could not be fulfilled ([SDL-demo #59](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/59)). Sterling budgeted each extension in dollars and setup hours, with liquid and pH demos under $200 and two hours and powder handling at $600-800 ([SDL-demo #77](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/77)), and replaced parts that would not scale in manufacturing ([SDL-demo #113](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/113)). At the AC the same habit produced a standard Pi Zero 2W camera kit, documented with a priced BOM and bought in bulk, with internal and external rollout in mind ([ac-dev-lab #289](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/289), [ac-dev-lab #340](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/340), [ac-dev-lab #328](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/328)). Stripping parts out had a cost: the course's 3D-printed sensor housing wore out and the charging base's coil worked loose, which Sterling attributed to his own push for a low-hardware design ([ac-dev-lab #552](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/552)).

- "Unless you have a really great connection at RPi, it is very unlikely that you will be able to get enough RPi 4s to fulfill a campaign." — sgbaird, 2022-08-13, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/59#discussioncomment-3615629) (Helen Leigh of Crowd Supply, posted by sgbaird)
- "was almost exclusively driven by the market constraints on finding RPi 4B's, Zero W's, Zero 2 W's, etc. and the less user-friendly ecosystems (or high cost) of other systems" — sgbaird, 2022-11-21, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/127#discussioncomment-4199624) (on the decision to switch to the Pico W)
- "While sculpting wire may not be a very scalable option from a manufacturing perspective, it can probably be replaced by a suitable (somewhat more expensive) gooseneck fixture." — sgbaird, 2022-10-22, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/113)
- "More from a long-term perspective (rolling out same solution internally and externally, ~5-50x)." — sgbaird, 2025-06-18, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/328#issuecomment-2986014324)
- "my overzealousness in asking for a low-hardware approach when Yanghuang was designing it (i.e., minimize number of items on bill of materials like nuts and screws and required tools)" — sgbaird, 2026-02-06, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/552#issuecomment-3861659650)

### 3. Licensing, IP and who may sell

Codes: `licensing_ip`.

Licensing is discussed explicitly only in SDL-demo, in August 2023 notes headed "IP Transfer" ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124)). The hardware is CERN-OHL-P-2.0 and the software MIT, so anyone, the AC included, may make and sell kits as long as the notices travel with them; GroupGets contracts leave the IP with the merchant. Sterling also excerpted Michael Weinberg's argument for a "commercialization-as-a-service" layer that moves lab hardware to market. The AC saw the other side of permissive licences: a regulator sold by LabMaker arrived apparently faulty, and its creator had not known it was being sold ([ac-dev-lab #139](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/139)). Elsewhere in ac-dev-lab, licensing is housekeeping (an MIT licence for a Kaggle competition in [ac-dev-lab #362](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/362), a third-party licence file kept beside its code in [ac-dev-lab #127](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/127)) plus one limit set by a vendor: whether its SEM API documentation could go in a public repository ([ac-dev-lab #532](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/532)).

- "provided that [the AC] ensure that the recipient of the Product has access to any Notices applicable to the Product" — sgbaird, 2023-08-15, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124#discussioncomment-6733941) (sgbaird quoting CERN-OHL-P-2.0, with [the AC] inserted)
- "GroupGets has a clause that IP is retained by the merchant (i.e., the individual/entity that signs the contract with GroupGets)." — sgbaird, 2023-08-15, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124#discussioncomment-6733941)
- "Creating commercialization-as-a-service could make it much more likely that open hardware developed in one lab becomes available to the research community more broadly." — sgbaird, 2022-11-06, [link](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124#discussioncomment-4066769) (Michael Weinberg's essay, quoted by sgbaird)
- "As a random aside, the creator of the regulator didn't realize labmaker was selling these products it seems." — sgbaird, 2025-03-25, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/139#issuecomment-2751835463)
- "Not sure if they'd be ok with us committing it to a public repo verbatim" — sgbaird, 2025-12-05, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/532#issuecomment-3617679281) (on the vendor's SEM API documentation)
- "I was planning to use an open-source license, probably MIT or Apache" — sgbaird, 2025-07-26, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/362#issuecomment-3120951540) (Amanichab's message, moved into the thread by sgbaird, who replied "MIT license is good.")

### 4. Commercial vendors: closed firmware, missing APIs and thin support

Codes: `vendors_lockin`, `platform_opentrons`, `platform_bambu`.

Most commercial contact in ac-dev-lab is with equipment vendors, and the recurring problem is closedness. The MyCobot arm's proprietary firmware left the lab dependent on a vendor whose GitHub was rarely monitored, and another user's arm failed within weeks and the vendor would not replace the board without proof of fault ([ac-dev-lab #56](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/56)). A centrifuge with no API was driven through a reverse-engineered community driver ([ac-dev-lab #174](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/174)). A capper/decapper had no system-level documentation ([ac-dev-lab #123](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/123)). A Robotiq connector was proprietary and expensive ([ac-dev-lab #328](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/328), [ac-dev-lab #339](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/339)). The OT-2's deck format limits integration ([ac-dev-lab #214](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/214)), and the Bambu A1 mini is driven through a community API ([ac-dev-lab #147](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/147)). The team still buys commercial products when nothing open matches them on price ([ac-dev-lab #270](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/270)), and it began choosing brands for how easily they can be automated ([ac-dev-lab #374](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/374)). SDL-demo had met the same problem earlier: commercial powder dispensers are cheap but generally have no API ([SDL-demo #92](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/92), [SDL-demo #153](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/153)).

- "I'm also realizing that the firmware is proprietary and isn't open source (it simply downloads proprietary binaries). The Python API is still open source, but it creates a dependency on ER to address bugs like these ones." — sgbaird, 2024-09-25, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/56)
- "Ultimately, they could not replace the expansion board without us being able to prove that it wasn't our fault, so at ~$88USD I just opted to purchase a replacement board directly." — aclasky, 2025-01-22, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/56#issuecomment-2607357670)
- "The MyCobot 280 Pi has been so frustrating in a lot of ways, but there's still not really a good alternative at that price point and it being a commercial (non-DIY) product." — sgbaird, 2025-06-17, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/270#issuecomment-2980762859)
- "Looking through their code, I see they have reverse-engineered commands and are sending raw binary." — linx5o, 2025-03-01, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/174#issuecomment-2692329187) (on the community driver for a centrifuge whose maker released no API)
- "The manuals are specific to the individual components, not for the capping/decapping system as a whole (which has virtually no documentation)." — sgbaird, 2025-01-23, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/123#issuecomment-2608694609)
- "For integrating many off-the-shelf lab equipment, the deck format and height restriction is a barrier for integration." — kelvinchow23, 2025-03-31, [link](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/214) (on the Opentrons OT-2)

### 5. Partners, industry and start-ups

Codes: `partners_industry`.

Neither repository discusses forming a company, spinning off or licensing out. Industry appears as suppliers and helpers. PCBWay offered free prototyping ([SDL-demo #52](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/52)), and Crowd Supply and GroupGets gave advice ([SDL-demo #59](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/59), [SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124)). The chocolate-printer maker Cocoa Press described its own plan for a sub-$2,000 kit ([SDL-demo #232](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/232)), a BAM researcher offered to cooperate ([SDL-demo #53](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/53)), and a BayBE user depended on the "sdl kit" ([SDL-demo #265](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/265)). At the AC the closest thing to an industry partnership was a Kardex vertical lift module. Johnstone Equipment supplied vibration data, a demo machine and an installation walkthrough, and then the purchase was cancelled although the technical requirements were met ([ac-dev-lab #216](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/216), [ac-dev-lab #371](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/371), [ac-dev-lab #471](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/471)). Institutional identity mattered too: an internet provider did not recognise the Acceleration Consortium as a business ([ac-dev-lab #445](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/445)).

### 6. Cloud-lab access: from pricing ideas to course credits

Codes: `cloud_access`.

In October 2022 Sterling laid out access models for a cloud lab whose capacity is scarce, unlike compute: an open first-in-first-out queue, SLURM-style priorities, paid access split between commercial and non-commercial users, credits, metered billing, a "science-as-a-service" subscription, and competitions ([SDL-demo #91](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/91), [SDL-demo #85](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/85)). What got built was simpler: per-user database keys, per-device broker credentials and a public demo broker ([SDL-demo #94](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/94), [SDL-demo #127](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/127), [SDL-demo #226](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/226)). An outside contributor argued that the real barrier is running a facility, not writing an API. At the AC in 2025 the credit idea came back as a teaching device, with per-student budgets and a queue on the OT-2, rather than as a business model ([ac-dev-lab #152](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/152), [ac-dev-lab #553](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/553)). Expensive instruments stayed private ([ac-dev-lab #133](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/133)), public access raised safety questions ([ac-dev-lab #76](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/76)), and a Stripe credits example was opened as an issue but never developed ([ac-dev-lab #244](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/244)).

### 7. Sustainability: free tiers, churn, turnover and funding

Codes: `sustainability_funding`.

Both projects run on free tiers by design, and pay for it in breakage and support. HiveMQ certificate changes broke users' builds more than once, MongoDB's Atlas Data API deprecation broke logging, and a published protocol could not be corrected ([SDL-demo #245](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/245), [SDL-demo #253](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/253), [SDL-demo #261](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/261)). At the AC, paid tiers were dropped when they got expensive (HiveMQ Starter, [ac-dev-lab #155](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/155)) or questioned (Tailscale, while seeking a free or academic plan, [ac-dev-lab #184](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/184)). A lab robot depended on a student's personal free account ([ac-dev-lab #18](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/18)), and a project stalled when its student left ([ac-dev-lab #336](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/336)). Funding is barely discussed. One passage in [SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124) notes that grant-funded open hardware has already been paid for by the grantor. AC SDLs run as independent units with their own funding ([ac-dev-lab #529](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/529)), and Kaggle prizes of up to 10k USD were possible but none were offered ([ac-dev-lab #262](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/262), [ac-dev-lab #362](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/362)).

## (e) What was decided and what was left open

### Decided

**self-driving-lab-demo**

- Build around the Pico W rather than the RPi 4, because of supply ([SDL-demo #59](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/59), [SDL-demo #127](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/127)).
- Sell pre-assembled kits through GroupGets group buys and keep a DigiKey cart for do-it-yourself buyers ([SDL-demo #51](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/51), [SDL-demo #173](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/173)). From the next round, hand ordering, assembly and testing to GroupGets ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124), 2023-05-04).
- Release hardware under CERN-OHL-P-2.0 and software under MIT, with notices travelling with products ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124), 2023-08-15).
- Control access with per-user database keys and per-device broker credentials on free tiers, while publishing demo credentials so anyone can try it ([SDL-demo #94](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/94), [SDL-demo #127](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/127), [SDL-demo #194](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/194), [SDL-demo #226](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/226)).
- Prefer "forever free-tier" services and modular tools ([SDL-demo #233](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/233)). Make Ax optional so downstream users are not blocked ([SDL-demo #265](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/265)).

**ac-dev-lab**

- Standardise the Pi Zero 2W camera kit, publish a priced BOM and buy kits in bulk for AC labs ([ac-dev-lab #289](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/289), [ac-dev-lab #297](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/297), [ac-dev-lab #340](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/340), [ac-dev-lab #243](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/243), [ac-dev-lab #251](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/251), [ac-dev-lab #301](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/301), [ac-dev-lab #302](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/302), [ac-dev-lab #303](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/303), [ac-dev-lab #496](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/496), [ac-dev-lab #507](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/507)).
- Use per-student credits and a queue in the OT-2 course practical, and run it again in 2026 ([ac-dev-lab #152](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/152), [ac-dev-lab #552](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/552), [ac-dev-lab #553](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/553)).
- Keep SEM control private ([ac-dev-lab #133](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/133)). Delete the paid HiveMQ Starter cluster ([ac-dev-lab #155](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/155)).
- Run the Branin competition under MIT, with the AC as organiser and no prizes ([ac-dev-lab #362](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/362)).
- Choose an automation-friendly UPS brand ([ac-dev-lab #374](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/374)). Do not buy the vertical lift module ([ac-dev-lab #471](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/471), [ac-dev-lab #371](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/371), [ac-dev-lab #373](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/373)).

- **Vendor alternatives**: Sterling was still "wondering about alternatives" to the MyCobot maker in March 2025 ([ac-dev-lab #56](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/56)), the decapper may not be bought again ([ac-dev-lab #336](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/336)), and an "opentrons-cloud" fork was floated without a decision ([ac-dev-lab #459](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/459)).
- **Openness limits**: whether vendor API documentation can be published ([ac-dev-lab #532](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/532)), and how to keep a published protocol current ([SDL-demo #245](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/245)).
- **Fit and safety of open hardware**: whether OpenFlexure can become "a cheap option that just works" ([ac-dev-lab #142](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/142)), whether the Pioreactor suits SDL6 ([ac-dev-lab #174](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/174)), safeguards before public Pioreactor access ([ac-dev-lab #76](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/76)), and a Pi Zero 2W variant to address the "number one" Wi-Fi problem ([SDL-demo #258](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/258)).

## (f) Lessons and comparison

### Evidence-based lessons for commercializing open lab hardware and SDLs

1. **For an academic project, selling kits is a distribution service, not income.** At $80 the margin was $2.24 per kit, and the fix was to outsource fulfilment rather than raise prices ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124)). Price for cost recovery, and let an intermediary carry tax, returns and support.
2. **Choose the venue for the work you do not want to do.** Crowd Supply wants original boards and a near-final prototype, GroupGets handles listing and returns for about 13% plus $5, and Tindie leaves sales tax to the seller ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124), [SDL-demo #59](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/59)).
3. **Design the BOM for supply chains and for strangers.** Use in-stock parts, one or two carts, no soldering and parts that scale ([SDL-demo #52](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/52), [SDL-demo #59](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/59), [SDL-demo #113](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/113)). But do not strip hardware so far that durability suffers ([ac-dev-lab #552](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/552)).
4. **Permissive licences invite resale, so plan for it.** CERN-OHL-P and MIT let anyone, including the developer's own institution, make and sell kits ([SDL-demo #124](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/124)). Expect unannounced third-party sellers and quality problems that reflect on the design ([ac-dev-lab #139](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/139)).
5. **When buying equipment, openness is worth paying for.** Closed firmware, missing APIs and missing documentation cost the institution more effort than list prices suggest ([ac-dev-lab #56](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/56), [ac-dev-lab #123](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/123), [ac-dev-lab #174](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/174), [ac-dev-lab #214](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/214), [ac-dev-lab #532](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/532)). Prefer brands that can be automated ([ac-dev-lab #374](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/374)) and support community drivers ([ac-dev-lab #147](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/147), [ac-dev-lab #174](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/174)).
6. **Users pay commercial vendors for turnkey reliability.** Open hardware competes when it "just works", because staff time otherwise outweighs the saving ([ac-dev-lab #142](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/142), [ac-dev-lab #270](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/270)).
7. **Cloud access is an operations problem before it is a pricing problem.** The hard parts are consumables, maintenance, safety and instrument cost ([SDL-demo #127](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/discussions/127), [ac-dev-lab #76](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/76), [ac-dev-lab #133](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/133)). Credits worked as a teaching tool ([ac-dev-lab #152](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/152)) long before anyone built them as a business ([ac-dev-lab #244](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/244)).
8. **Free tiers lower the barrier to entry but pass risk to users.** Budget for churn such as certificate changes and API deprecations, and keep a living changelog, because published papers cannot be updated ([SDL-demo #245](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/245), [SDL-demo #253](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/253), [SDL-demo #261](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/261)).
9. **Institutions add scale and built-in users, but also risk aversion and turnover.** Cameras reached at least five AC SDLs and another group's lab ([ac-dev-lab #340](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/340), [ac-dev-lab #243](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/243), [ac-dev-lab #507](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/507)), yet a technically feasible lift module was cancelled "due to perceived risks" ([ac-dev-lab #471](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/471)), and work stalled when students left ([ac-dev-lab #336](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/336)).
10. **AI agents speed up sourcing and documentation of open hardware, but need verification.** Distributor anti-bot measures limit them, and the errors were mundane but frequent ([ac-dev-lab #289](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/289), [ac-dev-lab #296](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/296), [ac-dev-lab #339](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/339)). Open, machine-readable BOMs and documentation are what make the agents useful at all.

### ac-dev-lab (institutional) vs self-driving-lab-demo (individual and academic)

| Dimension | ac-dev-lab (institutional) | self-driving-lab-demo (individual and academic) |
|---|---|---|
| Period and volume | 2024-02 to 2026-11; 4232 records; 549 threads | 2022-07 to 2025-11; 696 records; 267 threads (255 after merging converted pairs) |
| Who writes | 48 human authors; sgbaird 54% of records; Copilot 8% | 27 human authors; sgbaird 70% of records |
| Direction of money | Buys: $1,461.87 DigiKey kit order, USD 1,345.93 and CA$3,335.35 outfitting orders, ~$500/month internet quote | Sells: $80 kit with $2.24 profit; free-tier services; donated PCB prototyping |
| Commercial counterparts | Lab-equipment vendors (Elephant Robotics, DH Robotics, Robotiq, Kardex via Johnstone, Opentrons, Bambu, LabMaker) | Maker-ecosystem intermediaries (Crowd Supply, GroupGets, Tindie, Tayda, Pimoroni, PCBWay) |
| Main commercial problem | Closed firmware, missing APIs and documentation, support refusals, risk-averse procurement | Thin margins, tax and compliance overhead, component shortages |
| Licensing | MIT for competitions; vendor limits on publishing docs; little explicit hardware licensing | Explicit CERN-OHL-P-2.0 (hardware) and MIT (software); IP-transfer notes |
| Access model | Built: course credits and queues, private control of expensive instruments, safety review | Designed: pricing and credit tiers, science-as-a-service, competitions; built: per-device keys and a public demo |
| Dissemination | Internal rollouts to at least five AC SDLs and another group's lab; microcourses, practicals, workshops, Kaggle | Kits, Star Protocols article, videos; outside adopters (researchers, university teams, a company employee) |
| Sustainability risk | Student turnover, paid-plan decisions, contact change in 2026, sparse 2026 activity | One maintainer, free-tier churn, slow replies to outside users |
| AI use | Heavy: Copilot BOMs, sourcing, docs and reviews | Light: a few Copilot PRs in 2025 to loosen dependencies |

The individual project explored commercialization more openly and more fully. It covered venues, taxes, FCC rules, licences and pricing models, but it lacked the scale to make selling pay, so it pushed fulfilment to an intermediary. The institutional project had budgets, staff and built-in users, which let it turn the same open-hardware habit (cheap, documented kits) into infrastructure for many labs and courses. It did not try to sell, and its commercial energy went into coping with closed vendors. The AC microcourses and the 2025-26 practicals show the two strands converging: the individual demo became teaching material, and the institution reused the SDL-demo access ideas as course quotas ([SDL-demo #252](https://github.com/sparks-baird/self-driving-lab-demo/issues/252), [ac-dev-lab #152](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/152)). For issue 380, the corpus suggests that what made the work reusable, by other labs and later by AI agents, was openness of designs, BOMs and interfaces. Revenue played no part ([ac-dev-lab #289](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/pull/289), [ac-dev-lab #555](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/discussions/555)).
