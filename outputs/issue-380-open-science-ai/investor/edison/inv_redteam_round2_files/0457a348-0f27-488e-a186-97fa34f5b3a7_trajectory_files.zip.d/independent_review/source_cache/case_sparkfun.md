### SparkFun Electronics

founded 2003 · US · origin: founder_startup · openness 3→3 · confidence: high · tool: WebFetch (search budget exhausted)

*Computed:* age 23 yrs · 0 closing / 0 opening moves

**What openness did.** SparkFun shows that open hardware plus no patents is compatible with long-term operation. It has run for 23 years with about 140-150 staff (2015-2018) while publishing everything, and it holds the second-largest OSHWA portfolio in this group (about 264 by 2020). Like Adafruit it stayed mid-sized and private. Value comes from in-house manufacturing, curation and education, not exclusivity. Its recorded legal disputes are about trademark and trade dress (SPARC, Fluke), not about copying of its designs.

**Takeaway.** SparkFun is the long-lived, mid-sized open-hardware manufacturer. It was founded by a student in 2003 and still operates from Colorado, publishing schematics and EAGLE files for 400+ in-house designs, with an explicit no-patents policy and an early role in writing the OSHW definition. No valuation, funding or revenue is public, and no recent headcount was found beyond about 140 in 2018. It supports the pattern that open hardware firms can be durable but stay small and private. The trademark and trade-dress disputes (SPARC 2009-2010, Fluke 2014) show that brand, not design IP, is where open-hardware firms meet legal risk.

**Risk events.** 2009-10-16: SPARC International sent a cease-and-desist over the SparkFun name and domain. The two signed a trademark coexistence agreement on 2010-04-02.; 2014-03-07: US Customs seized a shipment of multimeters whose yellow jackets resembled Fluke's trade dress. SparkFun had them destroyed, and Fluke later sent genuine units as goodwill.; 2016: founder Nathan Seidle stepped down as CEO and Glenn Samala took over.; No-patent policy: by design there is no IP barrier to copying the products.

**Robustness.** SparkFun has operated for 23 years (2003-2026) as a privately held company and makes 400+ open designs in-house. Its value lies in manufacturing, curation, documentation and education rather than IP, since it holds no patents. Every design is published and many are OSHWA-certified, so the hardware could outlive the company. Funding history and finances are not public.

**Physical-science relevance.** SparkFun breakout and development boards (sensors, GNSS, motor drivers) are common building blocks in low-cost scientific instruments, and the published schematics allow them to be adapted for lab use.
