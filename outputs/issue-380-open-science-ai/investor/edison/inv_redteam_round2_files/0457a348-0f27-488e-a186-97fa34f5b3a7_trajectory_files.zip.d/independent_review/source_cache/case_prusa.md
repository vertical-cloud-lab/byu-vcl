### Prusa Research a.s.

founded 2012 · CZ · origin: founder_startup · openness 3→2 · private_bootstrapped · revenue $157.0M (2024) · confidence: medium · tool: WebSearch + WebFetch (search budget exhausted)

*Computed:* age 14 yrs · 1 closing / 1 opening moves

**What openness did.** Open GPL designs and the RepRap community gave Prusa fast, cheap brand growth from a one-person start-up to roughly CZK 3.65B revenue (2024) with no disclosed outside capital. From 2023 the company progressively withheld hardware files, citing 1:1 clones and Chinese patent filings, yet Hackaday notes the feared Prusa clones are hard to find; the bigger competitive threat became closed, subsidised Chinese printers (Bambu Lab) that reused Prusa's open slicer. Closing did not prevent slowing sales and the first layoffs in 2026, suggesting price competition rather than cloning was the binding constraint.

**Takeaway.** Prusa is the clearest case of an open-hardware pioneer retreating to 'open software, source-available hardware': it kept firmware (GPL) and slicer (AGPL) open but moved its newest printers' CAD to a custom non-commercial Open Community License. Openness built the brand and community cheaply, but it did not protect against well-funded closed competitors, and the protective licence came after market share had already shifted.

**Timeline.** 2023: GPL design files (i3 up to MK3) → STL-only printable parts, withheld electronics and Nextruder design files (MK4) (closing); 2025: withheld CORE One CAD → Open Community License (non-commercial-sale, source-available) (opening)

**Risk events.** 2023-03: Prusa blog cites 1:1 commercial clones, stripped copyright headers and local patents filed on open-source work; 2023: MK4 launched with design files withheld (STL only); PCB layouts withheld to avoid clone boards; 2024-11: CORE One announced as closed hardware; Hackaday: 'Prusa's open source hardware dream quietly dies'; 2025-08: Josef Prusa post 'open hardware desktop 3D printing is dead' blames below-cost Chinese printers and state-reimbursed Chinese patent filings; 2026-03: first-ever layoffs, about 70 non-production roles of about 1,144, as sales targets were missed; Competitive pressure from closed Bambu Lab, whose Bambu Studio slicer derives from Prusa's open PrusaSlicer

**Robustness.** Founder-controlled and bootstrapped (no disclosed outside funding), profitable but thin 2024 net margin (CZK 47.3M net on CZK 3.65B revenue); vertically integrated (Prusament filament, Printables community site) with a Delaware factory (Prusa USA, formerly Printed Solid, renamed July 2026) serving a market that is about 35% of revenue. Its open software (PrusaSlicer) is widely forked, so that part would outlive the company.

**Physical-science relevance.** RepRap-lineage open FFF printers are a standard tool for fabricating custom labware and open scientific hardware; GPL-era i3 designs remain reusable by labs, while OCL designs allow lab use and spare parts but not commercial resale.
