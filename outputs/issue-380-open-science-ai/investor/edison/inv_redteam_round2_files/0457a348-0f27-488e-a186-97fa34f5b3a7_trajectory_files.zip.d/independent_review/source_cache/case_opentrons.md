### Opentrons Labworks, Inc.

founded 2014 · US · origin: founder_startup · openness 3→2 · private_vc · $170.2M (private_valuation, 2026-10) · funding $206.1M · confidence: medium · tool: WebSearch (+ WebFetch of cited pages)

*Computed:* age 12 yrs · 1 closing / 0 opening moves · latest value ÷ funding raised ≈ 0.8×

**What openness did.** Openness was the founding go-to-market: a $2,000 open-hardware OT-One crowdfunded from the DIYbio/academic community built the brand ('the PC of biotech labs', TechCrunch 2016) and a user-written protocol ecosystem. Opentrons then closed the hardware with the OT-2 (2018) while keeping the software open, which preserved scriptability and SDL integration without handing designs to cloners; no fork of the robot emerged. The 2021 peak ($1.8B) and the 2022-2025 collapse (~$162M) were driven by COVID testing and its end, not by the openness choice.

**Takeaway.** Opentrons is the canonical open-hardware lab-automation test case, and its arc is 'open to enter, close the hardware to scale': the open OT-One won early users cheaply, but revenue came from a closed-hardware OT-2/Flex whose open Apache-2.0 API is the remaining open asset and the reason it dominates academic self-driving-lab builds. Its valuation fell about 91% from the 2021 SoftBank round to the 2025 Series D, a COVID boom-and-bust rather than a verdict on openness. In 2026 it repositioned as 'open, AI-native' infrastructure connecting AI to the wet lab, marketing 'open' as API access rather than open designs.

**Timeline.** 2018: Open hardware files (OT-One, Apache-2.0) + open software → Proprietary hardware (OT-2) + open software (closing)

**Risk events.** 2018: OT-2 launched as closed hardware; open-hardware status lost (software stayed Apache-2.0); Sep 2021: $200M Series C led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2 at ~$1.8B (Bloomberg), up from ~$90M in 2020, on COVID-testing growth; Dec 31 2022: Pandemic Response Lab (subsidiary ReOpen Diagnostics, NYC COVID testing) wound down, all ~185 jobs eliminated (Bloomberg); 2022-2025: headcount drifted from ~276 to ~223 (Revelio Labs); anonymous Glassdoor reviews describe repeated layoffs incl. the PRL division (unverified); Jul 2025 (announced 30 Jan 2026): CEO changed from co-founder Jon Brennan-Badal to James Atwood (ex-PerkinElmer/MOBILion, GM of robotics unit since Apr 2023); 20 Nov 2025: Series D/D-1 (~$20M, BroadOak, SOSV) at ~$162M post-money per Forge, roughly 91% below the 2021 valuation (my arithmetic)

**Robustness.** Single-vendor company with no foundation, but the whole control stack (app, robot server, Python API, labware schema) is Apache-2.0 on GitHub, so protocols and integrations could outlive the firm. Current hardware designs are proprietary, so the community could not build or repair OT-2/Flex robots independently. Large installed base (>11,000 robots in >80 countries, 14 of top-15 biopharmas, every top-20 US research university per the 2026 release) and survival through a ~91% valuation reset suggest a durable niche.

**Physical-science relevance.** Built for life-science liquid handling, but the OT-2 is one of the most common low-cost liquid handlers in academic materials/chemistry automation: e.g. precursor preparation for high-throughput sonochemical synthesis (RSC Digital Discovery 2023), CdSe nanocrystal synthesis paired with the Jubilee platform (MRS 2024), and Turkevich gold-nanoparticle teaching protocols (LAPSE 2026). Limited to pipettable liquids (no powders/solids). The requesting lab (BYU VCL) itself runs an OT-2 in its color-mixing SDL.
