### Strateos, Inc. (formerly Transcriptic)

founded 2012 · US · origin: founder_startup · openness 1→1 · confidence: low · tool: WebSearch (+ WebFetch of cited pages); budget exhausted after 8 queries

*Computed:* age 14 yrs · 0 closing / 0 opening moves

**What openness did.** Transcriptic tried to seed an ecosystem with an open protocol standard (Autoprotocol, BSD) while keeping the lab and execution proprietary. The standard did not become an industry lingua franca, and the company still had to restrict Autoprotocol submission to paying customers. The 2023 retreat to private on-site labs points to unit-economics and demand problems with centralized hosted labs, not to openness, as the decisive factor.

**Takeaway.** Strateos (ex-Transcriptic) is the cautionary tale among US cloud labs. Over a decade it raised roughly $80M+ in announced rounds, won Eli Lilly as an anchor partner, and opened only a peripheral protocol standard. In April 2023 it abandoned the hosted cloud-lab model for on-site private-lab consulting, and since then its web presence has gone stale and its ownership is unconfirmed. Its open Autoprotocol library outlived the business model but never achieved network effects, which suggests an open interface standard alone cannot rescue a capital-intensive, closed, centralized lab service.

**Timeline.** 2019: merger, 3Scan

**Risk events.** 2012-2016: raised ~$27M as Transcriptic (seed $1.2M 2012; $8.5M 2015; $13.4M 2016, Data Collective); Jun 2019: merged with 3Scan to form Strateos; Jan 2020: Eli Lilly and Strateos launched an 11,500 sq ft remote-controlled robotic lab in San Diego (>100 instruments, 5M-compound store); Jun 2021: closed a $56M Series B (Lilly among investors); 18 Apr 2023: 'strategic shift' away from hosted cloud lab toward designing and deploying customers' own on-site private cloud labs on its LodeStar software, plus a staffing reorganization; 2023-2026: website appears unmaintained (footer (c) 2022, last news item Feb 2023, contains spam text); aggregators conflict (Craft 'Active'; PitchBook 'acquired by Daiichi'; another directory says acquired by Multiply Labs Dec 2023). Neither acquisition is confirmed by a primary source; a targeted search found no Multiply Labs release mentioning Strateos.

**Robustness.** Fragile. Hosted cloud-lab capacity depended on a single pharma partner (Lilly's San Diego lab) and the company retreated from hosted labs in 2023. Its one open asset, Autoprotocol, is a BSD library and standard that can outlive the company but saw limited outside adoption (Emerald's SLL and others did not adopt it). After 2023 the company appears dormant or absorbed, with no primary-source confirmation either way.

**Physical-science relevance.** Moderate. The focus was biology and drug discovery (small-molecule screening, medicinal chemistry, imaging), but Transcriptic/Strateos was, with Emerald, one of the two pioneering US commercial cloud labs. Its trajectory is the main failure case for the hosted cloud-lab model that materials self-driving-lab efforts (and NSF's programmable cloud lab network) are now revisiting.
