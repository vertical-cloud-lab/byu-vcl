# 2151 Overview of the Changes to 35 U.S.C. 102 and 103 in the AIA \[R-01.2024]

*\[Editor Note: This MPEP section is **only applicable** to applications
subject to examination under the first inventor to file (FITF) provisions of the AIA as
set forth in **[35 U.S.C. 100 (note)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d917_1bef1_2b)**. See **[MPEP § 2159](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2159.html#ch2100_d20034_1dc34_1dd)** et seq. to determine whether an application is subject to
examination under the FITF provisions, and **[MPEP § 2131](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2131.html#d0e202959)**\-**[MPEP § 2138](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2138.html#d0e207005)** for
examination of applications subject to **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302383)**.]*

After the AIA, **[35 U.S.C. 102](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_234ed_52)** continues to set forth
the scope of prior art that will preclude the grant of a patent on a claimed invention, but
revises what qualifies as prior art. Specifically, **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)** and **[(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)** set forth what qualifies as
prior art. **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)** provides that a person is not entitled to a
patent if the claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in
public use, on sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date
of the claimed invention. **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)** provides that a
person is not entitled to a patent if the claimed invention was described in a patent
issued under **[35 U.S.C. 151](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e303440)**, or in an application for patent published or deemed published
under **[35 U.S.C. 122(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e303063)**, in which the patent or application, as the case may be, names
another inventor, and was effectively filed before the effective filing date of the claimed
invention. **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_156)** sets forth exceptions to prior art established in **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_2ee)**. Specifically, **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_172)** sets forth exceptions to prior art established in **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)**, and **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_19a)** sets forth
exceptions to prior art established in **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)**.

The AIA also provides definitions in **[35 U.S.C. 100](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302338313)** of
the meaning of the terms “claimed invention,” “effective filing date,” “inventor,” and
“joint inventor” (or “coinventor”). The AIA defines the term “claimed invention” in **[35 U.S.C. 100(j)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e22_345)** as the subject matter defined by a claim in a patent or an
application for a patent. The AIA defines the term “effective filing date” for a claimed
invention in a patent or application for patent (other than a reissue application or
reissued patent) in **[35 U.S.C. 100(i)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e1a_3b9)** as meaning the
earlier of: (1) The actual filing date of the patent or the application for the patent
containing the claimed invention; or (2) the filing date of the earliest provisional,
nonprovisional, international (PCT), or foreign patent application to which the patent or
application is entitled to benefit or priority as to such claimed invention. The AIA
defines the term “inventor” in **[35 U.S.C. 100(f)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302367313)** as the individual or,
if a joint invention, the individuals collectively who invented or discovered the subject
matter of the invention, and in **[35 U.S.C. 100(g)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e0c_133)** defines the term
“joint inventor” and “coinventor” to mean any one of the individuals who invented or
discovered the subject matter of a joint invention.

As discussed previously, **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)** provides that a person is not entitled to a patent if the
claimed invention was patented, described in a printed publication, or in public use, on
sale, or otherwise available to the public before the effective filing date of the claimed
invention. Under **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302391)** and **[(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302395)**, knowledge or use of the invention
(**[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302391)**), or public use or sale of the invention (**[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302395)**), was required to be in the United States to qualify as a
prior art activity. Under the AIA, a prior public use, sale activity, or other disclosure
has no geographic requirement (i.e., need not be in the United States) to qualify as prior
art.

**[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_172)** provides that certain disclosures made one year or less
before the effective filing date of a claimed invention shall not be prior art under **[35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)** with respect to the claimed invention if: (1) The
disclosure was made by the inventor or joint inventor or by another who obtained the
subject matter disclosed directly or indirectly from the inventor or a joint inventor; or
(2) the subject matter disclosed had, before such disclosure, been publicly disclosed by
the inventor or a joint inventor or by another who obtained the subject matter disclosed
directly or indirectly from the inventor or a joint inventor. Thus, **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_172)** effectively provides a one-year grace period (grace period)
after a first inventor-originated disclosure of an invention (i.e., a disclosure by an
inventor or joint inventor, or another who obtained directly or indirectly from the
inventor or a joint inventor) within which the inventor, assignee, obligated assignee, or
other party having sufficient interest may file a patent application which is not subject
to such disclosure and certain other disclosures as prior art. The one-year grace period in **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_172)** is measured from the filing date of the earliest
U.S. or foreign patent application to which a proper benefit or priority claim to such
invention has been asserted in the patent or application and the earlier application
supports the claimed invention in the manner required by **[35 U.S.C. 112(a)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_2ae60_3d5)**. Under **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302383)**, the one-year
grace period is measured from the filing date of the earliest application filed in the
United States (directly or through the PCT) and not from the dates of earlier filed foreign
patent applications. Similar to **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302383)**, the one-year
grace period in **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_172)** is extended to the next succeeding business
day if the end of the one-year grace period otherwise falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or
federal holiday. *Ex parte Olah, 131 USPQ 41* (Bd. App. 1960) and **[35 U.S.C. 21(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e301443)**. The provisions of **[35 U.S.C. 21(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e301443)** still apply
notwithstanding the provisions of **[37 CFR 1.6(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9020-appx-r.html#d0e313465)** and **[37 CFR 1.10](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9020-appx-r.html#d0e313951)**, which
accord a filing date as of the date of deposit as Priority Mail
Express® with the U.S. Postal Service, and **[37 CFR 1.6(a)(4)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9020-appx-r.html#d0e313476)**,
which accords a filing date as of the date of submission using the USPTO patent electronic
filing system. See also **[MPEP §§ 2153.01(a)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html#ch2100_d20033_24e66_2ac)** and **[2153.02](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html#ch2100_d20033_25935_125)**.

The date of invention is not relevant under **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_234ed_52)**. Thus, a prior art disclosure could not be antedated by showing
that the inventor invented the claimed invention prior to the effective date of the prior
art disclosure of the subject matter (e.g., under the provisions of **[37 CFR 1.131](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9020-appx-r.html#aia_d0e323504)**).

As discussed previously, **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)** provides that a person is not entitled to a patent if the
claimed invention was described in a U.S. patent, a U.S. patent application publication, or
an application for patent deemed published under **[35 U.S.C. 122(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e303063)** (collectively referred
to as “U.S. patent documents”), that names another inventor and was effectively filed
before the effective filing date of the claimed invention. Under **[35 U.S.C. 374](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e307311313)**, a
World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) publication of a Patent Cooperation Treaty
(PCT) international application that designates the United States is an application for
patent deemed published under **[35 U.S.C. 122(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e303063)** for purposes of **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)**. Thus, under the AIA, WIPO publications of PCT
applications that designate the United States are treated as U.S. patent application
publications for prior art purposes, regardless of the international filing date, whether
they are published in English, or whether the PCT international application enters the
national stage in the United States. Accordingly, a WIPO publication of a PCT application
(WIPO published application) that designates the United States, a U.S. patent, or a U.S.
patent application publication that names another inventor and was effectively filed before
the effective filing date of the claimed invention, is prior art under **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)**. Under **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(e)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302407)**, a WIPO
published application designating the United States is treated as a U.S. patent application
publication only if the PCT application was filed on or after November 29, 2000, and
published under **[PCT Article 21(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9025-appx-t.html#d0e363634)** in the English language. See **[MPEP § 2136.03](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2136.html#d0e205924)**,
subsection III.

**[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(d)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11ecb_1d1)** defines “effectively filed” for the purpose of determining
whether a particular U.S. patent document is prior art under **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)** to a claimed invention. A U.S. patent document is
considered to have been effectively filed for purposes of its prior art effect under **[35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)** with respect to any subject matter it describes on the
earlier of: (1) The actual filing date of the patent or the application for patent; or (2)
if the patent or application for patent is entitled to claim the benefit of, or priority
to, the filing date of an earlier U.S. provisional, U.S. nonprovisional, international
(PCT), or foreign patent application, the filing date of the earliest such application that
describes the subject matter of the claimed invention. Thus, a U.S. patent document is
effective as prior art as of the filing date of the earliest application to which benefit
or priority is claimed and which describes the subject matter relied upon, regardless of
whether the earliest such application is a U.S. provisional or nonprovisional application,
an international (PCT) application, or a foreign patent application.

**[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(A)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_1a7)** and **[(B)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_1b3)** provide that a disclosure shall not
be prior art to a claimed invention under **[35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)** if: (1) The subject
matter disclosed was obtained directly or indirectly from the inventor or a joint inventor;
or (2) the subject matter disclosed had, before such subject matter was effectively filed
under **[35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)**, been publicly disclosed by the inventor or a joint
inventor or another who obtained the subject matter disclosed directly or indirectly from
the inventor or a joint inventor. Thus, under the AIA, a U.S. patent document that was not
issued or published more than one year before the effective filing date of the claimed
invention is not prior art to the claimed invention if: (1) The U.S. patent document was by
another who obtained the subject matter disclosed from the inventor or a joint inventor; or
(2) the inventor or a joint inventor, or another who obtained the subject matter disclosed
from an inventor or joint inventor, had publicly disclosed the subject matter before the **[35 U.S.C. 102(d)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11ecb_1d1)** (“effectively filed”) date of the U.S. patent document.

Additionally, **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_1bf)** provides
that a disclosure made in a U.S. patent document shall not be prior art to a claimed
invention under **[35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)** if, not later than the effective filing date of
the claimed invention, the subject matter disclosed and the claimed invention were owned by
the same person or subject to an obligation of assignment to the same person. This
provision replaces the exception in **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(c)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302521)** that applied
only in the context of an obviousness analysis under **[35 U.S.C. 103(a)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_19797_b0)** to prior art that was commonly owned at the time the claimed
invention was made, and which qualified as prior art only under **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(e)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302407)**, **[(f)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302420)**, and/or **[(g)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302424)**. Thus, the AIA
provides that certain prior patents and published patent applications of co-workers and
collaborators are not prior art either for purposes of determining novelty
(**[35 U.S.C. 102](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_234ed_52)**) or nonobviousness (**[35 U.S.C. 103](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_19797_b0)**). This exception,
however, only removes disclosures as prior art under **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(2)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_315)**, namely, U.S. patent documents that were not published
before, but that were effectively filed before, the effective filing date of the claimed
invention. This exception is not effective to except prior art that is also available under **[35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)**, that is, patents, printed publications, public uses, sale
activities, or other publicly available disclosures published or occurring before the
effective filing date of the claimed invention. A prior disclosure, as defined in **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)**, by a co-worker or collaborator is prior art under **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e72_307)** unless it falls within an exception under **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(1)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_172)**, regardless of whether the subject matter of the
prior disclosure and the claimed invention was commonly owned not later than the effective
filing date of the claimed invention.

The AIA eliminates the provisions in **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(c)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302399)** (abandonment of the invention), **[102(d)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302403)** (premature
foreign patenting), **[102(f)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302420)** (derivation), and **[102(g)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302424)** (prior invention by another, but
see below for discussion of continued limited applicability). Under the AIA, abandonment of
the invention or premature foreign patenting is not relevant to patentability. Prior
invention by another is likewise not relevant to patentability under the AIA unless there
is a prior disclosure or filing of an application by another. A situation in which an
application names a person who is not the actual inventor as the inventor
(**[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(f)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302420)**) will be handled in a derivation proceeding under **[35 U.S.C. 135](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_19834_19b)**, by a correction of inventorship under **[37 CFR 1.48](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9020-appx-r.html#d0e317930)** to name
the actual inventor, or through a rejection under **[35 U.S.C. 101](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302376)** and **[35 U.S.C. 115](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302875912)**. See **[MPEP § 2157](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2157.html#ch2100_d20034_1bb92_e7)**.

**[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(c)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e8f_148)** provides for deemed common ownership of subject matter made
pursuant to joint research agreements. Under **[35 U.S.C. 100(h)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e10_359)**, the term “joint
research agreement” as used in **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(c)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e8f_148)** is defined as a
written contract, grant, or cooperative agreement entered into by two or more persons or
entities for the performance of experimental, developmental, or research work in the field
of the claimed invention. **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(c)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e8f_148)** specifically
provides that subject matter disclosed and a claimed invention shall be deemed to have been
owned by the same person or subject to an obligation of assignment to the same person in
applying the provisions of **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_1bf)** if: (1) The
subject matter disclosed was developed and the claimed invention was made by, or on behalf
of, one or more parties to a joint research agreement that was in effect on or before the
effective filing date of the claimed invention; (2) the claimed invention was made as a
result of activities undertaken within the scope of the joint research agreement; and (3)
the application for patent for the claimed invention discloses or is amended to disclose
the names of the parties to the joint research agreement.

**[AIA 35 U.S.C. 103](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_19797_b0)** provides that a patent for a claimed invention may not be
obtained, notwithstanding that the claimed invention is not identically disclosed as set
forth in **[35 U.S.C. 102](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_234ed_52)**, if the differences between the claimed invention and the
prior art are such that the claimed invention as a whole would have been obvious before the
effective filing date of the claimed invention to a person having ordinary skill in the art
to which the claimed invention pertains. In addition, **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 103](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1fbe1_19797_b0)** provides that patentability shall not be negated by the manner in
which the invention was made. This provision tracks **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(a)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302455)**, except that
the temporal focus for the obviousness inquiry is before the effective filing date of the
claimed invention, rather than at the time of the invention. The provisions of **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(c)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302521)** have been replaced with **[AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b)(2)(C)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e7d_1bf)** and **[(c)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#al_d1d85b_11e8f_148)**, and the provisions of **[pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 103(b)](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/mpep-9015-appx-l.html#d0e302462)** pertaining to biotechnological processes have been
eliminated.
