### Desktop Metal, Inc.

founded 2015 · US · origin: founder_startup · openness 0→0 · acquired · funding $438.0M · revenue $148.8M (2024) · confidence: medium · tool: WebFetch (search budget exhausted)

*Computed:* age 11 yrs · 0 closing / 0 opening moves

**What openness did.** Openness played no role: Desktop Metal was closed from founding and competed on patents (>1,000 by 2023) and proprietary software. Its arc was driven by capital markets: a $2.5B SPAC valuation, an acquisition spree, a failed Stratasys merger, a contested sale to Nano Dimension and a Chapter 11 filing within about five years of listing. Proprietary control did not protect value, and customers had no open fallback when the vendor failed.

**Takeaway.** Desktop Metal is the clearest bust of the 2020-21 AM SPAC wave: an MIT-founded, $438M-venture-funded closed metal-AM company that listed at up to $2.5B in December 2020, lost most of its value, was bought by Nano Dimension for $5.295 a share in April 2025 and filed for Chapter 11 in July 2025. For a lab buying closed metal-AM tools, it shows that a vendor's survival risk becomes the lab's: sintering recipes, software and service disappear with the company unless the lab owns open process data.

**Timeline.** 2020: merger $2.50B, Trine Acquisition Corp. (SPAC, NYSE: TRNE); 2025: acquisition, Nano Dimension Ltd.; 2025: bankruptcy

**Risk events.** Dec 2020: listed through the Trine SPAC at an implied equity value up to $2.5B before its high-volume Production System shipped in volume; 2021: acquisition spree, including EnvisionTEC (Feb 2021), Adaptive3D, Aerosint, Aidro, several dental labs, and the ExOne brand; May-Sep 2023: all-stock combination with Stratasys (combined value ~$1.8B, Desktop Metal holders ~41%) voted down by Stratasys shareholders and terminated; 2024: revenue fell to $148.8M from $189.7M (2023); GAAP net loss $219.5M; year-end cash and short-term investments only $20.4M (preliminary, 8-K 2025-03-26); Jul 2024-Apr 2025: Nano Dimension acquisition contested in court (a 2025-03-24 decision went against Nano, per Markforged's 10-K) before closing on 2025-04-02; Jul 2025: Chapter 11 filing three months after the takeover, with a plan to sell assets (Bloomberg: 'facing liquidation'); an IndustryWeek caption (Jan 2026) refers to 'Desktop Metal's sale to Arc Public Benefit Corp.' (details not confirmed)

**Robustness.** Low. Every layer (printers, firmware, Live Suite software, sintering recipes) was proprietary and single-vendor, so the installed base depends on whoever acquired the assets in the 2025 bankruptcy, which could not be confirmed. Nothing was released openly that the user community could maintain.

**Physical-science relevance.** Sinter-based metal AM (binder jetting, bound metal deposition) rather than laser powder-bed fusion: parts are printed green and sintered, so microstructures and alloy design rules differ from LPBF. The 10-K lists 'more than 20 standard metals, including aluminum, titanium, stainless steels, tool steels' for binder jetting, and the P-1 was positioned for 'process and materials development', which made it one of the few commercial metal systems sold for alloy R&D. No open API or SDL integration was documented. For a LPBF aluminium-alloy lab it is a closed comparator and a warning about vendor continuity.
