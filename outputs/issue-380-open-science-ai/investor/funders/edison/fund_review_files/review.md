# Independent review of `FUNDERS.md`

**Scope of review, 26 November 2026.** I treated `PROTOCOL.md` as the precommitted inclusion rule and independently recomputed the claims from the seven supplied CSVs, without changing them. CSV row numbers below are physical file line numbers (header is line 1); Markdown line numbers refer to the supplied `FUNDERS.md`. My reproducible checks, including per-cell comparisons, are in [`review_support/audit_checks.csv`](review_support/audit_checks.csv) and [`review_support/recompute.py`](review_support/recompute.py). External primary-source URLs below are documented with full BibTeX in [`review_support/references.bib`](review_support/references.bib). I treat separate scope, alias, and transaction corrections as **sensitivities**, not as a fully reconstructed clean dataset.

## Overall assessment

**I would retain the inventory, but not endorse the current “accurate” or “every count is a lower bound” wording.** Most published rankings reproduce exactly; the stronger problems are definitional and evidential: acquisitions counted as finance, duplicate companies and investor aliases, explicitly non-open or nonprofit entries, and a historical audit presented alongside augmented data. The broad “long tail with a recognizable head” finding survives the conservative corrections I tested. The list is a useful starting point—not a complete or directly actionable fundraising map for an open physical-science company.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Critical — acquisition proceeds, secondary sellers and an initial public offering are called venture funding

**Evidence.** `PROTOCOL.md`, “What counts as a funder,” explicitly excludes acquirers and initial-public-offering (IPO) buyers. Yet `deals.csv` rows **864** (KKR and Clayton, Dubilier & Rice's **$5.3 billion Cloudera buyout**), **898–902** (SUSE acquired by Novell for **$210 million**, sold to EQT for **$2.535 billion**, EQT take-private, Attachmate's **$2.2 billion Novell acquisition**, and SUSE IPO) and **933** (Thoma Bravo's **$2.3 billion ForgeRock acquisition**) all have `counted=yes`. Row **846** records the Red Hat IPO with `counted=yes`, albeit without a named buyer. Cloudera's own [announcement](https://www.cloudera.com/about/news-and-blogs/press-releases/2021-06-01-cloudera-enters-into-definitive-agreement-to-be-acquired-by-clayton-dubilier-and-rice-and-kkr-for-5-point-3-billion-dollars.html) explicitly calls $5.3 billion an *all-cash acquisition price*, not growth capital [`cloudera2021`]. `deals.csv` rows **202–203** list shareholders *selling* Odoo shares in secondary transactions as “investors”; those names must not be inferred to have invested in that transaction, even though they may have invested earlier. For just eight indisputable acquisition/IPO rows (846, 864, 898–902, 933), removing the deal-derived links eliminates **six unique funder–venture pairs** and changes **2,024 to 2,021** funders in U1–U4 (**1,697 to 1,694** in U1–U3). The complete amount contamination cannot be computed without adjudicating every sale and secondary transaction.

**Correction.** Split financing paid **to the venture** from ownership transfers, take-privates, shareholder sales and IPOs; move the latter to an exits/secondary table with `counted=no`. Recalculate funder and deal counts and prohibit summing buyout prices into funding totals. Keep original investor names only where an independently sourced *earlier* capital investment is documented. This matters more than a small rank change: a $5.3 billion buyout misrepresents the sort of cheque available to a new lab venture.

### 2. High — ventures and grants that the protocol excludes or cannot establish as open remain in the headline universe

**Evidence.** `PROTOCOL.md` excludes nonprofits from the **deal** list and requires the **main product** to pass an openness test. In `ventures.csv`, rows **846** (`u4-our-world-in-data`, five investor links) and **942** (`u4-votingworks`, 14 links) are nonprofits: their own [funding page](https://ourworldindata.org/funding) calls Our World in Data's publisher a registered charity [`owid2026`], and VotingWorks [calls itself a 501(c)(3) nonprofit](https://www.voting.works/about) [`votingworks2026`]. Row **657** (`u4-arc-prize-foundation`, one accelerator link) is also a foundation; its corporate form needs checking before treating that link as venture financing. These three rows account for **20 links**, without proving that every listed supporter is wrongly recorded as a donor. `ventures.csv` explicitly marks **six** U4 ventures `open_ok=no`; four funded ones—rows **650** Airtrain AI, **653** Alpaca (brokerage API, not an open main product), **680** Cakework and **882** Rownd—supply **15 more links** (`investor_links.csv` rows **46, 1,072, 1,165, 1,700, 2,492, 2,818, 3,023, 3,295, 3,330, 3,585, 3,651, 3,824, 3,827, 3,852, 4,048**). Non-U4 cases also warrant actual open-product checks: `ventures.csv` row **609**, Reflection AI, says no weights release had been confirmed yet (**14 investor links**); row **471**, Trilobio, describes the **lab-automation platform as closed**, with only API/tooling repositories open (**12 links**); rows **403** SiFive and **470** Tenstorrent say their principal chip designs are proprietary. An open instruction-set architecture or software *around* closed chips is not proof of openly licensed main-hardware design. U2 has **37/97** `open_ok=partial` entries.

**Correction.** Exclude explicit `open_ok=no` ventures, transfer nonprofit funders to `programs.csv`, and re-audit each `partial` row against the *main-product* test at the financing date. Publish strict and broad counts, with platforms and open-weight-but-closed-product cases separate. The U1–U3 headline is not directly changed by the U4 `no` rows, but U2 comparables and all-group/early-round advice are affected.

### 3. High — repeated company aliases inflate the U4 early-stage and angel rankings

**Evidence.** Identical company histories have been counted under separate U1/U4 keys: `ventures.csv` rows **77/977** (`mem0ai`/Embedchain), **205/973** (Onyx/Danswer), **256/1,011** (Reflex/Pynecone), **23/969** (Comfy Org/ComfyUI). In three pairs the two investor sets are **identical** (15, 6 and 13 names respectively); the Comfy names overlap in **4/6** each. Two more duplicate repo/company pairs appear at rows **957/960** (AITable/APITable; same `open_url`) and **1,041/1,042** (Zinc/ZincSearch; same `open_url`). Conservatively merging only these **six** identified pairs changes U1–U4 from **1,042 to 1,036 distinct ventures**, and funder–venture links from **4,178 to 4,136**; these are the same companies, not six additional financing opportunities. `investors.csv`/`investor_links.csv` then give **Y Combinator 310**, rather than **313**; **Paul Copplestone 7**, rather than **9**; **Guillermo Rauch 19**, rather than **20**. This does not disturb U1–U3-only rankings, but it directly changes the bottom-line people/accelerator examples (the bottom line uses all four groups).

**Correction.** Create a venture-entity alias table before deduplicating investor–venture pairs; assign one canonical key per company, allow name histories and subsidiaries as attributes, and separately label genuine successor companies. Recompute U4 and all-universe top-funder/early-round tables.

### 4. High — the “only 1,320 funding events” statement is not reproducible, while the other headline totals are

**Evidence.** `FUNDERS.md` line **23** claims **1,320 funding events** across the 640 U1–U3 ventures. In `deals.csv`, those universes contain **1,386 rows**, **1,381** with `counted=yes`; restricting to rows with a named funder yields **1,265**. Even an *approximate*, not validated, duplicate-event grouping by `(key, date, type, amount_usd)` yields **1,319 counted groups**, not 1,320. Different month labels, vague dates, inconsistent labels and extra investor lists make grouping by those four fields unreliable: for example, `deals.csv` rows **63, 64 and 66** repeat Grafana's August 2024 $270 million financing, while rows **62 and 65** cover its February 2022 $240 million financing. Identical rows **64/66** and **360/362** are exact duplicates. `ventures.csv.n_rounds` sums to **1,386** in U1–U3, matching *rows*, not unique financings. I cannot determine which events were intended to constitute **1,320** without the missing build/deduplication rule. The reported **1,697** distinct U1–U3 funders, **1,294** one-venture funders, **102** with at least five, and top-ten share of **291/3,137=9.28% (~9%)** are exactly reproducible from `investor_links.csv`.

**Correction.** Assign a reviewed `round_id` to each real financing, merge sources and investor lists into that event, explicitly mark uncertain/undated events and separately count named-funder and unnamed events. Either publish the resulting event count and its script or replace “1,320 funding events” with “1,381 counted deal **records**, including duplicates and non-financing records” pending repair.

### 5. High — the “accurate but incomplete; every count is a lower bound” claim overstates precision

**Evidence.** The source checker (`source_checks_deals.csv`) reports **4,815 `found`**, **164 `in page markup`**, **139 `not on page`** and **214 `unavailable`** out of **5,332 checked name occurrences**. `FUNDERS.md` line **586** correctly computes **4,815/5,118=94.08%** *text matches on fetchable pages*, or **4,979/5,118=97.28%** if markup matches count; neither measures whether the person or institution actually **invested**. Examples in `source_checks_deals.csv` rows **88–95** are Vercel investor names absent from its cited About page; row **133** flags a purported YC participant on the cited Nous Research page; others may have independent support. `investor_links.csv` flags **143/4,178** links as *database/Wikipedia-only*, and `FUNDERS.md` does not supply the protocol-promised source-quality sensitivity: omitting those flags yields **4,035 links / 1,968 funders**, versus **4,178 / 2,024** in U1–U4. That flag is narrower than “any link ever touched by Wikipedia”: it identifies links whose sources are *only* database/Wikipedia. Independently, Findings 1–3 demonstrate overcounting. The audit overlap measures incomplete recall **relative to another list**, not true positive predictive value. Moreover, `ventures.csv` shows **nine `funding_found=yes` ventures without a named link** and one `funding_found=none_found` venture with a link (ARC Prize Foundation). Therefore, *every count is a lower bound* is false for observed event, venture and funder counts: missing funders and spurious ones operate in opposite directions.

**Correction.** Say **“a disclosed, selectively researched inventory with both omissions and false inclusions; its raw counts are neither population estimates nor certified lower bounds.”** Report source quality by investor/link, validate roles in context, and publish separate strict-source, permissive-source and scope-corrected tables. Do not use the 94% string-match rate as a 94% investment-verification rate.

### 6. Medium-high — repeated funders, funder types and corporate-parent rollups remain insufficiently normalized

**Evidence.** `investors.csv` rows **38/120** record **Sapphire Ventures** as a venture firm with **13** ventures and **SAP Ventures** as corporate venture with **5**. Their own [2014 announcement](https://sapphireventures.com/press/sap-ventures-becomes-sapphire-ventures/) states SAP Ventures *became* Sapphire Ventures [`sapphire2014`]. A firm-level rollup across those names yields **17 distinct ventures** (one in common), which lifts it from the 13-venture tier; do not treat the independently operating post-2014 firm as SAP's current corporate arm. `investors.csv` rows **316/1,612** similarly split **Partech** (venture firm, 2) from **Partech International** (*government grant*, 1)—Partech describes itself as an independent venture investor, with a historical Paribas spinout [`partech2022`]. Other transparent case-only duplications include `System.One`/`System.one` (rows **508/1,840**), `Translink Capital`/`TransLink Capital` (**517/1,886**) and `ZhenFund`/`Zhenfund` (**525/2,014**). Merging just Sapphire/SAP, Partech/Partech International, plus seven exact case-variant pairs, takes U1–U3 **1,697 to 1,690** funders, singletons **1,294 to 1,284**, and funders with at least five ventures **102 to 101**; one overlapping Sapphire/SAP venture removes one U1–U3 link. Do **not** automatically roll up parent vs arm, similarly named firms or individuals without checking control and dates (e.g. `Fidelity` versus `Fidelity Ventures`). `investors.csv` also puts **Partech International** in `government_grant` and **National Social Security Fund Zhongguancun Fund (Legend Capital)** (row **640**) in `government_grant` despite being described as a fund; `Wells Fargo` (row **521**) is `unclassified` despite lender links in `deals.csv`. **1,197/2,024** investor-type assignments are `heuristic`, and **69** are still `unclassified` (**70 links**).

**Correction.** Apply a dated entity/vehicle hierarchy with per-row evidence, distinguish historical corporate ownership from current type, and review all high-count and grant/public-money categories manually. Recompute the long-tail/type mix with a disclosed alias table; the present head and tail are descriptive, not robustly normalized.

### 7. Medium-high — the physical-science section conflates a single crowdfunding label with a dominant funding channel

**Evidence.** The line “Crowdfunding is the most common single source” (`FUNDERS.md` lines **412–414**) is true **only as a single repeated investor label**: U2 has **Kickstarter backers on 13/97** ventures (the broader U1-science plus U2 table gives **14**, consistent with lines 370–389). At the **type** level, U2 has **178 venture-firm** links and **56 corporate/strategic** links, versus **24 crowdfunding** links; its crowdfunding links occur across **19/97** ventures versus **40/97** ventures with a venture firm and **29/97** with a corporate investor (`investor_links.csv` + `investors.csv`). Program-level support is a separate pool: `programs.csv` has **167 `funds_companies=no`**, **85 `yes`**, **43 `sometimes`**, **7 `unknown`** rows, but is not an exhaustive independent count of unique grant programs. The claim that **only 13 funders backed at least three U2 ventures** is correct (`investor_links.csv` with `universe=U2`). The section header expressly includes U1 science-topic ventures as well as U2 (line **366**), whereas the bottom-line figure of **97** and **13** concerns U2 alone; these are not interchangeable denominators. `ventures.csv` records `source_list=github_core` for **all 387 U1** and no science-topic membership column in the supplied data. Thus the U1-science-plus-U2 ranking (e.g. Kickstarter **14**) cannot be independently recreated exactly from these files without the earlier science-topic classification or an explicit key list.

**Correction.** Say “Kickstarter is the most frequent *individual crowd label*; venture firms are the most common **funder type** in U2.” The early-round table is also not a “first-cheque” table: YC has **372 early participation records for 307 keys**, and Series A may be a repeat investment; replace the “first cheques” characterization at line **455**. Publish the U1-science key list, separate U2-only and expanded science/comparable tables, and stratify by hardware, instruments, scientific software, AI-for-science and DeSci; show **first cheques and leads**, not just name frequency. For an open physical-science founder, prioritize grant eligibility, prototype/manufacturing stage, present-day cheque size and equipment expertise rather than transplanting general COSS rankings.

### 8. Medium — independent audit supports incompleteness, but its percentages are historical and some discrepancies are aliases

**Evidence.** In `edison_audit_comparison.csv` (40 ventures), `ours=180`, `edison=296`, `both=154`: hence **154/180=85.56% (~86%)** and **154/296=52.03% (~52%)**, as `FUNDERS.md` line **589** says. There are **26 only-ours** and **142 only-Edison** name occurrences; eight original ventures had zero in “ours” but some in “edison” (rows **2, 4, 7, 9, 19, 20, 23, 26**). This is not a precision/recall gold standard: on the `radixark` row **39**, for example, the `only_ours` string **“ldvp”** and `only_edison` **“ldv partners”** may refer to one entity. `FUNDERS.md` line **592** says higher-confidence Edison findings were subsequently added (`xed`), so the same 40 keys now have **304 investor links** in `investor_links.csv`, not 180; the 86% and 52% describe a **pre-augmentation** snapshot, not current recall. The statement that the literature check found “the two missing” Indiegogo backers and Footprint Coalition (line **590**) is already stale: `investors.csv` row **1,304** explicitly includes **Indiegogo backers** (one Open Bionics link), and `FUNDERS.md` line **572** also names them.

**Correction.** Date and archive the pre-audit snapshot, normalize both audits' funder aliases and funding types, and re-evaluate unmatched names and missing rounds against the current table. Remove Indiegogo from the still-missing list; retain Footprint until added. Do not quote the 86% as a current or statistically valid accuracy estimate.

### 9. Medium — material missing investor and program examples, notably science publishing and experimental instruments

**Evidence.** `ventures.csv` contains **no PeerJ**, and `investors.csv` contains neither **SAGE** as a publisher-investor nor PeerJ funding from its backer O'Reilly AlphaTech Ventures (the latter has one *other* portfolio link, `investors.csv` row **645**). PeerJ's [own 9 July 2014 financing announcement](https://peerj.com/blog/post/91251985913/exciting-times-peerj-secures-next-round-of-funding-led-by-sage-and-oreilly/) names **SAGE** and **O'Reilly**, describes their capital as a new investment, and says **O'Reilly Media and OATV** supported the open-access publisher from inception [`peerj2014`]. Record verified corporate and venture backers under the actual investing legal entities without double counting O'Reilly Media/OATV. `programs.csv` (302 rows) contains no **Tool Foundry** or **Footprint Coalition** program. The [Tool Foundry](https://www.toolfoundry.org/) accelerator states it gave **$50,000 per selected team**, supported by **Moore Foundation and Schmidt Futures** [`toolfoundry2019`]; [Experiment's Low-Cost Tools for Science](https://experiment.com/grants/tools) lists Footprint Coalition among supporters and identifies funded *open-source* instrument projects [`experiment2026`]. Neither is proven to be a currently open corporate-equity opportunity, so list them as **historical/project-level** funding routes. OpenFlexure's [own funding ledger](https://openflexure.org/about/funding) identifies **UK Research and Innovation/Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council, the Royal Society**, and other research and challenge-grant funders for a specific open microscope project [`openflexure2026`]; the Royal Society is missing from `programs.csv` and `investors.csv`, but OpenFlexure itself is a **project**, not necessarily an eligible venture. Astera's [2026 announcement](https://astera.org/astera-partnering-with-the-national-science-foundation/) describes up to **$20 million** in matched Astera support alongside **$380 million** of National Science Foundation support for a programmable-cloud-laboratories test bed [`astera2026`]; `programs.csv` includes Astera generally but not this targeted partnership. **Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy)** is another genuine missing *program-level funder*: the [Our World in Data funding page](https://ourworldindata.org/funding) names it, the Skip Foundation and Mohn Westlake Foundation, but none has a funder entry in the supplied deal/program tables; the only “Coefficient” occurrence in `programs.csv` row **13** is a note saying it is not named. The [Center for Open Science](https://www.cos.io/benchmarking-llm-agents-on-scientific-tasks) independently identifies Coefficient support for its scientific-task benchmarking project [`cos2026`]. These examples broaden open-science philanthropy, not evidence of willingness to invest equity in instruments companies. The existing `programs.csv` rows **2/27** give conflicting **$51.8 million for CZI cycles 1–5** versus **$47 million for cycles 1–6**, and rows **26/28** repeat the same **$20 million** Open Source for Science Fund commitment. Those are not additive budgets.

**Correction.** Add and source PeerJ/SAGE/OATV as a true for-profit venture-financing example; add Tool Foundry, Experiment/Footprint, Royal Society/OpenFlexure, Coefficient Giving and Astera/NSF lab infrastructure as **different program categories**, with dated eligibility and whether money went to a business, university or project. Reconcile conflicting CZI totals against primary award reports, and collapse double-entered commitments. An open-lab founder needs a **program/eligibility map** as much as an equity-investor roll call; a past grant to a university is not evidence that the same funder would finance a company.

### 10. Medium — statements about direct company grants and “open-source theses” are too categorical

**Evidence.** `FUNDERS.md` lines **499–505** assert “Moore granted to Julia Computing and, for Numba and Dask, to Continuum Analytics.” The only cited evidence in the supplied dataset is **`programs.csv` rows 243–245**, with recipient field `companies` and $600,000, $1,057,443 and $700,000; the corresponding `ventures.csv` rows **408** Anaconda and **435** JuliaHub do **not** have Moore in `investor_links.csv`. JuliaHub's own `ventures.csv.notes` says “Moore Foundation/Intel supported Julia generally, **not tied to company**.” The Moore URLs in `programs.csv` could not be parsed beyond “Grant Detail” during this check, so I **cannot** verify the actual legal grantees or upgrade project support to corporate financing. The statement “26 funders ... state an open-source thesis in their own words” (`FUNDERS.md` lines **45–47**) equals **26 nonempty `investors.csv.stated_open_source_thesis` cells**, not 26 verified dedicated COSS investors. Examples: M12's quoted line in `FUNDERS.md` **180** concerns appetite from acquirers/public markets, not an explicit investment mandate; Zerodha (line **191**) describes support for free and open-source software rather than venture investing. Conversely `programs.csv` rows **156** Heavybit, **162** Crane and **189** OpenCapital.vc are labelled “open-source investment thesis” but not captured by that 26-row flag. `FUNDERS.md`'s “most are large generalists” is a subjective characterization, not a reproducible size measure.

**Correction.** Differentiate a dedicated open-source fund mandate, occasional favorable investor commentary, strategic open-source grantmaking and a direct corporate grant. Cite the actual recipient/payee and investment terms before calling a grant company financing. Provide a transparent criteria field and date for “states a thesis,” rather than one boolean constructed from heterogeneous quotes.

### 11. Medium — this delivery cannot independently substantiate every linked example, historical claim or program amount

**Evidence.** The supplied directory contains only `FUNDERS.md`, `PROTOCOL.md` and seven CSVs, not the listed `FUNDER_LIST.md`, `data/investor_aliases.csv`, `data/yc_open_source.csv`, original `edison/` audit/literature reports, science-topic membership, or charts (`FUNDERS.md` lines **1–19, 590, 596–670**). The write-up's `data/` links also do not match these top-level CSV paths in the supplied workspace. I independently checked the **380 numeric cells** in the principal rank/lead/early-round/thesis tables, **12 parent rollups** and **56 type-mix cells**; all **448** table-cell comparisons agreed with their referenced aggregated CSVs. The audit ledger contains **454 verified** comparisons including six other matched bottom-line tests, **one mismatch** (event count), and **88 additional recomputed measures**; exact matches may inherit errors from the common deal input. `PROTOCOL.md` fixes **392 U1 companies and three universes**, whereas the files have **387 U1 companies and a fourth universe**: U4 is openly acknowledged as added after collection started (lines **606–608**), but the five missing U1 records have no supplied exclusion/change log. The three-universe protocol therefore does not fully precommit the delivered sample; the U1 selection itself is systematic by GitHub popularity, **not a random sample of ventures**. The owner classification “frozen before collection,” source-review correction of **99** names (68/16/10/5), hand review of **12** links (9/3), portfolio-page check **105/77/70/68**, YC directory **183/289**, earlier science-topic membership, and broad literature assertion **45/47**, and literature claims of **~28,000 Kickstarter projects / 39% greater relative funding / 33% less for patent-based projects** (lines **575–577**) are **not independently verifiable from these files**. I also cannot confirm the universal claim that no study measures investor returns from open source simply because an unseen literature answer found none. Many program budgets have inconsistent horizons, in-kind contributions or overlapping grant rounds; do not sum the `committed_usd` column indiscriminately.

**Correction.** Supply a 392-to-387 U1 reconciliation and a dated scope-amendment log, as well as the versioned source artifacts, alias and type rules, science-topic key list, original audit snapshots and original citation pages alongside a corrected funder list. Label third-party spot checks as reported rather than independently repeated, and put the seven actual CSVs at the paths used by hyperlinks. Do not treat arithmetic agreement with the generated tables as external fact-checking.

**Method and limits.** I reconstructed deal participants from `lead` and `investors` in `counted=yes` rows and verified they produce precisely the **4,178** published investor–venture links; all seven numeric `investors.csv` aggregation fields also match `investor_links.csv`. No source census, funding-return study, validity-weighted random sample of all open ventures, systematic worldwide grant search, or primary confirmation of every deal was possible. The independent audit is of 40 previously sampled ventures and has been partly incorporated into the dataset. Absence from this selected inventory does **not** establish a funder has never invested. A non-random corpus of notable COSS/AI companies (U3), a YC/Runa-heavy U4, and topic-selected U2 cannot estimate the size or investor mix of a general open-science market. ## Discretionary analytical decisions

- I compared headline totals with **U1–U3 only**, but angel, studio, thesis and program claims against the **U1–U4** rows specified in each section; this avoids shifting denominators quietly.
- I counted one unique `(investor, venture key)` pair for rankings and expanded every named investor in a `counted=yes` deal for event-lead counts; I did **not** treat a shared source URL as proof that two event rows are identical. The early-round table measures participations through Series A, not necessarily each investor’s first cheque to a company (YC has **372 early records across 307 venture keys**).
- I used conservative, explicitly enumerated alias and transaction exclusions as **separate sensitivity checks**, not as estimates of the “true” fully corrected list, because further identities and round roles need human validation.
- I relied on the companies' or funders' own public pages for external examples where accessible and withheld conclusions about the Moore grants' direct corporate recipients when the relevant detail pages were unreadable.
