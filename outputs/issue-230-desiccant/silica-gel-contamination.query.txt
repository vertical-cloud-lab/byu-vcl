Can silica gel desiccant contaminate reactive metal powders stored with it in the same sealed container?

Context: a university lab has no glovebox yet. It intends to store aluminium-based LPBF/ultrasonic-atomization feedstock powders, 150-300 um particle size: elemental Al, Mg, Si, Mn, Cr, Ti, Fe, Ni, Cu, Zn, Sn powders plus brittle crushed Al-Zr, Al-Sc, Al-Er, Al-Ce and Al-Li master-alloy granules in airtight containers together with loose silica gel desiccant packets (McMaster-Carr type), at room temperature, possibly for several months, before melting and ultrasonic atomization.

Please address, with literature evidence and quantitative values where available:
1. Is amorphous silica gel (SiO2) chemically inert toward aluminium, magnesium, and the other metal powders listed at room temperature? Is there any solid-state or vapour-mediated reaction, or is the only realistic contamination route physical (silica dust/fines shedding from the beads and mixing into the powder)?
2. What does silica-dust cross-contamination do to the downstream process: SiO2 inclusions in an aluminium melt, the thermodynamics of SiO2 reduction by molten Al or Mg (2Al + 3SiO2 -> ..., Mg + SiO2 -> MgO + Si), oxide inclusions and lack-of-fusion porosity in the atomized product?
3. Do indicating silica gels pose an additional hazard? Specifically cobalt(II) chloride blue indicating gel (CoCl2) and methyl-violet orange indicating gel: can cobalt or chloride transfer to the powder, and does chloride promote pitting corrosion of aluminium powder?
4. What equilibrium relative humidity can silica gel actually hold in a sealed container at 20-25 C, and how does that compare with the RH needed to prevent hydroxide/hydrate growth and hydrogen pickup on Al and Mg powder surfaces over months? Does silica gel desorb water if the container warms?
5. If silica gel is inadequate, which desiccant is preferred for reactive metal powder storage - molecular sieve (zeolite 3A/4A), activated alumina, anhydrous calcium sulfate (Drierite), calcium oxide - and which should be avoided (e.g. deliquescent calcium chloride, phosphorus pentoxide)? Give the equilibrium RH each can reach and the practical failure modes.
6. What is best practice for physically separating the desiccant from the powder (breathable sachets vs loose beads, secondary containment, keeping powder in its unopened supplier jar inside a desiccated outer box)?
