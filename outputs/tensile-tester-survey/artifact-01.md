| Crosshead-derived modulus error | MT-02 underestimated Young’s modulus by **48%** relative to a commercial frame using video extensometry; crosshead-derived strain differed by approximately a factor of two. | Parallel tests on the MT-02 and a ZwickRoell frame; comparison of motor-step-derived crosshead strain with optically measured gauge strain. | Use a video extensometer or DIC on the gauge section. If crosshead data must be used, characterize and subtract load-train compliance. | Demmel 2024 (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000013) |
| Machine and fixture compliance | Reported values include **2.5 µm/N** for one machine/fixture, approximately **0.8 µm/N** for the Deben pin-mounted configuration, and **<0.001 µm/N** when the Deben system was checked using a very stiff specimen. | Load a reference specimen much stiffer than the intended specimen and fit the force-dependent extra displacement. Compare stage displacement with direct image tracking. | Determine configuration-specific total compliance, `C_total`, and calculate specimen extension as `ΔL = ΔX − C_total·P`. Recalibrate after changing grips, load cell, fixtures, or specimen geometry. | Meyer 2023; Koko et al. 2024 (pqac-00000020, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000048) |
| Specimen misalignment | A **2°** angular error changed the observed failure from predominantly tensile to shear, although UTS changed only marginally from **46.8 to 46.6 MPa**. Thus, agreement in UTS alone can conceal a seriously non-uniaxial test. | Compare fracture mode and full-field strain/stress distribution for aligned and deliberately misaligned specimens. Use opposing strain gauges or DIC fields to quantify bending. | Use precision-machined, self-aligning fixtures; control grip parallelism and specimen centering; verify alignment under preload and qualify the train using ASTM E1012 procedures. | Koko et al. 2024 (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000050) |
| Dimensional uncertainty | Diameter variation as large as **70 µm** produced an estimated **14% uncertainty in calculated stress**. The problem becomes more severe as specimen cross-sectional area decreases. | Measure cross-section at multiple positions and propagate area uncertainty through `σ = F/A`; inspect AM specimens for taper, porosity, and non-circularity. | Measure every specimen individually with calibrated optical or dimensional metrology; use the local gauge-section area rather than nominal CAD dimensions; retain dimensional uncertainty in reported results. | Koko et al. 2024 (pqac-00000018) |
| Load-cell zero offset or “phantom force” | Apparent preload/phantom force was approximately **2.1 ± 1.3 N**—material for low-capacity cells and micro-specimens. | Record unloaded and lightly preloaded baselines; repeat zero checks before and after tests and monitor warm-up drift. | Warm up electronics, tare immediately before each test, use a consistent seating/preload protocol, calibrate with traceable masses or standards, and include zero drift in the uncertainty budget. | Koko et al. 2024 (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000050) |
| Stage-displacement inconsistency | In a 5 kN single-leadscrew rig, commanded/recorded displacement versus image-tracked displacement gave **R² = 0.79**, characterized as more than **20% inconsistency**. | Track a fixed feature using calibrated SEM or optical images while commanding known unloaded movements; compare actual and reported displacement over the usable travel. | Add an independent encoder, LVDT, optical scale, or image-based displacement channel; calibrate across travel, speed, direction, and load rather than relying on lead-screw pitch or step count alone. | Koko et al. 2024, NewTec appendix (pqac-00000053) |
| Dowel/pin grip seating and compliance | Pin-mounted specimens exhibited an initial **lead-in/bedding-in displacement** that masked the elastic response; independently tracked specimen elongation remained small while rig displacement increased. Clamped Instron grips showed a much faster initial force rise. | Compare machine displacement with direct optical gauge displacement and compare pin-mounted versus fully clamped fixtures. Examine loading–unloading cycles for seating, slip, and hysteresis. | Prefer clamped or positively located grips where feasible; preload and reseat consistently; exclude seating data; quantify fixture-specific compliance; monitor gauge marks for slip. | Koko et al. 2024 (pqac-00000018, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000051) |
| Limited validity of linear compliance correction | The demonstrated correction agreed with extensometry only in the linear region—approximately to **0.5% strain for AlMg3** and **1% for PA6**. A constant-compliance elastic correction is not valid through nonlinear deformation. | Compare corrected crosshead strain with extensometer strain over the complete stress–strain curve and identify where their slopes diverge. | Use `ΔL = ΔX − C_total·P` only where load-train response is linear; use direct extensometry/DIC or a validated nonlinear load-train model beyond the elastic regime. | Meyer et al. 2023 (pqac-00000021, pqac-00000024) |
