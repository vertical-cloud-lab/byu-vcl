# Edison queries (job-futurehouse-paperqa3-high), submitted 2026-10-07

## sieve_plan

```
I am setting up a hand-sieving step for metal powder made on an AMAZEMET rePowder ultrasonic atomizer (induction melting module, Al 4047 and later other Al alloys such as 6063 / AlSi10Mg), in a university lab, for use as laser powder bed fusion (LPBF / SLM) feedstock. Each atomization run yields only tens of grams (typically 10-100 g). The powder container is poured onto paper after the run and currently contains chunks (unatomized splats, beads several mm across) mixed with spherical powder. Please critically check the following sieve plan against the literature, ESPECIALLY the experimental/methods sections of papers that sieve or classify atomized powder for LPBF, and flag anything wrong, unusual, unsafe or missing. Be specific and cite.

THE PLAN: a stack of 3-inch (76 mm) diameter, full-height, all-stainless-steel ASTM E11 test sieves: No. 60 (250 um) on top to scalp out chunks, No. 230 (63 um) and No. 635 (20 um) below, over a pan, with a cover; the 20-63 um fraction retained on the 20 um sieve is to be the LPBF print fraction. Optional No. 325 (45 um) for a 20-45 um cut. Sieving by hand with tapping, cover on, dust mask, with the intent to buy a small sieve shaker later.

Q1. What particle size ranges do LPBF papers actually use for Al alloys (AlSi10Mg, Al-Si, 4047, 6xxx), and what sieve cut points do the EXPERIMENTAL SECTIONS report when they sieve atomized or recycled powder for LPBF (e.g. "sieved through 63 um", "classified to 20-63 um", "15-45 um", "<53 um")? Is a LOWER cut at 20 um by dry sieving standard practice, or do labs and suppliers remove fines by air classification (and often leave them in) instead? Is 63 um the usual upper cut for Al LPBF powders, and is the 45 um alternative common?
Q2. Papers that use ultrasonic atomization (AMAZEMET rePowder or similar) for Al alloys: what particle size distribution (D10/D50/D90) do they report, what sieve sizes do they use after atomization, and what yield falls within 20-63 um? Does the atomized powder typically need the fine (<20 um) fraction removed at all?
Q3. Practicality of a 20 um (No. 635) woven-wire test sieve: how prone is it to blinding with Al powder, how much mass can be put on a 76 mm (3 inch) sieve per charge without overloading (ASTM E11 / ISO 3310-1 / ASTM C136 / ISO 2591 / ASTM B214 guidance on sieve loading and residue limits), and is hand tapping sufficient or is a mechanical shaker / ultrasonic deblinding / air-jet sieving needed at 20-45 um? What does ASTM B214 (sieve analysis of metal powders) say about charge mass and sieve size? Note that ASTM B214 is a sieve-analysis method, not a production-sieving method; say what guidance applies to production sieving of small batches.
Q4. 3-inch vs 8-inch (203 mm) sieves for batches of tens of grams: is 3 inch the right choice; what does the literature or sieve-maker guidance say about small-diameter sieves and sample mass?
Q5. Safety: aluminium powder is a combustible dust (NFPA 484, NFPA 652 / 660). Do sieves and sieving stations used for Al/Ti LPBF powders need to be BONDED AND GROUNDED, and does the literature or any standard (NFPA 484, ISO/ASTM 52907, machine-maker guidance from EOS, SLM Solutions, Renishaw, Russell Finex, Farleygreene, etc.) say that sieving reactive metal powders should be done under inert gas or in a glovebox? Is an all-stainless sieve (vs brass frame / stainless cloth) the right choice for Al powder contamination (Cu/Zn pickup) and for ESD?
Q6. Are standard woven-wire ASTM E11 test sieves actually what LPBF labs use for small-batch production sieving of powder, or do they use vibratory sieving stations (e.g. Russell Finex AMPro, Farleygreene Sievgen, EOS/SLM/Renishaw sieve stations) with ultrasonic deblinding? What do research labs with small atomizers (ultrasonic or lab-scale gas atomizers producing 10-500 g) do?
Q7. Does sieving (shear and impact) damage or oxidize Al powder, pick up Fe/Cr from stainless mesh, or change flowability? Any evidence on sieving-induced contamination or satellite breakage?
Q8. Anything else a powder-metallurgy / AM process engineer would object to in the plan above, including record-keeping (weighing every fraction, labelling), moisture/humidity, and cleaning sieves between alloys.
```

## automation

```
Context: a university self-driving-lab group runs a small AMAZEMET rePowder ultrasonic atomizer producing tens of grams of Al alloy powder (Al 4047, 6063, AlSi10Mg) per run, and already has an automated gravimetric POWDER DOSER (a 3D-printed auger dispenser with a tube mounted horizontally that tilts for bulk pouring, a solenoid tapper that strikes the housing to free bridged powder, and a vibration motor; it meters powder into a bored Al cup on a balance; controlled from a Raspberry Pi / Pico). The atomized powder must be sieved (scalp out chunks > 250 um and cut to 20-63 um for LPBF) before it can be dosed, because larger particles clogged the doser's auger nozzle. We plan to start with a hand-sieved 3-inch ASTM E11 stack (No. 60 / No. 230 / No. 635 over a pan) and want to understand how sieving could be automated and whether it could be folded into the doser itself.

Please answer from the literature (methods sections of powder-handling, LPBF powder recycling, sieving and self-driving-lab papers, plus sieve-maker/standards guidance), citing specifically:

Q1. Sieving fundamentals for fine (20-63 um) cohesive metal powders: what limits throughput and cut sharpness on a vibrating or tapping screen? Give quantitative guidance on BED DEPTH / LOADING: how thick can the powder layer on the screen be before particles stop reaching the apertures (e.g. ISO 2591-1, ASTM C136 residue limits, "sieve overloading" rules such as max residue per sieve area, Whitby/Standish sieving-kinetics models, the "number of layers" rule). Is the worry that "a thick layer won't pass even with tapping or vibration" justified, and what is the usual remedy (feed slowly, continuous feed, larger screen, repeated small charges)?
Q2. Mechanisms for fine mesh: tapping vs. vibration (frequency/amplitude) vs. ultrasonic deblinding (e.g. 36 kHz on the mesh) vs. air-jet sieving for 20-45 um cuts. Q3. Safety constraints that an automated sieving step for Al powder must respect (combustible dust: NFPA 484 / NFPA 660 bonding and grounding, ignition sources from motors/solenoids, inert atmosphere, dust extraction, humidity), and whether an open-bench vibratory sieve with a 3D-printed (PLA/PETG, possibly ESD-safe filament) enclosure would be acceptable.
Q7. Based on all of the above, what is the most practical path for this lab: (a) hand sieving, (b) a benchtop sieve shaker (e.g. Gilson SS-3 / Retsch AS200 / Endecotts) with the same 3-inch stack, (c) an ultrasonic sieve deblinding kit on a single 63 um screen fed by the auger doser, (d) a commercial AM sieving station? Give the trade-offs with numbers where the literature supports them.
```
