No. # Automated corrosion testing of copper alloys: evidence through October 2026

**Bottom line.** I found closely related demonstrations, but **not a verified publication that combines robotic or gantry-based electrochemical corrosion screening of an additively manufactured or weld-repaired Cu–Ni, nickel-aluminum bronze (NAB), aluminum bronze, or brass specimen with element-resolved analysis of its test electrolyte**. The strongest overlaps are an automated scanning-flow-cell/ICP-MS experiment on *sputtered Pt–Cu*, an automated scanning-droplet experiment on *elemental Cu*, conventional corrosion tests on *wire-arc-built NAB*, and ICP-AES/AESEC on *arc-melted Cu–Al–Sn*. “Robotic” fabrication should not be mistaken for robotic corrosion testing. No publication by Sterling G. Baird is included. (schuppert2014potentialresolveddissolutionof pages 9-12, schuppert2014potentialresolveddissolutionof pages 6-9, decost2022towardsautomateddesign pages 10-13, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 14-14, sur2025revisitingtheinfluencea pages 8-10, peng2025investigationofprocess pages 2-4)

**Closest demonstrations, ranked by the part of the proposed combination they establish.** Counts below distinguish tested compositions or locations from independent physical coupons; *not reported* means the available methods did not establish a count.

1. **Automated scanning corrosion *and* online elemental analysis—but not a structural copper alloy:** Schuppert *et al.*, “Potential-resolved dissolution of Pt–Cu,” *Electrochimica Acta* (2014), **DOI: 10.1016/j.electacta.2014.07.113**. A magnetron-sputtered, annealed Pt–Cu composition-gradient film was contacted automatically at different locations by a scanning flow cell; its effluent went to **online ICP-MS for both Cu and Pt**. The corrosion/dissolution electrolyte was **0.1 M HClO₄**. Composition was characterized at **19 locations**; these are positions on a film, **not 19 bulk coupons**, and the number of electrochemically contacted positions is not independently established here. This is a particularly close precedent for coupling location-addressed electrochemistry to element-resolved dissolution, but its material was Pt–Cu electrocatalyst film, **not Cu–Ni, bronze, brass, AM metal, or a weld repair**. No ML-guided or closed-loop choice of locations is documented. (schuppert2014potentialresolveddissolutionof pages 9-12, schuppert2014potentialresolveddissolutionof pages 6-9)

2. **Autonomous droplet-cell corrosion of copper—but *pure Cu*, not an alloy:** DeCost *et al.*, “Towards Automated Design of Corrosion Resistant Alloy Coatings with an Autonomous Scanning Droplet Cell,” *JOM* (2022), **DOI: 10.1007/s11837-022-05367-0**. The platform electrodeposited **Cu metal** from **0.25 M CuSO₄ onto Au thin film** and evaluated its mean dissolution current during a two-minute potential hold in **1 M H₂SO₄**. Gaussian-process acquisition was used for a **Cu deposition/reflectance/dissolution** study; the illustrated model is shown after **ten active-learning iterations**, not ten established independent corrosion coupons. A separate **Ni–Co** electrodeposition demonstration used offline EDS for composition—not Cu–Ni alloy corrosion or electrolyte ICP. Its Al–Ni–Ti multiobjective corrosion-selection example was a **simulation using previously measured data**, not a newly executed closed-loop alloy-corrosion campaign. Neither Cu alloy AM/weld repair nor AESEC was tested. (decost2022towardsautomateddesign pages 10-13, decost2022towardsautomateddesign pages 4-7, decost2022towardsautomateddesign pages 7-10)

3. **Robotic liquid handling, O-ring flat cells, and genuinely closed-loop corrosion testing—but stainless steel:** Persaud *et al.*, “Materials Acceleration Platform for Electrochemistry,” *npj Materials Degradation* (2026), **DOI: 10.1038/s41529-026-00822-8**. MAP-E has **eight parallel, independently controlled flat cells**, each with a **1 cm² O-ring-sealed** working-sample opening, plus robotic sample/reference transfer and automated electrolyte mixing. Its ASTM-G61-analog reproducibility test made **32 measurements on 32 type-304 stainless-steel samples**, using **aerated 3.56 wt.% NaCl**. A subsequent type-304 study varied **pH and chloride in Britton–Robinson buffer**, with a **Gaussian-process uncertainty criterion selecting subsequent experiments in an operating feedback loop**; a separate, verified count of environmental points was not established. This is a strong precedent for the *specified architecture and ML*, but **no copper alloy, AM/weld-repaired copper, or effluent ICP/AESEC** was demonstrated. (persaud2026materialsaccelerationplatform pages 1-4, persaud2026materialsaccelerationplatform pages 9-12, persaud2026materialsaccelerationplatform pages 12-17, persaud2026materialsaccelerationplatform pages 4-6)

4. **Automated, solution-mixing scanning-droplet corrosion—but Zn–Ni, not Cu–Ni:** Joress *et al.*, “Development of an Automated Millifluidic Platform and Data-Analysis Pipeline for Rapid Electrochemical Corrosion Measurements: A pH Study on Zn–Ni” (2022 preprint), **DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.2204.06094**. Motorized positioning, a **~4.5-mm O-ring-bounded flow cell**, syringe-pump electrolyte mixing, and automated electrochemical analysis tested an **electroplated Zn₈₅Ni₁₅ coating on steel**. Acidic mixtures combined **0.5 M H₂SO₄/0.5 M K₂SO₄**; basic mixtures combined **1 M KOH/0.5 M K₂SO₄**. Each electrochemical set used a fresh plate location; **nine exposed XPS locations plus one untreated comparison** are identifiable, but that is **not a verified total count of electrochemical tests**. The demonstrated pH campaign used automated analysis, **not documented ML-selected experiments or ICP**; AM specimens and closed-loop synthesis were discussed as future extensions. A later journal publication is indicated in citations, but its final DOI was not verified here, so the verifiable preprint DOI is given. (joress2022developmentofan pages 1-4, joress2022developmentofan pages 4-7, joress2022developmentofan pages 14-18, joress2022developmentofan pages 11-14)

5. **Element-resolved dissolution of a copper-base alloy—but neither robotic nor AM:** Sur *et al.*, “Revisiting the Influence of Sn in Cu–Al Alloys,” *Journal of The Electrochemical Society* (2025), **DOI: 10.1149/1945-7111/ae0eb6**. **Nine listed Cu–Al and Cu–Al–Sn compositions** were vacuum-arc-melted and heat-treated, then evaluated with conventional electrochemistry in **deaerated 0.1 M Na₂SO₄ at pH 4**. Crucially, **two compositions—Cu–23Al and Cu–23Al–3Sn—also underwent flow-cell AESEC**, with online **ICP-AES measuring Cu, Al, and Sn** during polarization and potential holds. Nine is a **composition count**, not a verified coupon count; the AESEC subset is **two compositions**. It establishes copper-alloy electrolyte-speciation analysis, **not automated multi-specimen handling, AM, or ML-directed selection**. (sur2025revisitingtheinfluencea pages 5-8, sur2025revisitingtheinfluencea pages 8-10, sur2025revisitingtheinfluencea pages 22-24)

6. **Actual AM marine-bronze corrosion—but conventional testing:** Shahriari *et al.*, “Corrosion performance of wire arc additively manufactured NAB alloy,” *npj Materials Degradation* (2023), **DOI: 10.1038/s41529-023-00405-x**. **ER CuNiAl wire, GMAW/WAAM**, was compared as-built and in **three heat-treated conditions** against cast NAB: **five named material conditions**, *not* a proven total of five independent corrosion coupons. In **aerated 3.5 wt.% NaCl**, a conventional **single-specimen three-electrode cell** provided OCP, EIS, and cyclic polarization. No robotic corrosion-cell addressing, electrolyte ICP, or ML/closed-loop selection is reported. (shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 14-14, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 12-14)

7. **Further WAAM NAB corrosion and an instructive robotic-fabrication distinction:** Zhang *et al.*, “Effect of low-level Ti microalloying … WAAM-processed nickel-aluminium bronze,” *Advanced Materials Joining* (October 2026), **DOI: 10.1007/s44500-026-00016-x**, used immersion and **conventional three-electrode electrochemistry** in **3.5 wt.% NaCl**; it reports a **three-measurement average for mass loss**, but not a defensible total electrochemical-coupon count or automated testing/ICP/ML. Peng *et al.*, “Cu–Mn–Al alloy cladding … via cold metal transfer,” *Crystals* (2025), **DOI: 10.3390/cryst15100858**, used an **ABB robot to *deposit*** bronze wire cladding onto steel at **four welding currents**. Its corrosion measurements were instead performed in a **conventional three-electrode cell with a 0.5 cm² O-ring-exposed surface**; the corrosion-coupon replicate count is not established. Thus neither a robotic welder nor an O-ring on a *single conventional cell* proves robotic or array-based **corrosion testing**. No ICP or corrosion-directed ML was reported for either study. (zhang2026effectoflowlevel pages 2-4, peng2025investigationofprocess pages 4-7, peng2025investigationofprocess pages 2-4)

8. **High-throughput corrosion methods that establish the alloy mismatch:** Sur *et al.*, *Journal of The Electrochemical Society* (2023), **DOI: 10.1149/1945-7111/aceeb8**, automatically stepped a flowing scanning-droplet cell across **nine selected sputtered Al–Co–Cr–Fe–Ni film compositions** in **0.1 M H₂SO₄**, with bulk controls. A parent wafer yielding 177 diced pieces does **not** mean 177 corrosion measurements. There was automated metric extraction, **not demonstrated ML-driven selection or ICP**, and the films were not copper alloys. Joress *et al.*, *ACS Combinatorial Science* (2020), **DOI: 10.1021/acscombsci.9b00215**, recorded **109 droplet-cell electrochemical locations** on a sputtered **Ni–Ti–Al** composition spread in approximately **1.1 M NaCl**. Its ML predictions preceded experimental validation; they were **not a documented iterative corrosion-selection loop**. Again, these were film locations, not 109 discrete fabricated copper-alloy coupons. (sur2023ahighthroughput pages 2-3, joress2020ahighthroughputstructural pages 5-9, joress2020ahighthroughputstructural pages 1-5)

**Scope qualification.** Element-resolved analysis of a *flowing test electrolyte* is demonstrated for both Pt–Cu/ICP-MS and Cu–Al–Sn/AESEC, but those are different experimental systems; they cannot be combined into a claim that someone performed ICP/AESEC on a robotic AM-bronze screening platform. The retrieved record also identifies brass dezincification investigated by flow-cell AESEC (Zhou *et al.*, *Electrochimica Acta*, 2017, **DOI: 10.1016/j.electacta.2017.01.078**), but its full methods were unavailable here; electrolyte, specimen count and automation therefore cannot be responsibly specified from this search. This is a **literature-search conclusion, not proof that no such experiment exists**. (sur2025revisitingtheinfluencea pages 8-10, schuppert2014potentialresolveddissolutionof pages 9-12, schuppert2014potentialresolveddissolutionof pages 6-9, shahriari2023corrosionperformanceof pages 14-14)
