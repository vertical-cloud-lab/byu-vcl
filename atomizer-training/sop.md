# rePowder ultrasonic atomizer: standard operating procedure

BYU Vertical Cloud Lab, AMAZEMET rePowder (induction module + ultrasonic chamber in one unit). Draft 1, 2026-10-03, synthesized from the training-video indexes. Every step links to the moment it comes from; the link opens the video paused at that second.

## Scope and sources

The rePowder melts a charge in a boron-nitride-coated graphite crucible heated indirectly by an induction coil ([T4 02:39](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=159)), with a graphite sealing rod stoppering a graphite nozzle (0.5–0.7 mm bore, [T1 39:54](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2394)) in the crucible floor. Argon overpressure in the furnace pushes the melt stream out of the nozzle onto a consumable plate held by a sonotrode stack (titanium connector rod, tungsten-alloy top sonotrode per the Whisper transcript of [T5 11:19](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=679)) vibrating at 40 kHz ([T1 22:32](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1352)); droplets fly off the plate inside the argon-filled chamber, solidify, and fall through a cone into the powder container ([T4 08:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=488)). This SOP is built from the per-video indexes in [`notes/`](notes/) of the training recordings made with Bartosz Kalicki (AMAZEMET) on Tue–Wed Sep 29–30 2026 (trainees Gage Erickson, Ronnie Guymon, Sterling Baird), the team's first unsupervised run on Fri Oct 2 2026, the [2026-09-17 pre-install call](../docs/meetings/2026-09-17-repowder-install/transcript.md), and [issue #249](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/issues/249) on powder bagging. The indexes were drafted from YouTube auto-captions and then re-checked line by line against Whisper large-v3-turbo transcripts, which now exist for all 26 videos ([`transcripts/whisper/`](transcripts/whisper/)). Where the two disagree the notes say which reading is kept, and the lines still in dispute after that were re-decoded word by word from the audio ([`recheck-clips.json`](transcripts/whisper/recheck-clips.json)). The videos remain the authority: when this document and the footage disagree, the footage wins, and anything marked "(inferred)" was not said on camera. Six videos have no YouTube captions. Three are indexed from Whisper alone: [cartridge cleaning](https://www.youtube.com/embed/f8KL31PN8bA?start=0), [#70 drill bit on a graphite nozzle](https://www.youtube.com/embed/LSQmxwmlTkQ?start=0) and the narrated [Al 4047 dosing session](https://www.youtube.com/embed/dXRB7c6GeDw?start=0). The other three have no usable speech at all (Whisper, even without its voice filter, returns only hallucinated filler) and are indexed from frames of the footage: the [expert cleaning POV](https://www.youtube.com/embed/u-KjR5TENN4?start=0) and the two silent dosing recordings ([AlSi10Mg for nzyjn0](https://www.youtube.com/embed/prj_xgeuQtM?start=0), [Al 4047](https://www.youtube.com/embed/QXSj0j1OqL8?start=0)). Of the six, only the dosing sessions add anything here (the charge preparation under Known runs); the cleaning POV shows, without words, the cleaning that Video 4 and Video 2 narrate.

## Safety and PPE

- Gloves for everything: a lab rule for oils and powder, and the trainer's own practice together with the ESD coat ([pre-install call](../docs/meetings/2026-09-17-repowder-install/transcript.md)). Hands go inside the chamber to clean, so wear the lab coat too [T2 41:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=2472).
- ESD (anti-static) coat with wrist strap for powder handling; a discharge needs visible powder suspended in air, so this is precaution, not a routine hazard (pre-install call).
- Full-face respirators for any powder handling and for post-run cleaning [T5 50:11](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3011) [T4 07:24](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=444); mask on before the chamber opens with powder inside [T2 38:41](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=2321). Each mask is labelled per person; replace the visor sticker, not the mask [T5 73:48](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=4428).
- Hearing protection whenever ultrasonics or induction run; the ultrasonic noise harms even if it does not bother you [T5 42:31](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=2551) [SP 13:02](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=782).
- Eye protection or a filter when looking at the melt: at 1300 °C it is too bright to watch unfiltered [T5 54:24](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3264).
- The ultrasonic cable carries ~1000 V. Keep it secured and in good condition; nobody stands on it [T7 19:45](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1185).
- Never drop, heat, wet or overheat the transducer; always air-cool it; a repair costs ~2,000 [T5 07:00](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=420) [SP 08:28](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=508). Hold it while freeing the stack [T7 20:04](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1204); carry by the housing or booster ring, never by the vibrating parts [T7 41:03](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2463).
- Graphite oxidizes fast above 400–500 °C in air: open the chamber only below 400 °C [T2 33:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1996) [OCT2b 28:52](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=1732).
- A used HEPA filter goes in a metal tray or basket with sand nearby: fine dust (magnesium, overheated evaporate) can self-ignite [T1 08:51](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=531).
- Any displayed error stops heating instantly [T1 17:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1069); the machine does not block a run on high O₂, you watch the value and set the alarms yourself [T5 33:00](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1980).
- The chamber door locks whenever pressure is off atmospheric; it cannot be opened under pressure or vacuum [T5 44:19](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=2659) [T1 30:22](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1822). Always vent before opening [T5 77:17](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=4637).
- Fit a room oxygen sensor: the trainer judged the argon flow too small to be a danger but agreed one should be fitted [T4 09:30](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=570). Tacky mats at the enclosure exit keep powder off shoes (pre-install call).
- Prolonged vibration with solidified metal on the plate can break the plate [T2 32:09](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1929). Hot metals such as copper splash when atomization goes wrong; the splash plates exist for that [T5 02:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=141).

## Before a run

On the day the order is: utilities, the furnace, the chamber, and the stack into the door last, then the door closes. The graphite nut is reached through the open chamber door before the stack is in it ([T1 46:33](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2793), no stack in the door), and the splash disc and container go on before the stack ([T5 02:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=141)–[05:41](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=341), stack into the door at [T5 24:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1489)). The stack can be assembled, torqued and scanned on the bench at any time before that. The sections below follow the parts, not that order.

Budget about 1 h per aluminium run including prep and cleaning [T5 21:50](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1310); the pour itself lasts 2–5 min [T9 25:42](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1542). Prepare the next charge while the furnace cools [T5 70:10](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=4210); a full cooldown needs ~30 min before shutdown [T3 49:52](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2992).

### Ultrasonic stack

Choose the booster for the powder you want. The one physical 1:1.5 booster is flipped to give both ratios; the KF50 flange is always the top, so the end you want faces the chamber [T7 22:11](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1331) [T7 33:17](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1997).

| Booster | Effect | Use | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1:1.5 (amplifying, 150 %) | Largest particles, fastest, easiest; "immediately destroys" metal plates, CF plate only | Training, coarse Al | [T5 08:43](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=523) [T5 09:48](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=588) |
| 1:1 (slimmer) | Middle amplitude | Copper, magnesium, metal plates | [T5 09:24](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=564) [T3 16:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=972) |
| 1.5:1 (reverse) | Lowest amplitude, finest powder, plates last longest; needs good wetting and a slow controlled pour, and the material's parameters known | Fine Al; frequency reads under 40 kHz, acceptable for short runs | [T7 31:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1876) [T5 08:56](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=536) [T7 41:40](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2500) |

1. Remove the plate first, then unscrew the stack; order does not matter. The housing comes off the booster with four hex screws [T7 00:19](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=19) [T7 00:41](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=41). Disconnect air and cable before the clamp; loosen the small locking nut if the connector will not come out [T7 18:55](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1135) [T7 19:20](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1160).
2. A seized stud: jam two M10 nuts together on it and unscrew with the nuts; it is the only thing that works [T7 30:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1816). Threads are M10 everywhere except M10 fine at the transducer; the connector stud is M8 at the plate end [T7 29:31](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1771) [T5 10:28](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=628).
3. Clean every thread with IPA; cavitation dust on a thread shows up as squeaking or a bad scan, so when in doubt strip and clean all of them [T5 10:59](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=659) [T5 22:56](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1376).
4. Fit the smaller housing before reassembly (it will not pass over the transducer later); tighten its screws only enough to seat the rubber seals [T7 33:03](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1983) [T7 34:58](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2098). The connector stud goes in with the M8 thread and wrench flats facing up [T7 35:18](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2118).
5. Torque in a vise with soft jaws: transducer–booster 65, booster–sonotrode 60, plate connector 50 (55 allowed, 50 is safer). The unit is spoken once, "Newton meters", in the Whisper transcript of T5 13:30 [T5 13:30](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=810) [T7 35:50](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2150). Too tight damages the thread, loose leaves friction that blocks the ultrasound [T5 13:30](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=810). Set the wrench by pulling the collar down and rotating until the base scale plus vernier reads the value (60 + 5 = 65); tighten until it clicks; check the arrow and reverse the ratchet for the other direction; counter-hold with a second wrench [T5 16:59](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1019) [T7 38:33](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2313) [T7 40:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2421) [T7 37:56](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2276).
6. Bolt the protective cover over the transducer; two to three minutes that protect a part worth thousands [T7 43:32](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2612). Push the cable connector in and lock it (unlock before removal); connect air [T7 40:55](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2455) [T5 06:40](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=400).
7. Test on the bench: scan, then a short run, and confirm nothing snapped [T7 40:26](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2426). The scan sweeps 39–41 kHz and should show one wide peak; F-start/F-stop are set automatically at +200/−600 Hz because frequency falls as the parts heat [T1 21:07](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1267) [T1 22:32](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1352) [T1 22:53](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1373). Expect a little over 40 kHz [T2 18:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1088); a double peak or interruption usually clears after a short burst of vibration reseats the parts [T3 12:04](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=724). Power at idle is 0 W; expect ≤100–150 W in use, ~50 W on most plates; 300–500 W may trip the transducer [OCT2a 21:09](https://www.youtube.com/embed/qYyT39D5Yzo?start=1269) [T5 24:18](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1458).
8. Put the splash/protective plate into the chamber first (hard to do afterwards), check the O-ring is in, lock the door open, then insert the stack and fit both clamps without touching the safety cover [T7 43:09](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2589) [T7 44:56](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2696) [T5 24:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1489).
9. Mount the plate with the stack in the housing: thread the connector fully into the sonotrode before the plate so the plate is never forced through the 8 mm ring (it snapped at the edge once), then the plate, then the top sonotrode; check flat contact with no gaps; 50 N·m with two 17 mm wrenches, following the arrow [T7 46:29](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2789) [T7 47:37](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2857) [T7 48:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2892) [T5 25:43](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1543).
10. Rescan with the plate on (power reads higher), then wet-test: a few drops of water should atomize over the whole surface. A straight pattern is good; an angled pattern, or only half the plate working, means a crack [T5 26:39](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1599) [T5 27:32](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1652) [T3 12:42](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=762) [T7 49:30](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=2970).
11. Position the plate: the holder moves up/down/left/right; loosen to turn, and up/down needs simultaneous rotation. Melt should land as high as possible without going over the top, in the middle; final adjustment at first flow [T5 28:46](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1726) [T5 29:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1761) [T2 22:04](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1324).

### Furnace and charge

1. Strip in this order: thermocouple (its thin ceramic cover cracks normally; align the replacement with the outlet), sealing rod up (the SEALING ROD button lifts the lever and rod), safety pin out, lever swung up on its post at the right of the deck, rod out; top insulation; side insulation; bottom insulation; then unscrew the crucible while holding the graphite nut so nothing falls [T1 35:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2149) [T1 36:24](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2184) [T1 37:01](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2221). The stack under the crucible is graphite nut, graphite seal, nozzle holder, second graphite seal [T1 37:45](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2265). Put the thermocouple back during maintenance or the HMI raises "master temperature sensor" and bleeds argon [T1 38:06](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2286).
2. Nozzle: 0.5 mm is standard; Al-Si-Mg works at 0.5 but is more reliable at 0.7; drill to 0.7, 1 mm maximum; inconsistent flow or clogging means go bigger [T1 39:54](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2394) [T1 41:30](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2490) [T1 41:05](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2465). Look through it for light before every run [OCT2a 10:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/qYyT39D5Yzo?start=608); unclog with a needle or precise drill [T1 39:18](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2358); graphite drills easily by hand with a 0.6 bit or a Dremel [T2 25:52](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1552). Keep both sizes: a fresh 0.7 and a 0.5 Gage machined (said as "0.05" per Whisper, "005 mm" per the captions; 0.5 mm is inferred) [T8 00:57](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=57). When flow is too fast for the amplitude, go back to 0.5 [T9 27:39](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1659). Boron-nitride nozzles flow more smoothly with reactive melts such as Al–Ca [T9 34:56](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=2096).
3. Rebuild: nozzle into the holder with the white side on top [T1 45:54](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2754). The easier order is nozzle screwed into the crucible first, then the holder nut on as a pair [T8 01:28](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=88); thread engagement is tiny, "you just barely have to get it on" [T8 02:26](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=146). Tighten the crucible until it feels tight and no further [T1 46:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2768); overtightening cracks the holder thread [T9 33:45](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=2025). Then graphite seal, bottom insulation, and the crucible straight down into the coil; its thread comes through the furnace floor into the chamber, and the nut goes on it from below [T1 46:33](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2793) [T1 47:02](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2822). Reach in through the chamber's left door and thread the thin graphite nut (and the lower seal) onto it while the crucible is held still at the top; before the nut is fully tight, turn the crucible so its thermocouple hole ends up where the thermocouple can reach it, at the back right [T1 47:02](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2822). A second person holding the crucible makes this easier; working alone, keep one hand on the crucible and go slowly.
   - **Be careful with the graphite.** Crucible, holder and nut are brittle: overtightened, they crack. But they must be snug, or the joint will not seal. Tighten by feel until it is snug and stop there; this is a feel that Gage and Sterling got hands-on from Bartosz, so have someone show you the first time [T1 46:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2768) [T9 33:45](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=2025).
4. Coat the crucible with boron-nitride spray the night before and let it dry: a very thin layer lasts a few processes [T1 41:59](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2519) [T1 42:23](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2543) [SP 10:34](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=634). Peel slag off the bottom between alloys [SP 11:37](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=697).
5. Insulation goes in first, then the thermocouple: line the side-insulation hole up with the thermocouple port, which is at the back right of the furnace, and bend the flexible thermocouple so it sits close to the crucible [T4 00:31](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=31) [T8 04:23](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=263). The silica–alumina insulation is dusty and breaks if dropped; vacuum the furnace area afterwards [T4 01:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=68) [T8 04:09](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=249). The denser blue insulation is for silver, gold or high-purity work [T9 33:04](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1984).
6. Sealing rod: the tip must be clean, smooth and undamaged or it will not seal; shaft damage does not matter [T4 01:52](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=112) [T8 06:13](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=373) [SP 12:09](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=729). Graphite is brittle and breaks if dropped [T4 01:41](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=101); use tweezers, and do not drop the graphite parts [OCT2b 03:15](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=195). Alumina rods exist for reactive materials; a thick rod coating can block the nozzle [T1 43:23](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2603). With the lever still swung up, stand the rod in the crucible on the nozzle, swing the lever down onto the adapter's top, push the safety pin in, and press SEALING ROD to bring it down; then the charge goes in, beside the lever [T4 02:02](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=122) [T4 02:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=128) [T4 04:36](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=276). Lower the sealing rod before the charge goes in (the captions' "remove the sealing block" is "we move the sealing rod down" in the Whisper transcript, followed by "now we can add the material") [T4 02:18](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=138).
7. Charge:
   - Always clean the feedstock [T4 04:32](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=272).
   - Maximum diameter 20 mm (sealing rod to crucible wall), inner height ~10–12 cm; nothing that overfills once molten (pre-install call).
   - 250–300 g per round: a small sample is gone before you can tune, a full crucible can kill the plate [T9 29:05](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1745).
   - Rods may stand above the furnace and sink as they melt [T4 04:37](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=277); long rods need a higher initial setpoint because the top stays cool [T4 04:43](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=283); a compact charge melts faster [T4 05:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=308).
   - Loose powder does not melt on its own; it needs a molten carrier, and an arc-melted master alloy is better (pre-install call). Any consolidation helps; pure powder tends to get lost [SP 14:27](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=867).
   - Powder cups: Al 6063 rod cut 1/4 in over length, faced to 2.75 in, centre-drilled and bored with a 1/2 in drill, capped with a press-fit plug turned from the offcut [LATHE 00:01](https://www.youtube.com/embed/z6rwmQW_3Vg?start=1) [LATHE 01:02](https://www.youtube.com/embed/z6rwmQW_3Vg?start=62) [LATHE 01:14](https://www.youtube.com/embed/z6rwmQW_3Vg?start=74). The Oct 2 plug was ~0.3 in long and barely engaged [OCT2b 22:15](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=1335). The cups are filled on the lab's powder doser; sieve own-atomized powder first, since its larger particles clogged the doser's auger nozzle on Sep 30 ("or me just sitting [sifting] it … like I probably should have"; sieving is inferred) [DOSE2 23:14](https://www.youtube.com/embed/dXRB7c6GeDw?start=1394). The plugs are drilled through with a #60 bit so that air trapped under the plug can leave during pump-down instead of through the powder (Gage's 2026-09-27 note in [#222](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/issues/222) and the `atomizer-charge/` design in #232); the vent is not visible on camera.
8. Keep per-alloy consumable sets (an aluminium set, a copper set) [T8 07:30](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=450).
9. Close the furnace lid and secure the latch just tight enough to seal; if it hisses, loosen, adjust the latch and retighten [T4 05:17](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=317) [T8 09:09](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=549). Rub residue flakes off the lid faces first [T8 11:26](https://www.youtube.com/embed/HTlUrAr5HVU?start=686).
10. Check the temperature setpoint before heating: it keeps the last value entered, which once started melting the charge before the 250 °C wash [T2 03:23](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=203). Thermocouple is type N to 1300 °C [T1 34:11](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2051); the generator could reach 1600 °C but the plates would not survive, and Fe/Ni react with graphite [T4 03:35](https://www.youtube.com/embed/1F9_4ccwhss?start=215). Program setup holds max heating power (normally 100) and heat ramps for ceramics [T1 33:03](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1983).

### Chamber and container

1. Put the splash-protection plate in first: a round disc that sits in the container's top flange; one is enough for aluminium [T5 02:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=141) [T5 02:47](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=167). Then mount the container: lift it into place under the outlet and clamp it, finger-tightening the flange [T5 03:44](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=224) [T5 04:48](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=288). A second person makes this easier and safer, one holding the weight while the other closes the clamp. Working alone, keep the container fully supported until the clamp is closed, and take extra care: it is heavier than it looks. Two types exist, the valved one that shows its contents and a plain stainless tube [T5 00:00](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=0).
2. (The splash disc is already in, step 1.)
3. Put the jewelry gold-melting bowl in the chamber; it catches un-atomized melt for reuse and protects the chamber if a plate breaks [T5 05:01](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=301) [SP 09:17](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=557).
4. Wipe the plate; hang the covers over the chamber openings and door so powder can be swept from the edge [T5 05:41](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=341).
5. Blow out paper-towel dust with compressed air only while there is no powder in the chamber [T5 00:54](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=54).
6. Gas-wash mechanics:

- The furnace wash fills to ~500 mbar and pumps out without reaching the leak-prone vacuum limit [T1 26:48](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1608); the furnace runs its 5 cycles on a counter with no sensor [T5 33:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=2029) [T9 04:58](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=298).
- The pump always pulls on the furnace; the chamber needs the big valve opened by hand [T1 05:41](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=341) [T5 38:28](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=2308).
- The other button runs one cycle instead of five [OCT2b 10:35](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=635); automatic chamber prep can repeat by O₂ level [T1 27:51](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1671).
- Raise temperature only after an argon backfill [RUN1 04:03](https://www.youtube.com/embed/TFpU4uqVF9c?start=243).
- For bismuth one purge was enough; the double heated wash pays off for Al and Mg [T5 31:17](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1877).
- The furnace controller is a separate locked product, so the 250/500 sequence cannot be automated from the HMI [T3 32:58](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=1978) [OCT2b 13:18](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=798).

### Pressure logic

- Chamber: pressure control holds 150 mbar overpressure and vents or adds automatically during the pour [T5 41:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=2472) [T1 27:20](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1640). Keep a small bleed through the O₂ sensor; feeding argon next to it reads falsely low [T5 32:05](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1925) [T1 07:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=436).
- Furnace, melting pressure: slightly below chamber pressure, so nothing flows and the furnace holds overpressure between cycles [T1 31:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1881) [RUN1 00:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/TFpU4uqVF9c?start=16).
- Furnace, draining pressure (HMI label heard as "graining"): above chamber pressure to push the melt out; only the differential matters, and too little gives splatter instead of a stream [T1 31:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1881) [T9 25:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1508). Begin and end values let the pressure rise as the crucible empties [T1 33:36](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2016). Too much pressure makes the melt shoot out and bypass the plate [T3 42:14](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2534) [OCT2b 27:26](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=1646).
- Turbo: 1.5 bar in the furnace while held, drops on release; heats the plate at the start, stabilises a wandering stream, clears small debris, and pushes the last drops [T1 31:54](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1914) [T2 19:44](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1184) [T2 22:58](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1378).
- Little material: run pressure control manually and keep draining pressure low [RUN1 19:27](https://www.youtube.com/embed/TFpU4uqVF9c?start=1167).
- Vent valve relieves overpressure; a mechanical safety valve opens if the chamber exceeds 0.8 bar even if the vent fails (the captions heard "8 bar"; the Whisper transcript says 0.8, and 8 bar is the compressed-air supply) [T1 06:39](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=399).

### Pour

Best results need an operator at the window for the whole 2–3 min, adjusting amplitude, turbo and plate position [T5 58:53](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3533) [T9 45:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=2712).

1. Transducer cooling on; vibration cannot start without it [T9 19:34](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1174) [T2 32:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1969) [T3 40:55](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2455).
2. Rescan if the last scan is old; it must read a bit over 40 kHz with one peak; if not, re-tighten the stack, a loose joint shifts the frequency [T2 18:08](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1088) [T2 18:53](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1133).
3. Amplitude ~90: the slider is a percentage of generator current, 100 is high, 80–90 is best, lower gives finer powder but risks no atomization; start high and reduce once stable [T3 40:00](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2400) [T3 38:55](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2335) [T7 23:36](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1416).
4. Vibration on [T5 59:51](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3591).
5. Draining pressure on [T3 41:48](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2508).
6. Sealing rod up [T5 59:51](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3591).
7. Turbo as needed, all of this "as quickly as possible" [T3 41:48](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2508) [T2 19:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1152).

While pouring:

- The first droplet is usually lost to heating the plate; once the plate is hot every drop atomizes, and metal plates heat faster [T9 20:24](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1224) [T5 61:07](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3667).
- A thin stream gathers on the plate instead of atomizing because the plate never heats; counter-intuitively, pour more [T2 20:29](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1229) [T2 23:29](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1409).
- Wetting is what lets vibration into the melt; a dry droplet bounces [T2 24:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1456).
- Pour straight onto the plate from the start, aiming high so the melt has a long path [T2 23:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1429) [T5 63:58](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3838); steer with plate position when the stream lands too far [T5 60:27](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3627).
- Short turbo pulses stabilise the stream and prevent clogging; stop if too much gathers [T2 21:10](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1270).
- If melt shoots out too fast, lower the draining pressure [T3 44:46](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2686).
- Oxide at the nozzle bends the stream; compensate with the plate [T5 62:24](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3744).
- Raise the amplitude by the slider only if it is not atomizing; otherwise aim low [T9 45:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=2712).
- Fine-powder recipe: very small nozzle, very low amplitude (reverse booster), slow controlled pour; the best such run converted ~90 % to powder [T9 24:39](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1479) [T9 22:59](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1379). A flow too fast for the amplitude atomizes at the top and drips off the bottom [T9 21:07](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1267).
- High amplitude is faster and coarser; low-density metals give larger particles whatever you do [T9 26:45](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1605) [SP 06:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/2wMgeI-E7zw?start=409).

### End of pour

When the crucible is empty, within seconds, in this order (the trainer said order is irrelevant, speed is not) [T2 31:27](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1887):

1. Turbo to push the last drops and clear the nozzle [T5 62:12](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3732) [T2 22:29](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1349).
2. Sealing rod down.
3. Melting pressure.
4. Generator stop (stops heating).
5. Ultrasonics stop; solidified metal on a vibrating plate cracks the plate [T2 32:09](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1929). T9 stops vibration first; either way, finish in seconds [T9 21:39](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1299).

Turbo during cooldown will not keep the nozzle clear [T2 30:37](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1837). Set the temperature to 250 °C for next time [T2 24:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1489). Transducer cooling: the notes disagree on whether this unit stops it by itself 1 min after vibration or needs it switched off by hand, so switch it off yourself once the plate has cooled [T1 21:53](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1313) [T3 41:14](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2474).

## After a run

Cooldown and opening:

- Generator stop means no heating, so lower the setpoint to 250 °C only as a habit for the next run [T2 24:49](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1489) [OCT2b 27:59](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=1679).
