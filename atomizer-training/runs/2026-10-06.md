## How the times were found

1. **The stream's clock.** The overlay (`YYYY-MM-DD_HH-MM-SS`, lab time) was cropped every 30 s and read with
   tesseract ([`stream_clock.csv`](2026-10-06/stream_clock.csv)). Every legible reading fits *time = start + offset*:
   460 of the 461 legible readings for the afternoon broadcast give 13:01:11 (50 crops were not legible), and 957 of 958
   for the morning give 05:01:09–05:01:10. The odd one out each time is the broadcast's first frame, 7 s late. So, unlike the stalled archive in
   `CLAUDE.md`, neither broadcast has a step, and an offset converts straight to wall time.
2. **The phone videos.** YouTube keeps no recording time for them (`fileDetails` has no creation time and
   `recordingDetails` is empty). The phone is on Gage's collar, so its picture moves when he moves, and the ceiling
   camera sees him move. The frame-to-frame change of each, every 0.5 s, was cross-correlated
   ([`../tools/stream_sync.py`](../tools/stream_sync.py) `align`, [`alignment.json`](2026-10-06/alignment.json)):

   | | Offset in the stream | Correlation | Next-best elsewhere | First half / second half alone |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | V1 | 367.5 s | 0.48 | 0.30 | 367.5 / 368.5 s |
   | V2 | 3923.5 s | 0.76 | 0.28 | 3923.5 / 3923.5 s |
   | V3 | 5786.0 s | 0.80 | 0.25 | 5786.0 / 5786.0 s |

   V1 is a conversation, mostly standing, often with the camera covered, hence its weaker peak; each of its halves
   finds the same offset on its own.
3. **Checked by eye, and against an independent clock.** [`alignment_check.jpg`](2026-10-06/alignment_check.jpg) puts
   phone and stream frames side by side: Paul walking back to the bench, the spray test, Gage out in the main lab
   while the room is empty. And at [V3 28:58](https://www.youtube.com/embed/DWH1CEygsTI?start=1738) the phone in Gage's hand, showing his checklist, reads **3:06** in its
   status bar; the alignment puts that moment at 15:06:35–15:06:38.

### What to expect in the powder (`4yghtr`)

- **Yield.** Very low: most of the 180 g is in the bag or still frozen on the sonotrode, the plate and the bowl. The jar
  looks like a few grams, a few per cent at most (inference from the photo). Weigh every fraction (jar, bag, and what
  is scraped off each part) for a mass balance. The trainer's best run turned about 90 % into powder
  ([T9 24:39](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1479)).
- **The bag is un-atomized melt.** A droplet that does not wet the surface bounces
  ([T2 24:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/naePD8o9_Gk?start=1456)). To re-melt it as charge, clean it first: "the more
  contaminated and oxidized the material, the worse the flow will be" ([T1 41:42](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=2502)).
- **Did the vibration run at all?** Unclear. #261 says it should have been started before the sealing pin, which reads
  as started late, but the HMI shows 0 %, 0 Hz and 0 W just after the pour ([OCT6c 33:20](https://www.youtube.com/embed/DWH1CEygsTI?start=2000)).
  SEM of the jar answers it:
  - **round particles** mean it ran once the stack was pulled back. Expect them coarse: carbon fibre gives coarse powder
    ([T5 11:53](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=713)), ultrasonic aluminium is "quite big … more into
    like DED and LPBF size" ([T5 04:34](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=274)) but "very uniform,
    spherical and narrow" ([T3 15:25](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=925)), and capillary-wave
    atomization at 40 kHz puts the median droplet of liquid aluminium near 60 µm (Lang's relation,
    d ≈ 0.34·(8πσ/ρf²)^⅓ with σ ≈ 0.87 N/m and ρ ≈ 2.37 g/cm³; inference). Gage expects "about 80" of the earlier
    batches ([OCT6c 19:35](https://www.youtube.com/embed/DWH1CEygsTI?start=1175)).
  - **flakes, ligaments and irregular bits** mean splash debris from melt hitting a still sonotrode and plate.
- **Sieve before measuring.** Whatever atomized is mixed with splash debris, so a size distribution of the jar as it is
  would mostly measure the debris. There is no sieve yet ([OCT6c 22:16](https://www.youtube.com/embed/DWH1CEygsTI?start=1336)).
- **More oxide than a clean run.** The chamber was at 121–140 ppm by the end, against a best of 40–50
  ([T1 25:15](https://www.youtube.com/embed/wRc8p2_FnJo?start=1515)). Expect thicker oxide skins and more irregular,
  satellite-covered particles (inference).
- **Composition (inference).** Some magnesium lost to oxide and evaporation; carbon where melt sat on the graphite and
  the carbon-fibre plate; and possibly tungsten, nickel, iron or titanium in the lumps that touched the stack. SEM with
  EDS (O, Mg, Si, C, W, Ni, Fe, Ti) on the jar and on a lump would show it.
- **As a repeatability data point** (#261 is titled *Atomizer Runs For Testing Repeatability*), this run measures the two
  mistakes, not carbon fibre, a 1:1 booster, 840 °C and 0.2 bar. Log it as a failed pour so it is not averaged in.

### If the daily SOP stays as written

The steps that shaped this run, and what each would keep doing to the powder (suggestions; the daily SOP is Gage's):

- **No step starts the vibration**, so the next pour can go the same way. Between the scan and the draining pressure:
  amplitude about 90, then vibration on ([T5 59:51](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=3591)). Lower than
  80 "there is a chance that there will be no [atomization] suddenly, because the vibrations could be too weak"
  ([T3 40:21](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2421)), so #261's 50 risks under-atomizing even with the
  vibration on. The slider is a percentage of the generator's set power, 0 to 100
  ([T7 23:35](https://www.youtube.com/embed/FDRTt68Vfvo?start=1415)).
- **No step checks where the stream will land.** As high on the plate as possible, in the middle, and not on the top
  sonotrode ([T5 28:46](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1726), [T5 29:03](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=1743)).
- **"Begin purge process" has no target.** With both heated washes and ≤100 ppm (best 40–50) before the melt, the
  oxygen in the powder stops depending on the day.
- **Draining pressure 0.2 bar** is above the 0.17 bar that was "definitely too high" on Oct 2
  ([OCT2b 27:26](https://www.youtube.com/embed/of5-LhkX_VQ?start=1646)); with a small charge Bartosz used "just barely
  anything" ([RUN1 19:28](https://www.youtube.com/embed/TFpU4uqVF9c?start=1168)). Too much flow for the amplitude
  atomizes poorly ([T9 20:33](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1233)) or bypasses the plate
  ([T3 42:14](https://www.youtube.com/embed/txH397FGTAU?start=2534)). Worth settling against the HMI before the
  repeatability series, since it sets the flow rate and so the particle size (open question in
  [`sop.md`](../sop.md#open-questions)).
- **Carbon-fibre plates, "the cheapest"**, give coarse powder ([T5 11:53](https://www.youtube.com/embed/58wJ_Khwgyk?start=713));
  the trainer's carbon-fibre run used the 1:1.5 booster and paired the 1:1 with metal plates
  ([`sop.md`](../sop.md#ultrasonic-stack)), and molybdenum is his "gold standard" for aluminium
  ([T9 40:48](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=2448)). Fine for repeatability, as long as each run logs
  its plate and booster.
- **180 g** is less than the trainer recommends: "for most processes we recommend to go with like 300 … slightly longer
  process that will allow you to get more control. If you have a very small sample, like before you change parameter,
  establish something, it may already be gone" ([T9 29:05](https://www.youtube.com/embed/9kn-HhXCr1o?start=1745)).
