A faculty member who co-founds a venture-funded company while remaining a
full-time professor needs a defined set of university approvals. This document
lays out that path for department and university leadership. Part~1 is the
approval sequence --- who signs what, in what order, and what the university
gains from the arrangement. Part~2 is the proposed conflict-of-interest
management plan itself, ready to be attached to the university's disclosure
paperwork and signed.

\noticebox{\textbf{How this fits BYU's own process.} BYU's
\href{https://spo.byu.edu/0000018e-c41e-dbed-afaf-e6ffc57a0001/financial-disclosure-form-6-30-2023-docx}{Financial
Disclosure Form} (with its
\href{https://spo.byu.edu/0000018e-c41e-dbed-afaf-e6ffc7110002/financial-disclosure-form-instructions-6-30-2023-docx}{instructions},
both on the \href{https://spo.byu.edu/policies}{Research Administration
Office's policies page}) asks whether ``a proposed conflict of interest
management plan is attached,'' and the
\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/financial-conflict-of-interest-in-research-policy}{Financial
Conflict of Interest in Research Policy} has the faculty member develop that
plan in consultation with the Research Administration Office, for approval by
the Associate Academic Vice President for Research. BYU publishes no plan
template, so Part~2 follows the management tools BYU's policies name and the

(\href{https://hr.uiowa.edu/sites/hr.uiowa.edu/files/2025-06/Management_Plan_Template_eCOIE_Updated_6.4.25_0.docx}{Iowa},

% ====================================================== situation & numbers ==
\parthead{The situation, and what two numbers already decide}
\label{sec:numbers}

The plan in Part~2 is written for one specific arrangement:

\begin{itemize}
\item The faculty member remains a \textbf{full-time professor} --- no leave of
absence. Teaching, the research lab, and student mentoring continue unchanged.
\item The faculty member co-founded the company, holds \textbf{more than ten
percent} of its stock, and serves only in an advisory role.
\item The company has raised \textbf{more than \$3~million} from investors.
\item The company expects to license BYU-developed technology (BYU may take
stock in the company as part of the price), may sponsor research in the
faculty member's lab, may place equipment there, and the lab may sell
instrument time to outside users at cost-recovery rates.
\item The lab's outputs are released as open-source software and hardware by
default.
\end{itemize}

BYU policy attaches three specific consequences to the ownership and
fundraising numbers. This plan accepts all three rather than seeking
exceptions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{The faculty member stays out of every BYU--company deal.} Above
ten percent ownership, the company is a ``Related Party'' under the
\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy}{Conflict
of Time or Interest Policy}, and ``negotiating, influencing, or attempting to
influence the negotiations of contracts or agreements between the university
and a third party'' for its benefit is on the policy's \emph{prohibited} list
--- the conflicts BYU says cannot be managed at all. Someone else negotiates
and signs on each side (\S4).
\item \textbf{No voting board seat.} The
\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/intellectual-property-policy}{Intellectual
Property Policy} allows a faculty member a voting seat on the board of a
company that licenses BYU technology only if the company has raised no more
than \$3~million and has no non-accredited investors. This company is past
that ceiling, so board involvement is limited to a non-voting observer or
advisory role (\S2.2).
\item \textbf{The inventor's royalty share is given up.} Under the same
policy, an inventor who controls ten percent or more of the licensee forfeits
the personal 45~percent share of BYU's licensing income --- and with it the
option to steer that share, with university matching, into a research account.
The faculty member's financial interest is the company stock alone. (A written
exception is possible with dean, AAVP--Research, and Technology Transfer
Office approval; this plan does not assume one.)
\end{enumerate}

% ================================================================= part 1 ==
\parthead{Part 1 --- The approval sequence}

``Department approval'' is the first layer of a stack rather than a single
event. The department controls the disclosure, the written authorization of
the outside role, and this management plan; each later transaction (license,
research agreement, equipment placement) then adds university-level approvers.
Two rules run through all of it: \textbf{disclose before acting}, and
\textbf{every BYU--company arrangement lives in a written agreement signed by
the university} --- never by the faculty member.

Three notes on the sequence:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The standard the chair and dean apply is written down.} They
``do not approve activities they believe would unduly limit a faculty member's
availability to students or compromise\ldots\ teaching, scholarship, or
citizenship performance''
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy}{Conflict
of Time or Interest Policy}}. The step-1 briefing should therefore lead with
the time budget and the teaching plan, not the company.
\item \textbf{Timing matters more than sequence.} Disclosure must come before
the activity, and a plan approved early --- while the stakes are small --- is
the dated record that answers scrutiny later if the company succeeds.
\item \textbf{Steps 7--11 recur with each transaction}, and the faculty member
is out of the negotiation every time (\S4).
\end{itemize}

\subhead{What the university gains}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Instrument access for students.} Company-owned equipment hosted
in the lab under university-signed agreements gives research students hands-on
access to instrumentation the university would find hard to buy on its own
budgets.
\item \textbf{Outside revenue that stays in the lab.} Instrument time billed
to outside users at external cost-recovery rates --- and company research
sponsorship --- fund student wages, future equipment, and student travel.
Because these are not federal grant funds, they carry fewer spending
restrictions (a federal small-business subaward, if one comes later, would
remain federal money with federal rules). None of it goes to the faculty
member's own salary or travel (\S5.4).
\item \textbf{New research funding.} Company sponsorship arrives as
arm's-length research agreements, with federal small-business
(SBIR/STTR) subawards possible later.
\item \textbf{Industry connections for students:} internships, applications
for their research, and jobs after graduation.
\item \textbf{Open source keeps students publishing.} Because the company's
model is open software and open hardware, student manuscripts, conference
talks, and theses avoid the standing confidentiality regimes a conventional
corporate sponsor imposes. What remains is the university's own
invention-disclosure check before public release, which can add short,
bounded delays for patent filings (\S8.1) --- but no thesis is ever
embargoed for the company (\S6.3).
\end{enumerate}

\psection{The relationship being managed}

\clause{1.1} The Faculty Member co-founded the Company and holds \textbf{more
than ten percent} of it (currently [\,\textbullet\,]\%, counting all stock,
options, and rights to acquire stock; interests of immediate family members
are included in every disclosure; updated after each financing under \S9.2).

\clause{1.2} The Faculty Member's only Company roles are stockholder and
\textbf{[Chief Scientific Advisor]} --- an advisory role with no officer
title, no employment relationship, no board vote, and no authority over
Company staff.

\clause{1.3} The Faculty Member receives \textbf{no pay from the Company}: no
salary, consulting fees, bonuses, or expense-paid travel. Stock is the only
financial interest. This also keeps the arrangement clear of the rule that a
faculty member may be a paid consultant to a company or participate in
research it sponsors, but not both at once
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/intellectual-property-policy}{Intellectual
Property Policy}; exceptions require chair, dean, and written AAVP--Research
approval}.

\clause{1.4} Because the ownership is ten percent or more, the Faculty Member
forfeits the inventor's personal 45\% share of any income BYU receives from
licensing to the Company, and the matched research-account option that goes
with it. Acknowledged; no exception is requested.

\clause{1.5} The Company has raised more than \$3~million. Expected BYU
touchpoints: a license or assignment of BYU technology (BYU may hold Company
stock as part of the price); Company-sponsored research in the Faculty
Member's lab; Company equipment placed in the lab; possible sales of lab
instrument time.

\clause{1.6} The lab releases its outputs as open-source software and
hardware by default, after the invention-disclosure step in \S8.1.

\clause{1.7} The Faculty Member remains on a full-time faculty appointment
and is not on leave. A change in any fact in this section requires a plan
amendment \emph{first} (\S10).

\psection{Role and boundaries}

\clause{2.1} \textbf{Advisory titles only.} The Faculty Member will not serve
as an officer of the Company ``in title or in fact,'' nor as an employee or
manager of Company staff, while a full-time faculty member
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy}{Conflict
of Time or Interest Policy};
\href{https://finserve.byu.edu/00000193-6566-d995-a9db-eff6a47a0001/sponsored-programs-handbook}{Sponsored
Projects Handbook}: ``Faculty members not on leave are not to be line officers
in outside companies''}. This is the same line
\href{https://doresearch.stanford.edu/policies/research-policy-handbook/conflicts-commitment-and-interest/faculty-policy-conflict-commitment-and-interest}{Stanford
draws}: advisory roles yes, management roles and titles no.

\clause{2.2} \textbf{No voting board seat} --- foreclosed by the Company's
fundraising, as explained on page~\pageref{sec:numbers}. Any non-voting
observer or advisory-board seat is named in the step-3 written authorization
from the chair and dean, not assumed.

\clause{2.3} \textbf{Neither hat speaks for the other.} The Faculty Member
does not represent BYU in any Company matter or the Company in any BYU
matter, and is not authorized to commit either organization to anything. The
Company does not use BYU's name, logo, or facilities in its advertising or
investor materials
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/intellectual-property-policy}{Intellectual
Property Policy}: ``The name of the university shall not be used\ldots\ in any
advertising''}.

\psection{Time, teaching, and availability to students}

\clause{3.1} All Company activity fits inside BYU's outside-activity
allowance: an average of \textbf{one day a week, at most four days a month},
across every outside activity combined. The Faculty Member keeps a log of
days spent and reports it to the chair with the annual disclosure.

\clause{3.2} \textbf{Scheduled teaching is never displaced.} Classes, class
preparation, and office hours are held as scheduled; Company time taken
during a workday is made up on evenings or weekends that same week
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy}{Conflict
of Time or Interest Policy} compensating-time expectation}.

\clause{3.3} \textbf{Students are calendared first.} Student meetings and
research-group meetings are placed on the calendar before Company
commitments, which fit around them --- not the reverse.

\clause{3.4} The chair reviews the time log at annual review against the
policy's standard (availability to students; teaching, scholarship, and
citizenship performance) and may require Company time to be reduced if any of
these show strain.

\psection{Contracts: who negotiates and who signs}

\clause{4.1} The Faculty Member takes \textbf{no part in negotiating,
approving, or influencing any agreement between BYU and the Company --- on
either side of the table}. This covers the license, every research agreement,
every equipment placement, and any gift. The Company is represented by a
non-founder officer ([name/title]); BYU is represented by the Research
Administration Office, the Technology Transfer Office, or the Office of
General Counsel
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy}{Conflict
of Time or Interest Policy}, prohibited conflicts: ``negotiating,
influencing, or attempting to influence the negotiations of contracts or
agreements between the university and a third party for the benefit of the
employee or a Related Party''}.

\clause{4.2} The Faculty Member does not advocate for any BYU--Company deal
or its terms with the chair, dean, Research Administration Office, Technology
Transfer Office, or AAVP--Research, directly or through colleagues.

\clause{4.3} \textbf{What remains permitted}, each an ordinary duty: telling
the Technology Transfer Office that the Company is a potential licensee;
technical input on the scientific scope of work, submitted through the
Research Administration Office's designated contact; serving as the named
technical contact once an agreement is signed; signing the inventor's
acknowledgment of a license; and signing any royalty Distribution Agreement
--- a contract between BYU and its own employee (given \S1.4, no
distribution is expected from this license)
\policyref{Technology Transfer Office,
\href{https://techtransfer.byu.edu/resources}{``Working with the Technology
Transfer Office''}}.

\clause{4.4} \textbf{If the Company ever sells to BYU} (instrument time,
equipment, services), the sale happens only after the Faculty Member is
approved as an employee-vendor by Purchasing \& Travel, Compensation, and
Regulatory Accounting, and the Faculty Member takes no part in any BYU
purchasing decision involving the Company. Both parties acknowledge the
policy's hard stop: an employee whose access to BYU's non-public information
would give their company an unfair advantage may not do business with the
university at all
\policyref{Employee-Vendor Policy, university policy library
(\href{https://policy.byu.edu}{policy.byu.edu}, sign-in)}.

\psection{Company-sponsored research and cost recovery}

\clause{5.1} Any Company use of lab personnel, students, or facilities is
carried out under a written agreement selected and signed by the authorized
university office: a \textbf{sponsored research agreement} for research work
--- the default, negotiated by the Research Administration Office at BYU's
standard terms, with no lasting restrictions on publication --- a
cost-recovery account for purchases of instrument time (\S5.4), or an
equipment agreement (\S7.3). No Company work happens in the lab outside a
signed agreement's scope of work
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/intellectual-property-policy}{Intellectual
Property Policy}: activities ``must be administered as sponsored research
projects when there is a substantial use of university resources or when BYU
students are employed''}.

\clause{5.2} \textbf{Independent eyes on the science.} A senior faculty
member with no Company interest, designated by the dean ([\,\textbullet\,]),
reviews the design of each Company-sponsored project before work begins and
the work at least annually thereafter --- funding, student roles,
publication freedom, and the separation between university and Company work
--- reporting to the chair and AAVP--Research; the chair may pause affected
work while a concern is resolved. (Staffing note: research associates and
postdoctoral fellows may serve as co-investigators on such projects but not
as principal investigators, so faculty-level responsibility stays with
regular faculty.)

\clause{5.3} Publications and presentations from Company-sponsored or
license-related work disclose the Faculty Member's Company interest, and
BYU's stock position if BYU holds one. If BYU accepts Company stock, the
university's own interest is reviewed outside the conflicted chain --- by
the AAVP--Research with the Office of General Counsel --- separately from
this individual plan.

\clause{5.4} \textbf{Cost recovery, and where that money goes.} Sales of lab
instrument time outside a research agreement run only through a cost-recovery
(recharge) account approved by the chair, dean, and Regulatory Accounting,
with outside users billed at the external rate --- overhead included --- and
the tax treatment of outside sales (``unrelated business income'') settled
with Regulatory Accounting before billing starts
\policyref{\href{https://spo.byu.edu/00000190-3687-d777-a591-37af392d0001/rechargecenterpolicyprocedures-pdf}{Recharge
Center Policy and Procedures}}. \textbf{Self-imposed boundary:} revenue from
Company-related cost recovery or sponsorship is budgeted to student wages,
equipment, and student travel --- none of it funds the Faculty Member's
salary (including summer salary) or the Faculty Member's own travel.

\psection{Students}

BYU policy requires disclosure but does not otherwise spell out protections
for students around a faculty member's company. This section creates them.

\clause{6.1} \textbf{Written notice.} Every member of the lab receives
written notice of the Faculty Member's Company interest (and BYU's, if BYU
holds stock) when they join and annually after; a copy goes to the chair.

\clause{6.2} \textbf{An independent advocate.} The graduate coordinator or
associate chair --- someone with no Company interest --- meets the lab's
students at least once a semester without the Faculty Member present, and
students may go to them, the chair, or the dean's office at any time.

\clause{6.3} \textbf{Student time is protected, and participation is
voluntary.} Working on Company-related projects is a choice: a comparable
non-Company project, funding source, and supervisor are available without
penalty, retaliation is prohibited, and a student's choice never affects
grades, authorship, funding, or letters of recommendation. No student is
asked to do Company work as part of any BYU role; Company tasks exist only
inside the sponsored agreement's scope of work. Thesis topics, timelines, and
publication schedules are set on academic grounds --- never by Company
deadlines --- and no thesis is embargoed and no defense put under a
confidentiality agreement for the Company's convenience. Where patent timing
requires quiet, the answer is a provisional patent filing before the defense,
not restricting the student's work.

\clause{6.4} \textbf{Hours and double employment.} University employment
respects BYU's caps --- 20 hours a week during fall and winter semesters,
with stricter limits for international students, whose visa status can be at
stake. A student working for BYU and the Company at the same time on related
subject matter must disclose it (a policy requirement, not just a plan term),
and the advocate reviews the student's combined load each semester so
assistantship duties and degree progress are not squeezed
\policyref{Student Hiring and Employment Policy and
\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy}{Conflict
of Time or Interest Policy} (student disclosure trigger)}.

\clause{6.5} \textbf{Company jobs and internships.} A student who wants to
work for the Company is referred to the advocate and the dean's office for
advice independent of the Faculty Member --- the practice
\href{https://web.stanford.edu/group/OTL/documents/bp_faculty_sus.pdf}{Stanford
recommends} for faculty startups --- and the advocate reviews the offer's
terms (duties, hours, pay, invention-assignment and confidentiality
obligations, visa implications) before it is accepted. The Faculty Member
does not supervise the
same student's thesis and Company work; a Company internship taken for
academic credit is arranged and graded by someone else. (BYU's internship
rules bar credit under a family member's supervision but are silent on a
faculty advisor's own company; this plan closes that gap.) Any Company offer
to a student the Faculty Member currently supervises triggers a plan
amendment first (\S10).

\clause{6.6} \textbf{Lab membership and Company personnel.} Everyone working
in the lab signs BYU's standard participation and invention-assignment
agreement. Company employees do not work in the lab informally or as
volunteers; their presence on campus runs through a written visiting
appointment or the sponsored agreement.

\psection{University resources and equipment}

\clause{7.1} \textbf{The rule is categorical}: ``University Employees may not
use university resources, facilities, or work time to engage in commercial
activities, businesses, or `start-up' companies''
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/intellectual-property-policy}{Intellectual
Property Policy}}. Accordingly, all Company work runs on Company-owned
laptops, accounts, software subscriptions, and storage from day one. On
campus, Company devices use their own cellular connection --- not BYU
networks, whose use for outside commercial purposes requires written
authorization from the responsible vice president and the university's chief
information officer, and which the university may monitor. No Company data on
BYU systems; no non-public BYU information on Company systems.

\clause{7.2} \textbf{Campus office.} Use of the campus office for the Company
is limited to occasional calls and correspondence on Company devices ---
none of the Company's records, operations, meetings, or visitors. Using a
campus office for business purposes requires explicit written approval,
which university policy places with the chair and dean; that approval is
requested together with step~3, and until it is granted this allowance does
not operate --- there is simply no Company use of the office.

\clause{7.3} \textbf{Equipment placements.} Company equipment enters the lab
only under a university-signed agreement --- an equipment loan or a term of
the research agreement --- settling ownership, insurance, maintenance,
access, export-control screening, and removal before delivery. The Faculty
Member signs nothing. Students use placed equipment for university research
or within the sponsored agreement, and the equipment is reported as outside
support on federal grant applications where required.

\psection{Inventions and open-source release}

\clause{8.1} \textbf{Disclose first, release second.} New inventions are
disclosed to the Technology Transfer Office \emph{before} anything is made
public --- a public code repository, preprint, conference talk, or thesis
included --- because public release first can permanently destroy foreign
patent rights. After the office files a provisional application or releases
the rights, open-source release proceeds. The lab keeps a standing
pre-release checklist, and review is bounded: normally 30~days, with up to
60~more only when a patent filing is under way --- and it never delays a
thesis defense, thesis deposit, or graduation (\S6.3)
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/intellectual-property-policy}{Intellectual
Property Policy}; Technology Transfer Office,
\href{https://techtransfer.byu.edu/resources}{``Preparing a Patent
Application''}}.

\clause{8.2} \textbf{Open-source boundaries are set upstream.} What is
released openly versus kept proprietary is fixed in the lab's published
open-science policy and the Company's published licensing policy --- both
adopted outside any BYU negotiation --- so the Faculty Member never argues
release terms at a BYU--Company table (\S4). BYU's own ownership and
approval rights (\S8.1) still govern every release.

\clause{8.3} \textbf{A clean line between the two employers' inventions.}
The lab and the Company keep records showing where, when, and with what
resources each invention was made. Because BYU's ownership claim extends to
inventions ``related to the current or demonstrably anticipated business,
research, or development of the university,'' Company-side inventions near
the lab's field are covered by written Technology Transfer Office releases or
license carve-outs --- not by records alone.

\psection{Reporting}

\clause{9.1} \textbf{Annually:} the outside-activity disclosure in Faculty
Profile; the financial disclosure with the Research Administration Office;
the time log to the chair (\S3.1); the advocate's student review (\S6.2); and
the independent reviewer's report (\S5.2). The chair monitors compliance
with this plan through completion of the affected projects --- not only at
annual review
\policyref{\href{https://policy.byu.edu/view/financial-conflict-of-interest-in-research-policy}{Financial
Conflict of Interest in Research Policy}: chair monitoring responsibility}.

\clause{9.2} \textbf{Within 30 days:} any Company financing, change in the
Faculty Member's ownership percentage, change of role or title, new proposed
BYU--Company transaction, or contemplated leave is reported to the chair and
the Research Administration Office. (The 30-day update of \emph{financial}
disclosures is required by policy; the other triggers are terms of this
plan.)

\clause{9.3} \textbf{Acknowledged consequences.} On research funded by the
U.S.\ Public Health Service (for example, the National Institutes of
Health), BYU must answer public requests about disclosed financial interests
within five business days. An untimely, unreviewed, or unmanaged financial
conflict on covered research triggers a formal retrospective review by the
AAVP--Research, chair, associate dean, and Research Administration Office
director
\policyref{\href{https://spo.byu.edu/policies}{Financial Disclosure Form
Instructions}}.

\psection{Changes that require amending this plan first}

\begin{itemize}
\item any officer title, employment relationship, or voting board role at the
Company;
\item any leave of absence;
\item Company employment of a student the Faculty Member currently
supervises;
\item the Company selling anything to BYU (\S4.4);
\item a request for an exception to the royalty forfeiture (\S1.4);
\item any research involving human subjects that touches the Company;
\item any change in the facts of \S1.
\end{itemize}

\psection{Review, duration, and remedies}

\clause{11.1} The AAVP--Research reviews this plan annually with the chair
and dean and may strengthen it at any time --- the tools named in policy run
from an independent monitor to removal from a project to reduction or
elimination of the financial interest --- or end it when the interests in
\S1 no longer exist, confirmed in writing. Noncompliance is handled under
the governing policies.

\clause{11.2} Commitments in this plan about Company conduct --- no use of
BYU's name (\S2.3), no informal approaches to students (\S6.5--6.6), no
Company personnel or equipment in the lab outside signed agreements
(\S6.6, \S7.3) --- are confirmed in a one-page acknowledgment signed by an
authorized Company officer and attached to this plan.
