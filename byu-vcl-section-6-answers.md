# Answers to the twelve Section 6 questions — from the private 195-policy snapshot

**Source:** the authenticated snapshot in [`policies/`](../README.md), taken **2026-07-24** while signed in via CAS + Duo.
**Written for:** Section 6 of `vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl` PR 156 (twelve open questions about a faculty COI / start-up management plan).
**Answered:** 2026-08-19.

All quotes below are verbatim from the authenticated pages. Policy dates are the "Approved / Revised" line on each page as of the snapshot. **Three questions came back negative** — the policy simply does not exist in the library — and those negatives are as useful as the hits, because they tell us which questions have to go to an office rather than a document.

Two findings cut against the current draft plan, so they lead.

## ⚠️ Two things in the Intellectual Property Policy that change the plan

**(a) A flat prohibition on using university resources for a start-up.** [Intellectual Property Policy](../html/intellectual-property-policy.html) (Approved 17 Jun 2019 [Revised 16 Dec 2024]), *Management of Intellectual Property*:

> "University Employees may not use university resources, facilities, or work time to engage in commercial activities, businesses, or 'start-up' companies."

No approval valve is attached to that sentence. It sits in tension with our §7.1 clause authorizing incidental campus-office use for company communications. The Conflict of Time or Interest Policy's *Incidental Use* allowance ("occasional or infrequent personal use that results in little or no cost to the university") is the only doorway, and it is framed as *personal* use, not commercial use. **Recommendation:** either drop §7.1's office-use authorization, or keep it but get it approved in writing by the dean **and** the AAVP–R rather than the chair alone, and re-word it to sit inside "Incidental Use" (no company work performed there — communications only, no company records, no operations, no meetings).

**(b) Consulting and sponsored research with the same company are mutually exclusive.** Same policy, *Miscellaneous Ownership Issues*:

> "University Employees may either act as a paid consultant to or participate in sponsored research for the same company but may not perform both at the same time. Exceptions may be granted in rare situations when an appropriate management plan is approved by the department chair and college dean and written approval is obtained from the associate academic vice president – research and graduate studies."

This is a management-plan trigger the plan does not currently address, and it is directly on point: if the company both pays for consulting and sponsors an SRA, that is the "rare situation" requiring chair + dean + AAVP–R written approval. Three more clauses from the same policy that belong in the plan:

> "In all consulting arrangements, University Employees act as independent consultants and are not authorized to engage or obligate the university as a party to any contract. ... The name of the university shall not be used in the consulting services, in any advertising, or in any other way."

> "Activities must be administered as sponsored research projects when there is a substantial use of university resources or when BYU students are employed by the university in connection with such activities."

> "On rare occasions, there may be compelling reasons to allow University Employees to participate as a voting member of a company board; provided that (i) the company has not raised more than $3,000,000 and (ii) non-accredited investors are not involved. ... any such participation requires full disclosure to and prior written approval from the dean, the associate academic vice president – research and graduate studies, and the Technology Transfer Office, Creative Works Office, or Copyright Licensing Office, as applicable."

That $3M ceiling is a hard number to plan a board exit around. And one correction to the earlier read that the BYU royalty share is simply "not an SFI" — it can be zeroed out entirely:

> "Occasionally a Developer may become a principal equity owner of a company that licenses university Intellectual Property. In such cases, the developer will forfeit his or her distribution outlined above. ... a principal equity owner is one who controls ten percent or more equity in the company."

Finally, the shop-rights clause, which is the real answer to "can company people work in the lab" (see No. 7):

> "when any person not employed by the university either (a) engages in research or development of Intellectual Property under the supervision and direction of a faculty member in connection with a program or activity subject to this policy or (b) uses substantial university resources in connection with a research program or activity, the university retains the right to claim, as a condition of the student being allowed to participate in the project or use university resources, a non-exclusive, perpetual, royalty-free, paid-up, irrevocable license to exploit, use, and sublicense the resulting Intellectual Property."

## 1. Expectations of a Faculty Appointment — campus office / business telephone listing

[Expectations of a Faculty Appointment Policy](../html/expectations-of-a-faculty-appointment-policy.html) — Approved Dec 1995 [Revised 29 Nov 2021]. The exact clause, from the Area 3 discussion in *The Faculty Appointment*:

> "As a result of this condition, while under university contract, a faculty member should not serve as a line officer in a private business, maintain a business or professional telephone listing, maintain an off-campus office (other than in the home) or use his or her campus office for business purposes, participate in extended military or government service, solicit sales of a product, or hold a second job outside the university, **without the explicit written approval of the appropriate university supervisors (usually the dean and department chair)**."

The approver is named twice. Section 3 (*Approval and Clarification of Faculty Assignments*):

> "Written approval, again by the department chair and dean, is required for all Area 3 activities in figure 2 and should be granted only for unusual circumstances."

**Would a chair-signed COI management plan clause satisfy it?** No — on three counts. First, this policy names **chair *and* dean**, not chair alone. Second, the newer [Conflict of Time or Interest Policy](../html/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy.html) (Approved 7 Apr 2025) is the operative instrument now and it does *not* carry the campus-office sentence forward; it handles the same ground through two other doors, and its list of activities requiring written authorization from "the employee's department chair, dean, or supervisor" includes:

> "maintain an off-campus office to conduct work-related responsibilities, other than in an employee's home or a location approved in a Remote Work Agreement"
> "operate a business, serve as an officer (in title or in fact) of a business, or maintain employment with a business that requires attention during the employee's assigned work hours"
> "provide consulting services"

and separately, under *Use of University Resources*:

> "Incidental Use of university property and equipment is generally permissible when it does not conflict with normal university activities. Non-incidental use of university property and equipment, including the use of consumable supplies (e.g., copy machines and postage), requires prior written authorization from the employee's department chair or supervisor, which should provide for adequate reimbursement to the university. Some proposed personal uses may be so extensive or substantial that neither authorization nor reimbursement can make the use appropriate."

Third — and decisively — the Intellectual Property Policy prohibition quoted at the top has no chair-level waiver. **Net:** route the office-use authorization through the dean and AAVP–R, keep it inside "Incidental Use," and drop any suggestion that a chair signature alone clears it. Note also the plain reading of the Expectations text: serving as a **line officer** of a private business is itself an Area 3 activity requiring the same written approval.

## 2. Appropriate Use of Information Technology Resources — commercial use and personal devices

[Appropriate Use of Information Technology Resources Policy](../html/appropriate-use-of-information-technology-resources-policy.html) — Approved 26 Feb 2024 [Revised 12 Feb 2026]. Commercial-use clause, verbatim:

> "Users must not use IT Resources for non-university commercial purposes or non-university gain, unless authorized in writing by the individual user's responsible vice president and the university's chief information officer."

Both signatures, conjunctive. Note the sentence immediately before it grants an incidental allowance that pointedly does **not** extend to commercial use:

> "IT Resources may be used for incidental personal and ecclesiastical purposes. Personal or ecclesiastical use of IT Resources must not occur under circumstances that interfere with employee work responsibilities, displace other IT Resources, or require purchases of additional IT Resources."

**Does a company laptop on campus Wi-Fi fall under the monitoring provisions? Yes — explicitly.** *Privacy and Monitoring*:

> "The university provides no general expectation of privacy in the use of IT Resources except as required by law."
> "BYU reserves the right to monitor and report technology use, **including the use of personal devices connected to IT Resources**, to the maximum extent permitted by law. All users, by their use of IT Resources, consent to such monitoring and reporting."
> "As permitted by law, the university may disclose to third parties data residing in IT Resources."

The mechanism is the definition: "Information Technology Resources (IT Resources) means university-owned infrastructure, cloud services, software, and hardware with computing and/or networking capability. It includes, but is not limited to, ... networks and networking systems ...". The laptop is not an IT Resource; **campus Wi-Fi is**, so connecting to it makes the laptop "a personal device connected to IT Resources" and pulls it inside the monitoring clause — while the traffic itself would be use of an IT Resource "for non-university commercial purposes."

**Practical rule for the plan:** company work rides a company hotspot/LTE, never eduroam or BYU Wi-Fi, unless and until a responsible-VP-plus-CIO written authorization exists. Also relevant, from *Terms of Use*: users must "not attach any device that may, in any way, endanger or disrupt the continuous and stable operation of the university network or other IT Resources," and must "not share or transfer individual university accounts, including network IDs, passwords, or other access codes."

## 3. Student Hiring and Employment — hour caps, international students, outside internships

[Student Hiring and Employment Policy](../html/student-hiring-and-employment-policy.html) — Approved 30 Mar 2026. Caps, verbatim:

> **U.S. Citizens and Permanent Residents** — "During fall and winter semesters, undergraduate student employees who are U.S. citizens or permanent residents may not work more than 20 hours per week of university employment unless Human Resources grants an exception to the student employee's supervisor."

> **International Students** — "Undergraduate or graduate students who are not U.S. citizens or permanent residents and whose visas permit only on-campus employment must not exceed 20 hours of work per week while school is in session. Violation of this policy will result in an immediate order to stop work and can result in termination of employment and loss of the right to remain in the United States."
> "International students may exceed 20 hours in any workweek that includes work done during their annual vacation period or when school is not in session (i.e., during approved holidays and breaks) if approved by the student's supervisor and otherwise consistent with university policy. International students should check with International Student and Scholar Services to verify that they are permitted to exceed 20 hours prior to requesting or working additional hours."
> "International students may not work past their graduation date in their on-campus jobs."
> "Only students who are authorized to work in the United States are eligible for student employment."

**Aggregation** is defined only across *university* positions — this is the key limitation for our question:

> "Student employees may not be paid on both an hourly and contract basis at the same time. If a student employee accepts two or more student employment positions, any one of which is hourly, all time spent on work assignments must be tracked, reported, and paid as hourly employment."

Plus overtime at >40 hrs/week per the Staff and Student Employee Overtime Policy, and "Student employees in hourly positions are required to complete and submit accurate reporting of hours each week."

**Is a BYU RA-ship plus an outside internship addressed? Not in this policy — but it is addressed in the Conflict of Time or Interest Policy, and the trigger is precisely our case:**

> "Student employees are required to disclose Secondary Employment only when employed by more than one university department **or when simultaneously conducting research on the same issue for BYU and a third-party**."

That second clause squarely covers a student holding a BYU RA-ship on a topic while also working for the company on that topic — disclosure is mandatory, to the supervisor, and if a conflict is identified "the employee and supervisor collaborate to develop a written plan describing how the Conflict will be reduced, eliminated, or appropriately managed." Worth writing into the plan as a standing obligation for every student who touches both sides.

Two adjacent points. The [Internships Policy](../html/internships-policy.html) sets the credit mechanics — enrollment in 199R/299R/399R/496R/599R/688R (0.5–12 credits), "The minimum number of internship hours for each hour of credit earned is 42 hours," no retroactive credit — and bars family-adjacent arrangements: "students may not receive grades or internship credit under the supervision of any immediate family members" and "students may not fulfill internships for organizations owned or managed by any immediate family members." **There is no parallel clause for a faculty advisor's own company**, which is a genuine gap; treat it as a conflict to manage explicitly rather than a permitted-by-silence case.

**Gap to flag:** the minimum-credit-hour thresholds are delegated to the *Student Hiring and Employment Procedures*, which is **not in the snapshot** — procedures documents are generally not published on policy.byu.edu. That has to come from HR.

## 4. Non-CFS Track Academic Appointments — PI / co-PI eligibility

[Non-CFS Track Academic Appointments Policy](../html/non-cfs-track-academic-appointments-policy.html) — the *Research Personnel* section, verbatim:

> **Research Associate** — "A person—typically with a PhD, other terminal degree, or equivalent experience—who is hired full-time to participate actively in and supervise a portion of a university faculty member's research project. ... **A Research Associate may serve as co-principal investigator on externally funded research but may not serve as principal investigator.** While a Research Associate may supervise student research, he or she is without graduate faculty status ... and, therefore, is not permitted to serve as committee chair for graduate student theses or dissertations. With appropriate credentials and by petition to Graduate Studies, a Research Associate may serve as a member of a graduate student's advisory committee. Research Associates do not occupy a university FTE and may not be paid from appropriated funds."

> **Post-Doctoral Research Fellow** — "**A Post-Doctoral Research Fellow may serve as co-principal investigator on externally funded research but may not serve as principal investigator.** Normally, a Post-Doctoral Research Fellow would not supervise other full-time personnel on the research project."

> **Research Staff** — "Research Staff are not expected to perform independent research work. Instead, they fill supporting roles in the project and are responsible to the principal investigator."

**So yes — professional-track research faculty (Research Associate, Post-Doctoral Research Fellow) may co-PI an industry SRA, but may not PI it.** Term limits matter for any multi-year SRA staffing plan: appointments are "for one year or less," renewable but "not to be offered in more than three consecutive years," a fourth year only "upon petition to the AAVP – faculty relations, who may grant a petition at his or her sole discretion," and "in no case shall the cumulative time in service as Post-Doctoral Research Fellow and Research Associate exceed six years." Research Staff cannot transition into either role.

**Important caveat: no policy in the 195 states affirmatively who *may* serve as PI.** The rule is expressed only as these prohibitions. The [Research Personnel Policy](../html/research-personnel-policy.html) (26 Mar 2012 [Revised 19 Dec 2024]) is consistent but no more explicit — "The research associate would normally be responsible to the principal investigator of the project but could also be a co-principal investigator." PI eligibility for regular full-time faculty is inferable but uncited. That one needs RAO confirmation.

## 5. Employee-Vendor Policy — workflow, proprietary-information bar, no-cost arrangements

[Employee-Vendor Policy](../html/employee-vendor-policy.html) — Approved 14 Dec 2020 [Revised 18 May 2021]. Short policy; here it is nearly in full.

**Scope:** "In this policy, vendor or enterprise means any person or company **selling** goods or services, including consulting, to the university." Restrictions apply "whenever university personnel own or control any interest in an enterprise that **does, or seeks to do, business with the university**."

**Restrictions, verbatim:**
> "The individual must be an approved employee-vendor before doing business with the university."
> "The employee-vendor may not represent or influence the university in its dealings with the employee's enterprise."
> "**An employee-vendor who has access to the university's proprietary information that would give unfair advantage to the employee's enterprise may not do business with the university.**"
> "The restrictions above do not apply to individuals owning securities in a company commonly traded in the public markets."

Note the third one is an **absolute bar, not a manageable conflict** — no plan cures it.

**Approval workflow — three offices, each independently:**
> "To avoid potential conflicts of interest, the **Purchasing and Travel**, **Compensation**, and **Regulatory Accounting and Reporting** departments must each approve all employee-vendors. Any BYU employee acting as a vendor must apply for and receive approved employee-vendor status and conform to the requirements on the employee-vendor application."
> "The Human Resources Committee reserves the right to terminate any employee-vendor approval."

And "An employee-vendor who also meets the requirements to be an independent contractor must comply with this policy and the Independent Contractors Policy."

**Does it trigger on no-cost arrangements?** On the text alone, probably not — the definitional hook is *selling*, so a pure equipment placement or in-kind support with no payment from BYU arguably sits outside it. **But that does not make no-cost arrangements safe**, because the Conflict of Time or Interest Policy catches them in its *prohibited* list (not its manageable list):

> "**Prohibited Conflicts of Interest** — The following Conflicts of Interest are prohibited for all university employees because they are so significant that they cannot be managed in ways that protect the interests of the university.
> • Negotiating, influencing, or attempting to influence the negotiations of contracts or agreements between the university and a third party for the benefit of the employee or a Related Party (see Employee-Vendor Policy)."

and its definition: "**Related Party** also includes corporations and other business entities in which an employee has an ownership interest that **exceeds 10 percent**."

**So for a founder above 10 % equity, negotiating an equipment-placement or services agreement with BYU is a prohibited conflict regardless of whether money changes hands.** Someone else — chair, dean, or a designated unit representative — must negotiate and sign BYU's side, and the plan should say so in as many words. **Gap:** the *Employee Vendor Procedures* referenced by the policy is **not in the snapshot**; request it from Purchasing.

## 6. Legal Documents Policy — signature tiers and OGC/RMS review

[Legal Documents Policy](../html/legal-documents-policy.html) — Approved 4 Nov 2024 [Revised 2 Jul 2025]. The signature-authority table verbatim:

| Authorized Signer | Monetary Transaction Limits | Non-monetary Obligation Limits |
|---|---|---|
| President (or delegate) | No limit | No limit |
| President's Council member (or delegate) | Up to $500,000 | Binds the university for up to 5 years |
| Associate/Assistant VP or Dean/Director who reports to a member of the President's Council (or delegate) | Up to $250,000 | Binds the university for up to 3 years |
| Other non-student employee acting within the scope of employment and with approval from the employee's supervisor | Up to $100,000 | Binds the university for up to 1 year |

Qualifiers that matter:
> "The university president is the only authorized signer for Legal Documents involving the transfer of real property."
> "If a Legal Document has both monetary obligations and Non-monetary Obligations, the Legal Document must be signed by an individual with Signature Authority for both. **A Transaction may not be divided between two or more Legal Documents to avoid authorization limits.**"
> "**Transaction** means (1) an acquisition or purchase of any property or service by the university; (2) a sale, transfer, or other disposition of any university asset; or (3) a commitment of the university's employees or resources. **A series of related Transactions with the same party for the same subject matter is considered a single Transaction.**"

The non-monetary term limits do **not** apply to "nondisclosure or confidentiality agreements; licensing, transfer, or other disposition of intellectual property; allowing or requiring the university to control an asset for an indefinite period of time (e.g., stock transfer agreements); and standard provisions that typically survive the termination of an agreement" — those remain subject to the monetary limits. Delegation "must be in writing (email is acceptable), must be limited to the delegating individual's own Signature Authority, must not be sub-delegated, must not be granted to a student employee," is revocable at any time, and both parties keep electronic copies on file.

**OGC / RMS review is triggered by *provisions*, not by document type** — there is no "SRAs always go to OGC" rule. The rows most likely to fire on an equipment-loan or recharge-services agreement:

| Row | Provision | OGC | RMS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | "Release, waiver, or limitation of liability by or against the university, or any statement intended to settle any legal claim..." | X | |
| 4 | "Agreement for a third party to process or have access to nonpublic institutional data (as that term is defined in the Data Use, Privacy, and Security Policy)" | X | |
| 9 | "Any Transaction or Non-monetary Obligation involving real property owned in whole or in part by the university (e.g., a deed, lease, mortgage, easement, lien, or encumbrance)" | X | |
| 10 | "The creation, transfer, or other disposition of any university ownership interest in, voting rights in, or governance of any legal entity (e.g., a corporation or limited liability company)" | X | |
| 11 | "Any language that requires the university to indemnify, defend, or hold harmless another party" | X | X |
| 12 | "Any language establishing governing law, jurisdiction, or venue outside of the state of Utah, or requiring any form of alternative dispute resolution" | X | |
| 13 | "Any obligation to comply, or representation that BYU complies, with a specific legal standard or non-BYU policy" | X | |
| 14 | "Any agreement relating to an activity that presents a risk of substantial loss or liability (e.g., risk of bodily injury, property loss, data breach, etc.)" | X | X |
| 15 | "A commitment by the university to obtain insurance or to provide proof of insurance" | | X |

"Review and approval by the OGC or RMS are not required for a Contract of Adhesion." And once OGC has approved a form, "the OGC's subsequent review and approval of that Legal Document (or substantially similar provisions in the same type of Legal Document) is not required" — so getting one template blessed pays off repeatedly.

**Which tier signs an equipment-loan or recharge-services agreement?** The policy answers by value and term, not by type. A faculty PI sits in the bottom tier: **≤ $100,000 and binding for ≤ 1 year**. A typical multi-year equipment placement therefore needs at minimum an **Associate/Assistant VP or Dean/Director** (≤ 3 years, ≤ $250k), and anything longer than three years or above $250k goes to a **President's Council member**. Add rows 11, 14, and 15 and it needs OGC + RMS sign-off before anyone signs. The anti-splitting rule means you cannot break a five-year placement into five one-year agreements.

## 7. Volunteer Policy — outside-company personnel working unpaid in university labs

[Volunteer Policy](../html/volunteer-policy.html) — Approved 17 Apr 2023 [Revised 10 May 2023]. **There is no clause naming outside-company personnel.** The bar is structural, and it is solid. Four independent grounds:

**(a) The definition excludes them.**
> "**Volunteer Services** means services that are freely performed **in furtherance of the university's nonprofit objectives**, without contemplation or receipt of compensation or similar benefit, such as academic (including internship) credit, funding, or future employment."

Company personnel advancing company R&D are not furthering the university's nonprofit objectives, and they *are* receiving a benefit.

**(b) Commercial unpaid service is expressly prohibited under federal law.** From *Prohibited Activities*:
> "To comply with federal law, the following activities are not Volunteer Services and are prohibited: ... **Unpaid service in furtherance of the university's commercial activities**, such as in university dining, laundry, or retail facilities"

along with "Former employee training a replacement," "Hourly employee performing work assignments off the clock," and unpaid pre-hire work by prospective non-exempt employees.

**(c) Displacement bar.**
> "Volunteer Services must further the university's religious, charitable, civic, or humanitarian purposes; however, **Volunteers cannot, even on a temporary basis, displace employed workers.**"

**(d) Lab work triggers a mandatory RMS agreement, and some of it is flatly off-limits.** Volunteers are prohibited from "Operation of heavy equipment," "Services requiring the gathering or analysis of data that includes social security numbers or medical records," and "Any activity inappropriate or unsafe for an employee." A signed Volunteer Agreement is *required* where services involve "Hazardous materials," "Providing medical surveillance or wearing personal protective equipment," "Working with or near stored energy," "Access to rare or valuable items or collections," "Confidential information," or "Traveling off campus or in a university-provided vehicle." Agreements come from RMS, the unit must ensure a signed copy reaches RMS "before the Volunteer begins providing Volunteer Services," and ongoing services require annual renewal. Backstop: "The responsible vice president or Risk Management and Safety may prohibit Volunteers from engaging in other activities not specified above."

Also: "University employees must not represent or imply that Volunteers will receive any preference for future employment or future compensation," and reimbursement "must not be used as a substitute for compensation and cannot be linked to productivity."

**Bottom line:** company personnel should never be in a BYU lab as "volunteers." They belong there under a written agreement — visitor, SRA participant, or services contract — routed per the Legal Documents Policy, and subject to the IP Policy shop-rights clause quoted at the top, which lets BYU claim a perpetual royalty-free license over IP they develop using substantial university resources.

## 8. Recharge / service centers / external-rate sales — ❌ NOT IN THE SNAPSHOT

**Action:** this one has to go to Regulatory Accounting and Reporting directly. Ask (a) whether the 2005 Recharge Center Policy is current, (b) whether a Uniform Guidance-era replacement exists, and (c) what surcharge revenue may fund. Do not assume either way.

## 9. "Management plan," "Outside Activities Form," "Faculty Profile" — searched all 195

**No plan template exists anywhere in the library.** "management plan" appears in six policies: Conflict of Time or Interest, Conflict of Interest, Financial Conflict of Interest in Research, Intellectual Property, Nepotism, and Risk Management and Safety.

*(Snapshot artifact worth knowing: [`conflict-of-interest-policy`](../html/conflict-of-interest-policy.html) and [`conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy`](../html/conflict-of-time-or-interest-policy.html) are byte-identical — policy.byu.edu serves the same page at both slugs. Not two policies.)*

**The Conflict of Time or Interest Policy (Approved 7 Apr 2025) confirms the Faculty Outside Activities Form claim verbatim** and gives the full employment-side route:

> "Full-time faculty members must disclose potential Conflicts, current Conflicts, and Outside Activities using the **Faculty Outside Activities Form in the Annual Review section of the Faculty Profile System**. The full-time faculty member and department chair or dean meet annually in conjunction with the annual performance review ... If a Conflict is identified, the employee and chair or dean collaborate to develop a written plan describing how the Conflict will be reduced, eliminated, or appropriately managed. **The chair submits the conflict management plan to the dean.** If the dean notes any concerns, the chair may be directed to create a revised plan with the employee. **The dean submits all approved management plans through the Faculty Profile System.** The associate academic vice president – faculty development is responsible for training chairs and deans to monitor Conflicts or Potential Conflicts in their units. When complex conflicts of interest exist, particularly when a college functions as a single academic unit without departments, chairs and deans should consult with the associate academic vice president – faculty development."

**Required elements**, such as they are — this is the only "what goes in a plan" language on the employment side:

> "Management plans should be tailored to an employee's position, Conflicts, and potential Conflicts. These plans may include any actions sufficient to avoid, eliminate, or ensure against Conflicts or the appearance of Conflicts, including recusal from discussions or decisions if a potential Conflict exists."
> "If an employee and the employee's department chair or supervisor are unable to agree on a plan to resolve or manage a Conflict of Interest, the matter is referred to the next level of management for resolution."

**Two clauses that map onto our draft one-for-one.** The four-days-per-month cap (§3.3), with the approver named as the **chair alone**:

> "Unless otherwise provided by contract, a faculty member may (with prior written approval of the department chair) spend up to four days per month on Secondary Employment while under contract. If these approved activities are carried out during the workweek (Monday–Friday), there is an inherent expectation that the faculty member will not forego core functions of their faculty duties and will devote equivalent time on adjacent weekends and/or in the evenings to compensate for time away from their faculty responsibilities."

And the teaching-priority guardrail (§3.5), which is exactly the test a chair or dean applies:

> "Faculty members are expected to be accessible to students, both by keeping regular office hours and by being available in person at other times on campus. **Department chairs or deans do not approve activities they believe would unduly limit a faculty member's availability to students or compromise a faculty member's teaching, scholarship, or citizenship performance.**"

**On the research side, the [FCOI Policy](../html/financial-conflict-of-interest-in-research-policy.html) (Approved 7 Apr 2025)** matches what we already had, and adds specifics worth citing: the plan is investigator-proposed ("If a potential FCOI appears likely, or at the university's request, the Investigator must submit a proposed Management Plan with the Disclosure Form"), developed with RAO, approved by AAVP–R; the conditions list runs public disclosure → disclosure to human subjects → independent monitor → modification of the research plan → ... → "severance of relationships that create financial conflicts"; "**The department chair is responsible for monitoring Investigator compliance with the Management Plan through the completion of the project**"; the review chain is "the department chair, the Dean's Office, the RAO director, and the AAVP – R"; retrospective review is by a panel of "the AAVP – R, RAO director, the department chair, and the associate dean" and "is conducted within 120 days of the university's determination of noncompliance"; appeals run to the AAVP–R, who empanels an "ad hoc University Research Conflict of Interest Review Committee," with the AVP's decision final.

**Two concrete asks that came out of this.** First, the policy references "**Appendix A – Conflict of Interest Management Plan Approval and Appeal Process**" — that appendix is the closest thing to a BYU-authored process diagram and is not on the page; request it from RAO. Second, the NIH reporting section is effectively BYU's own de facto plan checklist, and I'd restructure our §§ to use these five headings verbatim:

> "a description of the key elements of the university's Management Plan, including
> • role and principal duties of the conflicted Investigator in the research project,
> • conditions of the Management Plan,
> • how the Management Plan is designed to safeguard objectivity in the research project,
> • confirmation of the Investigator's agreement to the Management Plan,
> • how the Management Plan is monitored to ensure Investigator compliance, and
> • other information as needed."

Finally, the [Nepotism Policy](../html/nepotism-policy.html) (Approved 18 Nov 2024) defines a distinct instrument — "**Near Relative Management Plan** means a written statement that outlines conditions that apply to prevent Nepotism between BYU Employees who are Near Relatives in a Close Employment Relationship," created by the hiring supervisor and "approved by the dean or director." Not applicable unless relatives are involved, but worth knowing the term is taken.

## 10. Third-party equipment in university space / lab-space criteria — ❌ NOT IN THE SNAPSHOT

Searched for `non-owned`, `borrowed`, `loaned`, `equipment loan`, `third party equipment`, `lab space`, `space allocation`, `space assignment`. **Nothing in the 195 addresses third-party-owned equipment residing long-term in university space, and no policy states criteria for lab-space assignment.** Space assignment is evidently a college/department discretionary matter, not policy-governed.

What does exist, and who to ask:

> [**Scheduling and Use of BYU Facilities Policy**](../html/scheduling-and-use-of-byu-facilities-policy.html) (Approved 16 Dec 2019) — four sentences in total: "**The Campus Planning and Use Committee is ultimately responsible for the use of all university space.** Space Management manages permanently assigned space in campus facilities for academic and church purposes and coordinates all Sunday space assignments ... Academic Class Scheduling in the Registrar's Office schedules all permanent classroom space ... The Campus Scheduling Office ... coordinates requests for indoor and outdoor campus event space from on-campus and off-campus groups."

So: **Campus Planning and Use Committee** is the approving body and **Space Management** the operating office for permanently assigned space.

> [**Physical Facilities Policy**](../html/physical-facilities-policy.html) (Approved 16 Apr 2009 [Revised 4 Aug 2021]) — the clauses most likely to bite on an equipment install: "**Contact with architects, planners, engineers, designers and interior decorators is to be made ONLY through the Physical Facilities Division**"; "Construction projects on campus involving outside contractors **are required to be managed by the Construction Department**"; "If a project estimate is over $5000 the requested work must be approved as a priority of the dean/director and vice president"; and on storage, "Departments should refer all requests for storage space to the University Storage Committee, c/o Space Management. Any use of off-campus storage is discouraged and must be reported to Space Management."

Meaning: utility hookups, benching, or ventilation for placed equipment go through Physical Facilities, not through the vendor's installer.

The nearest thing to a rule about outside parties using university resources is the IP Policy shop-rights clause quoted at the top of this document.

**Action:** ask Space Management and the Campus Planning and Use Committee whether a written space/equipment-placement agreement form exists. Whatever it is, its signature tier is set by the Legal Documents Policy table in No. 6.

## 11. Insuring borrowed equipment / liability for external users — ❌ LARGELY NOT IN THE SNAPSHOT

There is **no standalone insurance policy** in the 195. The [Risk Management and Safety Policy](../html/risk-management-and-safety-policy.html) (Approved 6 Oct 2025 [Revised 9 Jun 2026] — the most recently revised policy in the entire snapshot) is the only source, and it is about *who decides*, not about coverage terms. **Nothing on insuring non-owned or borrowed equipment; nothing on liability for external users of university facilities.**

The three operative sentences:

> "RMSD coordinates the university's risk management efforts ... including in the following areas: environmental management; emergency preparedness; **contractual risk transfer and insurance**; fire prevention and life safety; **claim management**; **academic and laboratory safety**; occupational safety and industrial hygiene; **event risk and safety review**; minor protection; drone permitting."

> "**Unless approved in writing by the responsible vice president or otherwise provided for in university policy, only RMSD is authorized to purchase insurance or security bonds on behalf of campus units.** Units who need insurance or bonds submit requests directly to RMSD."

> "Any liability claim against the university should be reported immediately to RMSD or to the Office of General Counsel. **Outside of RMSD and the Office of General Counsel, no university employee is authorized to respond to a claim or assertion of legal liability made against the university or to make any statement or admission of liability on behalf of the university.**"

Plus: "College deans, department chairs, division directors, and other unit leaders are responsible for risk management and safety issues within their respective organizations," and each employee must report "any known injury, accident, property loss, safety concern, hazard, and/or near-miss" through the RMSD Incident Reporting System.

**Where the real hooks live is the Legal Documents Policy**, not a risk policy: RMS must approve any "commitment by the university to obtain insurance or to provide proof of insurance" (row 15), all indemnify/defend/hold-harmless language (row 11, with OGC), and any "agreement relating to an activity that presents a risk of substantial loss or liability (e.g., risk of bodily injury, property loss, data breach, etc.)" (row 14, with OGC). Separately, the Volunteer Policy routes non-employees in hazardous or PPE settings through RMS-issued Volunteer Agreements.

**Practical consequence for the plan:** loss/damage allocation for placed equipment must be written into the agreement itself and routed to RMS early — a department cannot buy its own coverage, and RMS is the only office that can commit BYU to insurance at all.

## 12. Supplemental Research Compensation for Externally Sponsored Work

[Supplemental Research Compensation for Externally Sponsored Work Policy](../html/supplemental-research-compensation-for-externally-sponsored-work-policy.html) — Approved 14 Nov 2011 [Revised 6 Apr 2021]. **Hours-log requirement, verbatim:**

> "Participating faculty members are required to request approval of the activity funded by supplemental compensation **prior to commencing work** and **keep a log of the hours and days they are engaged in supplemental work** for which they will request supplemental compensation. **Time periods devoted to supplemental research must be kept separate from the time devoted to regular university activities.**"

**CFS-only eligibility — confirmed, verbatim:**

> "The option for supplemental compensation is intended to apply to **regular, full-time faculty in CFS-track positions**. Supplemental compensation is not available to part-time or temporary personnel."

Four more constraints worth carrying into the plan:

> "Supplemental research compensation can result when an externally funded research project has a need for work that cannot be met by the faculty principal investigator, requiring assistance from **another faculty member in another department who is not a co-principal investigator or co-investigator** on the sponsored research project."
> "Supplemental compensation for sponsored research is **limited to an average of one day per week** and must be accomplished in such fashion that the five-day full-time appointment of the faculty is not compromised."
> "The compensation rate is calculated from that faculty member's **8-month base** found in their university contract."
> "**Work for supplemental compensation may not be done during a buy-out period** on the same or any other sponsored research project, nor may it be done or compensated for during a period outside of the faculty member's academic contract appointment."

**One tension to resolve with Faculty Relations:** this policy's *one day per week* supplemental cap and the Conflict of Time or Interest Policy's *four days per month* Secondary Employment cap govern different things (BYU-paid overload vs. outside work) but both consume the same finite time. Ask whether they stack, and whether the same "make it up on evenings and weekends" expectation applies to both. Both policies use nearly identical make-up language, which suggests they were drafted as parallel ceilings — but that is inference, not text.
