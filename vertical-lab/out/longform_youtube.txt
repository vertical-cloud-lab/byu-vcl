A design study of an automated alloy lab at BYU's Vertical Cloud Lab, in one video: two Kardex Shuttle XP 500 vertical lift modules holding the instruments on powered trays, each tray with its own UPS and a Stäubli CombiTac coupling that docks in its slot and at the opening; two UR10e arms on MiR250 mobile bases; the alloy route from charge cups through atomizing, sieving, printing, cutting, heat treatment and tensile tests to sample prep and characterization; the atomizer's turnaround automated; maintenance booked into the schedule; and what it would take to go from one alloy a day to ten.

It is a 3-D animation, not footage of a working lab: built from makers' published sizes and photos, with our own designs where nothing exists yet, and every arm move checked for collisions frame by frame. Real footage, photos and data are badged as such; everything else on screen is computed or illustrative. The narration is a synthetic voice (Kokoro, an open text-to-speech model).

Code, notes and sources: https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/tree/28c80fa/vertical-lab
PR: https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/pull/318

20 of the clips are earlier 720p renders from the same code, scaled up; the rest were rendered at 1920 x 1080 for this video.
