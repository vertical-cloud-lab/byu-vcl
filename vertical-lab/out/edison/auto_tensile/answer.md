Question: What commercial and research systems automate tensile testing of metal specimens, including robotic specimen loading and unloading (e.g. Instron AT2/AT3/AT6 automated testing systems, ZwickRoell roboTest, Shimadzu), specimen magazines, automatic extensometers, and automated elevated-temperature tensile testing with furnaces or environmental chambers? How are specimens fed, gripped, centered and removed, and what throughputs are reported? Include high-throughput tensile testing of additively manufactured small specimens.

# Automated tensile testing of metal specimens

**The clearest documented routes to unattended specimen exchange are robotic transfer of individual coupons, indexed racks of small specimens, and carousels that move preassembled holders into a furnace.** These are distinct from a computer-controlled tensile frame or an automatically positioned extensometer: neither accessory, by itself, loads and removes specimens. The strongest quantified metal-tensile example found is Sandia’s research rack-feed system, at approximately **25 tests per hour**; model-specific commercial hourly rates could not be verified from the retrieved sources. (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 2-3, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 4-5, kohyama1992r&d pages 1-4)

## Commercial systems and specimen workflow

**Instron AT2, AT3 and AT6.** These are relevant automated-testing product names, and a published thesis identifies Instron’s **AT6 six-axis robotic automated testing system** and ZwickRoell’s **roboTest R** vendor pages. The retrieved material does **not** contain the AT2/AT3/AT6 specifications needed to establish how each model feeds, centers, grips or clears metal specimens, its magazine capacity, compatible extensometer, or tests-per-hour rate. Those details should not be inferred from “automated” in the product name. (bosio2025automatedmaterialtestinga pages 36-42)

**ZwickRoell roboTest.** A published metal-additive-manufacturing application used an **AllroundLine Z100 equipped with roboTest L** to test DIN 50125 A4×20 AlSi10Mg0.5 tensile specimens. Across 50 printing-parameter combinations it obtained **148 valid tensile results**, including yield strength, ultimate strength and elongation. This establishes use of the commercial robotic system for metal coupons, but the article does not specify its magazine geometry, centering or grip sequence, broken-half disposal, or hourly rate; 148 is a *study total*, not a throughput claim. The cited roboTest R vendor page identifies another configuration, not evidence that L and R use identical hardware. (funcke2026greyboxmodeling pages 4-5, bosio2025automatedmaterialtestinga pages 36-42)

**Shimadzu Autograph AG-X and accessories.** The manufacturer’s catalog specifies non-shift wedge grips for **metals**, controller-operable pneumatic-grip options, and the **SIE-560S Auto Extensometer**, which sets its gauge-marker positions and attaches to and removes itself from the specimen automatically. Its DVE video extensometer can measure through an environmentally sealed chamber. The **TCE thermostatic chamber is rated −70 to +280 °C**, and an in-chamber tensile fixture is described. The catalog does **not** demonstrate a robotic coupon magazine, automatic centering and fragment removal, or unattended tests per hour; automatically actuated grips and extensometry must not be presented as proof of a fully robotic Shimadzu loading line. (testerUnknownyearautographagxseries pages 9-12, testerUnknownyearautographagxseries pages 12-14, testerUnknownyearautographagxseries pages 14-16)

## Documented ways to feed, align and clear specimens

* **Indexed, as-printed metal racks—Sandia Generation II.** Laser-powder-bed-fused 316L specimens are manufactured as **25-coupon monolithic arrays**; as many as ten arrays, or **250 coupons, fit on a build plate**. Each miniature coupon has an approximately **1 × 1 × 4 mm reduced section** and a **45° dovetail gripping section**. An indexed, single-axis motor stage advances the rack so the next specimen occupies the tensile axis, rather than a six-axis robot picking up each coupon. After fracture, pressurized gas ejects material from the *upper* grip, that grip lowers, and the rack advances; the paper does not describe a separate robotic retrieval of both halves. Cameras determine width and thickness and provide noncontact digital-image-correlation strain. The authors report **420 tests**, approximately **25 tests/hour**, and capacity for **75 mounted specimens**, about ten times the rate of their conventional comparison. The rack, coupon and testing-stage layouts are shown in the retrieved figure crops. (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 3-4, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 2-3, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 4-5, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile media 837c562a, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile media 24273a69)
* **Preassembled holders indexed into a hot zone—MATRON.** For miniature irradiated-metal coupons, bowl/line feeders, vacuum tweezers and a pneumatic screwdriver successively supply **pins, washers, specimen, spacers and screws** to a holder. A **12-position turntable** aligns each loaded holder with the test axis; a push rod raises it into an electric furnace, and the holder converts push-rod motion to tension. Reverse handling collects broken specimens and hardware separately in baskets. The roughly **10 minutes per specimen** reported for the approximately 60-step *holder-assembly* procedure is **not** a complete hot-tensile-test cycle time. (kohyama1992r&d pages 1-4, kohyama1992r&d pages 4-5)
* **Individual-coupon cobot handling—developmental Volvo cell.** An ABB cobot’s padded jaws handle a DIC-painted specimen without contacting the sensitive gauge surface. The coupon is secured in the **lower machine grip first**; the upper grip approaches and closes while crosshead position is adjusted to avoid unintended compression. After fracture, a distance-laser-guided approach finds and removes the lower and upper pieces separately despite variable break location. The basic tensile cycle worked, but continuous-operation storage/pickup, DIC calibration and safety remained unfinished; **no tensile throughput was reported**. The same project’s completed *bending*, not tensile, cell averaged **176 seconds per specimen** with a 12-sample disposal rack. Its inclined measurement station let gravity register bending coupons against a corner before precise placement—an example of centering that should not be attributed to its unfinished tensile cell. (bosio2025automatedmaterialtestinga pages 20-26, bosio2025automatedmaterialtestinga pages 26-29, bosio2025automatedmaterialtestinga pages 29-36, bosio2025automatedmaterialtestinga pages 36-42)

## Elevated-temperature automation: what has been demonstrated

**MATRON** is an integrated example: its carousel runs **12 successive miniature-tensile specimens** under vacuum or inert gas, with an electric furnace permitting tests up to **600 °C**. Temperature programs and loading rates vary, so its magazine capacity does not imply a fixed tests-per-hour rate. **Shimadzu’s −70 to +280 °C chamber** documents environmental tensile capability, but not robotic exchange *inside* that chamber. Separately, a 2026 Argonne/APS report describes a **1,000 °C-rated split-tube furnace** on a tensile frame still being commissioned and UR5 demonstrations of picking up, installing and discarding small specimens, including vacuum-tweezer pickup. Those are **separate developments**, not a demonstrated unattended 1,000 °C robotic tensile-testing line. (kohyama1992r&d pages 1-4, kohyama1992r&d pages 4-5, testerUnknownyearautographagxseries pages 14-16, zhang2026fy26progressreport pages 21-26)

**Practical comparison:** for standard metal coupons, verify the *particular commercial configuration’s* magazine, centering/grip interface, extensometer retraction, fracture-clearance and chamber-access sequence before assigning a cycle time. For miniature AM coupons, printing mechanically indexed arrays can bypass individual robotic pickup and has a directly measured ~25-tests/hour result. For furnace work, distinguish an automatically controlled hot test from automated **exchange of specimens at temperature**; among the sources examined, MATRON documents both sequential hot testing and the holder-feeding/removal machinery. (heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 2-3, heckman2020automatedhighthroughputtensile pages 4-5, kohyama1992r&d pages 1-4, kohyama1992r&d pages 4-5)
