Question: What happens to precision laboratory instruments when they are moved routinely rather than left in place: analytical and micro balances, optical microscopes and profilometers, tabletop SEMs, X-ray diffractometers and XRF, nanoindenters and hardness testers, laser-diffraction particle sizers, potentiostats. Shock and vibration limits in transport versus in operation, loss of levelling or calibration after a move and the requalification or settling and warm-up time needed (for example balance guidance in USP <41> and <1251>, OIML R 76, manufacturers' relocation guidance), and evidence from instruments mounted on moving platforms such as mobile laboratories, ships, vehicles, AGVs or automated storage systems.

# Routine relocation of precision laboratory instruments

**Bottom line.** Moving an instrument does not necessarily damage it, but it changes the conditions under which its performance was established. The immediate risks are loss of level or mounting geometry, vibration-sensitive readings, thermal drift after disconnection or a change of room, and damage to unsecured mechanisms. A successful shipment test demonstrates survival in its *specified packaging and mounting*, not accuracy while rolling on a cart or operating on a vibrating vehicle. IEC 60068-2-47 explicitly treats packaged and unpackaged dynamic tests differently because packaging and restraint change the transmitted shock and vibration; it supplies no universal permissible acceleration for the instruments in this question. (standard2006testing— pages 8-10, standard2006testing— pages 4-8)

## What the evidence implies by instrument

| Instrument | Consequence of routine moves; check before reuse |
|---|---|
| **Analytical and microbalances** | A different bench can change level, vibration, airflow and temperature, increasing scatter or bias without any broken part. Re-level, allow the manufacturer-specified thermal warm-up, then check with traceable weights **at the new location**. A balance review gives a model-dependent **1–24 h** installation warm-up, not a universal post-move waiting period, and recommends recalibration after relocation. Microbalances warrant particular care with vibration. (holberg2022analyticaltechniquesused pages 7-7, morse2004laboratorybalanceshow pages 3-4, tiwari2025anelectronicanalytical pages 2-3, tiwari2025anelectronicanalytical pages 4-5, tiwari2025anelectronicanalytical pages 5-7) |
| **Optical microscopes and profilometers** | Transport can disturb focus, stage registration or measurement geometry; operating vibration and temperature changes can corrupt fine surface profiles. Check a stage micrometer or dimensional reference, and an appropriate flat/step-height/sphere standard for the profilometer, after its reading stabilizes. In one *on-machine confocal* study, displacement drift was about **200 nm over 24 h**, with temperature effects during roughly the first **5 h**; these are experimental observations, **not** relocation specifications for all microscopes. (duan2021profilemeasurementusing pages 3-5, duan2021atrialtoward pages 2-4) |
| **Tabletop SEMs** | Small size alone does not establish shock tolerance or accurate imaging during motion. Check the vacuum system, stage travel, beam alignment and a suitable imaging/magnification reference after transport; pump-induced vibration and stage oscillation are documented design concerns. The retrieved tabletop-SEM work provides **no validated transport-shock limit or post-move pump-down time**. (kuzyk2024lowcostscanningelectron pages 26-31, kuzyk2024lowcostscanningelectron pages 100-104, kuzyk2024lowcostscanningelectron pages 18-23) |
| **XRD and XRF** | Recheck X-ray enclosure/interlocks and instrument status, then diffraction peak positions/intensities against a reference or XRF response against an appropriate certified material before releasing data. Shipboard XRD has required correction of peak position using quartz at **3.343 Å** and analysis of standard mineral mixtures; shipboard XRF used standards and matrix correction. Such checks demonstrate performance in that installation, not that every benchtop unit is safe or accurate while in transit. Field X-ray work also requires radiation-safety controls. (whitley2012practicalapplicationof pages 80-85, saffer2017expedition365methods pages 16-17, saffer2017expedition365methods pages 15-16) |
| **Nanoindenters and hardness testers** | Re-establish specimen/indenter mounting, force and displacement performance, noise and drift, and test a reference material. An indentation guide describes **10–20 s** for *stage-move transients* to decay before estimating drift; this is **not** a waiting period after transporting the complete instrument. ISO 14577-2 calibration concerns include frame compliance and indenter area function. For **Rockwell** testers, an account of **ASTM E18-07** explicitly says *moving the machine triggers direct verification* of forces, depth measurement, hysteresis and test cycle; daily and periodic indirect verification are separate checks. Confirm requirements against the applicable current edition. (jennett2007anintroductionto pages 35-39, jennett2007anintroductionto pages 39-42, maculotti2025metrologicalcomparisonof pages 4-6, lowUnknownyearrecentchangesand pages 4-5) |
| **Laser-diffraction particle sizers** | Check optics/background and dispersion-system integrity, then verify the *actual wet or dry method* with an appropriate particle-size reference material. Certified materials **ERM-FD066/FD069** support this kind of QC. A metrology study says user calibration of the complete laser-diffraction measurement is not generally required, while underlying geometric/optical inputs require traceability; verification is not the same as automatically recalibrating after every move. Its test of *reference-material bottles* transported up to **1,000 km** found no significant size shift—this does **not** test instrument shock survival. (linsinger2019preparationandcharacterisation pages 21-25, linsinger2019preparationandcharacterisation pages 10-13, linsinger2019preparationandcharacterisation pages 25-28) |
| **Potentiostats** | The electronics do not have a balance-like gravity/levelling measurement, but connections, electrical noise and the electrochemical cell can change. After moving, verify the operating modes and impedance against a resistor–capacitor reference, followed by a relevant electrochemical control; do not assume a wearable/portable device validates a benchtop cell during vibration. One miniature EIS instrument was tested with known RC networks and compared with a commercial potentiostat over **100 Hz–25 kHz**. No general post-move warm-up or shock rating follows from that test. (franulovic2410aminiaturepotentiostat pages 25-28, vilasocadre2024recentadvanceson pages 3-4) |

## Balance standards and the meaning of “recalibrate”

A published quotation of **USP <41>** gives the repeatability criterion as twice the standard deviation divided by the nominal test load not exceeding **0.10%**. Thus the repeatability-derived minimum *net* weight is **2s/0.001 = 2,000s**; the reviewed procedure uses at least **10 weighings** and substitutes **0.41d** for a smaller measured standard deviation, where *d* is readability. This is a suitability test for the intended weighing, **not** permission to move a balance without checking it, and minimum weight is not the mass of the tared vessel. Guidance on location, levelling and qualification is operationally relevant to **USP <1251>**, but the retrieved sources do not establish a specific USP-mandated number of minutes to wait after relocation. One balance review calls for recalibration after relocation while distinguishing that event from minor levelling or built-in-weight adjustment. Here, *recalibration* means establishing measurement performance/traceability; a built-in adjustment alone is not a substitute for independent verification where the quality system requires it. (tiwari2025anelectronicanalytical pages 5-7, 加藤洋2015gwp®(goodweighingpractice)機器のライフサイクルマネージメントを効率的かつ科学的根拠より確立された計量器管理のグローバルスタンダード pages 4-7)

**OIML R 76 should not be turned into a generic transport rating.** The primary R 76 text and model-specific manufacturer relocation manuals were not established in the retrieved evidence, so no tilt angle, vibration acceleration, transport *g*-value or warm-up duration is attributed to them here. Obtain the applicable edition and the exact model’s installation, transport-lock, packaging and service instructions before specifying such limits. IEC 60068-2-47 makes clear why an approval test of a packaged product cannot simply be applied to an unpackaged instrument strapped to an AGV. (standard2006testing— pages 8-10, standard2006testing— pages 4-8)

## What moving-platform demonstrations actually show

**Transport and then measure:** A review distinguishes conventional *transportable* laboratory spectrometers, which require installation after moving, from purpose-designed mobile instruments intended to work after simpler deployment. A maritime portable-XRD study contemplated measurements with the vessel **pierside or in dry dock**, specifically to minimize rolling—not proof of accurate XRD measurements at sea. (whitley2012practicalapplicationof pages 80-85, vandenabeele2016mobilespectroscopicinstrumentation pages 1-2)

**Measure aboard a moving platform:** A research vessel used a **LISST 100X** laser-diffraction sensor continuously underway in a flow-through water circuit, with regular cleaning and field checks. Ocean-drilling expeditions also operated shipboard XRD/XRF with reference-based corrections. Curiosity has carried the purpose-designed **CheMin** XRD over more than **30 km** and analyzed many samples; it has sealed onboard standards and intentionally vibrates *the sample cell* to improve powder diffraction. These examples demonstrate engineered installations and ongoing QA, **not** that a conventional tabletop XRD can produce valid results while a vehicle is driving. (saffer2017expedition365methods pages 15-16, blake2024thechemistryand pages 4-7, bumke2012theexpeditionof pages 16-18, vaniman2013chemininstrumentperformance pages 1-2, bish2014thefirstxray pages 2-4)

**Move samples, leave instruments installed:** In a documented robotic bioprocess workflow, the mobile laboratory assistant transported **96-well plates to a fixed analyzer**. A ferry-emissions study likewise collected filters on the vessel but weighed them on a **Cahn C-32 microbalance in the land-based laboratory** before and after the voyage; it does not prove that ferry vibration *necessarily prevents* onboard weighing. These are important counterexamples to interpreting an AGV or mobile laboratory as routinely relocating its most sensitive analyzer. (kaspersetz2022automatedbioprocessfeedback pages 3-5, thompson2004reductionofemissions pages 2-3)

## A defensible routine-move protocol

1. **Before transport:** record an as-found reference result; follow model-specific shutdown, restraints, orientation and packaging instructions; secure samples, fluids, optics, stages and cables. Define a route-specific shock/vibration exposure if repeated cart or AGV moves are proposed. Packaged-shipment qualification must not be substituted for unprotected-cart qualification. (standard2006testing— pages 8-10, standard2006testing— pages 4-8)
2. **At the destination:** inspect for damage or leaks, secure the instrument on its intended stable support, restore required level and environmental services, and wait for the *documented model-specific* warm-up or for measured thermal/vacuum/noise stability. Avoid imposing one settling time on all devices. (holberg2022analyticaltechniquesused pages 6-6, tiwari2025anelectronicanalytical pages 4-5, duan2021profilemeasurementusing pages 3-5, jennett2007anintroductionto pages 39-42)
3. **Before releasing measurements:** repeat the pre-move performance check under the new conditions—weights and repeatability/minimum weight for balances; dimensional or image references for optical/SEM systems; peak/element standards for XRD/XRF; drift and reference indents or hardness blocks for mechanical testers; certified size distribution for laser diffraction; and electrical plus cell controls for potentiostats. Escalate failures to adjustment, calibration or service. **ASTM E18-07’s direct-verification requirement after moving a Rockwell tester is a specific documented exception to any assumption that a quick check is always enough.** (tiwari2025anelectronicanalytical pages 5-7, duan2021profilemeasurementusing pages 3-5, maculotti2025metrologicalcomparisonof pages 4-6, lowUnknownyearrecentchangesand pages 4-5, linsinger2019preparationandcharacterisation pages 21-25, saffer2017expedition365methods pages 15-16, franulovic2410aminiaturepotentiostat pages 25-28)

**Evidence limit:** The cited studies provide illustrative performance and operating practices, but not a comparable, model-by-model dataset of repeated-move failure rates, OEM transport-versus-operating vibration limits, or compulsory requalification times for all the listed instruments. Numerical shock limits and release criteria must therefore be taken from each exact instrument’s documentation and validated for the actual platform and measurement uncertainty, rather than inferred from its size or the fact that it was once shipped successfully. (standard2006testing— pages 8-10, kuzyk2024lowcostscanningelectron pages 26-31, whitley2012practicalapplicationof pages 80-85)
