- **Measured tray vibration (informal field tests, not vendor data): Kardex VLM at a dealer (Johnstone Equipment), handheld Reed R7000SD meter magnet-mounted on the tray, 1-s logging. Three repeat runs logged acceleration maxima of 1.3, 4.3 and 1.0 m/s²; a dealer-supplied run of the same kind logged velocity up to 17 mm/s (95th percentile 7.4 mm/s, median 0.3 mm/s, 509 s). Modula and "MSK" VLMs tested later: maxima 0.4 and 15.3 m/s², the tester saying the largest peak came from lifting the meter by hand. Tester's notes: vibration is strongest when the tray moves in and out of the opening, "not really when going up or down"; a tray in storage read ~0 m/s²; an OpenFlexure microscope on the tray stayed in focus and usable; a dealer said speed can be reduced at order to cut vibration** — Acceleration Consortium, public GitHub issue ac-dev-lab #216 "vertical lift module - vibration testing" (Apr-Nov 2025; closed "VLM no longer being ordered") — https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/ac-dev-lab/issues/216 ; raw data https://github.com/user-attachments/files/22660571/organized_data.csv and https://github.com/user-attachments/files/22350448/vertical-lab-vibration-data-VBA01001.xlsx — "The VLM vibrates with the most intensity when going in and out of the open port, not really when going up or down." / "The speed of the VLM can be reduced to reduce vibration by the manufacturer upon order." — VERIFIED (issue text and raw files read this pass; maxima, percentiles and the run-to-machine mapping are my arithmetic and inference from timestamps and comments, DERIVED). Caveats: 1 Hz logging under-samples short peaks; the meter's clock reads 2024 while the posts are 2025; whether values are peak or RMS and the filter band are not recorded; the first data file's VLM make is not stated in its comment.
- **Acceleration / jerk / vibration: NOT PUBLISHED by any VLM vendor found (Kardex, Hänel, Modula, SSI, Weland, ICAM, Vidir, Ferretto); the only measurements found are the informal tests in the bullet above** — searched again this pass ("vertical lift module" acceleration m/s²); the only m/s² values found were an assumed 3 m/s² lift acceleration in an academic simulation (Tavira et al. UNVERIFIED for every vendor: would need a written answer from Kardex.
- **Modula, SSI LogiMat, ICAM, Vidir, Ferretto publish no travel speeds at all** — EARLIER PASS (catalogues read in full).

- **Table A.1 (2020 ed.), row 1a: clearance > 500 mm on both sides and ahead, personnel detection active → maximum speed = the truck's "Rated speed", zone "Operating"; row 1b: same clearances, detection muted → 0.3 m/s, zone "Operating hazard"** — TÜV Rheinland white paper "ISO 3691-4:2020 A Standard for Automated Guided Vehicles" (2021), Fig. 5 extract of Table A.1 — https://tuv-rheinlandprod-media.e-spirit.cloud/f117df9a-386d-4f14-b01c-221f621297b3/example-media/industrial-lifecycle/tuv-rheinland-automatic-guided-vehicles-whitepaper-en_neu.pdf — "1a > 500 > 500 > 500 Active Rated speed Operating" / "1b Muted 0,3 m/s Operating hazard" — RE-VERIFIED (PDF text, earlier-extracted layer); notified body quoting the standard. Other rows (clearance < 500 mm) were not reproduced by any source found.
- **Personnel detection must stop the truck before contact with a *stationary* person; people stepping into the path are out of scope** — same TÜV paper — "trucks shall stop before contact between the rigid parts of the truck or load and a stationary person" / "Humans approaching the truck or stepping sideways into the path of the truck or out of scope" (sic) — RE-VERIFIED (PDF text); notified body.

With detection active and 0.5 m clear each side, the limit is the maker's rated speed (MiR250: 2.0 m/s). — DERIVED from the TÜV extract above.
- **2023 edition (2nd) replaces 2020; adds terms "active detection field" and "operational stop"; revises Clauses 4-6 and Annexes A and B; Pilz says it sets the framework for higher speeds in restricted zones; whether the 0.3 m/s value changed was not confirmed** — Pilz news 28 Sep 2023 https://www.pilz.com/en-INT/company/news/articles/238928 and ISO/ANSI front matter — SNIPPET (pages return 403). UNVERIFIED on the speed values.
- **Clause structure 2023: speed control 4.3; detection of persons 4.8.2; "Annex A (normative) Requirements for preparation of the operating zones"** — DIN listing of ISO 3691-4:2023-06 contents — https://www.din.de/de/mitwirken/normenausschuesse/nam/veroeffentlichungen/wdc-beuth:din21:369736225/toc-3446828/download — EARLIER PASS (PDF).
- **MiR applies it: muted docking areas are "operating hazard zones", at least 1 m around, marked per EN 1525 and ISO 3691-4, no workstations inside** — https://mirsupport.netlify.app/manuals/guides/commissioning/mfe/assess_safety/operating_hazard_zones — "The operating hazard zone borders must be at least one meter from the identified hazard in all directions." — EARLIER PASS (page); vendor manual via mirror.
- **Does ISO 3691-4 apply to a mobile manipulator?** Per the R15.08 chair's 2022 slides, R15.08-1 says "AGVs with manipulators or Type C's with forks shall comply with B56/5 or ISO 3691-4" (sic; see C2). ISO 3691-4's own scope text on manipulators: UNVERIFIED (not retrievable).

### C3. ISO 10218-1/-2 (2011; revised Feb 2025) and ISO/TS 15066 (2016)

- **250 mm/s is the manual (teach-mode) reduced speed** — OSHA Technical Manual Sec. IV Ch. 4 — https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-4-safety-hazards/chapter-4 — "Robot speeds during these programming sessions are at a reduced speed, less than 10 inches (250 mm) per second." — RE-VERIFIED (page); regulator summarising the standards.
- **2025 edition: "reduced speed" is a defined safety function, max 250 mm/s, distinct from "monitored speed"** — Hartmann et al., arXiv 2602.17822 (2026 review comparing ISO 10218 2011 vs 2025) — https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17822 — "Reduced speed (Max. 250 mm/s)" — RE-VERIFIED (PDF text, earlier-extracted layer); academic review. A forum post quotes EN ISO 10218-2:2025 clause 5.5.6.1: "the speed of accessible moving parts shall be no greater than 250mm/s" (https://forum.universal-robots.com/t/reduced-speed-in-collaborative-mode/40188, 12 Jun 2025, RE-VERIFIED page; forum, weak), and a reply reports unguarded collaborative applications run at 650-700 mm/s after force measurement (same thread; anecdote).
- **2025 edition: Class I robot = mass per manipulator ≤ 10 kg, max TCP speed ≤ 250 mm/s, max force ≤ 50 N; everything else Class II; where the base moves, the mass includes the base, so "moving robots will mostly fall into Class II"** — Ferraro, Freda, Pera (INAIL), "New standards for industrial robots", ESREL-SRA-E 2025, doi 10.3850/978-981-94-3281-3_ESREL-SRA-E2025-P3635-cd — https://rpsonline.com.sg/proceedings/esrel-sra-e2025/pdf/ESREL-SRA-E2025-P3635.pdf — "maximum achievable speed of the tool center point is 250 mm/s or less" / "Then the moving robots will mostly fall into Class II" — VERIFIED (PDF text, new this pass); peer-reviewed proceedings by a national safety institute. DERIVED: a UR10e/UR12e (33.5 kg, 4 m/s) is Class II.
- **The 2025 ISO 10218 definitions cover manipulators "fixed in place or fixed to a mobile platform"** — same INAIL paper — "which can be either fixed in place or fixed to a mobile platform" — VERIFIED (PDF text, new this pass). But the Hartmann review says the 2025 scope "now also excludes mobility in terms of integrating robots into unmanned trucks or mobile platforms" (RE-VERIFIED PDF text). The two secondary readings point different ways; the standard itself was not read (UNVERIFIED).
- **Collaboration in 2025: "collaborative" is a property of the application, not the robot; methods are hand-guided control, speed and separation monitoring (SSM), power and force limiting (PFL); ISO/TS 15066 body-region limits become informative Annex M of ISO 10218-2:2025** — Hartmann et al. (URL above) — "the collaborative nature is not an attribute of the robot, but exclusively a property of the application" — EARLIER PASS (PDF text); academic review.
- **ISO/TS 15066 gives force/pressure limits per body region, not speeds: e.g. hand/fingers 140 N quasi-static, chest 140 N, face 65 N; transient contact allowed ×2 except the head** — DGUV FB HM-080 (08/2017), whose Table A.2 the TS adopted — https://www.dguv.de/medien/fb-holzundmetall/publikationen-dokumente/infoblaetter/infobl_englisch/080_collaborativerobotsystems.pdf — "The biomechanical limit values according to Table A.2 have been taken over in ISO TS 15066." — EARLIER PASS (PDF text); German statutory accident insurer.
- **For a UR10e striking a hand (280 N transient limit), the TS energy model allows 1.25 m/s if the hand is free but 0.28 m/s if clamped; measured forces exceeded the model by > 100 N** — Kirschner, Mansfeld, Abdolshah, Haddadin, arXiv 2203.02706 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.02706 — "the contact force was underestimated by more than 100 N" — EARLIER PASS (PDF text); peer-reviewed. DERIVED: arm speed near people with a clamping risk is well under 0.28 m/s for this arm family.
- **Speed and separation monitoring uses an assumed human approach speed of 1.6 m/s (walking) / 2.0 m/s (hand), from ISO 13855; US press-guarding constant 63 in/s (1.6 m/s)** — OSHA 29 CFR 1910.217 — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.217 — "Hs = Hand speed constant of 63 inches per second (1.6 m/s)." — EARLIER PASS (page); regulator. ISO 13855 K values are SNIPPET (Pilz, 403). This is the speed of the person assumed in safety-distance sums, not a robot speed limit.
- **ANSI/ITSDF B56.5 (cited by MiR, Thermo): stop before contact with test pieces (70 x 400 mm, 200 x 600 mm cylinders), with an explicit exception for "a pedestrian stepping into the path of a vehicle at the last instant"** — Bostelman, Hong, Eastman (NIST), CLAWAR 2014 — https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=915310 — EARLIER PASS (PDF text); government lab quoting the standard.
