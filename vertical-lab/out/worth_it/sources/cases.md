# Is a vertical lab worth it? Sourced cases (automated labs, VLMs/stores in labs and elsewhere, practitioner accounts)

Compiled 2026-11-27 (UTC), web only, for the BYU alloy-lab question: desktop instruments living on Kardex Shuttle XP 500 trays,
served at the access opening by a MiR250 + UR arm, versus the same instruments on benches served by one or two mobile manipulators.

## A. Facility-scale automated labs and how they arrange instruments

### A.1 Cloud labs (Emerald, CMU, Strateos/Lilly)

- **ECL (original South San Francisco site): ~15,000 sq ft, 100+ instruments on ordinary workbenches, moved and loaded by a few staff with trolleys; operators were mostly ex-Amazon logistics staff** — Guardian, 11 Sep 2022 (CMU reprint) — https://www.cmu.edu/ambassadors/december-2022/pdf/guardian_cloud-labs.pdf — "more than 100 items of high-end bioscience equipment whirr away on workbenches largely unmanned" / "just a few staff in blue coats quietly following instructions from screens on their trolleys" / "they are, in fact, mostly former Amazon employees" — press; CARRIED (read directly by sibling agent).
- **ECL keeps people because some instruments can't be automated, and robots can't lift carboys or unwrap mailed samples** — same Guardian piece — same URL — "there are a few instruments that just can't be automated" — press; CARRIED. The carboy/unwrapping point is SNIPPET (search summary of the same article).
- **ECL's own model is "hybrid": human operators interface with automated instruments** — Arias & Taylor (CMU), "Scientific Discovery at the Press of a Button", NSF public-access copy — https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10573763 — "a hybrid automation model where human operators interface with automated instruments to complete protocols" — peer-reviewed author copy; CARRIED.
- **Throughput limit at ECL as seen by an academic user: often one instrument per technique (queueing), not transport** — same — same URL — "it is not uncommon for there to only be one instrument capable of doing a specific procedure" — peer-reviewed author copy; CARRIED.
- **ECL failure modes include operator mishandling as well as software** — same — same URL — "critical errors that have resulted in the loss of valuable samples, due to both software issues and operator mishandling" — peer-reviewed author copy; CARRIED.
- **ECL Austin: announced as 105,000 sq ft and 230+ instrument types (company release, 2023); a Texas licensing record lists a 40,000 sq ft renovation at $5 M — unresolved conflict** — ECL / PR Newswire; TDLR — https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/emerald-cloud-lab-to-relocate-state-of-the-art-facility-from-south-san-francisco-to-austin-301758704.html ; https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/TABS/Search/Print/TABS2023016373 — "The 105,000-square-foot facility" — vendor marketing (release) + gov record (SNIPPET); CARRIED.
- **2015 tour of ECL: "orchestrators" (lab techs) with rolling carts, lab coats and bar-code readers move samples** — Bio-IT World, 28 Sep 2015 — https://bio-itworld.com/2015/9/28/tour-of-cloud-emerald-cloud-labs-hardware-software.aspx — "orchestrators" ... "with rolling carts, lab coats, and bar code readers" — trade press; SNIPPET (page 403; phrase from two search summaries).
- **CMU Cloud Lab: $40 M, planned ~20,000 sq ft (2021), reported 16,000 sq ft with ~6 technicians running 24 h (2022); ~200 instrument types** — WSJ 30 Aug 2021 and Guardian 2022 (CMU reprints) — https://www.cmu.edu/ambassadors/december-2021/pdf/wsj_emerald-cloud-labs.pdf ; https://www.cmu.edu/ambassadors/december-2022/pdf/guardian_cloud-labs.pdf — "a $40 million science laboratory" / "just half a dozen technicians helping the place run 24 hours a day" — press; CARRIED.

### A.2 Is a vertical/automated store ever used for *instruments* (rather than consumables) in a lab?

- **Closest lab precedent found: a 2015 US patent for a lab "vertical lift system" whose carriage holds shelves of *active equipment* kept connected to electricity, data, vacuum, liquid and gas through raceways that travel with the carriage** — New England Laboratory Casework Co. (assignee), US 8,939,296 B2 (filed 2 Aug 2011, granted 27 Jan 2015) — https://patents.google.com/patent/US8939296 — "Modular raceways 44 connect active equipment on shelves 28, 30 to the delivery of electricity" / "provides the advantage of knowing that the active equipment would not be disconnected" — patent (FETCHED). **No product found**: a search of NELC literature (2018 furniture selector, Millipore case study) shows no vertical-lift product (SNIPPET). It is a single carriage moving shelves up/down at a bench, not a multi-tray store.
- **Kardex's own lab-storage guide lists consumables, reagents, samples, slides, paraffin blocks and "water bath racks & equipment storage" — nothing about running instruments inside** — Kardex blog, 11 Apr 2025 — https://www.kardex.com/en-us/blog/automated-laboratory-storage — "reduce storage space requirements by up to 75%" / "reported up to 60% faster retrieval times" — vendor marketing (FETCHED). Named lab users: Merck (cold store built from three VLMs), Galderma.
- **University lab VLM case: Wuhan University, Modula NEXT, research-logistics store; 700 sq ft of storage on a 79 sq ft footprint** — Modula case study — https://modula.us/experience/wuhan-univeristy-case-study/ — "gained 700 sq ft of storage capacity while occupying a ground footprint of only 79 sq ft" — vendor marketing; SNIPPET (403 on fetch). The snippet says only "inventory" and "sensitive assets"; what exactly is stored was not verified, and nothing indicates running instruments.
- **Powered trays exist in two VLM brands, for charging or cold boxes, not instruments: Modula "Electrified Tray" (240 V socket, max 10 trays per lift); Hänel Lean-Lift trays with a rear plug-in contact so cold boxes (to -80 °C) stay powered "while in the lift"** — Modula US options price list (2020) and EU page; Hänel brochure — see vlm_vendors.md §1.0 for URLs — "These enable cold boxes to be put into storage and supplied with power while in the lift" (Hänel) — vendor; CARRIED (VERIFIED-PDF there). Kardex Shuttle: no on-tray power found (only a socket at the workstation). No amps/watts rating published by either vendor.
- **Closest deployed precedent for an *energized device living in a VLM*: Hänel Lean-Lift "electrified" containers keep refrigerated cold boxes (to -80 °C) powered continuously while stored in the lift; the access opening is enlarged to suit the refrigeration unit; one system stores donated blood (no customer, date or power rating given). Whether power holds during travel is not stated (rear plug-in contact suggests it drops in transit)** — Hänel case-study and news pages (news images dated ~Apr 2021) — https://www.haenel.es/en/case-studies/storage-down-to-80-c/ ; https://www.haenel.de/en/newsroom/news/refrigerated-storage-reaching-80-c-in-the-haenel-lean-lift — "supplied with power while in the lift, ensuring constant cooling of the stored items" / "The size of the access point on the Lean-Lift® is adapted to the respective refrigeration system" / "This system, for example, is uesd [sic] to store donated blood." — vendor (FETCHED). Note the device is a self-contained cooler that needs only mains power and no operator interaction while stored — unlike an SEM/XRD (vacuum, cooling water or chiller, X-ray interlocks, levelling, data cable) or a balance (vibration, levelling, warm-up).
- **No facility, product or paper was found (here or in case_studies.md §3) in which an analytical instrument rides a VLM tray, is presented at the opening and is operated or kept running while stored.** Partial analogues only: vertical-carousel patent with powered carriers for refrigeration/charging (Vidir US 11,910,917 B2, 2024); EV charging in pallet car parks (power at the storage bay, off in transit); battery-formation AS/RS; powered incubator/plate hotels where the *store* is powered and plates are passive — case_studies.md §3 — CARRIED. Absence in a limited search is not proof of absence.

### A.3 Self-driving / robotic materials labs: layout and what limits throughput

- **A-Lab (LBNL): three fixed stations (prep, heating, characterization) with UR5e arms, one on a linear rail; four box furnaces loaded in batches of up to eight; 353 experiments in 17 days (~21/day); exception rate ~3.9% over 1.5 years; XRD holders still cleaned by hand** — Szymanski et al., Nature 2023 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10700133 — "Heating is performed in batches, with each furnace containing up to eight samples on an alumina tray" / "The average exception rate across all stations in the A-Lab is about 3.9% over 1.5 years of operation" — peer-reviewed; CARRIED. Failures were chemistry/computation, not handling: "slow reaction kinetics, precursor volatility, amorphization and computational inaccuracy" (CARRIED). Press capacity claims (100-200 samples/day, 8 furnaces) disagree with the paper (SNIPPET).
- **A-Lab design lesson: stations stay hand-serviceable so a fault doesn't stop the lab** — same — same URL — "Hardware exceptions can be handled manually in the affected station without interrupting operations" — peer-reviewed; CARRIED.
- **Liverpool mobile robotic chemist (Burger et al. 2020): one KUKA KMR iiwa (~400 kg) among ordinary benches; 688 experiments in 8 days; worked 172 of 192 h; drove 2.17 km in 319 moves (driving ~1% of elapsed time, arithmetic from mobile_manipulators.md); GC analysis (232 min/batch) was rate-limiting; robot charged-but-idle ~32% of the time** — Burger et al., Nature 583:237 (2020), accepted manuscript — https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/74759/1/Burger_etal_Nature_2020_A_mobile_robotic.pdf — "The robot was charged but idle for approximately 32% of the time" / "then 232 minutes per batch to complete the GC analysis" — peer-reviewed; CARRIED (text extracted there).
- **Same robot over a 50-day development period: manipulating 32.6% of the time, idle 17 days, charging/waiting 16.5 days, in error 4.5%; robot-caused errors 3 in 5,201 samples; whole-workflow error ~0.5% per sample, dominated by liquid dispensing/capping (60% of error time), not the mobile robot (17%)** — Burger et al. 2020 Supplementary Information — https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41586-020-2442-2/MediaObjects/41586_2020_2442_MOESM1_ESM.pdf — "16.5 days either charging or waiting at the charging station" — peer-reviewed SI; CARRIED.
- **Liverpool solid-state workflow (Lunt et al., Chem. Sci. 2024): KUKA KMR iiwa + ABB YuMi + unmodified PXRD; ~9 h per rack of 8 of which 8 h is PXRD scanning; transport a small fraction** — https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10866346/fullTextXML — "The transport time between the stations is a small fraction of the overall workflow cycle time." — peer-reviewed; CARRIED.
- **Liverpool modular mobile robots (Dai et al., Nature 2024): instruments left where they stand and shared with people; >1 year of development and debugging; door actuators added to the synthesizer** — https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11602721/fullTextXML — "allows the instruments to be shared with other automated workflows or used by human researchers" / "required more than a year of development and debugging before it was stable enough" — peer-reviewed; CARRIED.
- **Where handling dominates (short instrument steps), a mobile robot was slower than a person: ARChemist 12 min per vial** — Fakhruldeen et al. 2022 — https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.13571 — "Compared to an experiment run by a human chemist, this is slower." — preprint/conference; CARRIED.
- **Self-driving labs' demonstrated unattended lifetime is short: two days unassisted, up to one month assisted** — Volk & Abolhasani, Nat. Commun. 2024 — https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10866889/fullTextXML — "the demonstrated unassisted lifetime is two days." — peer-reviewed review; CARRIED.
- **Biofoundries: capacity rarely fully used; maintenance contracts up to 15%/yr of purchase price; 5-10 staff for a small foundry; running costs an order of magnitude above a normal lab** — Holowko et al., Synthetic Biology 2020 — https://academic.oup.com/synbio/article/6/1/ysaa026/6030103 — "The throughput capacity of biofoundries is rarely fully utilized" / "Maintenance contracts for machines can also run as high as 15% of the original purchase cost per annum." — peer-reviewed; CARRIED.
- **Industrial metals/minerals labs (the most mature lab automation, 200+ FLSmidth QCX/RoboLab systems; Herzog; Polysius POLAB) all keep instruments fixed and move passive samples by central robot, rail shuttle, conveyor or pneumatic tube, and keep every unit operable by hand** — FLSmidth/Fuller; Foundry Management & Technology — https://www.fuller-technologies.com/products/qcx-robolab-systems — "Today, more than 200 QCX/RoboLab systems have been installed worldwide." / "manually operable units help the laboratory keep working while other system parts are serviced." — vendor marketing; CARRIED.

### A.4 More layouts (added after the first pass)

- **Samsung ASTRAL (inorganic powder synthesis; Chen et al., Nature Synthesis 3:606, 2024): one Franka Panda arm on a 2.32 m linear rail, stations (Quantos powder dosing, liquid handler, one Nabertherm box furnace loaded 24 crucibles per tray, a benchtop Rigaku MiniFlex XRD) arranged around the perimeter; ball milling and XRD sample prep still done by a person with batch jigs** — Supplementary Information, §SI2.1 — https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs44160-024-00502-y/MediaObjects/44160_2024_502_MOESM1_ESM.pdf — "centered around a flexible collaborative robot arm mounted on a linear rail" / "the powder mixing by the ASTRAL platform is not fully automated" / "preparation for the X-ray diffraction requires some manual sample preparation by a human researcher" — peer-reviewed SI (FETCHED; text extracted locally by me). 224 reactions for 35 targets in "a few weeks" (OSTI highlight, CARRIED).
- **Ginkgo RACs: one instrument + small arm per cart, carts joined by plate track; only the benchtop RACs are "stackable"; uptime claimed but not quantified** — Ginkgo — https://www.ginkgo.bio/product/hardware — "Stackable (for high density installations)" — vendor; CARRIED.
- **Automata LINQ: instruments on up to four fixed tiers in one bench, SCARA arm inside, bridge/maglev transport between benches; "up to 300%" more work area vs single tier (vendor)** — Automata blog — https://automata.tech/blog/award-winning-modular-lab-bench/ — "supports multi-tier stacking of worktops, with double, triple or even quadruple layers" — vendor; CARRIED. This is the nearest lab analogue to "vertical density for instruments": instruments stacked on *fixed* tiers, reached by an arm, not moved vertically.
- **Polybot (Argonne CNM): a mobile robot (MiR base + arm) carries samples between fixed stations; every new experiment can require further customization** — IEEE Spectrum — https://spectrum.ieee.org/autonomous-lab-argonne-polybot — "a robot on wheels with a robotic arm that transports samples between stations" — press; CARRIED.
- **HighRes Biosolutions: instruments on wheeled carts docked around a central robot pod; the dock supplies power, Ethernet and gas; patent rationale: fixed instruments block integration and repair** — The Robot Report; US 7,560,071 — https://www.therobotreport.com/highres-biosolutions-accelerates-pharmaceutical-research-with-automation/ — "power, Ethernet, and gas hookups" / patent: fixed installation "greatly inhibits ... the repair and/or upgrading of the laboratory device" — press + patent; CARRIED. (The established way to make instruments *movable* is the docking cart with utilities at the dock, not a powered storage carrier.)

- **Acceleration Consortium (Toronto): six domain SDLs at U of T + one at UBC rather than one integrated facility; SDL1 (inorganic) is a fixed powder line (dosing, milling, calcination) with characterization (XRD, SEM, XPS) "downstream"; AC lists a KUKA-supplied free-roaming mobile robotic chemist among member projects; "robots do 90 per cent of the manual work"** — AC pages; U of T Magazine — https://acceleration.utoronto.ca/sdls/sdl1-inorganic ; https://acceleration.utoronto.ca/maps/mobile-robotic-chemist-2 ; https://magazine.utoronto.ca/campus/the-lab-that-almost-runs-itself/ — "downstream characterization workflows (XRD, SEM, XPS)" / "Research carried out by robots is highly dependent on fluid flow and transfer." — university; CARRIED except the KUKA item (SNIPPET).

### A.5 Batteries/electronics: powered items in an automated store (closest industrial analogue)

- **Li-ion cell formation and aging use high-bay AS/RS: stacker cranes load trays of cells into formation (charge/discharge) racks; vendors sell "pre-charge & formation ASRS systems"** — Schuler battery brochure; Cimcorp — https://www.schulergroup.com/INTERNET/major/download_center/broschueren_battery/download_battery/battery_broschuere_finisching_e.pdf ; https://cimcorp.com/battery/ — "Stacker crane is used for automatic loading and unloading of the trays" (Schuler, summary wording) — vendor; SNIPPET. How a tray is electrically connected at its rack position, and whether it is powered in transit, was not confirmed. Formation/aging is also cited as the highest fire-risk step in cell making (case_studies.md §3, SNIPPET). This is the one industrial case of *energized* items living in an automated store; the connection is made at the storage position, the crane moves passive trays, and the "instrument" (the charger channel) stays fixed in the rack.

### A.6 Additions on ECL and lab-building constraints of automated stores

- **ECL filed a WARN notice for 30 workers in South San Francisco (notice 14 Apr 2023, effective 30 Apr 2023), at the time of its move to Austin** — WARNTracker (aggregates California EDD WARN filings) — https://www.warntracker.com/layoff/emerald-cloud-lab-2023-04-30 — "Emerald Cloud Lab has filed 1 WARN Act notice affecting 30 workers." — aggregator of gov filings (FETCHED). Becker's reported "cuts jobs, shuts down San Francisco offices" (SNIPPET: https://beckershospitalreview.com/digital-health/digital-biotech-company-cuts-jobs-shuts-down-san-francisco-offices.html). No WARN notice for Strateos was found.
- **Automated sample stores impose building loads that ordinary lab floors and rooms don't meet: 250-350+ psf vs ~100-125 psf lab floor design; 10-14 ft units plus 24-36 in service clearance above; 20,000-40,000+ BTU/h heat rejection vs 2,000-3,000 for an upright freezer; 3-phase power, Ethernet, compressed air, chilled water; dual redundancy and generator backup advised** — Henderson, Lab Design News, "Automated Sample Storage: The 'Vending Machine' Wall" (2 Mar, year not shown) — https://www.labdesignnews.com/content/automated-sample-storage-the-vending-machine-wall — "A fully loaded, mid-sized automated compound store can easily exert a concentrated load of 250 to 350 psf or higher." / "Facilities must equip these systems with dual-redundancy." — trade press, lab-design practitioner (FETCHED). Compare Kardex Shuttle slab loads (vlm_vendors.md §4.4: ~258 psf for a small unit, ~950 psf derived for a 6.75 m unit at full rating).
- **Vendor floor-space claims for -80 °C stores vs manual freezers: up to ~72-73% (Azenta BioArc), "30%+" across automated systems; figures assume 1.9 mL tubes at 90% fill** — Azenta pages — https://www.azenta.com/learning-center/blog/maximizing-efficiency-and-space-savings-power-automated-sample-storage — (summary: "up to 72%") — vendor; SNIPPET.

- **ECL user paper (CMU): the speed-up a cloud lab gives is set by "the type and number of instruments available" for a procedure — parallelization is case-by-case because each instrument does one or a few things; diagnosing failures sometimes needed reruns "at a scale that would be unnecessary in traditional labs"** — Arias & Taylor, "Scientific Discovery at the Press of a Button: Navigating Emerging Cloud Laboratory Technology", NSF public-access copy — https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10573763 — "The acceleration one can expect for a specific experimental procedure is, therefore, governed by the type and number of instruments available" / "less commonplace procedures might not have the demand to warrant more than a single instrument" — peer-reviewed author copy (FETCHED; text extracted locally by me). Relevance: for a small alloy lab with one of each instrument, the queue at each instrument, not transport, is the throughput limit.

## B. VLMs, carousels and automated stores in labs, biobanks, pharmacies, cleanrooms, tool cribs and metals

### B.1 Hospital and community pharmacy (robots, carousels, BoxPicker)

- **Before/after study (UK, Royal Wolverhampton, Consis channel robot): footprint 14.3 → 10.2 m² (−4.1 m², ~29%, my arithmetic), storage volume −22%; dispensing/restocking staff −19% (2.6 WTE) despite +19% workload; errors at final check −16%** — Pharmaceutical Journal research article, 16 Jun 2009 — https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/research/evaluation-of-an-automated-dispensing-system-in-a-hospital-pharmacy-dispensary — "4.1m2 less floor space (2m3 less total space occupied) was required" / "There was a 16% reduction in dispensing errors identified at the final checking stage" / "2.6WTE (19%) fewer staff were needed in the dispensing and restocking process" — professional journal, research section (FETCHED). Note: modest space gain (~29%), not 85-90%.
- **Multi-site UK study of dispensary robots: errors fell 2.7% → 1.0% at one site; no conclusive effect on turnaround** — cited in search summary (Int. J. Pharm. Pract.) — https://academic.oup.com/ijpp/article/16/1/47/6130595 — "no conclusive impact on turnaround times" (summary wording) — peer-reviewed; SNIPPET.
- **Japanese hospital (Yuyama robot): prevented dispensing errors 0.204% → 0.044%; unprevented 0.015% → 0.002%** — J. Pharm. Health Care Sci. 2022 — https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s40780-022-00255-w — "the total incidence of prevented dispensing errors was significantly reduced" — peer-reviewed; SNIPPET.
- **US central pharmacy (Christ Hospital, Cincinnati, 555 beds): Omnicell carousel cut dispensing errors vs manual shelves; XR2 robot 0% fill errors and >70% of orders; savings 0.77 pharmacist + 1.76 technician FTE** — Lin et al., AJHP 2024/2025, doi 10.1093/ajhp/zxae225 (PMID 39330997) — https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/es/mdl-39330997 — "use of the Carousel system resulted in a significant reduction in dispensing errors compared to manual filling from the shelves" — peer-reviewed; SNIPPET (403/cookie wall on fetch).
- **Carousel in a university medical centre: 2.6 FTE labour saved; dispense accuracy 99.02% → 99.48%; inventory carrying cost −$25,059** — Temple & Ludwig, AJHP 2010;67:821 — https://doi.org/10.2146/ajhp090307 — "The inventory carrying cost was reduced by $25,059." — peer-reviewed; SNIPPET.
- **Swisslog BoxPicker at Cohen Children's (202 beds): >50% fewer picking errors, >30% less labour, >30% time saved; inventory count from 3 people × 1 day to ~1 h/day; one NYP site quotes 97-98% accuracy** — Swisslog case studies — https://www.swisslog-healthcare.com/-/media/swisslog-healthcare/documents/products-and-services/pharmacy-automation/boxpicker/bps-301-cohen-childrens-medical-center-case-study.pdf?rev=-1 — "a greater than 50% reduction in picking errors, a greater than 30% reduction in labor" — vendor marketing; SNIPPET (PDF timed out on fetch). No floor-space figure found for BoxPicker.
- **Carousel vendor-style case (Cookeville Regional): picking accuracy to 99.9%, picking time −60%** — slideshare case study (2008) — https://es.slideshare.net/PROmanager/case-study-cookevilleregionaljune2008 — (summary: "picking accuracy rising to 99.9% and a 60% reduction in medication picking time") — vendor marketing; SNIPPET.
- **Pharmacy robots last ~10 years; Medway NHS trust's 14-year-old robot had increasingly frequent breakdowns, scarce spare parts and "sustained unplanned periods of downtime"; replacement took 4 months with temporary manual dispensing and temp staff; ballpark £1 M** — Neeves et al., Pharmaceutical Journal, 7 Jun 2024 — https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/ld/replacing-automated-medication-dispensing-machines-how-to-plan-and-implement-change-when-equipment-reaches-end-of-life — "Breakdowns were becoming more frequent" / "it was often difficult to obtain replacement parts" / "A ballpark figure for the replacement of an automated dispensing system is £1m." — professional journal (FETCHED).
- **Single point of failure in practice: a community pharmacy's Omnicell robot "failed on day one"; outages "sometimes a few minutes, sometimes days"; vendor says urgent calls resolved in 1.9 days on average** — Independent Community Pharmacist, 2 Oct 2024 — https://www.independentpharmacist.co.uk/news/pharmacist-considers-legal-action-against-omnicell-over-robot-claims — "failed on day one and has continued to fail ever since." — trade press, one site (FETCHED). ### C.1 Operating a VLM / vertical store: faults, outages, service, safety

- **Fatality: a certified field technician was crushed inside a "vertical storage machine" during repair (Oregon, 12 Jul 2017): a carrier-tray roller fell out; he climbed in to fix it; a trainee cycled the energized machine; pry bars were needed to free him** — NIOSH / Oregon FACE report 2017-22-1 (pub. 1 Jun 2019) — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/164913 — "Work being performed inside of an energized machine that was not treated as a confined space." / "The machine advanced the Technician over the top of the vertical storage unit, which had very limited space." — government investigation (FETCHED). Report does not say carousel vs VLM or brand. Relevance: clearing a jam or recovering an item inside is confined-space, lockout/tagout work for trained technicians, not lab staff.
- **Kardex's own fault list: light curtain blocked as doors move (2103); height-check interrupted by overhanging goods (1926); tray not seated against its stops (1915); "no suitable location" when a tray is too tall for free slots (1808); never power-cycle on an error; worst case the machine loses position** — Kardex Remstar US knowledge base — https://info.kardex-remstar.com/knowledge/error-code-shuttle-xp-with-logic-control — "never switch the power off - this will confuse the machine." / "something may lose its position preventing a simple error recovery and continued operation" — vendor support pages; CARRIED (vlm_vendors.md §4.1). Relevance: cables, hoses or anything protruding from an instrument tray will trip height/light-curtain checks.
- **Power loss mid-move: "lift has no reference" needs the vendor's service login; recovery is by phone support. Hand cranks are documented for carousels, not for the Shuttle VLM** — Kardex Remstar knowledge base "recover from power outage" — https://info.kardex-remstar.com/knowledge/recover-from-power-outage — "If machines display an error regarding "lift has no reference", this indicates the power was lost during vertical extractor movement." — vendor; FETCHED-by-sibling, CARRIED (vlm_vendors.md §4.2). ## Reading across (facts only; inferences flagged)

1. **Precedent for running instruments stored in a VLM: none found.** Closest: Hänel Lean-Lift electrified containers powering −80 °C cold boxes while stored (vendor, one blood-storage example); Modula "Electrified Tray" (240 V socket, ≤10 trays per lift, sold for battery charging); a 2015 NELC patent for a single lab lift carriage with utility raceways (no product found); Vidir's 2024 patent for powered carousel carriers. Every lab/biobank/pharmacy/fab/steel-lab automation found keeps the *measuring* device fixed and moves passive samples (or, at HighRes/Ginkgo, puts instruments on docking carts with utilities at the dock).
2. **Floor-space figures from real deployments are well below the 85-90% marketing ceiling when measured independently:** 29% footprint (Wolverhampton pharmacy robot, 2009), 42-71% (TU Eindhoven VLM case), ~72-73% claimed for −80 °C stores (vendor), 92% (Gurecky, vendor-fed trade press). Dense passive storage is what produces the big numbers.
3. **Throughput figures:** VLM 35-72 trays/h single-tray (modelled, human picker), vendor ceilings 120-140 trays/h; biobank store measured ~660-1,800 tubes/h; one VLM under-performed an open cabinet in most scenarios (TU/e). Pharmacy robots cut errors (e.g., 0.204% → 0.044%; carousel 99.02% → 99.48% accuracy) and staff time (2.6 WTE at Wolverhampton, FETCHED; 2.6 FTE in Temple & Ludwig's carousel study, SNIPPET) but turnaround effects were mixed.
4. **What limited throughput in automated labs:** instrument time and instrument count (ECL: often one instrument per procedure; Liverpool: GC 232 min/batch, PXRD 8 of 9 h per rack; A-Lab: furnace batches), chemistry/computation (A-Lab), consumables and manual prep steps (ASTRAL ball-milling and XRD prep by hand; A-Lab XRD holders cleaned by hand), robustness and debugging (Liverpool: >1 year; SDLs: ~2 days unattended). Where measured, transport was ~1% of elapsed time (Burger) or "a small fraction" (Lunt). No source reported transport as the bottleneck.
5. **Operating reality of automated stores:** single point of failure (vendor itself: everything in a machine is unavailable while it is down); certified-technician-only service with campus delays (UBC); pharmacy robots with multi-day outages; a fatal entrapment during repair inside a vertical storage machine (NIOSH 2017); fire protection inside enclosed VLMs needs special systems; power-cut recovery may need the vendor's service login.
6. **Cloud-lab business outcomes:** ECL kept people-plus-benches at 100-230 instrument types and moved to Austin (30-person WARN notice in South San Francisco, Apr 2023); Strateos pivoted away from its public robotic lab in Apr 2023 (closure unverified); Lilly sold its Strateos-run L2S2 to Arctoris in Sept 2024 (moved to Oxford).
