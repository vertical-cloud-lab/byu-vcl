# Colour coding: source digest
Source digest gathered on 2026-11-20 for the vertical-lab colour-coding proposal. Every fact carries its link; text in quotation marks is exact; "(snippet)" means only a search-result snippet supported it, "(secondary)" means a vendor, trade-press, wiki or third-party transcription rather than the publisher.

## 1. Safety colours: ANSI Z535.1 / Z535.2, OSHA 1910.144, ISO 3864

- **Z535.1 no longer gives meanings.** The public copy of ANSI Z535.1-2006 (an "edict of government" copy, because OSHA 1910.145 incorporates "ANSI Z535.1-2006(R2011)" by reference) says in its foreword: "The seventh revision of the ANSI Z535.1 Safety Color Code, in 2002, had two major changes. The first is the deletion of information concerning the application of the safety colors." and "The application of the colors (i.e. how they are to be used) properly belongs to the other standards in the ANSI Z535 series". Its scope (§2): "technical definitions, color standards, and color tolerances for safety colors." [law.resource.org, Z535.1-2006](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/ansi.nema.z535.1.2006.pdf)
- **Current edition** is ANSI Z535.1-2022, approved June 2022, described as minor updates for consistency with the rest of the Z535 family (snippet, [ANSI blog](https://blog.ansi.org/ansi/ansi-z535-1-2022-safety-colors-standard/), page returned 403 to a direct fetch). The 2017 revision harmonised with 49 CFR 172.407 and changed the PANTONE references (snippet, [ANSI blog 2017](https://blog.ansi.org/2017/12/ansi-z5351-2017-safety-colors-changes/)).
- **The ten colours it specifies** (Table 1 of the 2006 copy, Munsell notation and CIE x, y, Y under Illuminant C, read from an OCR'd scan, so check before relying on a digit): Safety Red 7.5R 4.0/14 (0.5959, 0.3269, 12.00); Safety Orange 5.0YR 6.0/15 (0.5510, 0.4214, 30.05); Safety Yellow 5.0Y 8.0/12 (0.4562, 0.4788, 59.10); Safety Green 7.5G 4.0/9 (0.2111, 0.4121, 12.00); Safety Blue 2.5PB 3.5/10 (0.1691, 0.1744, 9.00); Safety Purple 10.0P 4.5/10 (0.3307, 0.2245, 15.57); Safety Brown 5.0YR 2.75/5 (0.4766, 0.3816, 5.52); Safety White N 9.0 (Y 78.00); Safety Gray N 5.0 (Y 19.80); Safety Black N 1.5 (Y 2.02). [Z535.1-2006](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/ansi.nema.z535.1.2006.pdf)
- Z535.1-2006 §1: "Too many colors appearing simultaneously in the visual field can be both confusing and fatiguing. Study each location to minimize the number of markings, thereby enhancing the perceptual impact of the markings used." [Z535.1-2006](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/ansi.nema.z535.1.2006.pdf)
- Z535.1-2006 §4.4: "dark colors (red, brown, green, blue, and purple) should be used with white letters, while light colors (orange and yellow) are better seen contrasted with black." §5.1.4: the colours were chosen "to provide maximum feasible recognition by both normal and color-deficient (specifically red-green confusing) observers." Foreword: candidate colours must "be examined under the actual light sources to be used". [Z535.1-2006](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/ansi.nema.z535.1.2006.pdf)
- **Where the meanings live: Z535.2 (facility signs).** ANSI Z535.2-2011 (R2017), §7.2: "The word DANGER shall be in safety white letters on a safety red background."; "The word WARNING shall be in safety black letters on a safety orange background."; "The word CAUTION shall be in safety black letters on a safety yellow background."; "The word NOTICE shall be in italicized safety white letters on a safety blue background."; SAFETY INSTRUCTIONS "in safety white letters on a safety green background"; safety-equipment location signs white on green; fire-equipment location signs white on red. §7.3.1: message panels black on white or white on black. [law.resource.org, Z535.2-2011](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/ansi.nema.z535.2.2011.pdf)
- Z535.2 §4.11: DANGER "will result in death or serious injury"; WARNING "could result in death or serious injury"; CAUTION "could result in minor or moderate injury"; NOTICE is for "safety practices not directly related to physical injury" (§4.10.1.2). Foreword: using CAUTION for property damage "is phased out in the 2011 edition"; NOTICE replaces it. [Z535.2-2011](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/ansi.nema.z535.2.2011.pdf)
- **Purple / magenta = radiation in US law:** 10 CFR 20.1901(a): the radiation symbol "shall use the colors magenta, or purple, or black on yellow background." [10 CFR 20.1901, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/10/20.1901). MIL-STD-1472H Table XL also lists magenta as "Radiation hazard" (see §12).
- **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.144 names only red and yellow** ((a)(2) and (b) are "[Reserved]"). (a)(1): "Red shall be the basic color for the identification of:" (i) fire protection equipment "[Reserved]"; (ii) Danger: flammable-liquid safety cans painted red with "a yellow band around the can" or the contents name in yellow; "Red lights shall be provided at barricades and at temporary obstructions."; "Danger signs shall be painted red."; (iii) Stop: "Emergency stop bars on hazardous machines ... shall be red." and stop buttons "used for emergency stopping of machinery shall be red." (a)(3): "Yellow shall be the basic color for designating caution and for marking physical hazards such as: Striking against, stumbling, falling, tripping, and "caught in between."" [OSHA 1910.144](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.144)
- OSHA 1910.145(d)(4) caution signs: "The standard color of the background shall be yellow; and the panel, black with yellow letters." (d)(6) safety instruction signs: "The standard color of the background shall be white; and the panel, green with white letters." Warning tags ((f)(7)) are defined by the signal word only, with no colour named. [OSHA 1910.145](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.145)
- **ISO 3864-1:2011** defines a safety colour as "Colour with special properties to which a safety meaning is attributed." [ERA terminology, citing ISO 3864-1:2011](https://www.era.europa.eu/era-railway-terminology-collection/safetycolour/term_1/international_standard). ISO lists the 2011 edition as current, confirmed 2022 (snippet, [ISO 51021](https://www.iso.org/standard/51021.html)).
- ISO 3864 meanings (secondary, [Wikipedia ISO 3864](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3864)): red = prohibition / fire equipment; yellow = warning; blue = mandatory; green = safe condition; white and black as contrast/symbol colours; safety markings as alternating 45° stripes yellow/black, red/white, blue/white, green/white. ISO 3864-4 gives the colorimetric and photometric specifications; Wikipedia's RAL equivalents are signal yellow RAL 1003, signal red RAL 3001, signal blue RAL 5005, signal green RAL 6032, signal white RAL 9003, signal black RAL 9004 (secondary).
- **EU law in the same scheme (primary source):** Directive 92/58/EEC Annex I point 4: red = prohibition sign (dangerous behaviour), danger alarm (stop, shutdown, emergency cut-out devices, evacuate), fire-fighting equipment (identification and location); yellow or amber = warning sign ("Be careful, take precautions", "Examine"); blue = mandatory sign ("Specific behaviour or action", "Wear personal protective equipment"); green = emergency escape / first aid sign (doors, exits, routes, equipment, facilities) and "No danger" / "Return to normal". Annex V 1.1: obstacles and dangerous locations "should be marked with alternating yellow and black, or red and white stripes"; 1.3: stripes "at an angle of approximately 45o and of more or less equal size"; 2.1: traffic routes by "continous stripes in a clearly visible colour, preferably white or yellow" (spelling as in source). [EUR-Lex 31992L0058](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:31992L0058)
- **Conflicts:** orange is a US safety colour (WARNING) with no ISO 3864 counterpart; blue is US NOTICE (no injury risk) but ISO/EU "mandatory action"; purple/magenta is effectively reserved for radiation in US practice.
- Unverified / not found: the text of Z535.1-2022 or Z535.2's current edition (only the 2006 and 2011 (R2017) public copies were read; ANSI pages 403); ISO 3864-1:2011 Table 1 itself (paywalled; the iTeh preview carries no table text).

## 2. Pipe marking: ASME A13.1

- **The 2020 edition is two editions out of date.** The current edition is "ASME A13.1-2026 (Revision of ASME A13.1-2023)", "Date of Issuance: July 15, 2026", and "The next edition of this Standard is scheduled for publication in 2029." [ASME A13.1-2026 front matter](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/4bb2f1e2-b568-4a74-8c79-8e1a07247d56/c-t_a13_1_2026.pdf)
- Its contents list "4.1-1 GHS Pictograms", "4.1-2 Flow Direction to the Right", "4.1-3 Flow in Both Directions (Double-Headed Arrow)", "4.1-4 Flow in Both Directions (Arrows Pointed in Opposite Directions)", "4.2-1 Designation of Colors", "4.2-2 Standard Non-Safety Colors Gray and Brown", "4.3-2 Legends on Piping Where a Change in Direction Occurs" and "4.4-1 Dimensions of Color Fields and Letters in Legends". So gray and brown are now called non-safety colours. [ASME A13.1-2026 front matter](https://www.asme.org/getmedia/4bb2f1e2-b568-4a74-8c79-8e1a07247d56/c-t_a13_1_2026.pdf)
- **2020 colour pairs** (secondary: Marking Services Inc. summary, "six standard color combinations, and four defined by user combinations"): fire-quenching fluids, white on Safety Red; toxic and corrosive fluids, black on Safety Orange; flammable and oxidizing fluids, black on Safety Yellow; combustible fluids, white on Safety Brown; potable, cooling, boiler and other water, white on Safety Green; compressed air, white on Safety Blue; defined by user: white on Safety Purple, black on Safety White, white on Safety Grey, white on Safety Black. [MSI A13.1-2020 summary](https://www.markserv.com/sites/default/files/2021-03/ANSI.ASME%205.1.2020.pdf)
- Legends and arrows (MSI, secondary): markers show "both the contents of the pipe and its direction of flow. Arrows at one or both ends indicate flow". Placement: "Adjacent to all valves and flanges", "Adjacent to all changes of direction", "On both sides of wall or floor penetrations", "At intervals on straight pipe runs sufficient for identification"; markers below the centreline on overhead pipe. Sizes run from an 8 in field with 0.5 in letters (pipe OD 0.7-1.3 in) to a 32 in field with 3.5 in letters (OD over 10 in). Piping connected to GHS-labelled containers "may" carry a corresponding GHS label. [MSI](https://www.markserv.com/sites/default/files/2021-03/ANSI.ASME%205.1.2020.pdf). Wikipedia gives the straight-run interval as "Approximately every 25 feet (7.6 m) to 50 feet (15 m)" (secondary, [Pipe marking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_marking)).
- The pre-2007 scheme was hazard-level only: yellow = inherently hazardous, green = low-hazard liquids, blue = low-hazard gases, red = fire (MSI; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_marking), both secondary). MSI says existing schemes can still meet the 2020 edition "if the schemes are described in writing or employees are trained".
- **Vendor charts disagree,** so the table itself should be checked before use: Wikipedia puts toxic/corrosive as white on orange (MSI: black on orange), and one chart puts "Flammable fluids" on brown and "Oxidizing fluids" on yellow ([projectmaterials](https://blog.projectmaterials.com/pipes/asme-a13-1-pipe-color-coding), secondary, apparently wrong).
- **Inert gases, vacuum, chilled water:** no A13.1 text naming them was found. In practice argon and nitrogen are labelled in the compressed-air colours, white on blue: a vendor sells an "ASME A13.1 Inert Gas Pipe Label" in white on blue (snippet, [ComplianceSigns](https://www.compliancesigns.com/pd/asme-a13-1-inert-gas-pipe-label-pipe-23760-white-on-blue)), and one chart lists "Instrument air, plant air, nitrogen" under compressed air ([projectmaterials](https://blog.projectmaterials.com/pipes/asme-a13-1-pipe-color-coding), secondary). Chilled water fits the "cooling ... water" class (white on green) by its name (inference from the class title). Vacuum: not found; medical/lab vacuum has its own NFPA 99 labels (secondary, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_marking)).
- MIL-STD-1472H §5.4.3.7: "Redundant labeling shall be used for installations such as pipes that take several runs and can be viewed from several planes". [MIL-STD-1472H](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/MIL-STD-1472H_57041/)
- Unverified / not found: Table 4.2-1 of the 2023 or 2026 edition (paywalled), so whether any pair changed after 2020 is unknown; how A13.1 classes inert gases or vacuum; the OSHA reference to A13.1 (Wikipedia says OSHA points to it via 1910.261, which covers pulp and paper mills; not checked).

## 3. Gas cylinders: CGA C-7, EN 1089-3

- **Colour does not identify contents in the US.** CGA C-7-2020 (incorporated by reference, public copy) §3: "The handler or user of compressed gas containers shall not rely on the color of the container to identify the product. The availability of more than one hundred gases and innumerable mixtures makes it impossible to develop a practical color marking system for industrial containers." Same section: "The most important information on any gas container label or marking is the name of the product in the container." [CGA C-7-2020](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/cga.c-07.2020.pdf)
- C-7 Appendix B.5: FDA has codified medical-gas colour marking, but "CGA strongly recommends that color marking systems should not be relied upon to identify the contents of any compressed gas container. The only means for identification of the content of any container of compressed gas should be the chemical name or other commonly accepted name of the material legibly marked on the exterior of the container." It points to CGA PS-2 for the reasoning. [CGA C-7-2020](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/cfr/ibr/inc/ansi/cga.c-07.2020.pdf)
- The only US legal colour code is for medical gases. 21 CFR 201.328(b): "Each high-pressure medical gas cylinder must be colored on the shoulder portion of the cylinder"; (c): Medical Air yellow, Carbon Dioxide gray, Helium brown, Nitrogen black, Nitrous Oxide blue, Oxygen green, mixtures "Colors corresponding to each component gas." [21 CFR 201.328, LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/201.328)
- **US suppliers do not colour-code industrial cylinders.** Airgas: "Airgas does not use color coding to identify cylinder contents. Cylinder color should never be used to identify contents. Please read the contents label." and "Airgas uses a teal paint to identify specialty gas cylinders." [Airgas catalog page](https://www.airgas.com/airgascatalog/catalog/ap005.pdf)
- **EN 1089-3 (Europe), shoulder colours,** as summarised by the British Compressed Gases Association (TIS 6 Rev 3, 2018): argon dark green RAL 6001; nitrogen black RAL 9005; helium brown RAL 8008; carbon dioxide grey RAL 7037; oxygen white RAL 9010; acetylene maroon (RAL 3009 in EN 1089-3, RAL 3007 by UK law). Gases without their own colour show their hazard: inert bright green RAL 6018, oxidising light blue RAL 5012, flammable red RAL 3000, toxic and/or corrosive yellow RAL 1018. "The label is the primary method of identifying the contents."; "In the case of gas mixtures there is no specific guidance in the standard"; the body colour is not coded (except medical white, acetylene, UK hydrogen red); the stencilled "N" that shows EN 1089-3 compliance "is not necessary in the UK". [BCGA TIS 6 Rev 3](https://www.bfbi.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/21479_bcga-tis-6-rev-3-gas-cylinder-identification-label-colour-code-requirements.pdf); superseded by Rev 4 (2023), [BCGA](https://bcga.co.uk/?p=5666). The standard is EN 1089-3:2011 (snippet, [iTeh listing](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/cen/09fe00c8-1a00-44cf-9126-439a6e8f1d71/en-1089-3-2004)).
- **Conflicts:** green on a shoulder is oxygen on a US medical cylinder but argon (dark green) or any inert gas (bright green) under EN 1089-3. Yellow is US medical air, EN 1089-3 toxic/corrosive, and A13.1 flammable/oxidizing pipe. MIL-STD-1472H Table XL adds a third set: red = oxygen, blue = nitrogen (see §12).
- Unverified / not found: any US industrial-gas supplier stating that it follows EN 1089-3 (none found); the text of EN 1089-3, CGA C-9 and CGA PS-2 (not read).

## 4. Electrical: NEC, IEC 60445, receptacles

- NEC 250.119: insulated equipment grounding conductors "must have an uninterrupted surface colored green or green with one or more yellow stripes", and that colour code may not be used for grounded or ungrounded conductors (secondary paraphrase of the 2023 NEC, [EEPower, Aug 2023](https://eepower.com/technical-articles/nec-2023-basics-identifying-wire-type-equipment-grounding-conductors/)).
- NEC 200.6: insulated neutrals "must be identified as white or gray"; phase conductors "can be any color but white [Sec. 200.7] or green [Sec. 250.119]", and the NEC does not require a specific colour code for phase conductors (secondary, Mike Holt in [EC&M, Mar 2023](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/qa/article/21262479/code-qa-nec-requirements-for-conductor-identification)). The three continuous white or gray stripes "on other than green insulation" option is snippet-only.
- **Phase colours are not mandated; identification is.** NEC 210.5(C)(1): where branch circuits come from more than one nominal voltage system, each ungrounded conductor is identified "by system and phase (or line)" at "all termination, connection, and splice points", e.g. "by separate color coding or marking tape" [210.5(C)(1)(a)], and "Whatever identification means you use must be documented [210.5(C)(1)(b)]", posted at each panelboard if the documentation is not readily available (secondary, [EC&M MRO Insider](https://www.ecmweb.com/mro-insider/nec-identification-requirements-branch-circuits-part-4), 2011/2014 NEC). Feeders: 215.12(C)(1).
- Convention: "Using black, red, and blue insulation to identify the ungrounded conductors of 120V/208V, 3-phase systems is a widespread practice"; on 277/480 V, "brown insulation was specifically reserved for phase 'A', orange for phase 'B,' and yellow for phase 'C.'" The same article says colour alone "may be a little trickier than it seems" for 210.5(C)(1) (secondary, [EC&M, Dec 2025](https://ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/article/55332007/practically-speaking-choose-your-colors-wisely)). Some jurisdictions write colours into local amendments, e.g. San Francisco (snippet, [SF code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/san_francisco/57e5f03c-2e0f-4ad9-8c7f-f6fef7e7e402/sf_building/0-0-0-84843), 403 on fetch).
- NEC 110.15 (4-wire delta high leg): "durably and permanently marked by an outer finish that is orange in color or by other effective means" ([IAEI, 2011](https://iaeimagazine.org/electrical-inspections/article-110-requirements-for-electrical-installations-2)).
- **IEC 60445:2021 (Ed. 7.0, 2021-07)**: "the provisions for colour to be used for identification of certain designated conductors are made requirements and not only recommendations" ([VDE preview](https://www.vde-verlag.de/iec-normen/preview-pdf/info_iec60445{ed7.0.RLV}en.pdf)). The harmonised colours, as in BS 7671 Table 51: protective conductor green-and-yellow, neutral blue, L1 brown, L2 black, L3 grey. The UK proposed "brown, black and grey" and Europe adopted it; the old UK colours were red/yellow/blue phases with a black neutral ([IEE Wiring Matters, Spring 2004](https://electrical.theiet.org/media/1720/harmonised-colours-and-alphanumeric-marketing.pdf)).
- Inside machines, EN 60204-1 §13.2: green-and-yellow "strictly reserved for the protective conductor"; neutral "BLUE"; recommended BLACK for a.c. and d.c. power, RED for a.c. control, BLUE for d.c. control, ORANGE for "excepted circuits in accordance with 5.3.5" (circuits left live when the supply disconnector is off) (secondary, a garbled online transcription of DSTU EN 60204-1:2014, [dnaop](https://dnaop.com/html/61446_11.html)).
- **Receptacles.** Isolated-ground receptacles (NEC 406.3): "These receptacles are often completely orange in color" but "all that is required by Code is an orange triangle on the face of the receptacle" ([IAEI, 2014, NEC 2011 basis](https://iaeimagazine.org/magazine/2014/12/02/article-406-receptacles-cord-connectors-and-attachment-plugs/)); the subsection letter moved between (D) and (E) across editions (snippet). Health care: NEC 517.31(E) (2017 to 2023): receptacles or cover plates "supplied from the life safety and critical branches shall have a distinctive color or marking so as to be readily identifiable"; the same rule appears at 517.19(A) (critical-care bed locations, added 2017) and 517.42(E) (Type 2 essential electrical systems) (secondary, maker's summary: [Leviton Q-899C](https://leviton.com/content/dam/leviton/commercial-industrial/product_documents/take_one/NEC_Code_Change_Summary_for_Health_Care_Facilities_Q-899C.pdf)). Red is the custom, not a requirement (snippet, Mike Holt forum). 700.10(A) and 708.10 carry similar marking rules for emergency and COPS receptacles (snippet).
- **Conflicts for a lab with both US and European equipment:** blue is phase C in US 208Y/120 practice but the neutral under IEC 60445 and EN 60204-1; grey is a permitted US neutral (200.6) but IEC L3; black is US phase A but IEC L2 (and the old UK neutral); orange is a US high leg, an isolated-ground receptacle mark, and an IEC 60204-1 "still live" circuit. Green-and-yellow means protective earth everywhere.
- Unverified / not found: 2026 NEC numbering (not checked; sources cite 2011 to 2023); the NFPA 99 clause that requires distinctive colour (Leviton lists only related NFPA 99-2018 clauses); the original wording of IEC 60204-1 §13.2.4.

## 5. Indicator and stack lights: IEC 60204-1, IEC 60073

- IEC 60204-1:2016+AMD1:2021 CSV is Edition 6.1, published 2021-09-15 (snippet, [IEC webstore](https://webstore.iec.ch/publication/71256)).
- Indicator lights (EN 60204-1 Table 4, "Colours for indicator lights and their meanings with respect to the condition of the machine"; secondary transcription of DSTU EN 60204-1:2014, clause numbers inferred, [dnaop](https://dnaop.com/html/61446_9.html)): RED "Emergency", "Hazardous condition", "Immediate action to deal with hazardous condition"; YELLOW "Abnormal", "Abnormal condition" / "Impending critical condition", "Monitoring and/or intervention"; BLUE "Mandatory", "Indication of a condition that requires action by the operator", "Mandatory action"; GREEN "Normal", "Normal condition", "Optional"; WHITE "Neutral", "Other conditions; may be used whenever doubt exists about the application of RED, YELLOW, GREEN, BLUE", "Monitoring".
- Push-buttons (same source): "The colour RED shall be used for emergency stop and emergency switching off actuators."; START/ON "should be WHITE, GREY, BLACK or GREEN with a preference for WHITE" and "RED shall not be used"; STOP/OFF "should be BLACK, GREY, or WHITE with a preference for BLACK" and "GREEN shall not be used"; reset "BLUE, WHITE, GREY, or BLACK".
- IEC 60073:2002 (coding principles for indicators and actuators), Introduction: "The use of only a single means of coding is often insufficient to ensure unambiguous representation of information."; the code may relate "to the state of equipment (or part of it), to the condition of a process, and/or to the effects which this condition has on persons, property and the environment. The user is required to decide on which of these criteria the coding for the relevant application is to be based." [VDE preview](https://www.vde-verlag.de/iec-normen/preview-pdf/info_iec60073{ed6.0}b.pdf). A draft revision (BS EN IEC 60073) appeared in January 2025 (snippet, [BSI](https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/draft-bs-en-iec-60073-basic-and-safety-principles-for-man-machine-interface-marking-and-identification-coding-principles-for-indicators-and-actuators)).
- MIL-STD-1472H Table XV (simple indicator lights): red, 25 mm or larger, flashing 3-5 Hz: "Emergency condition (impending personnel or equipment disaster)"; red, 13 mm or smaller, steady: "Malfunction; action stopped; failure; stop action"; yellow: "Delay; check; recheck" (small) or "Extreme caution (impending danger)" (large); green: "Go ahead; in tolerance; acceptable; ready". [MIL-STD-1472H](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/MIL-STD-1472H_57041/)
- **The MiR250 already speaks its own colour language** (MiR250 User Guide v1.2, 07/2020): power button "Blue: The robot is off.", "Blinking green: The robot is starting up.", "Green: Normal operation.", "Blinking red: The battery level is too low ... or, the robot is shutting down."; Resume button "Blinking blue: The robot is waiting for a user action"; status lights turn red in Protective and Emergency stop and "waver yellow" while starting and shutting down; signal lights are "white at the front, red at the back"; "all signal lights blink yellow" when driving with personnel detection muted; status-light colours "may also be used as part of missions". [MiR250 User Guide v1.2](https://jk.solemedia.de/fileadmin/daten/Bilder/Intralogistik/MiR_Transportsystem/MiR100_MiR200/mir250_user_guide_12_en.pdf)
- **Conflicts:** green is "normal / ready" on lights (IEC, MIL) but "safety equipment" on signs (Z535) and "safe condition / escape" in ISO 3864; blue is IEC "mandatory action", ANSI NOTICE, and MiR "off".
- Unverified / not found: the yellow background behind emergency-stop actuators (IEC 60204-1 §10.7.3 / ISO 13850; not read); NFPA 79's US equivalent table (not read); any standard specifically for stack-light (tower) order.

## 6. Floor marking: OSHA, visual workplace, ISO 3691-4, R15.08

- OSHA 1910.176(a): "Permanent aisles and passageways shall be appropriately marked." [OSHA 1910.176](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.176)
- **OSHA prescribes no floor colours.** 1972 letter: "The lines used to delineate the aisles may be any color so long as they clearly define the area considered as aisle space."; "any width 2 inches or more is considered acceptable"; aisles "at least 3 feet wider than the largest equipment to be utilized" or "a minimum of 4 feet". [OSHA 1972-05-15](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/1972-05-15). 1977 letter: the standard "does not require marking by painted yellow lines only." [OSHA 1977-02-14](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/1977-02-14). Both carry the archive notice that they "may no longer represent OSHA Policy". The only OSHA colour rule that touches floors is 1910.144(a)(3) yellow for physical hazards (§1).
- **Visual-workplace convention (Brady, via Plant Services, 2010, secondary):** yellow "Aisleways, traffic lanes and work cells"; white "Equipment and fixtures ... not otherwise color coded"; blue, green and/or black "Materials and components"; orange "Materials or product held for inspection"; red "Defects, scrap, rework and red tag areas"; red and white "Areas to be kept clear for safety/compliance reasons" (electrical panels, eyewash stations); black and white "Areas to be kept clear for operational purposes"; black and yellow "Areas that may expose employees to special physical or health hazards". "There are currently no government-mandated or widely accepted industry standards" for floor colours. ## 8. Colour-coded cleaning

- **NPSA scheme:** "Colour coding hospital cleaning materials and equipment", Safer Practice Notice 15, National Patient Safety Agency, January 2007, restated in "The Revised Healthcare Cleaning Manual (NRLS, 2009)" ([Torbay and South Devon NHS FT cleaning policy, 2024 draft](https://www.torbayandsouthdevon.nhs.uk/uploads/cleaning-policy.pdf)). NRIC lists it as published 10/01/2007, with purpose: it "ensures that these items are not used in multiple areas, therefore reducing the risk of cross-infection", and gives the level of evidence as "Unspecified" ([NRIC](https://nric.org.uk/node/52727)).
- Scope: "All cleaning items, for example, cloths (re-usable and disposable), mops, buckets, aprons and gloves, should be colour coded. This also includes those items used to clean catering departments." ([NHS Lothian poster, reviewed Dec 2024](https://policyonline.nhslothian.scot/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Domestic_Colour_Coding.pdf))
- Yellow: "The National Standards for Healthcare Cleanliness 2021 requires adoption of the 'National colour coding scheme for cleaning materials and equipment'"; "yellow cleaning materials and equipment should be used for isolation areas" (NHS Estates and Facilities, July 2024, [addendum for general practice](https://infectionpreventioncontrol.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Addendum-GP-National-colour-coding-scheme-Isolation-Treatment-and-minor-operation-rooms-July-2024.pdf)).
- Red, blue, green (secondary, [Cleaning Matters, March 2013](https://www.cleaning-matters.co.uk/page_292507.asp)): red = general washrooms (toilets, showers, basins, bathroom floors); blue = general areas (wards, departments, offices, basins in public areas); green = catering departments and ward kitchens. - **BICSc** (secondary, same article): red = washrooms; red/white = higher-risk sanitary appliances and washroom floors; blue = general low-risk areas; green = general food and bar use (not commercial food preparation); yellow = clinical areas; white = site-specific or specialist. BICSc itself says it has "for more than 20 years called for the use of yellow janitorial equipment and hand protection to be used within clinical areas" ([BICSc](https://www.bics.org.uk/?p=16868)). BICSc puts public-area basins in red, NPSA in blue. A caterer's guide puts food areas under yellow (snippet, [Nisbets](https://www.nisbets.co.uk/colourcodedcleaning)).
- Colour overloading in one building: one NHS trust layers a green / blue / orange "Disc Notification System" for waste and cleaning level, yellow-and-black "tiger bags" and orange infectious-waste bags on top of the national cleaning colours ([Gloucestershire Health and Care cleaning policy, July 2022](https://www.ghc.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/CLP158CleaningPolicyV2issued6.7.22.pdf)).
- **Food-industry tools:** Vikan's hygiene range comes in "Up to 12 colors" ([Vikan US](https://www.vikan.com/us/services/vikan-hygiene-color-coding-range/)); colour coding "is often a requirement for GFSI-benchmarked food safety standards (including BRCGS, FSSC22000, IFS, and SQF)" ([Vikan](https://www.vikan.com/us/knowledge-center/hygiene-and-food-safety-management/colour-coding)). Vikan's own advice: "Limit the number of colours as much as possible."; "Don't try to assign different colours to each and every step of a complicated process."; "One in 12 men and one in 200 women are colour-blind."; check a scheme by taking "a photo of the colours" and converting it "into black and white"; "Support your colour-coding system with good signage" ([Vikan blog](https://www.vikan.com/au/knowledge-centre/the-vikan-blog/11-useful-tips-for-colour-coding-in-your-facility)). Hillbrush: "currently available across a broad palette of 10 colours" (an older catalogue says twelve, snippet); "Use only as many colours as you genuinely need."; "A simple system that employees understand is usually more useful than a complicated one involving too many exceptions."; "However, colour coding on its own does not remove allergens." ([Hillbrush](https://hillbrush.com/pages/colour-coded-cleaning-system-guide))
- **Evidence:** no controlled study of outcomes (cross-contamination or infection rates) for colour-coded cleaning was found. NRIC rates the NPSA notice's evidence "Unspecified"; makers' pages cite no data; NSW Health calls colour coding the most effective way to restrict equipment to areas, a claim rather than a measurement (snippet, [NSW CEC module 5](https://cec.health.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/258667/Module-5-Cleaning-equipment.pdf)).

## 9. Metal powders: NFPA 484 / 660, AM powder standards

- **NFPA 484 has been folded into NFPA 660.** NFPA 660 "consolidated the six commodity-specific combustible dust standards (NFPA 61, 484, 652, 654, 655 and 664)", "With an effective date of Dec. 6, 2024" ([Safety+Health, July 2026](https://www.safetyandhealthmagazine.com/?p=56716)); "The new standard combines and supersedes" them, NFPA 484 included ([Exponent, Jan 2025](https://www.exponent.com/article/new-nfpa-consolidated-standard-combustible-dusts)). Chapter 22 carries the former NFPA 484 (snippet, Camfil via search). "Test standards for portable vacuum cleaners used with combustible metals have been provided" ([Nilfisk, Feb 2025](https://www.nilfisk.com/en-us/professional/news-and-articles/nfpa-660-standard-for-combustible-dusts-and-particulate-solids-what-you-need-to-know/)).
- NFPA 484 provisions, via a dust-collector maker (secondary): "Restricting which dusts and fumes can be collected together"; "Dedicating work cells, ducting, and dust collectors to separate processes"; "NFPA 484 requires that metal dust be removed from the dust collector at least daily" ([Donaldson](https://www.donaldson.com/en/resources/technical-articles/combustible-metals-dust/)). OSHA, on NFPA 484-2015: it "does not permit indoor dry-type dust collectors for aluminum (NFPA 484 § 9.4.13.15.1)" and "prohibits self-contained dry down draft benches for the collection of combustible metals (NFPA 484 § 9.4.13.15.3)" ([OSHA interpretation 2016-05-23](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/standardinterpretations/2016-05-23)). A quoted NFPA 484 §12.7.2.2 (edition not given): equipment producing combustible aluminum may be used for other materials "only when the system is thoroughly cleaned of all incompatible materials prior and after its use" (snippet).
- **Aluminum Association guidance:** "Dust collection systems handling aluminum or aluminum alloy dusts should be dedicated for that use only. If the collection system is to be used for the collection of dusts of other metals, the system should be disassembled and thoroughly cleaned prior to and after use for dusts of other metals."; "Standard commercial industrial vacuum cleaners must not be used during cleaning. Vacuum cleaning systems, designed and certified for use with Group E combustible dusts may be used, with limitations (see NFPA 484)."; cleaning tools "must be a soft natural fiber brush or squeegee with non-sparking, conductive scoops"; "Synthetic fiber bristle brushes and plastic or other nonconductive scoops must not be used". [Aluminum Association, fines guidelines](https://www.aluminum.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/Safe_Handling-Aluminum_Fine_Particles.pdf). And: "Do not mix dusts from steel and aluminum."; "A common scenario favoring thermiting is the collection of aluminum and iron particles/dusts which include rust in co-mingled management devices such as baghouses or vacuum collection systems." [Aluminum Association, collision repair](https://www.aluminum.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/Aluminum_Dust-Automotive_Collision.pdf)
- **Al with oxidised copper is also a thermite:** for aluminium powder, "Heating a mixture with copper oxides caused a strong explosion"; "Reacts exothermically if mixed with metal oxides and heated (thermite process)." [NOAA CAMEO, aluminum powder, coated](https://cameochemicals.noaa.gov/chemical/14577). (Inference: the Al and Cu alloy families should not share a collector, vacuum or waste drum, as well as being kept apart for purity.)
- **AM standards (contents, not text):** ISO/ASTM 52907:2019 has 4.2 "Documentation and traceability", 4.9 "Contamination", 4.10 "Packaging, handling and storage" (4.10.2 "Packaging and handling", 4.10.3 "Storage") ([DIN contents](https://www.din.de/de/mitwirken/normenausschuesse/nwt/veroeffentlichungen/wdc-beuth:din21:317795828/toc-3131818/download)); its abstract says it "does not deal with safety aspects" (snippet). ISO/ASTM 52931:2023 has 7.4.2 "Rating of risks related to contamination, inhalation or skin contact", 8.2.5 "Powder storage", 8.4.2 "Training of personnel" ([DIN contents](https://www.din.de/de/mitwirken/normenausschuesse/nwt/veroeffentlichungen/wdc-beuth:din21:364023432/toc-3412756/download)). ISO/ASTM 52904:2024 (Ed. 2) has 6.3 "Auxiliary tools and equipment" and 7.2 "Control of feedstock" ([DIN contents](https://www.din.de/de/mitwirken/normenausschuesse/nwt/veroeffentlichungen/wdc-beuth:din21:382366027/toc-3560081/download)).
- ASTM F3456-22 §1.2: "does not cover powder specifications, recycling strategy, blending processes, lot control, or address contamination prevention"; §4.1: "a well-defined powder reuse schema may reduce the risk of feedstock contamination". ASTM F3049 is a guide to powder characterisation techniques for "virgin and used metal powders", with nothing on handling. ([ASTM F3456-22](https://store.astm.org/f3456-22.html), [ASTM F3049](https://store.astm.org/f3049-14r21.html); ASTM's pages prohibit AI use of their standards, so only the public scope was read.)
- Research: a small amount of cross-contamination "cannot be identified with conventional laboratory XRD equipment" (the authors' image-based method targets 1 wt% or less); "density is the key to successfully detecting cross-contamination in metal powder feedstock"; contaminant particles can be crack-initiation sites. [Santecchia et al., Materials 2019, 12(15):2342, doi:10.3390/ma12152342](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6696047/). A 2025 study of CuCr1Zr particles in AlSi10Mg feedstock exists (snippet; full text unreachable, [Augsburg repository](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/123552)).

## 11. Colour vision deficiency

- NEI: "About 1 in 12 men have color vision deficiency."; "Men have a much higher risk than women"; the most common type "makes it hard to tell the difference between red and green"; another "makes blue and yellow look the same." [NEI](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/color-blindness)
- MedlinePlus Genetics: "Among populations with Northern European ancestry," red-green deficiency "occurs in about 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women"; it "is less common in almost all other populations that have been studied" and "is also common in East Asian populations"; blue-yellow deficiency "affects men and women equally" and "occurs in fewer than 1 in 10,000 people worldwide". [MedlinePlus Genetics](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/color-vision-deficiency)
- Birch 2012: prevalence in European Caucasians "about 8% in men and about 0.4% in women and between 4% and 6.5% in men of Chinese and Japanese ethnicity"; "the male: female prevalence ratio is markedly different in Europeans and Asians"; prevalence "is rising in men of African ethnicity". [J Opt Soc Am A 29(3):313-320, doi:10.1364/JOSAA.29.000313, PubMed 22472762](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22472762/)
- Which pairs fail (FAA definitions): protan, "degrading their perception of red colors and discrimination between red and green colors"; deutan, "degrading their perception of green colors and discrimination between green and red colors"; tritan, "degrading their perception of blue colors and discrimination between blue and yellow colors". Brightness is not a safe back-up cue: "protans may not recognize brightness differences that are apparent to others". [FAA-HF-STD-010A](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/92012/dot_92012_DS1.pdf)
- Protective eyewear can induce colour deficiency (MIL-STD-1472H §5.17.25.12, see §12); relevant to laser-safety eyewear during LPBF service (inference).
- Unverified / not found: Birch's per-population figures for African populations (abstract gives only the trend).

## 12. Human-factors limits on colour codes

- **MIL-STD-1472H** (15 September 2020, superseding 1472G with Change 1): §5.4.5.6.3 (labels): "If color coding is used, the number of different colors shall not exceed five."; §5.17.25.5 (displays): "a maximum of 11 nameable colors shall be used to represent and distinguish between categorically different information"; §5.17.25.3: "Color coding shall be used consistently within a display and, where appropriate, across displays of other systems used by the same users."; §5.17.25.10: "If the user population includes color-blind users, every effort should be made for non-confusable colors to be used for coding."; §5.17.25.11: "If non-confusable colors cannot be used for coding for color-blind users, redundant coding shall be used."; §5.17.25.12 (filters and eyewear): "color coding in display design shall not be the only means of coding information for affected operational systems."; §5.1.1.4.5.9.1: "Color coding shall not be used as the primary identification medium if the spectral characteristics of the ambient light ... varies"; §5.6.11.12.2: "Color coding plus one other coding method shall be used for hydraulic lines and valves at each end of the line." [MIL-STD-1472H (EverySpec)](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/MIL-STD-1472H_57041/)
- MIL-STD-1472H Table XL, "Common color association meanings" (selected): red: danger or unsafe, stop, emergency, "Oxygen", "Heat or fire"; orange: "Alarm, alert, or hazard"; yellow: "Warning, caution, or hazard", "Oil", "Check or recheck"; green: "Normal", "Safe", "Open/flowing", "Ready, proceed, satisfactory"; blue: "Nitrogen", "Water or flooding", "Cool or cold"; magenta: "Radiation hazard"; purple: "Aviation fuels"; white: "Steam"; gray: "Smoke", "Inactive or unavailable options or actions". [MIL-STD-1472H](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/MIL-STD-1472H_57041/)
- **NASA-STD-3001 Vol. 2 Rev F** (approved 2026-07-14), [V2 10045]: "The system shall provide an additional cue when color is issued to convey meaning for critical information or for a critical task." Rationale: "Redundant coding is required to accommodate the variability in people's capability to see color under different lighting conditions and to increase the saliency of identification markings. Redundant cues can include labels, icons, and speech messages." [NASA-STD-3001 Vol 2 Rev F](https://standards.nasa.gov/system/files/tmp/NASA-STD-3001%20Vol%202%20Rev%20F.pdf)
- **FAA-HF-STD-010A** (24 March 2020, colour in ATC displays; cites FAA-HF-STD-001B 2016 among its sources): §4.2: "Color shall not be the sole means used to code critical ATC information"; colour "shall be used redundantly with at least one other coding dimension that is independent of the operator's color vision, such as size, shape, pattern, text content, position, or temporal modulation"; §4.2.2: "brightness coding shall not be used to add a redundant coding dimension to chromaticity"; §4.3: "Color differences shall not be introduced solely for aesthetic, stylistic, or decorative purposes because unnecessary color proliferation can increase visual search times, degrade legibility, cause operators to infer unintended meanings or groupings, and make the display appear cluttered."; §4.4: meanings "shall be consistent within a screen, within a system, and across related systems" and "shall be fixed and not change over time"; §5.9: at most 24 colours, but "it is best to use the fewest number possible", noting that "Several standards set much smaller limits for color-coding sets; that is, 5 to 8 colors (ANSI-HFES-100, 2007; ISO 9241-125, 2017; FAA JO 7210.3Z; MIL-HDBK-759C, 1995; MIL-STD-1472G, 2012; SAE ARP4032B, 2013)". The 11 focal colour names: "red, pink, orange, yellow, brown, green, blue, purple, black, white, and gray". [FAA-HF-STD-010A](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/92012/dot_92012_DS1.pdf)
- **Christ (1975)**, "Review and Analysis of Color Coding Research for Visual Displays", Human Factors 17(6):542-570: 42 studies (1952-1973); "color may be a very effective performance factor under some conditions, but that it can be detrimental under others." [Crossref, doi:10.1177/001872087501700602](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/001872087501700602). A related Christ and Corso report found colour generally shortened search/locate time and that familiar geometric shapes performed "quite simular" (snippet, [SWOV](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/color-research-visual-displays)).
- Same direction elsewhere: Z535.1 §1 ("minimize the number of markings", §1 above) and IEC 60073 ("single means of coding is often insufficient", §5).
- Unverified / not found: the FAA HF-STD-001B colour chapter itself (only cited via 010A); Christ's specific rankings of colour against shape, size and alphanumerics (full text not read).

## 13. A colour code that works: blood-collection tubes

- CLSI order of draw (GP41; CLSI blog, March 19, 2019): 1 "Blood culture tube or bottle"; 2 "Sodium citrate tube (eg, blue closure)"; 3 "Serum tubes, including those with clot activator and gels (eg, red, red-speckled, gold closures)"; 4 "Heparin tube with or without gel (eg, dark green, light green, speckled green closures)"; 5 "EDTA tube with or without gel separator (eg, lavender, pearl, pink closures)"; 6 "Sodium fluoride/potassium oxalate glycolytic inhibitor (eg, gray closure)". Clause 11: "Containers shall be identified by means of the letter code and/or a description of the contents ... and/or product name." [ISO 6710:2017 preview (iTeh)](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/69659/7482b8e66ef946489fdb1db6a722e072/ISO-6710-2017.pdf)
- **Same cap, different additive:** Sarstedt still sells S-Monovette EDTA K3E tubes with "cap red, colour code EU" ([Sarstedt](https://www.sarstedt.com/en/JP/c/pre-analytics/venous-blood-collection/s-monovette-blood-collection-tube/p/s-monovette-edta)), where the CLSI examples use red for serum and lavender or pink for EDTA. Sarstedt's EU-code citrate is green (ISO-code blue), lithium heparin orange and serum white (snippets, Sarstedt product pages); it describes an "'EU colour code' (based on BS 4851)" and a "'US colour code' (based on ISO 6710)" (snippet, [Sarstedt](https://sarstedt.com/en/products/new-products/colour-coding)).
