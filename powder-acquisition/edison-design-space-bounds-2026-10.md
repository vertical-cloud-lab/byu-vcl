# Design-Space Definition for a Bayesian-Optimization LPBF Aluminium-Alloy Campaign

## 1. Introduction and Scope

This document defines per-element composition bounds, physical ceilings, pairwise interaction constraints, total-solute considerations, and impurity tolerances for a 15-element Bayesian-optimization campaign on aluminium-based alloys for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF), with powder produced by ultrasonic atomization (UA) of ~100 g charges at ≤1300 °C under argon. The 15 solutes—Mn, Cr, Zr, Mg, Si, Cu, Ti, Fe, Ni, Ce, Sc, Li, Er, Zn, Sn—are varied independently in wt.% with Al as the balance.

### Element-by-element commentary

**Silicon (Si):** The most widely used LPBF Al-alloy family is Al-Si, spanning AlSi7Mg through hypereutectic AlSi20 (knoop2020atailoredalsimg pages 1-3, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). The Al-Si eutectic is approximately 12.6 wt.% Si; equilibrium solid solubility is only ~1.65 wt.%, extended to ~5 at.% under rapid solidification (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 90-93). Near-eutectic Si compositions narrow the freezing range and suppress hot cracking (aversa2019newaluminumalloys pages 1-3, hyer2021compositiondependentsolidificationcracking pages 2-3). The recommended upper bound of 12.6 wt.% covers the eutectic; hypereutectic compositions (up to 20%) are feasible but should be treated as a separate branch.

**Magnesium (Mg):** Scalmalloy contains 3.02–4.77 wt.% Mg, while AlMg5.7 and AlMg6 have been printed for LPBF (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). Equilibrium solubility is very high (~14.9 wt.%), but the practical ceiling is set by evaporation, oxidation, keyhole porosity, and hot cracking rather than solubility (okoro2021irradiationassistedcorrosion pages 22-27, rohilla2024investigationofalcu20si10mga pages 15-17). The tailored AlSi3.5Mg2.5 alloy demonstrates intermediate Mg levels in LPBF (knoop2020atailoredalsimg pages 1-3). Recommended range: 0–6 wt.%.

**Copper (Cu):** LPBF 2xxx-series alloys span AlCu2 through AlCu6.8 (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). Equilibrium solubility is ~5.65 wt.% (2.4 at.%), with rapid-solidification extension to 17–18 at.% (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 90-93). Cu-rich compositions form Al₂Cu precipitation sequences (GP → θ″ → θ′ → θ) that provide age-hardening (rohilla2024investigationofalcu20si10mga pages 19-22, rohilla2024investigationofalcu20si10mga pages 15-17). However, intermediate Cu levels produce broad freezing ranges and severe hot cracking. Recommended range: 0–6 wt.%, with printability screening above ~4.5%.

**Manganese (Mn):** Scalmalloy contains 0.20–0.53 wt.% Mn, and AlMn3 and Al-3Ce-1.9Mn have been printed (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5, wei2025compositionoptimizationand pages 2-4, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). Mn has low equilibrium solubility (~1.8 wt.%) and forms thermally stable Al₆Mn precipitates (wei2025compositionoptimizationand pages 2-4). Mn also beneficially converts harmful Fe-bearing intermetallics to less detrimental α-Al(Fe,Mn)Si morphologies (okoro2021irradiationassistedcorrosion pages 22-27). Recommended range: 0–2 wt.%. UA literature notes significant Mn evaporation during atomization (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 2-4).

**Chromium (Cr):** AlCr3 is listed among LPBF compositions, and an Al-Si-Ni-Cr-Fe alloy contained ~1.2 wt.% Cr (liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15, cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 132-135). Equilibrium solubility is only ~0.77 wt.% (0.44 at.%), though rapid solidification can extend this to 5–6 at.% (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 90-93). Above the solubility limit, Al₇Cr and Al₁₃Cr₂ form; combined with Fe and Mn, a "sludge factor" risk arises. Recommended range: 0–1 wt.%.

**Zirconium (Zr):** Scalmalloy contains 0.08–0.42 wt.% Zr, and various 2xxx/6xxx LPBF alloys use 0.13–0.18 wt.% (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5, cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 100-103, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). The Al-Zr peritectic is at ~0.28 wt.%; above it, primary Al₃Zr can form. The 1300 °C UA ceiling may limit dissolution of concentrated Zr master alloys. Recommended range: 0–0.5 wt.%, with positive levels ≥0.10 to test grain refinement.

**Titanium (Ti):** Used principally as a grain refiner at 0–0.20 wt.% in LPBF alloys (rodriguezgonzalez2023wirearcadditive pages 7-9, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). The Al-Ti peritectic is at ~1.3 wt.%; excess Ti forms coarse primary Al₃Ti. Si can bind Ti in silicides, reducing its grain-refining effectiveness. Recommended range: 0–0.20 wt.%.

**Iron (Fe):** LPBF compositions range from trace levels to AlFe3 (3 wt.%) and rapidly solidified Al-8.5Fe-1.3V-1.7Si (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 103-106, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). Equilibrium solubility is extremely low (~0.05 wt.%, 0.025 at.%), with rapid-solidification extension to 4–6 at.% (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 90-93). Fe promotes brittle β-AlFeSi plates unless Mn is present to modify morphology (okoro2021irradiationassistedcorrosion pages 22-27, cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 132-135). Recommended range: 0–2 wt.% if Fe is an intentional variable; otherwise ≤0.15–0.25 wt.% as an impurity.

**Nickel (Ni):** LPBF Al-Si-Ni alloys used ~5.1 wt.% Ni, near the ternary Al-Si-Ni eutectic (~4.9 Ni, 10.98 Si at 567 °C) (cattano2019feasibilitystudiesona pages 113-117, cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 113-117). Equilibrium solubility is negligible (~0.05 wt.%), but rapid solidification can extend it to 1.2–7.7 at.% (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 90-93). Al₃Ni forms readily; incomplete dissolution gives coarse Ni-rich agglomerates (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 132-135, cattano2019feasibilitystudiesona pages 126-132). Recommended range: 0–5 wt.%.

**Cerium (Ce):** Binary LPBF Al-10Ce has been printed crack-free; the eutectic is estimated at 10–12 wt.% Ce with a melting point of ~640 °C (hesselmann2022effectofprecipitationforming pages 1-3, liu2024reviewoflaser pages 2-4). Ce solubility in Al is negligible (~0.005–0.01 wt.% at RT) (liu2024reviewoflaser pages 2-4). Al₁₁Ce₃ eutectic structures provide thermal stability. Recommended range: 0–10 wt.%.

**Scandium (Sc):** Scalmalloy powders contain 0.20–0.71 wt.% Sc (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5, aversa2019newaluminumalloys pages 8-10, cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 100-103). Equilibrium solubility is ~0.35–0.4 wt.%, with the Al-Sc eutectic at ~0.55 wt.%; rapid solidification at LPBF cooling rates (10⁴–10⁶ K/s) can retain up to ~3 wt.% Sc in supersaturated solid solution (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 90-93, cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5). Primary Al₃(Sc,Zr) seed crystals nucleate equiaxed grains at melt-pool boundaries (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5). Recommended range: 0–0.80 wt.%.

**Lithium (Li):** LPBF Al-Cu-Li-Mg-Ag-Sc-Zr alloys have been produced with 1, 2, and 3 wt.% Li, and a binary Al-3.2Li has also been printed (shao2024microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2, ji2025influenceoflaser pages 2-4). Equilibrium solubility is 4.2 wt.% (shao2024microstructureandmechanical pages 12-14, shao2024microstructureandmechanical pages 14-15). The practical ceiling is set by preferential evaporation (~15% relative Li loss during SLM) and oxidation rather than solubility (shao2024microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). The 3 wt.% Li alloy achieved only ~92% densification (shao2024microstructureandmechanical pages 1-2). Recommended range: 0–3 wt.%, with dedicated inert-atmosphere protocols.

**Erbium (Er):** Al-Er-Zr-Y-Yb-Ni alloys have been designed via hybrid ML/CALPHAD for LPBF; one control composition used ~0.4 at.% Er with ~1 at.% Zr (taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 40-54, taherimousavi2024additivelymanufacturablehighstrength pages 1-4). Er has very low equilibrium solubility (~0.04–0.05 wt.%) and forms Al₃Er precipitates. Recommended range: 0–1 wt.% (provisional; direct wt.% LPBF evidence is sparse).

**Zinc (Zn):** LPBF 7xxx alloys contain 5.4–6.52 wt.% Zn, and Al-6Zn-2Mg has been reported (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 103-106, cattano2019feasibilitystudiesona pages 103-106, liu2024alloydesignfor pages 14-15). Zn has very high equilibrium solubility (~80 wt.% near the eutectic), so the practical ceiling is evaporation, composition drift, and hot cracking rather than solubility (cattano2019feasibilitystudiesona pages 103-106). Recommended range: 0–7 wt.%, with chemistry verification pre- and post-atomization.

**Tin (Sn):** LPBF Al-Sn miscibility-gap alloys have been produced at 20 vol.% Sn (~40 wt.% Sn) for bearing/phase-change applications (rusin2025effectoflaser pages 1-2, confalonieri2022effectofprocess pages 1-2, confalonieri2022effectofprocess pages 2-4). Sn is essentially insoluble in solid Al (~0.06 wt.%). At high levels, segregation and weak interfaces limit structural properties. Recommended range: 0–10 wt.% for a structural campaign; a separate Sn-rich branch (up to ~40 wt.%) may be created for functional applications.

## 4. Total-Solute Cap and Campaign Norms (e)

No published high-throughput or Bayesian-optimization LPBF aluminium-alloy campaign was found that specifies an explicit "Al ≥ X%" total-solute constraint. The most relevant BO campaigns are the BIRDSHOT and BRAVE projects on Al-V-Cr-Fe-Co-Ni(-Mn-Cu) high-entropy alloys, which used element-by-element upper bounds (e.g., Al ≤24 at.%, Ni ≤60 at.%) combined with CALPHAD phase-stability filters (FCC stability above 700 °C, solidification range <100 K), rather than a simple total-solute cap (mulukutla2026abravealloy pages 4-5, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 3-5, hastings2024aninteroperablemulti pages 2-3). The Mamun et al. (2025) study applied multi-objective BO (qEHVI) to aluminium alloy optimization but detailed composition bounds were not retrieved from the main text (mamun2025accelerateddevelopmentof pages 1-4, mamun2025accelerateddevelopmentof pages 4-8).

In practice, the vast majority of published LPBF aluminium alloys have Al ≥ 80 wt.% by the nature of the alloy system; the most solute-laden structural examples are Al-10Si-0.3Mg (~89% Al), Scalmalloy (~93.9% Al), and Al-10Ce (~90% Al). Only specialty compositions such as Al-20Si-5Fe-3Cu-1Mg (~71% Al) or Al-40Sn (~60% Al) fall below 80%. **A total-solute cap of Al ≥ 80 wt.% (i.e., ≤20 wt.% total solute) is recommended** as a pragmatic default for structural alloys. This is conservative enough to encompass all major alloy families (Al-Si, Al-Mg-Sc, Al-Cu, Al-Ce, Al-Zn-Mg) while excluding metallurgically impractical extremes. An Al ≥ 75% (25% solute) cap would additionally allow hypereutectic Al-Si and high-Sn compositions, which may be appropriate for specific functional applications.

### Key findings:

**AlSi10Mg** is the best-documented LPBF powder. Measured compositions include 10.7 wt.% Si, 0.48 wt.% Mg, 0.19 wt.% Fe, and 0.01 wt.% Ti (cattano2019feasibilitystudieson pages 126-132). The Fe tolerance of ≤0.55 wt.% is relatively generous because the near-eutectic Si content can accommodate Fe-bearing intermetallics.

**Scalmalloy** powders show Fe 0.01–0.13 wt.% and Si 0.01–0.06 wt.%, which are far tighter than AlSi10Mg, reflecting the sensitivity of Al-Mg-Sc alloys to Fe and Si impurities (cerri2024mainheattreatments pages 2-5).

**Master-alloy base selection:** Using 99.7% commercial-purity Al as the base introduces ~0.2% Fe and ~0.1% Si as background impurities. This is acceptable for Si-rich alloys (where Fe ≤0.55% is tolerated) but may be problematic for alloy corners where Fe and Si are both intentionally near zero (e.g., Al-Ce or Al-Mg-Sc compositions). For these cases, 99.99% high-purity Al (Fe+Si < 0.01%) is preferable, though more expensive. A practical compromise is to use 99.7% Al when Si ≥ 3% (since Si is already high) and 99.99% Al when Si < 1% and Fe must be ≤0.15%.

**Oxygen:** LPBF Al-alloy powder oxygen content is typically 200–800 ppm and increases with recycling and handling. One Al-Li alloy powder was reported at 1500 ppm O (ji2025influenceoflaser pages 2-4). UA under argon with induction melting should target <500 ppm O in fresh powder, verified by inert-gas fusion analysis for every batch.

**Hydrogen:** No retrieved source specified a commercial LPBF Al-powder H tolerance, but the casting industry standard of <0.1 mL/100g (equivalent to ~1 ppm by weight) is widely applied. UA under dry argon is expected to keep H well below this threshold.

## 6. Ultrasonic Atomization Constraints

UA with induction melting under argon at a ~1300 °C ceiling is compatible with all common aluminium alloy compositions, since the Al liquidus is ~660 °C and most binary eutectics are below 700 °C. The optimal atomization temperature is 1.3–1.5× the absolute melting temperature (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 2-4), which for pure Al corresponds to ~940–1130 °C; the 1300 °C ceiling provides adequate superheat for most alloys. Key UA-specific considerations include:

- **Volatile element loss:** UA literature reports significant Mn and Zn evaporation, especially in the finest powder fractions (scientific2023comparisonofultrasonic pages 2-4). Li and Mg are also expected to suffer evaporation losses. Pre- and post-atomization chemical analysis is essential for all batches, particularly those containing Li, Zn, Mg, or Mn.
- **Batch size:** ~100 g charges are standard for lab-scale UA, which is appropriate for the described campaign.
- **Efficiency:** Induction melting provides the highest UA efficiency (~50% usable powder) compared to arc or plasma sources (jedynak2024processabilityofaluminummatrix pages 3-6).
- **High-melting elements:** Zr (melting point 1855 °C), Cr (1907 °C), and Ti (1668 °C) must be supplied as pre-dissolved master alloys (e.g., Al-10Zr, Al-10Cr, Al-10Ti) rather than pure metals, to ensure dissolution at ≤1300 °C.

## 8. Evidence Gaps

The following areas lack direct LPBF-specific published evidence and rely on established phase-diagram knowledge or provisional estimates:
- Equilibrium solid solubility of Mn, Zr, Ti, Er, Zn, and Sn in Al (standard phase-diagram values were used but not retrieved from the LPBF literature).
- Si suppression of Ti/Zr grain-refining effectiveness under LPBF conditions.
- Mn-Cr-Fe sludge-factor applicability to LPBF (a casting heuristic).
- Complete A20X powder specification.
- Quantitative O and H specifications for commercial LPBF Al-alloy powders.
- Published Al-LPBF Bayesian-optimization campaigns with explicit total-solute caps.
- Er wt.% ranges validated in LPBF experiments (most data are in at.% from computational studies).
