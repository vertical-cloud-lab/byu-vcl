## Inert gas in the booth: no oxygen detection, within limits

**Rule.** At most **two** argon or nitrogen cylinders in the inert-gas booth in 154 CB, **not manifolded**: each cylinder feeds its own piece of equipment. **No dewar** (no liquid argon or nitrogen). Under that limit, no oxygen detection is required.

**Before going beyond it**, ask Bryant Brown first. That means a third cylinder, two cylinders manifolded together, a dewar, or any other change that raises the volume of inert gas in the booth.

**Contact.** Bryant Brown, Industrial Hygienist, College of Engineering.

**Date and source.** Email from Bryant Brown, 8 October 2026, about the inert-gas booth in 154 CB:

> So long as we limit the gas to one or two cylinders containing argon &/or nitrogen, then we don't need to pursue oxygen detection. ... Please let me know if the work/setup changes in a manner that necessitates an increased volume of inert gas and we can revisit whether oxygen detection is needed.

Sterling Baird's reply the same day set out the setup the ruling covers: one cylinder now (the 5N argon for the rePowder atomizer) and at most two later, not manifolded, each on its own piece of equipment, and no dewar.

**In practice.**

- A spare cylinder stored in the booth counts toward the two, as does a second instrument's cylinder (e.g. for an argon glove box, [#30](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/issues/30)).
- This ruling is the current answer to the atomizer trainer's suggestion of a room oxygen sensor ([atomizer SOP, Safety and PPE](../atomizer-training/sop.md#safety-and-ppe)). If the setup outgrows the rule, oxygen detection is back on the table and the decision is Bryant Brown's.
