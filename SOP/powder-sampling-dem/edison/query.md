Context: We make small batches (roughly 5-100 g) of aluminium alloy powder (AlSi10Mg, and blends of Al 6063 with Al 4047, spherical, mostly 15-63 µm) by ultrasonic atomization. The powder falls through a cone into a collection container (a stainless tube a few cm across), so the first powder made ends up at the bottom and the last at the top; powder brushed off the chamber walls is added on top at the end. We want to know whether per-particle composition (mainly Si, measured by SEM-EDS on about 30 particles per sample) drifts during a run, for example because the melt was not fully mixed, so we need top, middle and bottom samples whose composition reflects their depth in the container. Particle size is not the target here; compositional homogeneity is.

Question: Using discrete element method (DEM) simulations and tracer or layered-bed experiments in the literature, how well do these sampling approaches preserve the vertical (time-of-production) layering of a shallow bed of fine, somewhat cohesive powder, and which is best?
(1) an open glass tube pushed to the bottom, closed with a thumb or stopper, lifted out and emptied in thirds;
(2) a piston corer (a plunger held at the powder surface while the tube is pushed down around it, then the core extruded in thirds);
(3) a commercial or 3D-printed multi-compartment sampling thief;
(4) removing layers one at a time with a spatula or spoonula;
(5) pouring the container out while moving it along a straight line over a plate, then sampling at intervals along the line, so that position along the line maps to depth in the container.
For each, what does the evidence say about smearing at layer boundaries, drag-down of upper layers by the inserted tool, core shortening or plugging from wall friction, and whether pouring preserves order (first in, last out; mixing in the surface avalanche)? Give quantitative values where reported (mixing indices, boundary smear width in particle diameters, sampling bias), the DEM contact models, particle counts and coarse-graining used, and say how the conclusions might change for cohesive 15-63 µm aluminium powder. Finally, how many particles per sample and how many samples are needed for SEM-EDS to detect a given Si difference between layers?
