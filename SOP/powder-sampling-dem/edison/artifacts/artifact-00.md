| **1. Open glass tube pushed to bottom, sealed, then divided** | Likely appreciable: the open mouth admits powder continuously, while wall friction drags upper material downward. No direct value exists for this exact method; conventional side thieves showed **90% upper-layer material 10 mm below an interface**, and material could originate along the entire insertion path (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000027). | **High risk.** A comparable 15 mm core tube recovered only **60%** of insertion depth. | **2. Piston corer: plunger held at surface while tube advances, then core extruded** | Among insertion tools, relatively low but nonzero. Piston extrusion reverses powder motion, can require much greater stress, further compact the sample, and cause reverse drag (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000028). | Usually narrower than an ordinary thief’s, but boundaries are compressed and smeared downward during insertion and potentially upward during extrusion. | **Fair to good if corrected.** A continuous core retains sequence better than thieves, but thirds should be assigned using original-depth calibration rather than assumed from recovered length. | Cohesion helps retain the core but makes plugging and extrusion stress worse. Use a thin-wall, sharp, polished, large-bore tube; extrude slowly and discard narrow boundary slices (pqac-00000026, pqac-00000028). | **Second-best practical option** after layerwise removal; preferable to an open tube or ordinary thief. |
| **3. Multi-compartment commercial or 3D-printed thief** | Strongly design-dependent. Reported maximum composition errors were about **100%** for a commercial side thief, **30%** for a blunt slug/end thief, and **10–20%** for a streamlined pointed end thief. Even the best device collected particles initially **5–10 cm** from the target location; side and slug samplers had **90%** and **45%** upper-layer contamination 10 mm below an interface (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000014, pqac-00000027). | Side cavities may underfill when cohesive powder cannot flow into them; cavities can also fill before opening. Internal partitions do not prevent disturbance along the insertion path (pqac-00000012, pqac-00000013). | Conventional side-sampling produced mixed color within **two aperture diameters (3.25 mm)** of a boundary. Every compartment may sample a sheath already contaminated by insertion drag (pqac-00000013). | **Poor for chronological depth profiling.** Simultaneous nominal depths are attractive, but collected material may represent the insertion path rather than the original local layer. | Particularly unfavorable: cohesion impedes cavity filling, while tribocharging and rough 3D-printed surfaces can increase adhesion. Smear is set mainly by scoop penetration, surface roughness, and sidewall residue; avoid digging and exclude deliberate wall-brushings from the time series.
