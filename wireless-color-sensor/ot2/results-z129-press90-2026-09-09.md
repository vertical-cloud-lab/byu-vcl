# Slot 7 colour scan at read z 129 with a 0.5 mm deeper pickup press

**2026-09-09, ~02:21 UTC.** Requested on [#197](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/issues/197):
read the three colours 1 mm higher, and press 0.5 mm deeper at pickup for a
tighter fit, after the module worked loose and came off its base at the end of
the previous session.

    run_xscan_test.py --scan-slot 7 --read-z 129 --press-z 90.0

`--press-z` is new. It also shifts the release height by the same delta
(95.5 → 95.0), so the module is set back down from the absolute height the
proven recipe released it from rather than dropped from 0.5 mm up.

## Outcome

The cycle completed with no aborts and the module is back on its base.

| stage | result |
| --- | --- |
| Enclosure on the deck at the start | confirmed by photo before any motion |
| Seated baseline | 445 counts |
| Grip check | 445 → 2058 = **4.6×** (threshold 2.0) |
| Three positions | complete, 3 reads each |
| Reseat confirmation | **436** — back to the seated level |
| Deck after | module on its base, all three vials standing |

**One clean cycle is not yet evidence that the deeper press fixed the drop.**
The previous session's three-position cycle also completed cleanly; the module
was lost during the *second*, longer cycle that followed it. Testing the fix
means stressing it — a repeat cycle, or the nine-position sweep — not a single
pass.

The grip ratio (4.6× here, 5.1–5.3× in earlier runs) is **not** a measure of how
tight the fit is. It is a light reading at the lift height, and the deeper press
seats the nozzle further into the socket, so the module rides ~0.5 mm higher and
the aperture sits ~0.5 mm further from the deck at that same lift. The ratio
answers "did the module leave its base", nothing more.

## Colour: still only yellow, and probably not by direct view

The 440 nm share — the channel yellow paint absorbs — against the empty slot at
the same X:

| X (mm) | empty, z 128 | vials, z 128 | vials, z 129 | reading |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 33.88 | 3.73 % | **2.40 %** | **2.26 %** | yellow absorber, reproduced at two heights |
| 63.88 | 5.09 % | 5.01 % | 4.30 % | changed *between the two paint runs* — unresolved |
| 93.88 | 2.91 % | 2.91 % | 2.79 % | all three agree — bare deck |

470 nm tracks it (4.59 → 3.38 % at x = 33.88), and 550/583 nm rise. That is the
signature of yellow, and it holds at two different aperture heights, so it is
the sample and not the height.

**But the vials are not under the scan line.** They stand beside the enclosure's
base, near the slot 7 / slot 10 boundary at y ≈ 265–275. The scan reads at
**y = 225.0**, some 40–50 mm away, so the aperture never passes over a vial. What
x = 33.88 registers is light bleeding in from the nearest and brightest of the
three, not a measurement of a sample under the sensor. That also explains why
only yellow appears: it is by far the most reflective of the three, so it
dominates any off-axis stray light, and red and blue never rise above the deck.

The dip that appeared at the slot centre in this run but not the last one is
consistent with the same mechanism — a 1.5 mm higher aperture sees a wider field
— but it could equally be the rack having shifted when the module was reseated by
hand. It is not a confirmed detection either way.

`x = 93.88` is worth noting as a caution: its spectrum is markedly 620-heavy
(28.6 % against 20.9 % at the slot centre) in the empty run *and* both paint
runs. Bare deck is not spectrally flat and is not the same at every position, so
"looks reddish" is not evidence of a red sample. The per-position empty
reference is the only valid baseline.

## What the paint test needs

**Stand the three vials in slot 7, along X, on the read points themselves** —
(33.88, 225), (63.88, 225), (93.88, 225). Then each of the three positions sits
over one colour and the existing test reads all three with no code change. Today
the rack is one slot behind the scan line, which is why two of the three colours
have still never been measured.

Re-baseline the empty slot at whatever height is finally used, since the
per-position deck spectrum is the reference every reading is divided by.

`--rgb` remains inert — no LED lights at any level (`led-probe-2026-09-09.json`),
so every number here is ambient light reflected off the sample.
