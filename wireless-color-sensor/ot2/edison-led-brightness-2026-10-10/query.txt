We measure the colour of acrylic paint (about 200 uL per well, roughly 10:1 water:paint) in a clear polystyrene 96-well plate with an ams OSRAM AS7341 11-channel spectral sensor (8 visible channels centred near 415, 445, 480, 515, 555, 590, 630 and 680 nm, plus Clear and NIR). The sensor sits inside a 3D-printed white PLA enclosure that a pipetting robot holds a few millimetres above the well. The sensor board (an Adafruit AS7341 breakout) has an on-board phosphor-converted white LED driven by the AS7341's own LED driver; we can set it from 4 to 20 mA (the chip allows up to 258 mA). Stray light from the robot's overhead white-LED rail lights also reaches the sensor, partly through the clear plate. With the LED at 4 mA the sensor already saturates at 128x gain; at 32x gain 10 mA and 20 mA give about 2.4-2.5x and 5.1x the 4 mA reading, and the ratio differs by about 5% between the 440 nm and 470 nm channels.

Question: for each sample, is there meaningful additional information, spectral or for calibration and accuracy, from taking readings at several LED drive currents (for example off, 4 mA, 10 mA and 20 mA) rather than one reading at one current? Specifically:
1. Do white-LED emission spectra change shape with drive current (blue-to-phosphor ratio, blue peak shift, junction-temperature effects, phosphor saturation) enough to act as extra "illuminants" for multispectral reflectance estimation? How large are those changes for small SMD white LEDs between about 4 and 20 mA, and how stable are they with temperature?
2. Does a differential reading (LED on minus LED off, or the slope of counts against current) remove ambient light, stray light and sensor offsets better than a single LED-on reading, and what are its noise costs?
3. Does bracketing LED intensity, gain or integration time (HDR-style) improve accuracy for dark samples compared with a single well-exposed reading at fixed gain, and what limits it (shot noise, ADC linearity, gain-step calibration errors, saturation, LED warm-up drift)?
4. Are there published studies using the AS7341 or similar low-cost multichannel colour sensors (TCS34725, AS7262/AS7263, AS7265x) with multiple illumination intensities, multiple LEDs or LED on/off differencing for reflectance measurement or colorimetry, and what accuracy gains did they report?
5. For reflectance measurements of liquids in microplates with a co-located LED and detector, what geometry problems (specular reflection from the liquid surface, light piping through clear plate walls, enclosure-wall reflections) are known, and how are they usually mitigated?
Please give quantitative numbers where available and cite sources.
