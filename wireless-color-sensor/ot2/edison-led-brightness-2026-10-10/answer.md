Question: We measure the colour of acrylic paint (about 200 uL per well, roughly 10:1 water:paint) in a clear polystyrene 96-well plate with an ams OSRAM AS7341 11-channel spectral sensor (8 visible channels centred near 415, 445, 480, 515, 555, 590, 630 and 680 nm, plus Clear and NIR). The sensor sits inside a 3D-printed white PLA enclosure that a pipetting robot holds a few millimetres above the well. The sensor board (an Adafruit AS7341 breakout) has an on-board phosphor-converted white LED driven by the AS7341's own LED driver; we can set it from 4 to 20 mA (the chip allows up to 258 mA). Stray light from the robot's overhead white-LED rail lights also reaches the sensor, partly through the clear plate. With the LED at 4 mA the sensor already saturates at 128x gain; at 32x gain 10 mA and 20 mA give about 2.4-2.5x and 5.1x the 4 mA reading, and the ratio differs by about 5% between the 440 nm and 470 nm channels.

Question: for each sample, is there meaningful additional information, spectral or for calibration and accuracy, from taking readings at several LED drive currents (for example off, 4 mA, 10 mA and 20 mA) rather than one reading at one current? Specifically:
1. Do white-LED emission spectra change shape with drive current (blue-to-phosphor ratio, blue peak shift, junction-temperature effects, phosphor saturation) enough to act as extra "illuminants" for multispectral reflectance estimation? How large are those changes for small SMD white LEDs between about 4 and 20 mA, and how stable are they with temperature?
2. Does a differential reading (LED on minus LED off, or the slope of counts against current) remove ambient light, stray light and sensor offsets better than a single LED-on reading, and what are its noise costs?
3. Does bracketing LED intensity, gain or integration time (HDR-style) improve accuracy for dark samples compared with a single well-exposed reading at fixed gain, and what limits it (shot noise, ADC linearity, gain-step calibration errors, saturation, LED warm-up drift)?
4. Are there published studies using the AS7341 or similar low-cost multichannel colour sensors (TCS34725, AS7262/AS7263, AS7265x) with multiple illumination intensities, multiple LEDs or LED on/off differencing for reflectance measurement or colorimetry, and what accuracy gains did they report?
5. For reflectance measurements of liquids in microplates with a co-located LED and detector, what geometry problems (specular reflection from the liquid surface, light piping through clear plate walls, enclosure-wall reflections) are known, and how are they usually mitigated?
Please give quantitative numbers where available and cite sources.


## Assessment

**Take an LED-off reading and one well-exposed LED-on reading for each well.** This is likely to improve robustness more than collecting on-readings at 4, 10 and 20 mA. Use a second exposure only when the first saturates or is too close to the noise floor. A current sweep can be useful during *instrument characterization*, but the available evidence does not establish an accuracy gain from treating this particular white LED at 4–20 mA as several independent illuminants. (budnyk2024spectralfeaturesof pages 2-4, besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 10-10, finnerty2021howtodesign pages 2-3)

### 1. Does current change the illumination spectrum?

Yes, potentially: phosphor-white emission combines a comparatively narrow blue pump with broad converted emission. Changes in pump wavelength, blue-to-phosphor balance and junction temperature can change the normalized spectrum, not merely its brightness. In a directly relevant study of a white InGaN/YAG LED, the approximately 443-nm pump and 540- and 570-nm phosphor bands each increased approximately linearly over **1–20 mA**; the authors describe a slowly declining phosphor-conversion ratio as current increased, but do not give a defensible percentage change specifically between 4 and 20 mA. Thus, that study does *not* establish a useful second illuminant for the Adafruit board. (budnyk2024spectralfeaturesof pages 2-4, budnyk2024spectralfeaturesof pages 4-6, budnyk2024spectralfeaturesof media bfbb28b3, budnyk2024spectralfeaturesof media f7889644)

Temperature is a separate source of spectral change. For a different, higher-power white LED operated at 350 mA, raising temperature from **20 to 80 °C** reduced phosphor quantum efficiency by approximately **7%** and phosphor-conversion efficiency by approximately **6%**; these are *not* predictions for a 4–20-mA SMD LED. Blue-pump shifts with current and temperature can act in opposite directions. A review describes conventional YAG:Ce conversion as fast enough that phosphor-emission saturation is not expected at ordinary LED excitation fluxes; apparent changes at these currents should not automatically be attributed to phosphor saturation. (ryu2015analysisofthe pages 4-6, fan2017colorshiftfailure pages 3-4, smet2011selectingconversionphosphors pages 13-14)

**Interpret the observed 5% difference between the approximately 440- and 470-nm channel ratios as a diagnostic, not proof of an extra spectral measurement.** It could reflect a change in the blue pump or phosphor balance, but the same measurement also contains ambient illumination, sample-dependent reflections and possible detector nonlinearity. Test a *fixed white/neutral reference* at identical gain, integration time, position and LED timing, with LED-off subtraction. Compare each current’s **normalized, reference-corrected eight-channel vector** over repeated runs and board temperatures. Only spectral differences that exceed repeatability and remain stable across temperature warrant a multispectral model; measure the LED spectrum with a reference spectrometer if that distinction matters. Spectrally independently controlled LEDs are a substantially stronger approach: a tunable-illumination camera study reduced experimental ColorChecker mean ΔE*ab from **2.61 to 1.45**, but its multiple LED spectra cannot be equated with dimming one white LED. (budnyk2024spectralfeaturesof pages 2-4, zhu2022matchedilluminationusing pages 3-5, zhu2022matchedilluminationusing pages 13-15)

### 2. What does LED-on minus LED-off remove?

At unchanged gain and integration time, model a channel as `on = LED-mediated signal + ambient + electronic offset` and `off = ambient + electronic offset`. Their difference removes the latter two terms **to the extent they remain unchanged between exposures**. A fitted intercept and current slope provide a similar additive-background diagnostic; fit against the *measured reference response*, rather than assuming emitted flux is proportional to programmed milliamperes. LED-off sampling is established practice in reflective optical sensing. It does **not** remove LED light that bypasses the paint by reflecting from the meniscus, clear plastic, enclosure or sensor board: those paths turn on *with* the LED. Nor can subtraction recover an on-reading already saturated by LED plus ambient. (finnerty2021howtodesign pages 2-3, finnerty2021howtodesign pages 3-4, roales2023colorimetricgasdetection pages 4-6)

The noise cost follows directly from subtracting independent exposures. If on and off collect mean photoelectrons `S+B` and `B`, respectively, and each has input-referred read-noise variance `σᵣ²`, the difference has variance **`S + 2B + 2σᵣ²`**, versus **`S+B+σᵣ²`** for the uncorrected on-reading. For read-noise-dominated exposures of equal duration, subtraction therefore increases standard deviation by **√2** relative to one reading; its benefit is removal of *bias*, not intrinsically lower random noise. Average repeated on/off pairs if needed. Interleave them closely—or use off–on–off and interpolate the off baseline—to limit drift and overhead-rail flicker. Keep gain and integration time identical within each subtraction pair. Specialized pulsed optical front ends have reported **60 dB** ambient rejection, but that result must not be ascribed to sequential AS7341 readings under flickering rail lights. (finnerty2021howtodesign pages 3-4, finnerty2021howtodesign pages 2-3)

### 3. When does HDR-style bracketing help?

Bracketing extends **usable dynamic range** when bright wells or specular returns saturate an exposure that dark wells need. It does not improve on an already unsaturated, well-exposed measurement of a dark well at the same practical acquisition-time budget: acquiring more useful photons at an appropriate exposure, or averaging, is the benefit. Changing LED current adds possible source-spectrum and heating changes; changing analogue gain adds cross-setting calibration requirements. In an AS7341/AS7262/AS7343 characterization, normalized responses were substantially more stable when **integration time varied at fixed gain** (reported coefficient of variation of order **10⁻⁴**) than when gain varied (up to approximately **10⁻²**). The authors retained data from **10–80% of full scale**, excluding the low-noise-floor region and possible near-saturation compression, and obtained channel calibration fits with **R² > 0.998** using quadratic models. Another AS7341 study reported exposure-time linear fits of **R² > 0.99**. These are controlled-instrument results, not guaranteed error bounds for this plate and enclosure. (besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 10-10, besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 3-4, besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 6-7, song2025developmentandapplication pages 4-6)

For this setup, first choose a **fixed gain below the observed 128× saturation** and the longest practical integration time that leaves headroom in *every* channel, including Clear. If dark samples then require more signal, increase integration time or collect repeated pairs; use a shorter exposure for wells whose on-readings clip. Calibrate any exposure scaling with reference and dark measurements, and verify linearity rather than trusting gain labels or a 16-bit numerical maximum as a guarantee against earlier analogue saturation. Standardize the delay between setting LED current and integrating; an off–on–off sequence and interleaved reference wells can reveal warm-up or thermal drift. Do not combine a saturated high-current frame with unsaturated frames in a current-slope fit. (besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 3-4, besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 6-7, benman2023highthroughputfeedbackenabledoptogenetic pages 6-7)

### 4. What has been demonstrated with inexpensive sensors?

The published numbers distinguish *different improvements*, not a measured benefit of sweeping the AS7341 board LED:

| Study | Relevant finding | What the number does **not** show |
|---|---|---|
| AS7341/related-sensor calibration, Besozzi *et al.* | Under tested broadband sources, AS7341 photometric/radiometric reconstruction errors averaged approximately **8.3%**; exposure-time normalization was more stable than gain changes. | No paint-reflectance or multiple-board-LED-current comparison. (besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 10-10) |
| TCS34725 colorimetry, Strachan *et al.* | ColorChecker mean **ΔE*ab 28.89 → 11.62**, approximately **60% less error**, after color calibration. | Improvement is from calibration, **not** multiple illumination currents. (strachan2023maximisingaffordabilityof pages 17-18) |
| AS7265x measurement of water-based nutrient solutions, Stevens *et al.* | Changing from reflection to a mirror-backed **5-mm transflection** geometry improved the reported calibration-model **R² from 0.981 to 0.992** and made all 18 channels responsive in their concentration tests. | Geometry and analyte differ from acrylic paint; these are model-fit statistics, not reflectance-color errors. (stevens2023developmentandtesting pages 5-8) |
| TCS34725 dual source/geometry, Roales *et al.* | Internal LED for reflection and external LED for transmission were tested; the internal LED provided greater separation of RGB readings for films measured through a glass vial. | No quantitative gain for varying drive current of one LED. (roales2023colorimetricgasdetection pages 4-6, roales2023colorimetricgasdetection pages 8-10) |
| AS7265x handheld reflectance, Noguera *et al.* | The device permits adjustable LED current and took two spectra per grape sample, with a **53%-reflectance reference every 15 samples** to control drift. | No reported per-current comparison demonstrating extra spectral information. (noguera2022newlowcosthandheld pages 4-6, noguera2022newlowcosthandheld pages 6-8) |

A further AS7341 reflectance study used fixed white-LED illumination, ten 100-ms measurements, a closed light box and deliberately chosen source/detector angles to avoid specular light. It did **not** compare current sweeps or report an accuracy gain attributable to LED-off subtraction. I found no directly applicable published percentage improvement for **off/4/10/20-mA readings versus one properly exposed AS7341 reading** of paint in clear wells. (guenther2025multispectralreadoutof pages 5-7, guenther2025multispectralreadoutof pages 2-5)

### 5. Is the signal actually paint reflectance?

Not necessarily. A smooth liquid surface and the air–liquid and plastic interfaces can return relatively sample-independent light; meniscus shape, fill level, bubbles and robot Z-position change the rays collected. With a co-located emitter and detector, LED light can also reach the detector after scattering or reflecting from **clear walls, the well bottom, neighboring wells, white PLA or the board**, without making the intended passage through paint. Such LED-correlated background survives off-subtraction and can disproportionately flatten differences among dark samples. Published microplate instrumentation explicitly calibrates optical-density LEDs **with liquid present** to account for refraction, and reports that well calibration reduced OD-LED variation from **16.6% to 0.5% CV**; it notes run-to-run changes associated with sample refraction and alignment. An AS7341 reflectance setup likewise separated its target return from specular reflection by choosing illumination and detection angles and closing the housing against ambient light. (benman2023highthroughputfeedbackenabledoptogenetic pages 6-7, guenther2025multispectralreadoutof pages 5-7, guenther2025multispectralreadoutof pages 2-5)

Prioritize a **light-tight, matte-black shroud and internal baffle between emitter and detector**, with a reproducible height and aperture restricted to the central well; test whether white PLA itself supplies a substantial LED-on background. If feasible, move illumination off-axis while viewing near normal, or choose angles that send surface specular reflection away from the detector. Measure an empty well, a water-only well at the same fill, an opaque dark target and neighboring wells with different contents to map background and crosstalk; vary fill height and probe Z-position during validation. Use paint-matched reference wells and consistent dilution/mixing because the optical signal can depend on scattering and depth as well as pigment absorption. A matched blank characterizes a *specific* optical path; subtracting its full LED-on signal is not, by itself, a universally valid correction for every differently scattering paint. If quantitative liquid absorbance rather than top-view appearance is the objective, assess a controlled transmission or transflection geometry instead. Black housing and explicit ambient-light blocking were used in a TCS34725 optical prototype, while an AS7265x aqueous study found that changing the optical geometry materially changed analytical performance. (roales2023colorimetricgasdetection pages 4-6, stevens2023developmentandtesting pages 5-8, benman2023highthroughputfeedbackenabledoptogenetic pages 6-7)

**Practical decision:** characterize 4/10/20 mA once on stable references and representative paints, including repeated off measurements and a temperature/drift check. If reference-corrected channel shapes do not differ reproducibly—or their differences do not reduce held-out color error against an independent reference—retain only the current/exposure combination that gives the best unsaturated signal, plus a paired LED-off reading. Spend additional acquisition time on repeatability and optical-path control before treating a 5% current-dependent channel-ratio difference as independent spectral information. (budnyk2024spectralfeaturesof pages 2-4, besozzi2026flexiblecalibrationmethod pages 10-10, benman2023highthroughputfeedbackenabledoptogenetic pages 6-7)
