# Shopping search: power for the wrist-mounted Pi 5 (27 September 2026)

This is a shortlist of ways to get power up the PiPER to the Pi 5, searched through a lab Pi's
connection. Prices are as shown on 27 September 2026. All 69 candidates, with URLs, specs and how
each was checked, are in [`shopping_2026-09-27.json`](shopping_2026-09-27.json). Why 5 V struggles
over this distance is covered in [`README.md`](README.md) and [`voltage_drop.py`](voltage_drop.py).

## What the search found

- No 5.1 V / 5 A supply comes with a lead longer than 1.5 m. The longest are Raspberry Pi's own
  45 W supply (1.5 m, 17 AWG), Argon's 27 W (1.5 m) and TRASKIT's (59 in). CanaKit's 45 W lead is
  4 ft by Amazon's listing and 5 ft by CanaKit's own page.
- I found no 5.1 V / 5 A PD supply with a detachable cable. The three I could check (Raspberry
  Pi's 27 W and 45 W, and CanaKit's 45 W) have captive leads, and no listing offered a USB-C
  socket. That matters because a supply with a USB-C socket may only offer more than 3 A once it has
  detected a 5 A e-marked cable. So a long 240 W cable only gets the 5 A profile from a supply that
  offers 5.1 V / 5 A through a socket, and I found no such supply.
- Raspberry Pi's 45 W supply (SC1731, launched April 2025) offers 5.1 V / 5 A plus 9 V / 5 A,
  12 V / 3.75 A, 15 V / 3 A and 20 V / 2.25 A. PiShop.us had it in stock at $16.95. On Amazon it
  was only available from resellers at $39.99.
- Raspberry Pi's own PoE+ HAT for the Pi 5 is still not on sale. In March 2025 Raspberry Pi said it
  was "in the final stages of development", and I found no later launch. Waveshare's PoE HAT (J)
  and (G) state that they fit together with the Pi 5 Active Cooler.
- None of the USB-C PoE splitters claims PD. They all give plain 5 V at 4 to 5 A. UCTRONICS says
  so outright, and Waveshare's page doesn't mention PD.
- Only one buck converter is sold as PD for the Pi 5: an eleUniverse board, $20.99. Its listing
  never says outright that the output offers 5.1 V / 5 A, so treat that as unverified. PlusRoc's
  listing says plainly that its converter has no PD.
- No magnetic adapter publishes a pull-off force. That covers Adafruit 5521, CTL's breakaway
  adapter and the eight Amazon listings recorded.

A plain 5 V source (a PoE HAT, a PoE splitter or a non-PD buck) is workable. The Pi 5 then limits
its USB ports to 600 mA and warns at boot. That doesn't matter here, because both cameras are CSI.
If the source really can deliver 5 A, set `PSU_MAX_CURRENT=5000` in the bootloader EEPROM config,
which skips PD negotiation
([docs](https://github.com/raspberrypi/documentation/blob/master/documentation/asciidoc/computers/raspberry-pi/eeprom-bootloader.adoc)).
Alternatively, `usb_max_current_enable=1` in `/boot/firmware/config.txt` lifts the USB limit on
its own.

Also considered:

- The Waveshare industrial 5 V / 5 A splitter
  ([B0D7955R6N](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D7955R6N), $31.99; $19.99 from Waveshare). It is
  isolated, in an aluminium case of 102.5 x 29.6 x 24.8 mm.
- The 52Pi P30 PoE+ HAT, which comes with an Active Cooler
  ([B0D7927F4X](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D7927F4X), $33.24; 5.1 V / 4.5 A).
- The TRENDnet TPE-115GI injector ([B00BK4W8TQ](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00BK4W8TQ), $24.99,
  4.7 from 1,308 reviews).
- Raspberry Pi's own
  [PoE+ Injector](https://www.raspberrypi.com/news/raspberry-pi-poe-injector-on-sale-now-at-25/)
  ($25). It needs a separate IEC C13 mains lead.
- Waveshare's PoE HAT (F) and UCTRONICS's HATs, which bring their own fan in place of the Active
  Cooler. The UCTRONICS HAT sold as 802.3af-only
  ([B0DBHFQ1TC](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DBHFQ1TC)) gets about 13 W at the device, which leaves
  little or no headroom when the Pi draws 2.5 A.

A HAT stacks over the Active Cooler, which faces outward (+X) in the mount. The design hasn't
been checked for that extra height, so check the envelope before ordering one. The RJ45 latch also doesn't break away (README).

## How the search was done

I used headed Chrome on the CI runner, driven only by X input. Its traffic went through a SOCKS
tunnel (Tailscale SSH `-D`) to the CubXL Pi, capped at 300 kB/s, using `tools/headed-browser` from
branch `claude/issue-30-20260925-0724`. The Pi exits through BYU's campus network (AS6510), and
Amazon placed the delivery location in Provo.

- There were 21 page loads through the Pi: Amazon home 1, Amazon search 10, Amazon product pages 8,
  Adafruit 1 and PiShop.us 1.
- No CAPTCHA, "sorry" page or other bot check appeared.
- Each Amazon page took about 20 to 30 s to load at the cap. Over 27 minutes the relay carried about 196 MB from
  the Pi and 19 MB to it, averaging about 120 kB/s.
- Pages were saved with Ctrl+S (HTML only), which makes no new request. They were then parsed for
  ASIN, price, rating, reviews and specs.
