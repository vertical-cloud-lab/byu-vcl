# Shopping search: non-Raspberry Pi supplies long enough for the wrist (8 October 2026)

The question was whether any supply not made by Raspberry Pi gives the Pi 5 enough power and has
a lead long enough to reach the wrist, 3 to 3.5 m from the arm's base.

**How it was searched:** through the CubXL Pi's campus connection, with Amazon showing delivery to
Provo. There were six Amazon searches and seven product pages, with no bot checks. All 121 results,
the product pages' specs and the PiShop.us prices are in
[`shopping_2026-10-08.json`](shopping_2026-10-08.json). The voltages below are from
[`voltage_drop.py`](voltage_drop.py), at the 1.5 A the Pi draws streaming both cameras and the
2.5 A it draws with a busy CPU. The Pi flags under-voltage below 4.63 V.

## What the search found

- **No 5 V supply has both the power and the reach.**
  - Searches for 10 ft Pi 5 and Pi 4 supplies turned up no Pi supply with a longer lead. Where a
    supply with 3 A or more at 5 V gives its length, it's 1 to 1.5 m: Argon's 27 W (1.5 m),
    iUniker's 5 V / 4 A (5 ft) and Argon's 18 W (1 m). On 27 September it was the same for
    Raspberry Pi's 45 W (1.5 m), CanaKit's 45 W (4 to 5 ft) and TRASKIT's (59 in).
  - The Pi 5 supplies from Geekworm, RasTech, GeeekPi, iRasptek and Vilros don't give a length in
    their search results, and I didn't open each one.
  - The only 5 V supplies with 3 m or 5 m leads are 5 V / 2 A (10 W) adapters for security cameras,
    with USB-A to C cables. That's too little power, and with their thin cable the model puts the
    Pi at 4.17 V even at 1.5 A.
- **Chargers sold with 10 ft cables only give 3 A at 5 V.** An example is VOWSVOWS's 45 W 2-pack
  with two detachable 10 ft USB-C cables, at $27.99. Through 3 m of even a 5 A cable that's 4.64 V
  at 1.5 A and 4.39 V at 2.5 A: no better than the official supply through an extension.
- **The best 5 V-only option starts higher.** The [iUniker 5 V 4 A, B097P2NLVH](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B097P2NLVH)
  ($9.99, in stock) outputs 5.25 V on a 1.5 m lead. With the 2 m 240 W extension already on the
  list, that gives 4.90 V at 1.5 A and 4.66 V at 2.5 A, if its lead is 18 AWG.
  - That's 0.1 V better than the official 27 W supply through the same extension.
  - It has no USB-PD, so the Pi caps its USB ports at 600 mA and warns at boot. CSI cameras don't
    use USB, so that doesn't matter here.
  - No listing gives a wire gauge, so like the extension it's a cheap test, not a design.
- **What reaches with margin is 12 V on the USB-C cable, turned into 5 V on the carrier.** The
  [eleUniverse 5 V 5 A PD step-down board for Pi 5, B0FR8VRWFJ](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FR8VRWFJ)
  ($20.99, in stock) does this.
  - **What it does:** it asks a USB-C PD charger for 12 V and puts out 5 V at 5 A. Its input
    takes 9 to 24 V, and it also has a 5.5 × 2.1 mm DC jack.
  - **Margin:** at 2.5 A to the Pi it draws about 1.2 A at 12 V. Through 3 m of cable the board
    still sees 11.7 V and the Pi gets a regulated 5.1 V.
  - **The cable:** any 10 ft 240 W USB-C cable from the 27 September list will do. So will the
    official supply's own lead plus the 2 m extension, since the official supply offers 12 V /
    2.25 A.
  - **Size:** 34 g. It has USB-C and USB-A outputs, a jumper that adds 0.2 V, and a fan that runs
    above 3 A.
  - **Not verified:** whether the USB-C output speaks PD. If it doesn't, set
    `PSU_MAX_CURRENT=5000`.
- **The charger has to offer 12 V.** Many phone and laptop chargers offer only 5, 9, 15 and 20 V.
  Chargers sold for the Pi 5 list it, for example the
  [Vilros 27 W VILP274, B0CVJ7S3NF](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CVJ7S3NF) ($18.99, 10 left), which
  ships with a separate USB cable, and Raspberry Pi's own 27 W and 45 W.
  - I didn't confirm that the Vilros has a USB-C socket rather than a captive lead.
  - Its listing gives 5 V / 3 A in one place and 5 V / 5 A in another, which doesn't matter on
    this route.
- **PD-trigger-to-barrel cables and 12/24 V to 5 V converters are plentiful** (search 5 in the JSON).
  The PlusRoc converter from 27 September, $15.99, is potted and has no PD.

## The routes compared

| Route to the wrist | at 1.5 A | at 2.5 A |
|---|---|---|
| Official 27 W supply + 2 m 240 W extension, if 20 AWG (already on the list) | 4.78 V | 4.57 V (low) |
| iUniker 5.25 V / 4 A + the same extension, if its lead is 18 AWG | 4.90 V | 4.66 V |
| Any PD charger's 5 V / 3 A + a 3 m 240 W cable | 4.64 V | 4.39 V (low) |
| 5 V / 2 A camera adapter, its own 3 m cable, if 24 AWG | 4.17 V (low) | 3.62 V (low) |
| PD charger at 12 V + 3 m 240 W cable + step-down board on the carrier | 5.10 V | 5.10 V (board sees 11.7 V) |
| Official supply at 12 V + 2 m 240 W extension + step-down board on the carrier | 5.10 V | 5.10 V (board sees 11.7 V) |
| 24 V Mean Well + 3.5 m of 22 AWG + PlusRoc on the carrier (the 27 September plan) | 5.10 V | 5.10 V (converter sees 23.8 V) |
