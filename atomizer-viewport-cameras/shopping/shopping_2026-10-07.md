# Shopping list: three Pi 5 camera stations for the AMAZEMET rePowder (7 October 2026)

This is a priced bill of materials for three Raspberry Pi 5 stations that watch the atomizer's viewing
windows. Prices are as shown on 7 October 2026. Amazon pages were loaded through a lab Pi's campus
connection, and maker and reseller pages were fetched directly. Every candidate is in
[`shopping_2026-10-07.json`](shopping_2026-10-07.json), with its URL, specs and how it was checked.

**Amazon was running Prime Big Deal Days (7–8 October).** Some Amazon prices below are temporary sale
prices. Where Amazon showed a struck-through list price, the JSON records it.

## What the search found

- **Pi 5 boards cost about twice what they did a year ago.** Raspberry Pi has raised prices four times
  because memory costs rose: December 2025, February 2026, April 2026, and on 1 October 2026 for the 2 GB.
  List prices are now 2 GB $77.50, 4 GB $110 and 8 GB $175 (16 GB $305). PiShop.us sells at list, and all
  three were in stock. The 4 GB costs $126.49 on Amazon (only 3 left) and $130 at Adafruit.
- **Official Raspberry Pi parts cost far less at PiShop.us than from Amazon resellers.** On Amazon the HQ
  Camera was $99.99 (PiShop $55), the M12 HQ Camera $93.99 (PiShop $50), the 5-inch Touch Display 2
  $89.99 (PiShop $54.95) and the official 64 GB card $79.99 (PiShop $39.95). So every Raspberry Pi item
  is picked from PiShop, as one order.
- **The 5-inch Touch Display 2 exists.** Raspberry Pi launched it on 19 August 2025 at a $40 list price.
  It is 720 × 1280, with a 143.5 × 91.5 mm outline and a 110.4 × 62.1 mm active area, and it comes with
  the Pi 5's 22-pin cable. PiShop charges $54.95 and has it in stock.
- **The M12 HQ Camera (SC0870) comes without a lens**, so a lens has to be bought for it. Its back focus
  range is 2.6–11.8 mm. No lens seller lists a minimum focus distance, so whether these M12 lenses focus
  at 150–300 mm is unverified. Check this on the bench.
- **The filter threads rest on one source each, so measure before buying filters.** The Raspberry Pi
  16 mm lens is 37 mm according to The Pi Hut's spec table, with no pitch given. The Arducam 8–50 mm is
  M37.5 × 0.5 according to a snippet of Arducam's own page and a Newegg listing. 37 mm and 37.5 mm
  filters won't screw onto each other's lenses. To use one filter set on both lenses, step both up to
  46 mm (Sensei 37.5→46 mm is $4.95 at B&H).
- **The HQ Camera already has an IR-cut filter on the sensor.** At the top window, the hot mirror's job
  is to keep the melt's near-IR heat off the lens, and the ND filter brings the brightness into range.
  A shade-5 welding plate does the second job for $10 and can sit in a printed slot.
- **No display maker states its mounting-hole spacing as text.** Waveshare, LCDwiki and Elecrow show the
  holes only in drawing images. Elecrow's RC050 and LCDwiki's "5inch HDMI Display-B/C" share a
  121.11 × 95.24 mm outline (LCDwiki gives a 108.0 × 64.8 mm active area), so they look like one shared design.
  Download the drawing before designing the bezel.
- **Bambu's PETG HF is being discontinued** and won't be restocked once sold out. Bambu's PETG Basic
  and Polymaker's PETG are the same material class.
- **No bot check appeared.** None of the Pi-routed pages showed a CAPTCHA or "sorry" page.

## How the search was done

I used headed Chrome on the CI runner, driven only by X input. Its traffic went through a SOCKS tunnel
(Tailscale SSH `-D`) to the CubXL Pi, capped at 300 kB/s, using `tools/headed-browser` from branch
`claude/issue-30-20260925-0724`. The tunnel was the Pi's only job; nothing ran on it. The Pi exits
through BYU's campus network (AS6510), and Amazon placed the delivery location in Provo.

- **35 page loads went through the Pi:** the Amazon home page once and Amazon search results 34 times,
  counted from the browser's own history. One search (the Active Cooler) was loaded twice by mistake. No
  product pages were opened. Every Amazon figure comes from the search-results listings.
- **No CAPTCHA, "sorry" page or other bot check appeared.**
- **Pages were saved with Ctrl+S (HTML only), which makes no new request,** and then parsed for ASIN,
  price, struck-through price, rating, review count and stock notes.
- **Each search page was about 0.9–2.4 MB** and was read 12–16 s after the search was submitted.
